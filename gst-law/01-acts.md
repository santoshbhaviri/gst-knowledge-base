## 1. Constitutional base: 101st Amendment Act, 2016
- **Article 246A:** Parliament and the State legislatures both have power to make laws on GST. Only Parliament can legislate on inter-state supplies.
- **Article 269A:** GST on inter-state supplies (IGST) is levied by the Centre and apportioned between the Centre and the States as Parliament provides, on the Council's recommendation.
- **Article 279A:** sets up the GST Council.
  - Members: the Union FM as chair, the Union MoS (Finance), and the State FMs.
  - Decisions need three-fourths of the weighted votes: the Centre holds one-third of the votes and the States two-thirds.
  - The Council's recommendations are persuasive, not binding (Supreme Court, *Union of India v. Mohit Minerals*, 2022).
- **Article 366(12A):** defines GST as a tax on supply of goods or services, except alcoholic liquor for human consumption.
- **Five petroleum products** (crude, petrol, HSD, natural gas and ATF) are within GST in law, but GST on them starts only from a date the Council recommends. No such date has been set.
- **Section 18 of the Amendment Act:** compensation to States for 5 years. This led to the GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017.
- GST came into force on **1 July 2017**.

## 2. The Acts

| Act | Number | Who levies | Covers | Key notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CGST Act, 2017 | Act 12 of 2017 | Centre | Intra-state supplies (Central share) | 174 sections, 21 chapters, 3 schedules |
| IGST Act, 2017 | Act 13 of 2017 | Centre | Inter-state supplies, imports, exports, SEZ supplies | 25 sections, 9 chapters; borrows procedure from CGST via s.20 |
| UTGST Act, 2017 | Act 14 of 2017 | Centre | Intra-UT supplies in UTs **without a legislature** | 26 sections; borrows from CGST via s.21 |
| GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017 | Act 15 of 2017 | Centre | Cess on luxury and sin goods to fund State compensation | Compensation period ended 30.06.2022. The cess continued only to repay borrowings and ended for tobacco on 01.02.2026 |
| SGST Acts | One per State, plus Delhi, Puducherry and J&K (UTs with legislatures) | Each State | Intra-state supplies (State share) | Mirror the CGST Act section by section |
| CGST (Extension to J&K) Act, 2017 and IGST (Extension to J&K) Act, 2017 | Acts 26 and 27 of 2017 | Centre | Extended GST to J&K, which had special status at the time | Since 2019, J&K is a UT with a legislature |

The CGST, IGST, UTGST and Compensation Acts all received Presidential assent on 12 April 2017.

**UTs under UTGST:** Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Ladakh, and Chandigarh.

### How the SGST Acts relate to CGST
- **The law is the same.** Each SGST Act copies the CGST Act section by section: the same definitions, ITC, registration, returns and so on. CGST Rules are adopted as SGST Rules, and States issue matching notifications.
- **Administration differs.** Sections 3 to 6 appoint State officers. Section 6 of each Act cross-empowers Central and State officers, so a taxpayer is administered by one or the other (the 9th Council meeting set the split).
- **Section 6(2)(b) bars parallel proceedings** on the same subject matter.
- **Differences in practice:**
  - States amend their Acts separately after each Finance Act, so dates of effect can differ slightly.
  - Some States add their own levies under State law, such as Kerala's 1% flood cess (2019–21).
  - The "State tax" notification numbers differ from the CBIC numbers.
  - State circulars can add administrative guidance.
- **Practical rule:** read the CGST provision, check the matching State notification, and treat any difference as an exception.

**Provisions most relevant to this project:**
- **Section 7:** scope of supply.
- **Sections 9(3) and 9(4):** reverse charge.
- **Section 9(5):** e-commerce operators liable for tax.
- **Section 10:** composition levy.
- **Section 16(2):** ITC conditions, including 16(2)(aa), which requires the invoice to appear in GSTR-2B.
- **Section 17(5):** blocked credits.
- **Section 22:** registration thresholds of ₹20 lakh for services and ₹40 lakh for goods (lower in special-category States).
- **Section 37:** GSTR-1, and section 39 GSTR-3B.
- **Section 52:** TCS by e-commerce operators.
- **Section 54:** refunds.
- **Section 68:** documents in transit, which underpins the e-way bill.
- **Sections 73, 74 and 74A:** demands.
- **Section 122:** penalties.
- **Section 129:** detention.
- **Section 130:** confiscation.
- **Section 132:** prosecution.
- **Section 148A:** track and trace.
- **Section 150:** information returns. This is the legal basis for third-party data, which matters for the data-analytics goal.
- **Section 151:** power to collect statistics.
- **Section 158:** confidentiality.

## 4. Structure of the IGST Act, 2017 (key sections)
- **Section 5:** levy, including on imports with customs duty.
- **Section 5(3)-(4):** reverse charge.
- **Section 5(5):** e-commerce operators.
- **Sections 7 and 8:** inter-state and intra-state supply.
- **Sections 10 to 14A:** place of supply.
  - Section 10: goods.
  - Section 12: services where supplier and recipient are both in India.
  - Section 13: services where either is outside India.
  - Section 14: OIDAR (online information and database access or retrieval services).
  - Section 14A: online money gaming.
- **Section 15:** refund to tourists.
- **Section 16:** zero-rated supplies (exports and SEZ).
- **Section 17:** apportionment of IGST.
- **Section 20:** applies the CGST provisions.
