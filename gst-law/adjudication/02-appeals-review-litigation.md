# Appeals, review and litigation: a guide for the adjudicating Assistant Commissioner

**Position as on:** 14 Oct 2026
**Written for:** the Assistant Commissioner, CGST, Sangareddy Division (Medchal Commissionerate, Telangana). He passes orders that taxpayers appeal, and he handles the department's side of appeals, Tribunal cases and writ petitions.
**Part of:** the adjudication module in `/mnt/project-files/gst-law/adjudication/`.

## How to read this file

- **Sources.** Sections and rules are quoted from the current CBIC text in `gst-law/text/cgst-act.md` and `gst-law/text/cgst-rules.md`.
- **Circulars read in full from CBIC on 14 Oct 2026:** 111/30/2019, 132/2/2020, 207/1/2024, 224/18/2024, 250/07/2025, 254/11/2025 and 256/02/2026; Instructions 01/2024-GST and 02/2025-GST; Notification 05/2020-CT.
- **Case law.** Cases come from `gst-law/case-law/`. Every entry there is **Secondary**: it was built from web-search summaries, and no judgment text was read. **Read the judgment before you quote it in an order or an affidavit.**
- **What binds you:**
  - **Supreme Court:** binding on everyone.
  - **Telangana High Court:** **BINDING IN TELANGANA**.
  - **Other High Courts:** persuasive only.
  - **GSTAT:** binds the parties, and in practice is strongly persuasive.
- **Law versus proposals.** The 57th GST Council (8 Oct 2026) is **not law**. Finance Act 2026 ss.153-155 are **not in force**. Both are marked where they touch this subject.
- **Gaps.** Anything that could not be confirmed says "not confirmed" and is listed in the Gaps section at the end.

## 0. The five things that matter most

1. **The Appellate Authority cannot send your order back to you** (s.107(11)). If your order is weak, it will be modified or set aside, not remanded. Get the order right the first time: issue a full SCN, give a hearing (s.75(4)), give reasons (s.75(6)), and stay within the SCN (s.75(7)).
2. **Once the pre-deposit is paid and the appeal is filed, recovery of the balance stops by law** (s.107(7); s.112(9)). Do not attach accounts, debit ledgers or issue a DRC-13 for the stayed balance. Check the pre-deposit before you act on any demand.
3. **Departmental review runs on a strict clock:**
   - Against your order, the Commissioner must direct an application to the Appellate Authority within **6 months of communication** (s.107(2)).
   - Against an order-in-appeal, the application to GSTAT must be made within **6 months of the date the order was passed** (s.112(3)).
   - Below **₹20 lakh** (GSTAT), **₹1 crore** (High Court) and **₹2 crore** (Supreme Court), the department does not appeal unless an exclusion applies (Circular 207/1/2024-GST).
4. **The GSTAT Hyderabad bench has been hearing cases since 20 Apr 2026.** The backlog window closed on **31.07.2026**. New orders follow the normal limits of 3 months (taxpayer) and 6 months (department) under s.112. Circular 224/18/2024 (20% plus an undertaking) was the bridge while the Tribunal was not working. With GSTAT now working, it should no longer be the route for fresh orders.
5. **Follow appellate orders and Telangana HC judgments even when you disagree** (*Kamlakshi Finance*, SC). The only lawful responses are:
   - a departmental appeal or review;
   - withholding a refund by the Commissioner under s.54(11), with a hearing and reasons.

   Re-adjudication after a remand, or a fresh s.73 determination after a s.74 charge fails, must be completed **within 2 years** of the direction (s.75(3)).

## 1. Taxpayer appeal to the Appellate Authority (s.107)

### 1.1 Who hears the appeal
- **Orders of a Deputy or Assistant Commissioner or a Superintendent:** the appeal goes to an officer "not below the rank of Joint Commissioner (Appeals)" (rule 109A(1)(b)).
- **Orders of an Additional or Joint Commissioner:** the appeal goes to the Commissioner (Appeals) (rule 109A(1)(a)).
- Circular 132/2/2020-GST, para 3.3 repeats this.
- The division's refund template T8 names the appellate office for Sangareddy orders as "Commissioner (Appeals-II), Hyderabad, Basheerbagh". *The notification that maps it (Notification 02/2017-CT, Table III) was not re-checked for this file.*
- **Orders of a Common Adjudicating Authority in DGGI cases:** the appeal goes to the Commissioner (Appeals) for the area of the Commissionerate where the CAA is posted (Circular 250/07/2025-GST, para 4(c)).

### 1.2 Time limits
| Step | Limit | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Taxpayer appeal | **3 months** from the date the order is *communicated* | s.107(1) |
| Condonation | A further **1 month** only, for "sufficient cause" | s.107(4) |
| Self-certified copy of the order, if it is not on the portal | Within 7 days of filing APL-01. If filed later, the appeal counts as filed on the date the copy is submitted. | rule 108(3) |
| Appellate Authority's target to decide | **1 year** from filing, "where it is possible". Periods of stay by a court are excluded. | s.107(13) |
| Withdrawal of the appeal | Before the s.107(11) SCN or order. After APL-02 is issued, the AA must approve, and decides within 7 days. A fresh appeal must still be within s.107(1). | rule 109C (APL-01/03W) |

**Condonation beyond 3 + 1 months.**
- Several High Courts hold that the Appellate Authority cannot condone delay beyond the s.107(4) cap. They rely on *Singh Enterprises* (SC), a Central Excise case.
- The Supreme Court, on 30.08.2024, stayed a High Court ruling that s.5 of the Limitation Act applies. In July and August 2026 it issued notice on SLPs about appeals filed beyond the cap. **The point is pending.**
- Pointer: case-law/03, section 8 (*Singh Enterprises*; *GlaxoSmithKline*, SC 2020, an AP VAT case holding that a writ cannot be used to get round a time-barred appeal).

**Hearing on condonation.** The Telangana HC set aside a dismissal for delay because the appellant was not heard on the condonation application (s.107(8)). It also routinely lets taxpayers file late appeals, with condonation and pre-deposit, within windows it fixes, often 2 weeks to 45 days. **BINDING IN TELANGANA.**
- Pointers: case-law/03, section 4 (unnamed Telangana HC, s.107(8)); case-law/05, section 13 (Telangana batch, Jan 2025, 45-day window); case-law/05, section 4 (*Bollant Industries*; *Sri Sai Ram Traders*: delayed appeal allowed where the order sat only in the "Additional Notices" tab).

### 1.3 Pre-deposit (s.107(6))
No appeal can be filed unless the appellant has paid:
- (a) the **admitted** tax, interest, fine, fee and penalty, in full; and
- (b) **10% of the remaining tax in dispute**, subject to a maximum of **₹20 crore**. This cap was cut from ₹25 crore by s.141 of the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024.

**Penalty-only orders.** Where the order demands a penalty without any tax, the appellant must pay **10% of the penalty** (proviso to s.107(6), in force from **01.10.2025**, inserted by s.129 of the Finance (No. 7) Act 2025).
- Earlier, a pure penalty order needed no pre-deposit, because there was no "tax in dispute".
- The Supreme Court in *PVCON Engineering* (SLP(C) 28059/2026, 7 Sep 2026) issued notice on whether this proviso applies by reference to the SCN date or the appeal date. As interim relief it let that appellant go ahead without pre-deposit. Pointer: case-law/09, section 5.
- **Proposed, not law:** the 57th Council recommended a ₹40 crore cap (₹20 crore CGST plus ₹20 crore SGST) for penalty-only appeals under ss.107(6) and 112(8) (`05-gst-council-meetings.md`).

**Amounts paid by DRC-03 by mistake.** An amount paid through DRC-03 instead of against the demand can be adjusted towards the pre-deposit through **DRC-03A** (rule 142(2B), inserted by Notification 12/2024-CT; Circular 224/18/2024, paras 7.1 and 7.2). This is not possible once a DRC-05 has closed the DRC-03 (para 7.3).

**Pre-deposit on e-way bill seizures.** Once an appeal is filed with the pre-deposit, the sale of detained goods or a conveyance under rule 144A is deemed stayed. Perishable and hazardous goods are excepted (rule 144A(8)).

### 1.5 What the Appellate Authority can and cannot do
- **Hearing.** It must give the appellant a hearing (s.107(8)).
- **Adjournments.** It may adjourn for sufficient cause, with reasons in writing, but **not more than three times to a party** (s.107(9)).
- **New grounds.** It may allow a ground of appeal not stated in the memorandum, if the omission was not wilful or unreasonable (s.107(10)).
- **Powers on the merits.** It may confirm, modify or annul the order, "after making such further inquiry as may be necessary". It **shall not refer the case back** to the adjudicating authority (s.107(11)).
  - It can call for a remand report or verification from you as part of its "further inquiry". That is not a remand.
- **Enhancement.** It can enhance a fee, a penalty or a fine in lieu of confiscation, or reduce a refund or ITC, only after a show-cause opportunity (first proviso to s.107(11)).
  - It can demand tax that was not paid, short-paid or erroneously refunded, or ITC wrongly availed, only after a notice, and only within the s.73, 74 or 74A time limit (second proviso).
- **Reasons.** The order must state the points for determination, the decision on each, and the reasons (s.107(12)).
- **Copies.** The order is sent to the appellant, the respondent and the adjudicating authority (s.107(14)), and to the jurisdictional CGST and SGST Commissioners or their nominees (s.107(15)).
- **Finality.** The order is final, subject to ss.108, 113, 117 and 118 (s.107(16)).
- **Additional evidence (rule 112).** New evidence is admitted only on the four grounds in rule 112(1)(a)-(d), with reasons recorded (rule 112(2)). **You, as adjudicating authority, must be given a reasonable chance to examine it, cross-examine and lead rebuttal evidence (rule 112(3)).** Ask for this in writing whenever fresh documents are filed in appeal.
- **Converting s.74 to s.73.** When a s.74 charge fails, the s.73 re-determination under s.75(2) is done by the **adjudicating officer**, not the Appellate Authority.
  - Circular 254/11/2025-GST, para 6, makes the officer who adjudicated the s.74 SCN the proper officer for s.75(2).
  - GSTAT Principal Bench, *Sterling & Wilson* (APL/1/PB/2026, 11.02.2026), held that neither the first appellate authority nor GSTAT can itself confirm a s.73 demand. Pointer: case-law/09, section 8.
- **s.74A cases.** If fraud is not established, the penalty becomes the clause (i) penalty under s.74A(5) (s.75(2A)).
- **Interest and penalty follow tax.** If the tax is modified in appeal, interest and penalty stand modified with it (s.75(8)).

### 1.6 What the adjudicating authority must send and do
- **The statute imposes no express duty** to send records or comments to the Appellate Authority. Section 107 and rules 108-113 are silent.
- In practice, the Appellate Authority's notice asks for the case records and comments. The Act gives you a statutory right only under rule 112(3) (additional evidence) and as the "respondent" who receives the order (s.107(14)).
- *No CBIC circular or instruction setting a format or time limit for parawise comments on a taxpayer's appeal was found in the CBIC index (see Gaps).* Good practice:
  1. Send the full file **promptly**:
     - the SCN with its RUD list and DRC-01;
     - proof of service (s.169);
     - the reply;
     - hearing notices and the record of each hearing;
     - the order with its DRC-07;
     - the DIN or RFN;
     - the pre-deposit verification.
  2. Send **parawise comments** on each ground of appeal. Defend the order on its own reasons; do not add new grounds. The Supreme Court in *G.R. Infra Projects* (19.08.2026) held that defects in an SCN cannot be cured later by an affidavit, and the same logic applies to appeal comments. Pointer: case-law/03, section 6.
  3. Confirm the pre-deposit for each demand ID in Electronic Liability Register Part II, and mark the demand as stayed (s.107(7)).
  4. Attend the hearing, or send a representative, if the Appellate Authority allows it.

## 2. Departmental review, appeal and revision

### 2.1 Review of your order by the Commissioner (s.107(2) and (3))
- **What.** The Commissioner, on his own motion or at the request of the State or UT Commissioner, examines the record "for the purpose of satisfying himself as to the legality or propriety" of the order.
- **Outcome.** If he finds the order wrong, he directs a subordinate officer to apply to the Appellate Authority.
- **Time limit.** The application must be made **within 6 months from the date of communication of the order** (s.107(2)). Condonation is limited to a further 1 month (s.107(4)).
- **Form.** APL-03 (rule 109). It is heard as if it were an appeal, with the authorised officer as appellant (s.107(3)).
- **Who reviews CAA orders in DGGI cases.** The Principal Commissioner or Commissioner under whom the CAA is posted. He may seek DGGI's comments (Circular 250/07/2025-GST, paras 4(a), (d) and (e)).
- **Pre-deposit.** The department pays none. Section 107(6) applies to appeals under s.107(1) only.
- **Review committee.** **Not confirmed for GST.** The CGST Act vests review in "the Commissioner" alone (s.107(2), s.112(3)). A "Committee of Commissioners or Chief Commissioners" for deciding appeals exists in the pre-GST Customs and Central Excise structure (ss.129D and 35B). A departmental SOP on appellate litigation (DLA, CBIC) refers to it, but that SOP was not shown to apply to GST. For CGST orders, treat the reviewing authority as the jurisdictional Principal Commissioner or Commissioner (Gaps).
- **Monetary limits for a review appeal to the Appellate Authority.** Circular 207/1/2024-GST covers only **GSTAT, the High Court and the Supreme Court** (para 2). **No CBIC monetary limit was found for departmental applications to the first Appellate Authority under s.107(2).**
- **What to put in the review file.** The order, a short note on each point you consider wrong (on the merits or on revenue risk), the communication date, and the 6-month deadline in bold.

### 2.2 Monetary limits for departmental appeals: Circular 207/1/2024-GST (26.06.2024)
**The circular is numbered 207/1/2024-GST, not "207/1/2023".** It was issued under s.120 read with s.168, on the GST Council's recommendation. No later circular replacing it is in the CBIC index up to Circular 256 (25.07.2026).

| Forum | No appeal, application or SLP below |
|---|---|
| GSTAT | ₹20,00,000 |
| High Court | ₹1,00,00,000 |
| Supreme Court | ₹2,00,00,000 |

**How to count the amount (para 3):**
- **Tax disputes:** count only the aggregate *tax* in dispute, across CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST and cess. Interest and penalty are not added.
- **Interest only, penalty only or late fee only:** count that amount.
- **Combinations without tax:** add interest, penalty and late fee together.
- **Erroneous refund:** count the refund in dispute.
- **Composite orders** covering several appeals or notices: use the total.

**When the limits do not apply (para 4).** The decision to appeal is then taken on the merits, whatever the amount:
- a provision, rule, notification or circular has been held ultra vires;
- the issue concerns valuation, classification, refunds or place of supply, or any other recurring or interpretative issue;
- strictures, adverse comments or costs have been ordered against the department or its officers;
- any case the Board wants contested.

**What to record and say:**
- An amount above the limit is not a reason to appeal by itself; decide on the merits (para 5).
- Where no appeal is filed only because of the limit, the reviewing authority must record: *"even though the decision is not acceptable, appeal is not being filed as the amount involved is less than the monetary limit fixed by the Board"* (para 6). Such non-filing is not acquiescence (s.120(2)-(4); paras 6.1-6.3).
- When a taxpayer cites an "accepted" order, check whether it was accepted only because of the monetary limit before following it.

**Instruction 02/2025-GST (07.02.2025).** Where the tax under a s.73 demand has been paid in full, and the department appealed (or is reviewing) only on interest arithmetic or on penalty, and the taxpayer qualifies for s.128A waiver, the proper officer may withdraw the appeal or accept the order (para 4).

### 2.3 Revision (s.108)
- **Who revises.** For orders of a Deputy or Assistant Commissioner or a Superintendent, the **Additional or Joint Commissioner** is the Revisional Authority (Notification 05/2020-CT, 13.01.2020, clause (b)). For orders of an Additional or Joint Commissioner, it is the Principal Commissioner or Commissioner (clause (a)). For CAA orders in DGGI cases, see Circular 250/07/2025, para 4(b).
- **When it can act.** Where an order is "erroneous in so far as it is prejudicial to the interest of revenue" and is illegal or improper, or ignores material facts, or follows a CAG observation (s.108(1)).
- **Powers.** It may stay the order, and, after a hearing, enhance, modify or annul it.
- **Bars (s.108(2)).** No revision if:
  - (a) the order is already under appeal under s.107, 112, 117 or 118;
  - (b) the **s.107(2) six-month period has not yet expired**, or **more than 3 years have passed** since the order;
  - (c) the order has already been taken up in revision; or
  - (d) the order is itself a revision order.
  - Proviso: points not raised and decided in an appeal can still be revised, within 1 year of the appellate order or the 3 years, whichever is later.
- **Procedure.** Notice in **RVN-01** with a hearing (rule 109B(1)). Summary in **APL-04** (rule 109B(2)).
- **Appeal.** A revision order is appealable to GSTAT (s.112(1)).
- **Practical sequence.** Review under s.107(2) comes first, within 6 months. Revision under s.108 is possible only after that period ends, and within 3 years.

### 2.4 Non-appealable orders (s.121)
No appeal lies against:
- a transfer of proceedings;
- seizure or retention of books and documents;
- sanction of prosecution;
- an order under s.80 (payment in instalments).

## 3. GST Appellate Tribunal (ss.109-113)

### 3.1 Constitution and benches
- **Establishment.** GSTAT hears appeals against orders of the Appellate Authority and the Revisional Authority (s.109(1), as substituted from 01.08.2023).
- **Principal Bench, New Delhi.** The President, a Judicial Member, a Technical Member (Centre) and a Technical Member (State) (s.109(3)).
- **State Benches.** Two Judicial Members, a Technical Member (Centre) and a Technical Member (State) (s.109(4)).
- **Cases only the Principal Bench hears:** any case where one of the issues is **place of supply**; anti-profiteering (s.171(2)) matters; and other classes the Government notifies (provisos to s.109(5)).
  - From 01.04.2026, the Principal Bench also hears National Appellate Authority for Advance Ruling appeals (Notification 02/2026-CT; Finance Act 2026 s.156).
- **Single-Member benches.** Possible where the amount does not exceed ₹50 lakh and **no question of law** is involved, with the President's approval (s.109(8)).
  - Rule 110A (from 22.09.2025): the President or Vice-President screens these. A single Member must send the case back if a question of law emerges. The ₹50 lakh is counted across all issues and periods in the order.
  - The GSTAT President's order of 14.05.2026 sends all matters first to Division Benches (case-law/09, section 8, Secondary).
- **Territorial jurisdiction of benches:** notified by S.O. 3048(E) dated 31.07.2024, as amended (cited in Circular 256/02/2026, para 5).
- **Status (secondary sources, case-law/03 section 9 and case-law/09 section 8):**
  - President sworn in on 06.05.2024. Tribunal formally launched on 24.09.2025.
  - **Principal Bench** working since late 2025. First second-appeal judgment: *Sterling & Wilson*, 11.02.2026.
  - **Hyderabad State Bench** (covers Telangana CGST, IGST and TGST appeals) began hearing on **20.04.2026**, in a temporary office at GST Bhavan, Basheerbagh.
  - The members named in the sources (Justice Sushil Kumar Sharma, Justice A.P. Ravi, Dr D.K. Srinivas) are *not confirmed from an official notification*.
  - A sitting arrangement was revised from 05.10.2026 (single source).
  - **No Hyderabad bench decision had been reported by 14.10.2026.**

**Backlog deadline. The sources conflict; record both versions:**
- Before GSTAT existed, the Ninth Removal of Difficulties Order (Order 9/2019-CT, 03.12.2019) counted the 3 or 6 months from the later of (i) communication and (ii) the date the President or State President entered office (Circular 132/2/2020, para 4.2).
- **S.O. 4220(E), 17.09.2025:** appeals against orders communicated **before 01.04.2026** to be filed by **30.06.2026** (case-law/03, section 9; case-law/09, section 8).
- **Notification of 30.06.2026:** the date extended to **31.07.2026**.
  - Most sources say it covers taxpayer appeals against orders communicated **before 01.05.2026** and departmental applications against orders **passed before 01.02.2026**. One source says 01.04.2026.
  - It is reported as **S.O. 3502(E)**. The number comes from one source only (Moore Singhi), and the Gazette was not seen.
  - This notification is **not on the CBIC notification list** (`03-notifications.md`: "not backed by any notification on CBIC"); it may be a Department of Revenue S.O.
- **No extension beyond 31.07.2026 was found.** For orders after the cut-offs, use the normal s.112 limits.
- A GSTAT Principal Bench order of 10.07.2026 created a token mechanism for the rush of filings under rule 123 of the GSTAT Procedure Rules. Reported as not extending limitation (single source).
- **For the department:** list every order-in-appeal passed since 01.02.2026 and check that each review decision under s.112(3) is made inside 6 months from the **date of the order**.

### 3.3 Pre-deposit for GSTAT (s.112(8))
- **What must be paid:** (a) the admitted amounts in full; plus (b) **10% of the remaining tax in dispute, in addition to the s.107(6) deposit**, capped at **₹20 crore**.
  - Rates and caps as substituted by s.143 of the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024. The earlier figures were 20% and ₹50 crore; *the prior figures were not shown in the CBIC text read*.
- **Penalty-only orders:** 10% of the penalty, in addition to the first-stage 10% (proviso, from 01.10.2025).
- **Case law (pointers to case-law/09, section 8; all Secondary):**
  - *Manoranjan Dash* (GSTAT Cuttack, 2026): no further deposit was demanded where the first-stage deposit already covered the requirement.
  - Orissa HC (15.05.2026, party not named): the s.112(8) deposit is mandatory.
  - *PVCON Engineering* (SC interim, 07.09.2026): the timing of the penalty-only pre-deposit is under challenge.
  - **Before objecting to admission, check what was already paid at the first stage.** Do not take coercive steps against an appellant who holds a Supreme Court interim order.
- **Fees (rule 110(5)):** ₹1,000 per ₹1 lakh of tax, ITC, fine, fee or penalty involved, with a **minimum of ₹5,000** and a **maximum of ₹25,000**. ₹5,000 where the order has no demand. No fee for rectification applications (rule 110(6)).
- **Admission threshold:** GSTAT may decline to admit an appeal where the amount involved does not exceed **₹50,000** (s.112(2)).

- A GSTAT order reported that the departmental nodal officer must be **mapped on the portal** so that appeal papers reach the officer defending the case. **Check that the Medchal Commissionerate's nodal officer and your division are mapped.**

### 3.5 GSTAT (Procedure) Rules, 2025: key points (secondary; the Gazette was not read)
- **Notification:** G.S.R. 256(E) dated **24.04.2025**, made under s.111, with a corrigendum of 18.06.2025. Reported as 15 chapters, 124 rules and GSTAT FORM-01 to FORM-08.
- **Adjournments:** at most three per party. This matches the statutory limit in s.113(2).
- **Order pronouncement:** reported as within 30 days of the final hearing, excluding vacations. The wording varies between sources.
- **Defects:** reported as to be cured within 7 working days of return, after which the matter goes to the Registrar. A GSTAT committee has *proposed* 15 working days, but that is not adopted.
- **Rule numbers** for these points were **not confirmed**. Read G.S.R. 256(E) before citing any of them.
- **Statutory powers in s.111 (from the Act itself):**
  - GSTAT is not bound by the CPC but by natural justice.
  - It has civil-court powers to summon, require discovery, receive evidence on affidavit and requisition records.
  - It can dismiss for default and restore.
  - Its orders are executable like decrees.

### 3.6 Orders of GSTAT (s.113)
- **Unlike the first appellate authority, GSTAT can remand** to the Appellate Authority, the Revisional Authority or the original adjudicating authority, with directions for fresh adjudication (s.113(1)).
- **Copies** go to the authority concerned, the appellant and the jurisdictional Commissioners (s.113(5)).
- **Finality:** the order is final, subject to ss.117 and 118 (s.113(6)).
- **Early GSTAT signals on how it reviews officers' orders (case-law/09, section 8; Secondary):**
  - *Sterling & Wilson* (Principal Bench, 11.02.2026): a GSTR-1 versus GSTR-3B mismatch alone does not support s.74. Once s.74 fails, only the proper officer re-determines under s.73. Case remanded.
  - *Augustan Textile Colours* (Ernakulam, Sep 2026): a refund cannot be rejected as "ineligible ITC" without a s.73 or s.74 order. A vague SCN and an appellate finding outside the SCN vitiated the order.

### 3.7 Departmental applications to GSTAT (s.112(3) and (4))
- **Mechanism.** The Commissioner examines the order-in-appeal or revision order and directs a subordinate officer to apply in **APL-07** within **6 months from the date the order was passed** (s.112(3)). The application is heard as an appeal (s.112(4)).
- **Monetary limit.** ₹20 lakh, subject to the exclusions (Circular 207/1/2024).
- **CAA (DGGI) cases (Circular 256/02/2026-GST, para 6):**
  - The Appellate Authority emails and posts a copy of the order-in-appeal to the Commissioner over the CAA.
  - That Commissioner sends comments to the Commissioner with jurisdiction over each noticee.
  - **The noticee's jurisdictional Commissioner is the reviewing authority under s.112(3).**
  - **A separate appeal is filed for each noticee**, before the GSTAT bench with territorial jurisdiction over that noticee.
  - After filing, or after deciding not to file, the CAA's Commissioner must be informed.
  - **For Sangareddy:** where a DGGI case decided by a CAA elsewhere involves a noticee in this division, the Medchal Commissioner reviews the order and the Hyderabad bench hears the appeal.

## 4. High Court and Supreme Court

### 4.1 Statutory appeals (ss.117-119)
- **To the High Court.** From orders of a **State Bench** of GSTAT, only if the case involves a **substantial question of law** (s.117(1)).
  - Time limit: **180 days** from receipt of the order; delay can be condoned for sufficient cause (s.117(2)).
  - Form APL-08 (rule 114).
  - Heard by at least two judges, only on the question formulated (s.117(3) and (6)).
  - Effect is given on the certified copy (s.117(8)).
- **To the Supreme Court:**
  - directly from orders of the **Principal Bench** (s.118(1)(a)), for example place-of-supply cases; or
  - from a High Court judgment under s.117 that the High Court certifies as fit for appeal (s.118(1)(b)).
- **Sums remain payable.** Amounts due under a GSTAT order or a s.117 High Court order remain payable even though an appeal is pending in the High Court or the Supreme Court (s.119), unless a court stays them.
- **Final demand.** After the High Court or Supreme Court decides, the jurisdictional officer issues **APL-04** showing the final demand (rule 115).
- **Time to file in the Supreme Court.** Not stated in the CGST Act; s.118(2) applies the CPC. *The 90-day SLP limit commonly applied comes from general knowledge, not from a source in this project; not confirmed.*

### 4.2 Writ petitions under Article 226
**When the High Court will hear a writ despite the appeal remedy.**
- *Radha Krishan Industries v. State of H.P.* (SC, 20.04.2021, CA 1155/2021): an alternative remedy is a rule of discretion, not a bar. Writs lie where:
  - natural justice has been breached;
  - the authority lacks jurisdiction;
  - a fundamental right is in issue; or
  - a provision's vires is challenged.
- *The exact list in that judgment was not confirmed from the text.* Pointer: case-law/03, section 8.
- **What this means for you:** an order passed without a hearing, without reasons, on unproved service, or beyond the SCN can be quashed in a writ, **even though an appeal lies**.

**Telangana HC patterns (BINDING IN TELANGANA).** Pointers are to case-law/05.
- **Writs against SCNs are generally not entertained:** reply to the officer first (*S. Shivraj Reddy Constructions*, section 6).
- **Signature and DIN/RFN (changed 14.10.2026):** a Telangana **Full Bench** in *Gudla Vanaja* (30.09.2026, W.P. 20397/2025 batch, paras 154-155) held that SCNs and orders electronically authenticated and uploaded on the portal are valid without a visible signature, but that **a notice or order without a DIN or RFN is invalid**; limitation for appeal runs from the upload date. It overruled *Bigleap Technologies* (DB, 28.02.2025, W.P. 21101/2024) and supersedes *Silver Oak Villas*; the decisions that followed *Bigleap* (*Water Tech Engineers*; *Lakshmi Enterprises*) should not be relied on. See case-law/03, section 1.
- **No personal hearing means the order is set aside:** s.75(4) is the primary authority; *B.K. Steels* (W.P. 18654 and 18766 of 2024, 25.07.2024) set aside an order on that ground, but by consent, so its precedent weight is limited (case-law/03, section 4).
- **Portal-only service in the "Additional Notices" tab:** the Court allows a late appeal rather than deciding the merits (*Bollant Industries*; *Sri Sai Ram Traders*, section 4).
- **Writs filed only because the appeal is time-barred:** *GlaxoSmithKline* (SC 2020) says these should not be entertained. The Telangana HC has noted it but often still gives a window to appeal (case-law/03, section 8).

### 4.3 The department's role in High Court litigation
**Parawise comments and counter-affidavit.**
- The Act sets no procedure for these; it comes from Commissionerate litigation practice and the High Court's rules. *No GST-specific CBIC instruction setting time limits for counter-affidavits was found.*
- **Points for the AC preparing parawise comments:**
  1. Admit or deny each paragraph, with the documents.
  2. Prove service: portal upload plus the s.169 mode, with dates.
  3. Prove the hearing dates under s.75(4).
  4. Show the order's reasons. **Do not add new reasons:** *G.R. Infra* (SC 2026) held that defects in an SCN cannot be cured through a counter-affidavit (case-law/03, section 6).
  5. Plead the alternative remedy, citing *Radha Krishan* on its limits and *S. Shivraj Reddy* at the SCN stage.
  6. Plead limitation for late petitions (*GlaxoSmithKline*).
  7. Say whether the pre-deposit has been paid and whether recovery is stayed.

**Complying with High Court orders.**
- Comply within the time the Court fixes. Diarise every direction, for example "decide within 4 weeks" or "allow appeal within 45 days".
- An order to re-adjudicate is an order "in pursuance of the direction of ... a court", so **s.75(3)'s 2-year limit** applies (see section 6.3).
- Seek a stay or file an appeal through proper channels if the department disagrees; **never just ignore the order**.
- The Telangana HC has imposed costs on officers in clear cases: *Satyam Shivam Papers*, 02.06.2021, SLP dismissed 12.01.2022 (case-law/05, section 2).

**Decisions to appeal or file an SLP.**
- Monetary limits: High Court ₹1 crore, Supreme Court ₹2 crore, subject to the exclusions (Circular 207/1/2024).
- A CBIC instruction (reported by Taxscan, about October 2020; **original not seen**) says:
  - the Central Commissionerate authorised to defend a High Court matter stays responsible for remedial action, such as a review, writ appeal or SLP, after the judgment, even where the taxpayer is under State administration;
  - the Board processes SLPs;
  - zones should coordinate with State GST to avoid divergent views.
- *No GST-specific CBIC instruction fixing internal time limits for sending SLP proposals, or a committee for SLP decisions, was found* (Gaps). Until it is confirmed:
  - send the proposal, with the certified copy and a note on the substantial question of law, **as soon as the judgment is received**, through the Commissioner;
  - keep the 90-day SLP period in view (general knowledge, not confirmed).

### 4.4 Duty to follow appellate orders and the jurisdictional High Court
- *Union of India v. Kamlakshi Finance Corporation* (SC, 1991, reported 1992 Supp (1) SCC 648; the citation is not fully confirmed) holds that lower authorities must follow the orders of higher appellate authorities **unless they have been stayed**. Refusing to do so causes "undue harassment to assessees and chaos in administration of tax laws". Pointer: case-law/04, Part B-II.
- **Telangana HC judgments bind you.** Board circulars bind you but not the courts, and a circular contrary to a High Court or Supreme Court ruling cannot be enforced (*Ratan Melting & Wire*, SC Constitution Bench 2008; case-law/04, Part B-II).
- **Pending department appeal.** An appeal by the department does **not** by itself suspend the appellate order. Implement it, and make the implementation "subject to the outcome of the department's appeal" (template T8, para 11.5).

## 5. Stay and recovery while an appeal is pending

| Situation | Effect | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Order passed; appeal period running | No recovery for **3 months** from service, unless the proper officer, **for reasons recorded in writing**, requires earlier payment | s.78 and proviso |
| Early recovery needed | AC/DC places the case before the Pr. Commissioner or Commissioner with reasons. The **Commissioner** records reasons and issues directions. Only if the taxpayer defaults does the AC/DC recover under s.79(1). Reasons must rest on credible evidence of risk (closure, insolvency, likely default), "not ... in a mechanical manner". | **Instruction 01/2024-GST (30.05.2024), paras 3.1-3.4**; Circular 3/3/2017 (proper officer under the s.78 proviso is the Pr. Commissioner or Commissioner) |
| Appeal to the AA filed with the pre-deposit | **Recovery of the balance is deemed stayed** | s.107(7) |
| Appeal to GSTAT filed with the pre-deposit | Recovery of the balance deemed stayed **till disposal** | s.112(9) |
| Department appeal or application pending | **No stay** of the order in the taxpayer's favour. The refund must still be paid, unless the Commissioner withholds it under s.54(11) after a hearing. | s.54(11); case-law/09, Quick answer 4 (Orissa, Delhi, Patna and Telangana HCs) |
| High Court or Supreme Court appeal pending | Amounts due under the GSTAT or High Court order remain payable unless a court stays them | s.119 |

**Circular 224/18/2024-GST (11.07.2024): when GSTAT was not functioning.**
- If the taxpayer wanted to appeal an order-in-appeal to GSTAT, it could:
  1. pay an amount equal to the s.112(8) pre-deposit through *Services > Ledgers > Payment towards demand*, which maps the payment to the order in Electronic Liability Register Part II; and
  2. file an **undertaking** with the jurisdictional proper officer to appeal within the s.112 timelines (read with Order 9/2019-CT).
- Recovery of the balance then **stood stayed** under s.112(9) (paras 4 and 5).
- Without the payment or the undertaking, recovery could proceed (para 6). If no appeal is filed within time once GSTAT operates, the balance is recovered (paras 6 and 7.2).
- *Case:* the Telangana HC (Apr 2026, party not named) disposed of a writ directing compliance with Circular 224 and S.O. 4220(E): the deposit plus an undertaking within 15 days, after which recovery was stayed. **BINDING IN TELANGANA.** Pointer: case-law/05, section 13. The summary calls the deposit "20%". At the time, s.112(8) required 10% in addition to the first-stage 10%, a total of 20% of the disputed tax.
- **Now that GSTAT is working:**
  - Any taxpayer who relied on Circular 224 had to file APL-05 within the backlog window (31.07.2026, subject to the conflict noted in 3.2).
  - **If no appeal was filed, the stay lapses and the balance is recoverable** (para 6).
  - For orders-in-appeal after the cut-off, the ordinary route applies: file APL-05 with the pre-deposit. Circular 224 has not been withdrawn on the CBIC list, but its premise ("till Appellate Tribunal comes into operation") no longer holds.

**Recovery after the appeal period ends.**
- If no appeal is filed within 3 months (+1 month), or the appeal is dismissed and no further appeal with pre-deposit is filed, recover under s.79. Recover only what remains after the pre-deposit.
- Watch for:
  - **pending condonation applications** (*Bollant*-type High Court windows);
  - **Supreme Court interim orders** (*PVCON*).
- Case pointers (case-law/03, section 10; Secondary):
  - *Cargotec India* (Madras HC 2024): no recovery inside 3 months without recorded reasons.
  - Calcutta and Delhi HCs: once the pre-deposit is paid and the appeal filed, attachments must be lifted.
  - Madras HC 03.04.2026: an overdraft account cannot be attached.
  - *Kesari Nandan Mobile* (SC 2025 INSC 983) and *Greenfinch* (Telangana HC 22.12.2022, **BINDING IN TELANGANA**): a s.83 provisional attachment lapses after one year.

## 6. Implementing appellate orders

### 6.1 Refund of the pre-deposit (template T8)
- **Template.** `/mnt/project-files/gst-orders/templates/T8-appeal-pre-deposit.docx` (Markdown copy in `templates/markdown/T8-appeal-pre-deposit.md`).
- **Application.** The taxpayer files RFD-01 under "On account of assessment/provisional assessment/appeal/any other order", quoting the appellate order or the payment reference of the s.107(6) or s.112(8) amount (rule 89(2)(a)).
- **Limitation.** 2 years from the **date of communication** of the appellate order (s.54(1), Explanation 2(d)). T8's drafting note says: check the jurisdictional High Court's view on whether a pre-deposit, which is not tax, is subject to s.54(1) before rejecting a claim as time-barred.
- **Interest (s.115).** Interest at the s.56 rate runs **from the date of payment of the pre-deposit to the date of refund**. Unlike s.56, there is no 60-day waiting period.
  - Which rate applies, 6% under the main part of s.56 or 9% under its proviso for orders that have attained finality, is not settled in the text. T8 para 9.6 asks the officer to record the basis adopted.
  - The 57th Council proposes to make s.115 a standalone provision (not law).
- **Interest on other amounts.** Amounts paid towards the demand beyond the pre-deposit (for example by DRC-03) carry s.56 interest only for delay beyond 60 days from the application. The rate is 9% under the proviso if the order has attained finality (s.56).
- **Department's appeal is pending.** This is not a ground to refuse or delay the refund. Only the Commissioner can withhold, under s.54(11), with a hearing and recorded reasons (T8 para 6.3 and drafting note; case-law/09, Quick answer 4).
- **Partial relief or GSTAT pending.** Where the upheld part is before GSTAT, the amounts paid towards it are retained, because s.112(8)(b) requires the GSTAT deposit "in addition to" the s.107(6) deposit (T8 para 7.1).
- **GSTAT remand.** Whether the pre-deposit becomes refundable on remand is not settled in the statute. T8 Option C asks the officer to record the basis adopted (Gaps).

### 6.2 Re-credit and refund orders allowed in appeal
- **Rejected refunds and the credit ledger.** An ITC amount debited for a refund that was rejected is not re-credited while an appeal is pending. A refund counts as rejected only when the appeal is finally rejected, or when the claimant undertakes in writing not to appeal (rule 93(2) and Explanation).
- **Circular 111/30/2019-GST.** Where a rejection (RFD-06) is reversed in appeal:
  - the taxpayer files a **fresh** RFD-01 under the "appeal/any other order" category, without debiting the ledger again (para 3);
  - the proper officer sanctions the refund by RFD-06 and RFD-05, and **re-credits** any amount still rejected, through RFD-01B under the original ARN and PMT-03, provided no further appeal is pursued (paras 4 and 5).
- **Interest.** Under the Explanation to s.56, an appellate order allowing a refund is deemed to be an order under s.54(5). Interest under the proviso to s.56 (up to 9%) applies where the order has attained finality.
- **APL-04 / APL-04A.** Use the summary of the final demand to correct Electronic Liability Register Part II. Reduce or drop the demand ID so that no recovery runs on a demand that has been set aside (rules 113 and 115).

### 6.3 Fresh adjudication after a remand, and the 2-year limit (s.75(3))
- **Who can remand.** GSTAT (s.113(1)) and the High Courts can. **The first Appellate Authority cannot** (s.107(11)).
- **Time limit.** Where any order must be issued "in pursuance of the direction of the Appellate Authority or Appellate Tribunal or a court", it must be issued **within two years from the date of communication of the direction** (s.75(3)). This also covers the s.75(2) re-determination when a s.74 charge fails.
- **Proper officer for s.75(2).** The officer who adjudicated the original s.74 SCN (Circular 254/11/2025-GST, para 6).
- **Do the s.73 limits apply on conversion?** Unsettled. *Sterling & Wilson* is said to be silent on it (case-law/03, section 6; case-law/09, section 8). The *Sterling & Wilson* remand allowed the SCN to be amended within a month, with a hearing.
- **Checklist for the fresh order:**
  1. Note the date the direction was communicated, and diarise the 2-year date.
  2. Follow every direction in the remand order to the letter. Examine the evidence it names, and give the hearings it requires.
  3. Issue a fresh hearing notice with the date, time and venue (s.75(4)). Allow up to 3 adjournments (s.75(5)).
  4. Do not exceed the original SCN's amount or grounds (s.75(7)). A new ground needs a new SCN within limitation.
  5. Record the effect of the remand on the pre-deposit (stays in place or refund; see 6.1).
  6. Pass a reasoned order (s.75(6)), make sure it carries a DIN/RFN (mandatory: *Gudla Vanaja*, Telangana HC Full Bench, 30.09.2026), sign it digitally (good practice), and upload it with a DRC-07 where a demand results.
- **Exclusions from your own limitation:**
  - Where a court or the Tribunal has **stayed** issue of the notice or order, the stay period is excluded (s.75(1)).
  - Where the same issue is pending in a higher forum in **another** case after a decision against revenue, the period between those decisions is excluded in computing the s.73(10), 74(10) or 74A limits (s.75(11)).
