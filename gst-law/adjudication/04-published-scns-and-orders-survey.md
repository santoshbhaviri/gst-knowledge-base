# Published GST show cause notices and orders: what the best ones do, and model structures

**For:** Assistant Commissioner, CGST, Sangareddy Division (Medchal Commissionerate, Telangana).
**Compiled:** 14 Oct 2026.
**Companion files:** `01-adjudication-procedure.md` (procedure), `03-court-proof-checklist.md` (why notices and orders fail). This file adds only what those two do not already say.

> **Read this first: what could and could not be read.**
> - **No published GST SCN, Order-in-Original (OIO) or Order-in-Appeal (OIA) could be read in full.** Every site that hosts them was blocked here: commissionerate websites, stock-exchange archives where listed companies post the orders they receive, High Court sites that reproduce them, CESTAT and NACIN. Part 4 lists the hosts.
> - **What was read in full:** only official CBIC documents from `taxinformation.cbic.gov.in`. These are the CBEC Master Circular on SCN and adjudication (1053/02/2017-CX), its two follow-up circulars, two GST investigation instructions, the DRC-01 and DRC-07 forms, and several older Board instructions on SCN and order quality. They are listed in Part 1A and analysed in Part 2.
> - **Everything about specific published orders, and every High Court point below, comes from search snippets only.** Each is marked **"snippet only"**. Read the source before you rely on it.
> - Taxpayer names are replaced by **[Taxpayer]**. Where a URL's file name carries the taxpayer's name, the URL is not given. The host, date and issuing office are enough to find it. Case names of court judgments are kept because they are citations.

## Part 2. Analysis of the documents read in full

### 2.1 Master Circular 1053/02/2017-CX (R1): the best single drafting guide found

**Why it matters for GST.** No CBIC GST manual on SCNs or adjudication was found. R1 is the Board's most complete statement of how an SCN and an OIO should be built. Its principles carry over to GST because ss.73, 74, 74A and 75 rest on the same natural-justice ideas. Where it cites a Central Excise section (s.11A), read the GST equivalent (s.73, 74 or 74A), and do not quote the CX section in a GST notice.

**Structure (headings in order).**
- Part I, Show Cause Notice: 1 Demand; 2 SCN; 3 Structure of SCN (paras 2.2 to 2.10); 4 Authority to adjudicate; 5 Limitation; 6 Extended period (3.2 to 3.7); 7 Limitation for interest; 8 Audit or CERA demands; 9 Pre-SCN consultation; 10 Authority to issue the SCN; 11 Unjust enrichment in the SCN; 12 Changing a long-standing practice; 13 Waiver of SCN; 14 Call book.
- Part II, Adjudication: monetary limits; jurisdiction; Audit and DGCEI cases; 13.0 Service of the SCN and relied-upon documents (RUDs); 14.1 to 14.10 Stages of adjudication; 15 Corrigendum to an order; 16 Transfer of the adjudicating authority; 16.1 Signing; 17 CERA cases.
- Part III, Recovery. Part IV, Miscellaneous (24 de novo; 25 no SCN after voluntary payment).

**Standout features worth copying.**

1. **An eight-part SCN template** (para 2.2). Model passage:
   > "A SCN should ideally comprise of the following parts, though it may vary from case to case: a) Introduction of the case b). Legal frame work c). Factual statement and appreciation of evidences d). Discussion, facts and legal frame work, e). Discussion on Limitation f). Calculation of duty and other amounts due g). Statement of charges h). Authority to adjudicate." (R1, para 2.2)

2. **The introduction says how the case arose** (para 2.3). It covers the source (audit, scrutiny, intelligence), the "gist of audit objections/observations/ intelligence and a brief modus operandi", and the investigation done. Each noticee is described separately, "irrespective of the fact that, the persons are closely related to each other." Apply the same rule to s.122(1A) and s.74 co-noticees.

3. **Evidence laid out serially to prove each charge** (para 2.5):
   > "All evidences in form of documents, statements and material evidence resumed during the course of enquiry /investigation should be organised serially in a manner so as to establish the charges against the noticee. While discussing the facts and evidences, care should be taken to be precise and succinct in expression so that unnecessary details are avoided." (R1, para 2.5)

4. **Charges framed against each noticee separately** (para 2.6), after the facts are set against the legal framework.

5. **A separate limitation section that pleads the extended period with evidence** (paras 2.7, 3.2, 3.6):
   > "The onus of establishing that these ingredients are present in a given case is on revenue and these ingredients need to be clearly brought out in the Show Cause Notice alongwith evidence thereof. The active element of intent to evade duty by action or inaction needs to be present for invoking extended period." (R1, para 3.2)

   > "Indiscriminate use of such restricted powers leads to fruitless adjudications, appeals and reviews, inflates the figures of outstanding demands and above all causes unnecessary harassment of the assessees." (R1, para 3.6)

   R1 quotes *Cosmic Dye Chemical* (SC, 1995 (75) ELT 721): mis-statement or suppression "must be wilful". File 03 does not cite *Cosmic Dye*. It is the classic SC authority to quote in the s.74 grounds and findings.

6. **Disputed interpretation and the Board's own circulars** (paras 3.4, 3.5, 8). If the trade and the department both followed a practice until a Board circular corrected it, the extended period is "incorrect". If the circular only restates a practice the compliant segment already followed, the extended period may be used where its ingredients exist. A long-standing practice should not be overturned suddenly by SCN; refer it to the Board and confine the demand to the normal period. For GST, use the same test before choosing s.74 or s.74A(5)(ii) in rate and classification cases (see also R5 para 2(g)).

7. **A second SCN for the extended period** (para 3.7). Where the first SCN already used the extended period, a later SCN for the extended period on the same facts is hard to sustain, because the department already knew the facts. Issue later-period notices within the normal period. In GST, this maps to the s.73(3), 74(3) and 74A(3) statement route.

8. **Quantify the demand and state the method** (para 2.8). If exact quantification is impossible, "the principles and manner of computing" must still be laid down.

9. **Interest and the authority to adjudicate stated in the SCN** (paras 2.9, 2.11), "to make the SCN a self-contained notice of charges". A change of adjudicating authority is made by a corrigendum issued by the SCN-issuing authority.

10. **RUDs and non-RUDs** (para 13.0). Serve the RUDs with the SCN. Return non-relied documents under receipt. Put a clause in the SCN that non-relied records may be collected within 30 days, naming the designation and address of the officer responsible. File 03 does not have this clause.

11. **Personal hearing** (paras 14.3, 14.4). Give at least three opportunities, "with sufficient interval", each by a separate communication. Record reasons for any adjournment. Keep a record of the hearing and of written submissions made at it.

12. **What a speaking order is** (paras 14.5 to 14.8). Model passage:
    > "The adjudication order must be a speaking order. A speaking order is an order that speaks for itself. A good adjudication order is expected to stand the test of legality, fairness and reason at higher appellate forums. Such order should contain all the details of the issue, clear findings and a reasoned order." (R1, para 14.5)

    > "He is also expected to examine each of the points raised in the reply to the SCN and accept or reject them with cogent reasoning." (R1, para 14.6)

    > "The adjudication order should generally contain brief facts of the case, written and oral submissions by the party, observation of the adjudicating authority on the evidences on record and facts of omission and commission during personal hearing and finally the operating order." (R1, para 14.7)

    Para 14.8: the operative part must cite the provision under which the duty is confirmed and each penalty is imposed, and the confirmed amount cannot exceed the SCN amount.

13. **Cross-examination** (para 14.9). If a statement relied on in the SCN is to be used, the person must be produced for cross-examination when the noticee asks. Collect corroborative evidence during investigation in case cross-examination is not feasible or the statement is retracted. "Frivolous" requests may be refused, but say why.

14. **Timing and signature** (paras 14.10, 16, 16.1). Communicate the order within one month of the hearing. A successor officer must give a fresh hearing before deciding. The adjudicating authority signs the order and never delegates the signature. A corrigendum to an order is only for minor clerical slips, because after issuing the order the officer is *functus officio*.

15. **Independent application of mind on audit objections** (para 17.3, quoting *Simplex Infrastructure*, Cal HC):
    > "It is expected that the SCN is a consequence of independent examination carried out on receipt of CERA/CRA objection. Such independent findings should be incorporated in the show cause notice as well as in the adjudication order." (R1, para 17.3)

    Apply this to SCNs proposed on CAG, internal audit or DGGI-intimation material in GST.

**Weaknesses.**
- It is written for the Central Excise Act. Its sections, monetary limits and the 2-year and 5-year periods do not apply to GST.
- Para 2.9 says interest need not be stated explicitly. **Do not follow this in GST**: file 03 (B6, checks 16 and 21) shows GST orders fail where interest was not proposed.
- Para 2.8 tolerates an unquantified SCN (*Gwalior Rayon*, MP HC 1982). GST rule 142(1)(a) and the DRC-01 table require quantification. Quantify every time.
- Para 14.3 ("at least three opportunities") sits awkwardly with s.75(5), which caps adjournments at three. Treat it as: one hearing notice plus up to three adjournments, each by separate letter.
- It gives no sample paragraphs or tables. It describes, it does not model.

### 2.2 Circulars 1076/02/2020-CX and 1079/03/2021-CX (R2, R3): pre-SCN consultation

**Structure:** subject, background, clarification, rescission, signature.

**Points to copy.** Consultation is held by the officer who will issue the SCN. The fraud exclusion depends on the case, not on the formation:
> "it is hereby clarified that exclusion from pre-show cause notice consultation is case-specific and not formation specific." (R3, para 4)

**GST relevance.** GST has no Rs 50 lakh consultation rule. Its counterpart is DRC-01A under rule 142(1A), and file 01 (3.2) covers when it is used. What is new, from R3 read with C2 (*Jyoti Tar*): whatever the pre-SCN step, the SCN must engage with the pre-SCN reply. A replica SCN fails.

### 2.3 Instruction 02/2021-22 [GST-Investigation] (R4): issue SCNs in good time

**Structure:** problem (few SCNs in cases booked); s.73(2)/(10) and s.74(2)/(10) quoted; a table of annual-return due dates for FY 2017-18 to 2019-20; the Board's concern; directions.

**Model passage:**
> "If the issuance of SCNs is pushed to close proximity of the end dates/last dates, it may leave very little time with the adjudicating authority to pass orders within stipulated period ... This might result in a situation where either the adjudicating authority is not able to pass orders within prescribed time period or quality of adjudication suffers." (R4, para 4; the PDF's line breaks are scrambled, and the words are reproduced as printed)

**Feature worth copying.** It tabulates the limitation anchor (the annual-return due date and the notification that fixed it) for each year. Put the same table in every SCN: year, annual-return due date and source, s.73(10), 74(10) or 74A(7) order deadline, and SCN deadline.

**Weakness.** It is an administrative direction; it says nothing about content.

### 2.4 Instruction 01/2023-24-GST (Inv.) (R5): ease of business in investigation

**Structure:** paras 2(a) to 2(q2): approval to investigate; checking for parallel inquiries by DGGI or State; consulting the policy wing on interpretation issues; letters before summons for listed companies and PSUs; specific (not vague) letters; no fishing summons; prior approval of the summons content; uploading statements and panchnamas; finishing within one year and issuing the SCN without delay.

**Points that feed SCN quality:**
- Para 2(i): letters and summons must state "the specific nature of the inquiry". Vague phrases such as "GST evasion" must not be used. The same discipline should show in the SCN's introduction.
- Para 2(g): where the taxpayer followed a prevalent trade practice on a contested interpretation, a reference to GST Policy or TRU is "desirable" before the SCN. Record in the SCN that this was considered. It also bears on whether suppression can be alleged (see R1 paras 3.4, 3.5 and 8).
- Para 2(c) and (d): check for and record parallel inquiries on the same subject. File 03 (A1) covers the check; R5 is the Board's own source for it.
- Para 2(q1), model passage:
  > "An investigation initiated must reach the earliest conclusion which is not more than one year. It is not necessary to keep investigation pending till limitation in law approaches. Show cause notice should not be delayed after conclusion of investigation." (R5, para 2(q1))

### 2.5 FORM GST DRC-01 and DRC-07 (R6, R7)

**DRC-01 structure:** Reference No. and date; addressee (GSTIN or temporary ID, name, address); tax period, FY, Act; section or sub-section under which the SCN is issued; SCN reference number and date; (a) brief facts, (b) grounds, (c) a 13-column table of tax and other dues (tax rate, turnover, period from and to, Act, POS, tax, interest, penalty, fee, others, total); signature block with name, designation, jurisdiction and address.

**DRC-07 structure:** order number, date, FY, tax period; issues involved; goods or services with HSN; sections under which the demand is created; the same 13-column demand table; a payment direction ("You are hereby directed to make the payment by <Date> ..."); signature block.

**Drafting lessons:**
- The two forms ask for the **same 13 columns**. Build the SCN's quantification annexure and the OIO's operative table in those same columns, Act-wise (CGST, SGST, IGST, Cess) and period-wise. The portal summary can then be copied from the order without re-computing, and s.75(7) (order not above the SCN) can be checked line by line.
- DRC-01 needs one "Section / sub-section" field. Cite every section used (for example "74(1) read with 50(1), 74(1) penalty, 122(1A)") identically in the SCN heading, the body, the DRC-01 and the operative part (see C9).
- The note says the rate, turnover and period columns are "not mandatory". Fill them anyway. They make the demand verifiable.
- The forms have no field for the hearing date or the reply period. Those must be in the detailed SCN and in a separate hearing notice (file 03, D1).

### 2.6 Older Board instructions (R8 to R13): rescinded, but they show what the Board has long treated as poor quality

- **R8 (1996)**, model passage:
  > "It is absolutely necessary that the SCNs should clearly state the grounds for extended period of demand." (R8, para 1)

  Para 2 adds that where an SCN is late, the delay must not be covered by "issuing SCN for extended period without proper grounds"; responsibility for the delay is to be fixed. This is still sound management practice for s.74 and s.74A(5)(ii).
- **R9 (2003):** communicate the decision within 5 days of the hearing, at most one month. R1 para 14.10 keeps the one-month rule.
- **R10 (2000):** the draft SCN reaches the approving officer 3 months before the last date, for "proper scrutiny and application of mind". There is a central SCN register with columns for adjudication, appeal and recovery status, which "will reflect success rate of cases booked". This is a useful divisional practice for GST.
- **R11 (2015):** CESTAT imposed costs where orders ignored remand directions, natural justice, "the pleadings and evidences on record" or showed "non-application of mind". It also directed that **orders in favour of Revenue be reviewed as critically as orders in favour of assessees**. Adopt this as an internal review step before signing.
- **R12 (2014):** follow binding precedent and Board circulars (*Kamlakshi Finance*, SC). File 03 (E5) covers the substance.
- **R13 (2018):**
  > "It may be appreciated that an importer or an exporter has an inalienable right to know the reasons for loading of value, change of classification, any decision regarding entitlement to an exemption notification etc." (R13, para 2)

  It is a Customs instruction, but the idea of a right to reasons applies to every GST order.

### 2.7 Published SCNs and OIOs (P1 to P11): what the snippets show

These could not be read, so nothing can be said about their structure. From snippets only:
- **P1** shows the conventional CBIC findings opening ("I have carefully gone through the facts of the entire case as brought out in the Show Cause Notice and the material available on records"). It also shows the risk in an ex parte order: the order relies only on the SCN, which is the pattern C1 (*GlobeOp*) criticises. If there is no reply, the findings must still test the SCN's evidence independently.
- **P2** shows a CBIC SCN running **two separate issues**, each with its own tax figure, and a **penalty equal to tax** (s.74(1)). Issue-wise quantification is the norm in the better notices.
- **P4** combines s.74 with s.122 in one OIO. Penalties under different sections must be separately reasoned (file 03, E7, and s.75(13)).
- Most disclosed orders cover several years (P3 covers five, P4 three). Contrast this with the single-year safe course in file 03 (B7) and the split in C8.

## Part 3. Synthesis: model structures

These are drawn from R1 (structure), R4 and R5 (timing, specificity), R6 and R7 (tables), the statute (ss.73 to 75, 107(12)), the snippet-level case law in 1D, and file 03. The model passages below were **drafted for this file**; they are not quotations from any source.

### 3(a) Detailed SCN to accompany DRC-01 (s.73, s.74 or s.74A)

| # | Heading | What goes in it | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Cover block** | Office letterhead; SCN No. and date; DIN (non-portal copies) or RFN; GSTIN, legal name, address; FY (one per SCN); sections invoked, written the same way as in DRC-01; reply period (30 days) and a "Hearing" line (date, time, venue or video link, or "a separate notice will follow") | R6; file 03 checks 9, 19, 30; C9 |
| 1 | **Introduction and how the case arose** | Who the noticee is (business, registration date, principal place, HSN or SAC); the source (scrutiny ASMT-10, audit, DGGI or State intimation, investigation); the dates of each step; any DRC-01A and the reply to it, **with the reply summarised and answered** | R1 2.3; C2 |
| 2 | **Legal framework** | Every provision alleged to be breached, quoted briefly (charging section, ITC conditions, rule, notification and entry), plus the demand, interest and penalty sections | R1 2.4 |
| 3 | **Facts and evidence, issue by issue** | One sub-heading per issue (3.1, 3.2 ...). For each: the facts; the documents (numbered RUD-1, RUD-2 ...); extracts of statements with dates and the person's role; for ITC cases, supplier-wise evidence; for rate cases, the HSN or GRI analysis | R1 2.5, 2.6; C4 (what survived); file 03 H1 to H8 |
| 4 | **Discussion: the facts against the law** | For each issue: why the facts amount to the contravention; anticipated defences (circulars, trade practice, prior department knowledge) dealt with now; record of any reference to Policy or TRU (R5 2(g)) | R1 2.6, 3.4, 3.5 |
| 5 | **Limitation and grounds for s.74 / s.74A(5)(ii)** (separate heading) | A year-wise table: annual-return due date and source; order deadline; SCN deadline; the date of this SCN. If s.74 is used: the specific acts (what was suppressed or mis-stated, in which return, on what date), the evidence of each, and why it shows **intent to evade**; *Cosmic Dye* ("must be wilful"). Never use "fraud **or** wilful misstatement **or** suppression" as boilerplate | R1 2.7, 3.2, 3.6; R4 table; R8; *G.R. Infra* (file 03) |
| 6 | **Quantification** | An annexure in the **DRC-01 13-column format**, issue-wise and month- or period-wise, Act-wise; the method stated; credit for DRC-03 payments and reversals; interest under s.50(1) or 50(3) with the date it runs from (for 50(3), a utilisation table) | R1 2.8; R6; file 03 checks 16, 20, 37 |
| 7 | **Statement of charges** ("Now, therefore ...") | A numbered list, **each item one demand**: the amount and Act; the section; interest and its section; penalty and its exact clause; for each co-noticee, the s.122(1A) clause and role. Name the authority to which the noticee must reply | R1 2.10, 2.11; file 03 check 17 |
| 8 | **Noticee's options and rights** | Payment within 30 days under s.73(8) or 74(8) (or 60 days under s.74A(8)) with the reduced penalty; the right to inspect RUDs; the right to ask for cross-examination of named persons, with a deadline; the right to be heard and to adjournments (s.75(5)) | s.73(8), 74(8), 74A(8); C5; M2 |
| 9 | **Relied-upon and non-relied documents** | RUD list (number, description, pages, how served). A non-RUD clause: records not relied on may be collected within 30 days from [designation, address] | R1 13.0 |
| 10 | **Signature** | Digital signature of the proper officer; name, designation, jurisdiction; served by portal, e-mail and post | file 03 checks 10, 28 |

**Model wording for the s.74 heading (drafted here):**
> *"5.2 The noticee filed GSTR-3B for [months] declaring outward taxable value of Rs [x] while its e-way bills for the same months (RUD-4) show Rs [y]. The difference was not shown in GSTR-1, GSTR-3B or GSTR-9 (RUD-5). In his statement of [date] (RUD-6, Q.7) the [designation] admitted that [specific admission]. Omitting supplies already documented in its own e-way bills, across [n] returns, is not an error of interpretation. It is a wilful suppression made to evade tax, within s.74(1) read with Explanation 2."*

**Model wording for the non-RUD clause (drafted here):**
> *"Documents seized or obtained during the inquiry and not listed in Annexure-B are not relied upon. They may be collected from the Superintendent, [Range], [address], within 30 days of receipt of this notice."*

### 3(b) Personal-hearing notice (s.75(4) and (5))

A **separate**, signed notice for each hearing and each adjournment (R1 14.3; C7):

1. Reference, date, DIN or RFN; the SCN reference and date; GSTIN and name.
2. **Date, time, venue** (room and address) **and/or video-conference link**, with the officer's name. A vague phrase such as "within 7 days" is not a hearing notice (C7).
3. A statement that the hearing is offered under s.75(4) whether or not a reply was filed, and even if "No" was ticked in DRC-06 (C7).
4. What the noticee may bring: an authorised representative (with authorisation), documents, case law; a request to file any written submissions before or at the hearing.
5. **Any pending request** (cross-examination, inspection of RUDs, more time) and the decision on it, or the date by which it will be decided (C5).
6. Adjournment: available on sufficient cause, at most three times (s.75(5)); ask that requests be made in writing with reasons before the date.
7. Consequence: if the noticee does not appear, the case may be decided on the record. Say which date the record will close.
8. Modes of service used (portal, e-mail, post) recorded on the office copy.

After the hearing, a **record of personal hearing** signed by the officer and, where possible, the representative: who attended, points urged, documents handed over, any time allowed (R1 14.4).

**Model wording (drafted here):**
> *"Under section 75(4) of the CGST Act, you are given an opportunity of personal hearing on [date] at [time] in [room, address], or by video conference at [link]. This is offered even though [no reply has been filed / you indicated 'No' in DRC-06]. Your request dated [date] for cross-examination of [name] has been [allowed; he will be produced at this hearing / rejected for the reasons in the enclosed order]."*

### 3(c) Order-in-Original (s.73, 74 or 74A)

| # | Heading | Content | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Preamble** | OIO No., date, DIN or RFN; SCN reference; noticee; FY; "passed by"; the s.107 appeal clause (Commissioner (Appeals), 3 months, APL-01, pre-deposit) | file 03 checks 29 to 33 |
| 1 | **Brief facts** | A short summary of the SCN, issue by issue, with the amounts proposed (cross-referenced, not pasted) | R1 14.7; C1 |
| 2 | **Reply** | Each defence point numbered (R-1, R-2 ...) in the noticee's terms, with the documents filed | R1 14.6 |
| 3 | **Personal hearing** | Dates offered; adjournments and the reasons; who attended; additional points and documents; any cross-examination, with its outcome | R1 14.3, 14.4; s.75(5) |
| 4 | **Discussion and findings** | **Points for determination** first (borrowing the s.107(12) structure). Then, per issue: (a) the issue; (b) the evidence in the SCN; (c) the reply points that bear on it, each accepted or rejected **with reasons and the document relied on**; (d) case law and circulars cited by both sides, followed or distinguished; (e) the finding; (f) the figure. Use independent language; do not reuse SCN paragraphs | R1 14.5 to 14.8; C1; R11; R12 |
| 5 | **Limitation and s.74 / s.74A(5)(ii) finding** | A separate finding on each ingredient (the act, wilfulness, intent to evade) and the evidence. If not proved, re-determine under s.75(2) or (2A) and check the s.73 time limit | file 03 E4; C3 |
| 6 | **Quantification** | A table in the DRC-07 13-column format, beside the SCN figure: proposed / confirmed / dropped; credit for amounts paid | R7; s.75(7) |
| 7 | **Interest and penalty** | Interest: section and period. Each penalty: the clause, the breach and why that amount; the s.73(8)/74(8) benefit; s.126 checks; s.75(13) | file 03 E7 |
| 8 | **ORDER** (operative) | Numbered sub-paragraphs, one per demand: "I confirm / drop ... under section ... ; I order interest under section ...; I impose a penalty of Rs ... under section ...(clause) ...". Appropriate DRC-03 payments. For each co-noticee, a separate sub-paragraph | R1 14.8 |
| 9 | **Signature and service** | Signed by the adjudicating authority (never delegated); DRC-07 uploaded with the same figures; served by portal, e-mail and post | R1 16.1; file 03 checks 30, 33 |

**Model wording: answering a reply point (drafted here):**
> *"14.3 Reply point R-4: the noticee says the suppliers were active on the date of each invoice (Annexure-7 to the reply). I have checked this. Registration of suppliers S-2 and S-5 was active on the invoice dates, so this ground for disallowance is not pressed for them, and Rs [a] is dropped. For S-1, S-3 and S-4, the reply does not address the findings in RUD-9: no e-way bills, no transport documents, and payments routed back to the noticee's director's account within two days. Mere registration on the invoice date does not prove receipt of goods under s.16(2)(b). I reject R-4 for these three suppliers."*

**Model wording: closing the s.74 finding (drafted here):**
> *"16. Each ingredient is established on the record: (i) the act, omission of Rs [y] of e-way-bill-backed supplies from GSTR-1, 3B and 9 (RUD-4, 5); (ii) wilfulness, since the noticee's own statement admits knowledge (RUD-6); (iii) intent to evade, shown by repetition across [n] returns and the absence of any explanation in the reply. The extended period under s.74 is rightly invoked. Had any ingredient failed, I would have re-determined under s.73 by virtue of s.75(2)."*

### 3(d) Order-in-Appeal (short note)

s.107(12) prescribes the skeleton: "points for determination, the decision thereon and the reasons for such decision" (statute, read). The appellate authority confirms, modifies or annuls; under s.107(11) it shall not remand. Answer every ground of appeal by number, and record the pre-deposit and the hearing. Under Circular 256/02/2026-GST (snippet only), where the OIO was passed by a Common Adjudicating Authority, the OIA goes to the portal and to the CAA's Commissionerate.
