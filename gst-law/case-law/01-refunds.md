**Scope.** Case law on refunds under s.54-56 CGST Act, 2017 / s.16 IGST Act and rules 89-97 CGST Rules: inverted duty structure (IDS), rule 89(5) formula, Circulars 135/2020 and 173/2022, limitation under s.54(1) (directory vs mandatory, COVID exclusion), tax paid under the wrong head (s.77 / rule 89(1A)), natural justice before rejection (rule 92(3), RFD-08), deficiency memos (rule 90(3), RFD-03), interest under s.56, IGST export refunds and rule 96(10)/96(4), intermediary services, SEZ supplies, unjust enrichment, pre-deposits and amounts paid during investigation, deemed exports, withholding under s.54(10)/(11), and rule 89(4)/(4C) turnover. Prepared for the Assistant Commissioner, CGST, Sangareddy Division (Telangana).

## 1. Quick answers

**Inverted duty: input services.** The Supreme Court in *VKC Footsteps* (13.09.2021) upheld rule 89(5): IDS refund under s.54(3)(ii) covers ITC on **inputs** (goods) only, not input services or capital goods. It reversed Gujarat HC and affirmed Madras HC (*Transtonnelstroy Afcons*). *Takeaway:* deny the input-services part of an IDS claim by citing VKC Footsteps. A Finance Bill 2026 proposal to rewrite s.54(3) and add input services was reported, but **its enactment could not be confirmed**. Check the enacted Finance Act 2026 text before relying on the pre-2026 position for periods after 30.03.2026.

**Rule 89(5) formula amendment (Notf. 14/2022-CT, 05.07.2022).** Gujarat HC (*Ascent Meditech*) held the amended formula curative and clarificatory, so it applies retrospectively to claims filed within the s.54(1) period. It quashed Circular 181/13/2022 to that extent. One source reports that the SLP (28.03.2025) and a review petition were dismissed. *Takeaway:* apply the amended formula to all claims that are within time, including those for periods before July 2022. Do not reject a claim by applying the old formula.

**Circular 135/2020 para 3.2 (same input and output goods).** Telangana HC (*Micro Systems*, 2022) held Circular 173/05/2022 to be clarificatory and retrospective to 31.03.2020, and remanded the claim. **This binds Telangana officers.** Karnataka HC (*South Indian Oil Corp.*, 12.12.2025) held that a circular cannot cut down s.54(3), and that para 3.2 applies only where credit built up because the rate on the same goods changed over time. *Takeaway:* do not reject an IDS claim only because input and output are the same goods or carry the same rate, or because the supply is at a concessional rate. Record a finding on why the credit actually accumulated.

**Limitation under s.54(1).** The High Courts are split. Madras HC (*Lenovo*, 06.11.2023) called it directory (obiter: the claim was in time). AP HC (*Nspira*, 26.09.2025) held s.54 limitation inapplicable to tax paid without authority of law (exempt supplies), not that it is directory. A Karnataka HC Division Bench (*Merck Life Science*, 17.03.2026) holds it **mandatory**: the officer cannot condone delay, and only the HC can do so under Art. 226. No Telangana HC refund-limitation ruling was found. *Takeaway:* the officer should treat two years as mandatory, since only a court can condone delay. Before rejecting, (i) compute the relevant date correctly, (ii) **exclude 01.03.2020-28.02.2022 under Notf. 13/2022-CT** (the SC suo motu exclusion was also applied to refunds by Bombay HC in *Saiher*), and (iii) reject only after an RFD-08 notice and a hearing.

**Tax paid under the wrong head (s.77 / rule 89(1A)).** Patna HC (*Sai Steel*, 28.07.2025) and Karnataka HC hold that time runs from the date tax is paid under the **correct** head, following Circular 162/18/2021. AP HC (*Axis Bank*, 29.09.2022) remanded a time-barred rejection for fresh decision in the light of Circular 162/18/2021 and rule 89(1A). *Takeaway:* do not count limitation from the original erroneous payment.

**Rejection without RFD-08 or hearing.** The proviso to rule 92(3) bars rejection without a hearing. Rejections that skip the RFD-08 notice, ignore the reply, or give no reasons are routinely quashed (Telangana HC *DSV Air and Sea*, Gujarat HC *Deccan Fine Chemicals*, Madras HC *Pacific Wood Tech*). *Takeaway:* always issue RFD-08 stating specific grounds, consider the RFD-09 reply, give a personal hearing, and pass a speaking RFD-06 order that deals with every point.

**Deficiency memo (RFD-03).** A rectified application filed after a deficiency memo continues the original application for limitation purposes (Gujarat HC *Varidhi Cotspin*, 07.10.2025, and a Karnataka HC ruling). Time bar is not a "deficiency", so a claim cannot be rejected as time-barred through RFD-03 (Madras HC *Pacific Wood Tech*, 01.09.2026). *Takeaway:* use RFD-03 only for missing documents. Any substantive objection, including limitation, goes through RFD-08.

**Technical errors, second applications.** A refund cannot be refused on a technicality when the substantive conditions are met. A second application for the same period, for an amount or invoice left out, is maintainable within two years (Gujarat HC *Shree Renuka Sugars*, 13.07.2023; Bombay HC *Valmet Flow Control*, 22.04.2026). A clerical error corrected in the RFD-09 reply must be considered (Delhi HC *Shri Shyam Footwear*, 31.01.2023). *Takeaway:* do not reject a supplementary claim merely because an RFD-01 for that period has already been processed. Examine it on merits within the time limit.

**Interest under s.56.** Interest runs from the 61st day after the **original** application, even where the claim was rejected first and allowed later on appeal (Ranbaxy SC under s.11BB; Bombay HC *Altisource* 30.09.2025; Gujarat HC *Kuehne Nagel*). Telangana HC holds interest is automatic and compensatory (*Qualcom India*, 20.03.2024). Interest-only claims need no rule 89(2)(m) CA certificate (*Synchrony International*, 2026). Both are **BINDING IN TELANGANA**. *Takeaway:* when sanctioning a refund late, compute and grant s.56 interest suo motu from 60 days after the original ARN. Do not demand fresh certificates for interest alone.

**IGST export refunds, rule 96(10), rule 96(4).** The SC (*Cosmo Films*, 28.04.2023) upheld the pre-import condition for advance authorisations. Rule 96(10) was omitted by Notf. 20/2024-CT (08.10.2024) without a saving clause. Gujarat HC (*Addwrap*, 13.06.2025) held the omission applies to all pending proceedings, and the SC (*Goodluck India*, 06.08.2026) dismissed the Revenue's appeals. CBIC reportedly accepted this by an Office Memorandum dated 24.08.2026. *Takeaway:* do not deny or recover an export refund under rule 96(10) in any proceeding still pending. Withhold under rule 96(4) only on the grounds the rule lists. A "risky exporter" tag alone is not a ground (Gujarat HC *Choksi Exports*; *Agriex* not verified).

**Intermediary (export of services).** In Bombay HC (*Dharmendra M Jani*), after a split verdict, the referee judge (18.04.2023) upheld s.13(8)(b) IGST provided it is confined to IGST and not used to levy CGST/SGST. The Finance Act 2026 **omitted s.13(8)(b)**, reportedly from 30.03.2026, so the default s.13(2) rule (recipient's location) now applies. Whether the omission affects pending disputes is unsettled. Delhi HC (*Ernst & Young*, 23.03.2023) holds that a person supplying services on its own account is not an intermediary. Telangana HC (*Virtusa*, 05.01.2026; *Commscope*, 15.10.2025) quashed and remanded cryptic intermediary findings. *Takeaway:* an "intermediary" finding needs an analysis of the contract showing the supplier arranges or facilitates a supply between two other persons. A bare label is quashed as non-speaking.

**SEZ supplies.** Delay in the Specified Officer's endorsement, where that delay is attributable to the officer, should not defeat a refund (*Lenovo*, Madras). Madras HC (*Lenovo*) held the "authorised operations" endorsement cannot be insisted on for supplies before 01.10.2023. Bombay HC (*Lupin*, 21.11.2025) held an SEZ unit may itself claim refund of accumulated ITC (following Gujarat HC *Britannia*) and remanded for the endorsement point to be examined. *Takeaway:* if an endorsement is missing, flag it by deficiency memo and allow it to be cured. Do not reject outright.

**Withholding under s.54(10)/(11) and pending appeals.** A department's plan to appeal does not suspend an appellate order (s.107(16)). Withholding under s.54(11) needs a pending appeal, the Commissioner's opinion that the refund would adversely affect revenue because of malfeasance or fraud, and a hearing (Telangana HC *ADV Industrial Services*, Sept 2026). This is **BINDING IN TELANGANA**. *Takeaway:* do not reject a refund that follows an appellate order on the ground that "the department proposes to appeal". If the refund is to be withheld, use the s.54(11) procedure formally.

**Unjust enrichment.** For export of services, s.54(8)(d) does not bar the refund, because the incidence is not passed on to anyone in India (Bombay HC; party name not found). AP HC (*Shapoorji Pallonji*, 18.12.2024) remanded a rejection of a cash-ledger (left-over TDS) refund for reconsideration under Circular 166/22/2021-GST, without itself ruling on unjust enrichment. *Takeaway:* apply the unjust-enrichment test only to categories outside s.54(8)(a)-(e), and base it on evidence.

**Amounts paid during search or investigation, and pre-deposits.** A deposit made under coercion during a search, without a DRC-04 acknowledgement, is involuntary and refundable with interest (Delhi HC *Vallabh Textiles*; Bombay and Karnataka HCs). A pre-deposit refund cannot be withheld on extraneous grounds such as a pending SFIO probe (Odisha HC). *Takeaway:* a refund of a pre-deposit after a favourable appellate order should be processed promptly. Rejection cannot rest on the fact that some unrelated inquiry is pending.

### A. Inverted duty structure: input services and rule 89(5)

### Union of India v. VKC Footsteps India Pvt Ltd - Supreme Court - 13 Sep 2021
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal No. 4810 of 2021 and batch; AIR 2021 SC 4407 (Dr D.Y. Chandrachud & M.R. Shah JJ.; Indian Kanoon header lists B.V. Nagarathna J., but the signed judgment shows M.R. Shah J.)
- Holding: Rule 89(5) is valid (para 70-81, 112-113). IDS refund under s.54(3)(ii) is limited to unutilised ITC on inputs (goods); input services are excluded. The proviso to s.54(3) restricts refund and is not a condition of eligibility. The Court acknowledged anomalies in the formula and urged the GST Council to reconsider it.
- Binding: Binding on all authorities (Art. 141).
- Later history: Formula amended by Notf. 14/2022-CT (05.07.2022). A Finance Bill 2026 proposal reportedly rewrites s.54(3) to include input services and capital goods (TaxGuru), but **enactment is unconfirmed**. Separately, the Finance Act 2026 extended 90% provisional refunds under s.54(6) to IDS claims from a date to be notified.
- Use in orders: Cite it to exclude ITC on input services and capital goods from "Net ITC" in an IDS refund.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/92024267/); [Cleartax news](https://news.cleartax.in/supreme-court-upheld-the-madras-hc-decision-regarding-cgst-rule-895/7009/amp); [iPleaders case analysis](https://blog.ipleaders.in/case-analysis-of-union-of-india-vs-vkc-footsteps-india-private-limited/)
- Key words: "The proviso to Section 54(3) is not a condition of eligibility (as the assessees' Counsel submitted) but a restriction which must govern the grant of refund under Section 54(3)." (para 70)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; summary confirmed; case number confirmed, bench and AIR citation added. Para 113(iii) refers paras 104-111 (anomalies) to the GST Council.

### VKC Footsteps India Pvt Ltd v. Union of India - Gujarat HC (Division Bench) - 24 Jul 2020
- Citation / case no.: R/Special Civil Application No. 2792 of 2019 (per SC judgment, fn. 4); reported citation not found
- Holding: Explanation (a) to rule 89(5), which excluded ITC on input services from "Net ITC", was ultra vires s.54(3). Refund was directed treating ITC on input services as part of Net ITC.
- Binding: **Reversed**. No longer good law.
- Later history: Set aside by the SC on 13.09.2021.
- Use in orders: Do not follow it. Cite it only as the decision the SC reversed.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - SC judgment reversing it, paras 4 and 113](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/92024267/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1085598); [DPNC](https://www.dpncindia.com/gujarat-high-court-allows-refund-of-accumulated-credit-on-input-service-in-case-of-inverted-duty-structure)
- Key words: "Explanation (a) to Rule 89(5) which denies the refund of "unutilised input tax" paid on "input services" ... is ultra vires the provision of Section 54(3)" (Gujarat HC as quoted in the SC judgment, para 4)
- Verified: Primary (indirect) - Gujarat HC judgment itself not found on Indian Kanoon on 14 Oct 2026 (3 searches); date (24.07.2020) and holding confirmed from the SC judgment in VKC Footsteps, read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026

### Transtonnelstroy Afcons JV v. Union of India - Madras HC - 21 Sep 2020
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 8596 of 2019 and batch (21 writ petitions of 2019-2020); A.P. Sahi CJ & Senthilkumar Ramamoorthy J.; reserved 28.08.2020 and 03.09.2020, delivered 21.09.2020; reported citation not found
- Holding: Upheld s.54(3)(ii) (no breach of Art. 14) and rule 89(5) as amended. Declined to follow Gujarat HC.
- Binding: Persuasive; its view was affirmed by the SC in VKC Footsteps.

- Use in orders: Cite it together with VKC Footsteps.

### B. Inverted duty: Circular 135/2020 (same goods or same rate) and Circular 173/2022

### Micro Systems and Services v. Union of India - Telangana HC (Division Bench) - 05 Sep 2022
- Citation / case no.: WP No. 37465 of 2021 (Ujjal Bhuyan CJ & C.V. Bhaskar Reddy J.); 2022 (9) TMI 420 (not checked)
- Holding: Circular 173/05/2022 (06.07.2022) clarified that para 3.2 of Circular 135/2020 does not cover supplies under a concessional notification where the output rate is lower than the input rate on the same goods at the same time. Being clarificatory, it takes effect from 31.03.2020. The refund rejection (Rs 77,91,857, Apr 2019-Mar 2020, computers supplied to DRDO at 5%) and the appellate order were set aside and the claim remanded for decision within 8 weeks. The prayer to declare Circular 135 ultra vires was not decided.
- Binding: **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- Later history: none found
- Use in orders: Do not apply para 3.2 of Circular 135 to concessional-rate or structurally inverted supplies for any period from 31.03.2020.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/166094515/); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/refund-allowed-where-inputs-and-output-goods-are-same-but-supply-made-under-concessional-rate-notification-hc/); [taxo.online](https://taxo.online/latest-articles/circular-would-be-applicable-retrospectively-refund-in-case-of-inverted-duty-structure-allowed-telangana-high-court/)
- Key words: "Being clarificatory, Circular dated 06.07.2022 inserting the above clarification would have the effect from the date when Circular dated 31.03.2020 came into effect." (para 14)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; summary confirmed; date (05.09.2022) and bench confirmed

### BMG Informatics Pvt Ltd v. Union of India - Gauhati HC - 02 Sep 2021
- Citation / case no.: WP(C) No. 3878 of 2021 with WP(C) Nos. 3880, 3675 and 4120 of 2021; AIROnline 2021 Gau 779 (Achintya Malla Bujor Barua J., as listed on Indian Kanoon)
- Holding: CORRECTED: the case is about para 3.2 of Circular 135/2020 (same input and output goods), not input services. Para 3.2 conflicts with s.54(3)(ii) and must be ignored; a partial-exemption rate (Notf. 45/2017-CT(R), 2.5% to Govt/PSU/research institutions) is neither nil nor fully exempt. Both the AC's rejection and the appellate order were set aside and the claim (Rs 3,92,594, Oct-Dec 2018) remanded for a factual finding on input vs output rates within 6 weeks.
- Binding: Persuasive in Telangana. Consistent with Telangana HC in Micro Systems (Circular 135 para 3.2 not applied to concessional-rate supplies).
- Later history: none found. It pre-dates VKC Footsteps (SC, 13.09.2021) but does not deal with input services, so VKC does not overrule it.
- Use in orders: Do not reject an IDS claim only because input and output are the same goods; record a finding on the actual input and output rates.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/129124717/); [Cleartax news](https://news.cleartax.in/?p=7009)
- Key words: "the provisions of paragraph 3.2 of the circular No.135/05/2020-GST dated 31.03.2020 ... in cases where the input and output supplies are same, would have to be ignored." (para 28)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; MATERIAL CORRECTION: earlier summary (no distinction between inputs and input services; overtaken by VKC) was wrong; holding, date, case number and use rewritten

### South Indian Oil Corporation v. Assistant Commissioner of Central Tax - Karnataka HC - 12 Dec 2025
- Citation / case no.: WP No. 22068 of 2024 (T-RES); NC: 2025:KHC:53009; oral order of S.R. Krishna Kumar J. (single judge); 2025 (12) TMI 1537 (not checked)
- Holding: Edible oil bought in bulk at 5% and sold packed at 5%: ITC accumulated because other inputs (e.g. packing) carried higher rates. Rejections and appellate order quashed; refund with applicable interest directed within 3 months.
- Binding: Persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: none found
- Use in orders: Before invoking Circular 135, record a finding on why the credit actually accumulated.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/98841608/); [Taxheal](https://www.taxheal.com/22068-of-2024-t-res.html); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/inverted-duty-refund-allowed-even-when-input-and-output-gst-rates-are-same-hc)
- Key words: "the said Circular applies only to scenarios/situations where the ITC is accumulated on account of different rates being applicable at different points of time" (para 16 of Indian Oil Corporation, quoted and followed at paras 2-3)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; summary broadly confirmed; corrected: single-judge oral order following Indian Oil Corporation (WP 14414/2024); time for compliance is 3 months

### C. Limitation under s.54(1): directory or mandatory, and the COVID exclusion

### Lenovo (India) Pvt Ltd v. Joint Commissioner of GST (Appeals-1) - Madras HC - 06 Nov 2023
- Citation / case no.: WP Nos. 23604, 23605 and 23607 of 2022 (Krishnan Ramasamy J., single judge)
- Holding: (i) s.54(1) uses "may" and the two-year limit was described as directory (para 15.7), but the applications here were filed within two years, so this is obiter (para 15.8). (ii) Delay in the SEZ Authorised Officer's endorsement is the officer's fault and cannot bar the claim; mistakes in the endorsement are technical (paras 14.5-14.6). (iii) The "authorised operations" endorsement cannot be insisted on for supplies before 01.10.2023 (para 14.7). (iv) Where documents were lacking, rule 90(3) required a deficiency memo. Notf. 13/2022 exclusion also applied. Orders set aside; refund to issue within 30 days. (IGST on SEZ supplies, Dec 2019-Feb 2020.)
- Binding: Persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: Karnataka HC DB (Merck, 2026) takes the contrary view on limitation. No appeal found.
- Use in orders: Be aware of it, but do not condone delay yourself. Rely on rule 90(3) compliance.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/103219958/); [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://lakshmisri.com/newsroom/news-briefings/refund-time-limit-under-section-54-1-is-not-mandatory); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/madras-high-court/2-years-limitation-gst-refund-application-directory-mandatory-madras-high-court-242814)
- Key words: "The time limit fixed under Section 54 (1) is directory in nature and it is not mandatory." (para 15.7)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; summary confirmed; added that the directory remark is obiter (claim was within time) and the pre-01.10.2023 'authorised operations' finding

### Assistant Commissioner of Central Taxes v. Merck Life Science Pvt Ltd (batch) - Karnataka HC (Division Bench) - 17 Mar 2026
- Citation / case no.: Writ Appeal No. 110 of 2026 (T-RES) c/w WA Nos. 119, 122, 126 and 140 of 2026 (S.G. Pandit & K.V. Aravind JJ.); against single-judge order dated 07.11.2025 in WP No. 27259/2024 and batch; 2026 (3) TMI 1200 (not checked)
- Holding: The two-year period in s.54 is **mandatory**; the proper officer has no power to entertain a refund claim filed after two years (paras 9.5-9.8, 12(a)). The single judge's "directory" view was set aside. A claimant may seek condonation under Art. 226; if the court condones delay, the proper officer gets a corresponding extension of time for s.73/74 action (para 12). On the facts (IGST paid for Oct 2017 treating the service as export, later paid CGST/SGST as intra-State; claim filed 30.03.2024, about 6 months beyond the rule 89(1A) window; entitlement not disputed) delay was condoned and the claim directed to be considered within 60 days. Appeals allowed in part.
- Binding: Persuasive in Telangana. It is the most recent appellate-level view found.
- Later history: none found (SLP status not checked)
- Use in orders: Cite it for the point that the proper officer cannot entertain a time-barred claim. Leave condonation to the court.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/127010713/); [LiveLaw Biz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/gst/high-court/gst-refund-limitation-mandatory-delay-can-be-condoned-under-article-226-in-cases-of-genuine-hardship-karnataka-high-court-530592); [taxo.online 21.03.2026](https://taxo.online/latest-news/21-03-2026-refund-limitation-under-gst-is-mandatory-but-high-courts-can-condon-delay-in-genuine-hardship-cases-karnataka-high-court/)
- Key words: "In the absence of such enabling provisions, the period of two years remains mandatory in so far as the proper officer is concerned." (para 9.5)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; summary confirmed; case numbers, bench and single-judge date (07.11.2025) added

### Nspira Management Services Pvt Ltd v. Assistant/Deputy Commissioner of Central Tax - Andhra Pradesh HC (Division Bench) - 26 Sep 2025
- Citation / case no.: WP Nos. 18287 & 14905 of 2024 (R. Raghunandan Rao & T.C.D. Sekhar JJ.; common order by T.C.D. Sekhar J.)
- Holding: CORRECTED: the facts are GST charged by landlords on residential dwellings rented for student hostels, which are exempt under Entry 12 of Notf. 12/2017-CT(R) (refund of Rs 11.49 crore etc., Jul 2017-Jun 2022). It is not an IGST/CGST wrong-head case and the court did not say s.54 is directory. Following Gujarat HC (Comsol Energy), tax paid without authority of law is not "tax" and s.54 limitation does not apply (Art. 265). ### Lupin Ltd v. State of Maharashtra - Bombay HC (Nagpur Bench, Division Bench) - 21 Nov 2025
- Citation / case no.: WP No. 4164 of 2024 (Anil L. Pansare & Raj D. Wakode JJ.); reserved 14.11.2025
- Holding: CORRECTED: the SEZ unit itself (a recipient) claimed refund of ITC (ISD credit); State Tax authorities rejected it because only the supplier to an SEZ may claim under rule 89(1), and declined to follow Gujarat HC in *Britannia Industries* because an SLP was pending. The SC had since let Britannia stand (SLP not pressed for low tax effect) and dismissed the Revenue's SLP in *Meghmani Organochem* (SLP (C) 1239/2025, 22.09.2025) keeping the question of law open. Britannia therefore holds the field and binds authorities. Orders quashed; remanded to the Assistant Commissioner (State Tax) to decide afresh in the light of Britannia and after examining whether the services were for authorised operations as endorsed by the Specified Officer (a point first raised by the State in court). The judgment says nothing about 01.10.2023.
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: none found
- Use in orders: An SEZ unit's own refund claim for accumulated ITC cannot be rejected merely because it is a recipient (follow Britannia unless the SC rules otherwise). Raise any endorsement objection in the RFD-08 notice, not for the first time in court. (The 'authorised operations' requirement applying only from 01.10.2023 comes from Lenovo, Madras HC, para 14.7, not from this case.)
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/139356318/); [Taxheal](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=110979); [Masters India](https://www.mastersindia.co/blog/gst-refund-rights-key-court-judgments/)
- Key words: "the status as of now is that the law laid down by the Gujarat High Court in Britannia's case holds the field." (para 8)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; MATERIAL CORRECTION: the case is about an SEZ unit claiming refund of ITC (Britannia), remanded with a direction to also examine the authorised-operations endorsement; the 01.10.2023 reading in secondary sources is not in the judgment; bench added

(See also *Lenovo*, Madras HC, at C above: a delayed SEZ endorsement attributable to the officer does not defeat the claim. The Gujarat HC's *Britannia Industries* ruling (2020 (42) GSTL 3 (Guj)), that an SEZ unit may itself claim refund of accumulated ITC, stands: per Bombay HC in *Lupin* (21.11.2025), the SC let Britannia stand (SLP not pressed for low tax effect) and dismissed the Revenue's SLP in *Meghmani Organochem* (SLP (C) 1239/2025, 22.09.2025) keeping the question of law open. Britannia itself not read.)

### L. Unjust enrichment, cash ledger, deemed exports

### Shapoorji Pallonji and Co Pvt Ltd v. Union of India - Andhra Pradesh HC (Division Bench) - 18 Dec 2024
- Citation / case no.: WP No. 17717 of 2021 (R. Raghunandan Rao & Maheswara Rao Kuncheam JJ.)
- Holding: CORRECTED (overstated before): refund of left-over TDS credit in the electronic cash ledger was rejected (17.10.2019) and the appeal dismissed (26.12.2020) on unjust enrichment and other grounds. The court did not itself rule on unjust enrichment. It set aside both orders and remanded for reconsideration in the light of Circular 166/22/2021-GST (17.11.2021), which allows refund of TDS/TCS balance in the cash ledger under the proviso to s.54(1) read with s.49(6), within three months.
- Binding: Persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: none found
- Use in orders: For refund of excess cash-ledger balance (including TDS/TCS credit), follow Circular 166/22/2021-GST; do not rest a rejection on unjust enrichment without examining that circular.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/182536345/); [TaxGuru - unjust enrichment analysis](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/unjust-enrichment-comprehensive-analysis-judicial-precedents.html)
- Key words: "remanding the matter back to the respective respondents for reconsideration in the light of Circular No.166/22/2021-GST." (para 9)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; MATERIAL CORRECTION: a remand in the light of Circular 166/22/2021, not a holding that unjust enrichment does not apply; date, case number and bench added

### Bombay HC on s.54(8)(d) and export of services - Bombay HC - date and party name not found
- Citation / case no.: not found
- Holding: The unjust-enrichment bar in s.54(8)(d) applies only where the incidence of tax has been passed on to another person. For export of services to a foreign recipient that concern does not arise. Refund to be processed within 4 weeks.
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: none found
- Use in orders: Zero-rated refunds fall under s.54(8)(a), so no unjust-enrichment test applies.
- Source: [TaxGuru - unjust enrichment analysis](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/unjust-enrichment-comprehensive-analysis-judicial-precedents.html)
- Verified: Secondary - single source (not found on Indian Kanoon on 14 Oct 2026, 3 searches)

### Shah Paperplast Industries Ltd v. Union of India (batch) - Gujarat HC (Division Bench) - 13 Nov 2025
- Citation / case no.: R/Special Civil Application No. 18892 of 2023 with SCA Nos. 17246/2022, 17080/2023, 96/2025 and 8319/2025; reserved 10.10.2025 (Bhargav D. Karia & Pranav Trivedi JJ.)
- Holding: 100% EOUs that actually exported goods without payment of tax and claimed refund of accumulated ITC under rule 89(1)/89(4) were not deemed exporters: their suppliers had not claimed deemed-export refunds under Notf. 48/2017. Para 2.2 of Circular 172/04/2022 and rule 89(4A) therefore did not apply, and refunds could not be withdrawn, recovered or rejected on that basis. Withdrawal, recovery and rejection orders were quashed and refunds directed within 12 weeks. The vires/retrospectivity of the circular and the s.107(2) review / s.73-74 recovery questions were left open.
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: none found
- Use in orders: Apply the deemed-export restrictions only where deemed-export benefits were actually availed.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/140412757/); [taxo.online](https://taxo.online/?p=543798)
- Key words: "As the petitioners are exporters of the goods and has never claimed the input tax credit under Notification No.48/2017 as deemed exporter, para no.2.2 of clarificatory Circular No.172/04/2022-GST dated 06.07.2022 would not be applicable" (para 57)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date corrected (13.11.2025, not Dec 2025); holding confirmed; batch, bench and open questions added

### M. Amounts paid during search or investigation, and pre-deposits

### Vallabh Textiles v. Senior Intelligence Officer - Delhi HC (Division Bench) - 20 Dec 2022
- Citation / case no.: WP(C) 9834/2022; 2022/DHC/005682 (Rajiv Shakdher & Tara Vitasta Ganju JJ.); reserved 14.09.2022
- Holding: Rs 1,80,10,000 deposited on the petitioner's behalf during a search (17.02.2022), without a proper officer's ascertainment notice or DRC-04 acknowledgement and contrary to Gujarat HC's *Bhumi Associate* directions (no recovery during search; DRC-03 only after the search ends), lacked voluntariness. Refund directed with 6% simple interest from 17.02.2022 within ten days; CBIC directed to align Instruction 01/2022-23 (25.05.2022) with Bhumi Associate.
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: none found
- Use in orders: Payments during search are at risk of refund unless the voluntariness (DRC-03 and DRC-04 trail) is clearly recorded.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/188747488/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/tax-deposited-during-search-not-voluntary-delhi-high-court-directs-gst-dept-return-rs180-crores-217331); [Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/delhi-hc-directed-refund-of-amount-deposited-coercively-during-search-operations-at-midnight)
- Key words: "Failure to follow the prescribed procedure will, as in this case, have us conclude that the deposit of tax, interest and penalty was not voluntary." (para 40.1)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; summary confirmed; date (20.12.2022), case number, bench and the 6% interest from 17.02.2022 added

### Union of India v. Bundl Technologies Pvt Ltd - Karnataka HC (Division Bench) - 03 Mar 2022 (affirming single judge, 2021)
- Citation / case no.: W.A. No. 1274 of 2021 (T-RES) in WP No. 4467 of 2021 (Alok Aradhe & M.G.S. Kamal JJ.). Single judge's date not checked.
- Holding: Rs 15 crore (deposited at about 4 a.m. on 30.11.2019 during DGGI investigation) and Rs 12,51,44,157 (27.12.2019) were not self-ascertained deposits under s.74(5), the only provision permitting deposit during investigation; collection breached Arts. 265 and 300A. Refund cannot be made subject to the outcome of the pending investigation. The claim (filed within two years) and writ were not delayed. Revenue's writ appeal dismissed, affirming the single judge's refund direction (findings modified).
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: none found
- Use in orders: Same as Vallabh Textiles.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - DB judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/124175504/); [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://cn.lakshmisri.com/newsroom/news-briefings/right-of-refund-of-amount-paid-during-investigation)
- Key words: "the contention of the Department that amount under deposit be made subject to the outcome of the pending investigation can not be accepted. The Department, therefore, is liable to refund the amount" (para 31)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026 (Division Bench judgment); summary confirmed; DB affirmation (03.03.2022) added as the citable ruling

(Odisha HC: s.54 read with s.107 covers refund of pre-deposit, and rejection because an SFIO probe is pending is unsustainable. Refund with interest. Party name not found: [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/refund-with-interest-allowed-as-no-sfio-probe-was-pending-hc/). A further Karnataka HC ruling (party not found) held that no interest is payable on a refund of amounts adjusted from the electronic credit ledger during a search unless a refund claim had been made earlier: [Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/no-interest-is-payable-on-refund-of-amount-deposited-during-search-by-way-of-adjustment-of-credit-amount-in-ecl-hc).)

## 3. Statutory developments to check alongside the case law

- **Notf. 14/2022-CT (05.07.2022):** amended the rule 89(5) formula. Treated as retrospective by Gujarat HC (*Ascent Meditech*).
- **Circular 173/05/2022 (06.07.2022):** substituted para 3.2 of Circular 135/2020. Treated as retrospective by Telangana HC (*Micro Systems*).
- **Notf. 13/2022-CT (05.07.2022):** excludes 01.03.2020-28.02.2022 from limitation for s.54/55 refunds.
- **Circular 162/18/2021:** relevant date for s.77 wrong-head refunds.
- **Notf. 20/2024-CT (08.10.2024):** omitted rule 96(10). Applies to pending proceedings (*Goodluck India*, SC, 06.08.2026). CBIC OM dated 24.08.2026 reportedly accepts this.
- **Finance Act 2026 (assent 30.03.2026, per secondary sources):** (i) s.13(8)(b) IGST omitted; (ii) s.54(6) 90% provisional refund extended to IDS claims (date to be notified); (iii) the ₹1,000 floor in s.54(14) no longer applies to export refunds with payment of tax; (iv) a reported rewrite of s.54(3) adding input services and capital goods, **enactment unconfirmed**.
