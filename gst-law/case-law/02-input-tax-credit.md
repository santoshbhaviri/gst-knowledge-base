# GST Case Law - Input Tax Credit (ITC)

**Scope.** Leading Supreme Court and High Court rulings on input tax credit under the CGST Act, 2017, for use by a CGST Assistant Commissioner (Sangareddy Division, Telangana) when drafting show cause notices and orders. Covers: s.16(2)(c) supplier non-payment and the "bona fide purchaser" defence; s.16(2)(aa) and GSTR-2A/2B mismatches; s.16(4) time limit and the 2024 relief in s.16(5)/(6); s.17(5)(d) after Safari Retreats and the Finance Act 2025 amendment; burden of proof on the dealer claiming ITC (Ecom Gill); other blocked credits (CSR, motor vehicles); rule 86A blocking of the electronic credit ledger; retrospective cancellation of a supplier's registration; fake invoices and circular trading; transitional credit (TRAN-1, s.140); and rule 42/43 reversals. Telangana High Court rulings found are flagged.

## Quick answers

**1. Supplier did not pay tax - can the recipient's ITC be denied (s.16(2)(c))?**
The position changed in 2026. The Supreme Court in *Bhandari Scrap Traders v. Union of India* (24 Jul 2026) dismissed the SLP against the Gujarat HC's *Maruti Enterprise* ruling (1 May 2026). It upheld s.16(2)(c) and refused to read it down for bona fide buyers. It also held that Delhi VAT precedents (*Arise India*, *On Quest*, *Shanti Kiran*) cannot be carried over to GST. Earlier pro-recipient rulings (*Suncraft Energy*, Calcutta HC, SLP dismissed Dec 2023; *D.Y. Beathel*, Madras HC 2021) were mainly about procedure: proceed against the supplier first, and take the recipient's ITC only as a last resort. A Tripura HC read-down (*Sahil Enterprises*, Jan 2026) is under SLP (the SC's order in *Bhandari* records that the SLP has been entertained).
*Takeaway:* s.16(2)(c) is valid and binding. Commentators still read *Bhandari* as not allowing mechanical denial. In the order: (a) record a verified finding that the supplier actually failed to deposit the tax, not just an arithmetic 2A/3B gap; (b) record what action was taken or attempted against the supplier; (c) consider the recipient's evidence; (d) note that the recipient can re-avail the credit once the supplier pays. Do not rely on a bare mismatch.

**2. GSTR-2A/2B mismatch (s.16(2)(aa)).**
s.16(2)(aa) has applied only from 1 Jan 2022. For earlier periods, High Courts (Kerala *Diya Agencies* 2023; Karnataka *Biocon*) have set aside denials that rested only on non-reflection in GSTR-2A. They remand so the recipient can prove the transaction was genuine. Circular 183/15/2022-GST (FY 2017-18 and 2018-19) lays down a supplier-certificate / CA-certificate procedure, and courts set aside orders that ignore it.
*Takeaway:* for pre-2022 periods, never deny ITC on 2A non-reflection alone. Call for the Circular 183 documents and examine them in the order. For periods from 2022, s.16(2)(aa) is a statutory condition, but still give a hearing and deal with the documents produced.

**3. Time limit (s.16(4)) and 2024 relief (s.16(5)/(6)).**
The vires of s.16(4) was upheld by the AP HC (*Thirumalakonda Plywoods*, 18 Jul 2023) and the Patna HC (*Gobinda Construction*, 8 Sep 2023; SC issued notice Jan 2024, outcome not found). Paying a late fee does not cure the time-bar. s.16(5) (FY 2017-18 to 2020-21, returns filed by 30 Nov 2021) and s.16(6) (revocation after cancellation) were inserted retrospectively from 1 Jul 2017. Courts set aside s.16(4) denials covered by s.16(5), and Circular 237/31/2024 provided a rectification route.
*Takeaway:* before confirming a s.16(4) demand, check s.16(5) and s.16(6) expressly and record why they do not apply. Any pending s.16(4) demand for FY 2017-21 where GSTR-3B was filed by 30.11.2021 is unsustainable.

**4. Immovable property / "plant or machinery" (s.17(5)(d)).**
*Safari Retreats* (SC, 3 Oct 2024) laid down a functionality test, under which a building can be "plant". The review was dismissed on 20 May 2025. Section 119 of the Finance Act 2025 then retrospectively (from 1 Jul 2017) substituted "plant and machinery" and overrides contrary decisions.
*Takeaway:* apply s.17(5)(d) as amended and cite the Finance Act 2025 amendment and its overriding clause, not the functionality test. Watch for constitutional challenges to the retrospective amendment (none found in results).

**5. Burden of proof on the claimant.**
*State of Karnataka v. Ecom Gill Coffee Trading* (SC, 13 Mar 2023, KVAT s.70) held that the dealer claiming ITC must prove that the transaction and the physical movement of goods are genuine. Invoices and bank payments alone are not enough. CGST s.155 has the same burden rule. HCs (Kerala *Diya Agencies*) cite *Ecom Gill* together with relief on 2A mismatch.
*Takeaway:* in fake-invoice or doubtful-supplier cases, ask for e-way bills, transport and freight proof, delivery records and stock entries. Record what was produced and why it fails. This is the strongest safeguard for an order.

**6. Rule 86A blocking.**
Blocking is limited to the balance actually in the ledger. "Negative blocking" is invalid. This is the view of Gujarat (*Samay Alloys*, 2022), Delhi (*Best Crop Science*, 24 Sep 2024; SLPs reportedly dismissed 2025) and **Telangana (*Laxmi Fine Chem*, 2024 - binding)**. Blocking also needs recorded independent "reasons to believe" and natural justice (**Telangana *Bhavani Oxides*, 26 Jun 2024**). Rule 86A is not a recovery tool; recovery is under s.73/74.
*Takeaway:* block only what is in the ECL on the date of the order. Record your own reasons based on material, not borrowed satisfaction. Give the taxpayer a hearing (the Telangana HC in *Bhavani Oxides* reads natural justice into rule 86A; Gujarat requires at least a post-decisional hearing within two weeks), and note that rule 86A(3) makes the restriction lapse after one year.

**7. Retrospective cancellation of the supplier's registration.**
HCs (Calcutta *LGW Industries* 2021, *Gargo Traders* 2023; Madras *Fathima Traders*; Delhi *Aryan Timber* on cancellation itself) hold that a later retrospective cancellation does not by itself defeat the recipient's ITC. The officer must examine whether the supplier was registered on the transaction date and whether the transaction was genuine (payment through bank, movement of goods, collusion). No Telangana merits ruling was found.
*Takeaway:* never deny ITC only because the supplier's GSTIN was later cancelled from an earlier date. Examine the genuineness of the transaction on evidence.

**8. Fake invoices / circular trading.**
HCs (Calcutta, MP, Delhi, Chhattisgarh; Telangana in *Inam Impex* on a related point) usually decline writ jurisdiction where fraud or fake invoices are alleged and send parties to appeal under s.107. They do insist that relied-upon documents be supplied (Madras *S.S. Metals*).
*Takeaway:* give the noticee every relied-upon document (supplier statements, investigation reports). Make specific findings per supplier. Use s.74 only with a clear fraud finding.

**9. Transitional credit (TRAN-1 / s.140).**
*Union of India v. Filco Trade Centre* (SC, 22 Jul 2022) directed the portal to be opened for TRAN-1/TRAN-2 for two months (later extended), for all assessees, followed by 90-day verification. It left High Court questions of law on s.140 open. Earlier, the SC dismissed the SLP in *Adfert* (28 Feb 2020) and stayed *Brand Equity* (19 Jun 2020). The Telangana HC followed *Filco* (*Parle Agro*, 2022).
*Takeaway:* for claims outside the Filco window, rely on the retrospective s.140 amendment (Finance Act 2020) and Filco's closure. In verification, decide eligibility on merits with a speaking order.

**10. Rule 42/43 reversal.**
The Calcutta HC (*Hemraj Rice Mill*, 16 Jun 2025) set aside a reversal confirmed without applying the rule 42/43 formula and remanded it, holding that the appellate authority should have called for the reconciliation statements.
*Takeaway:* show the formula computation in the order and call for the reconciliation statement. Do not apply rule 42 where there is no exempt turnover (non-GST / no-supply entries in GSTR-9 Table 5 are not automatically "exempt").

## Issue 1 - s.16(2)(c): supplier non-payment and the "bona fide purchaser"

### Bhandari Scrap Traders v. Union of India - Supreme Court - 24 Jul 2026
- Citation / case no.: SLP(C) No. 23931 of 2026 with SLP(C) Nos. 24088 and 24103 of 2026; order dated 24.07.2026 (Record of Proceedings, reasoned dismissal). Bench: Sanjay Kumar and Sanjeev Sachdeva JJ. Against Gujarat HC judgment dated 01.05.2026 in SCA No. 749 of 2025. Reported as 2026 LiveLaw (SC) 725 (secondary; not checked).
- Holding: dismissed the SLPs and affirmed the Gujarat HC, expressing "complete and respectful agreement" with it. The HC was right not to declare s.16(2)(c) unconstitutional or read it down. Relying on the HC's analysis (para 42 onwards and para 56), there is "no possibility of drawing parity" between the Delhi VAT Act and the CGST Act so as to treat a CGST purchaser as a bona fide purchaser under DVAT. Noted the HC's reliance on s.41 (re-availment once the supplier is made to pay) and ss.73/74. The order notes that an SLP against the Tripura HC in *Sahil Enterprises* has been entertained, but says the Tripura HC did not undertake the Gujarat HC's exercise. It does not mention *Suncraft* or a "pursue the supplier first" rule.
- Key words: "the High Court was fully justified in holding that no grounds were made out to declare Section 16(2)(c) of the CGST Act as unconstitutional or read down the provisions thereof." (order, p.2)
- Binding: **Yes - Supreme Court.** It is a short reasoned order affirming the HC (not a bare dismissal), so the affirmed HC reasoning carries SC approval. It is not a full judgment; cite it with the Gujarat HC judgment.
- Later history: SLP in *Sahil Enterprises* (Tripura) pending per this order. Commentary says it does not authorise automatic or mechanical denial.
- Use in orders: cite as the controlling authority that the supplier's actual payment of tax is a condition for ITC. Pair it with a factual finding of non-payment and an opportunity to the recipient.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - SC order 24.07.2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/161729461/); [LiveLaw - SC upholds validity of s.16(2)(c)](https://www.livelaw.in/supreme-court/gst-itc-available-to-buyer-only-if-supplier-paid-tax-supreme-court-upholds-validity-of-s-162c-of-cgst-act-543149); [KS&K newsletter](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/section-162c-cgst-act/); [HNA - s.16(2)(c) and Bhandari Scrap Traders](https://hnallp.com/a/section-162c-and-supreme-court-decision-of-bhandari-scrap-traders)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; added companion SLPs, HC case number (SCA 749/2025) and the form of order (short reasoned order); holding confirmed.

### Maruti Enterprise v. Union of India - Gujarat High Court - 1 May 2026
- Citation / case no.: [2026] 186 taxmann.com 90 (Guj). The SC order in *Bhandari Scrap Traders* confirms a Gujarat HC judgment dated 01.05.2026 in SCA No. 749 of 2025 upholding s.16(2)(c) (paras 42 and 56 discuss DVAT and the ITC scheme). Division Bench (A.S. Supehia and Pranav Trivedi JJ, per one source). One source names the lead matter *Jigneshbhai Bharatbhai Tarpara v. Union of India*. The title is uncertain.
- Holding: upheld s.16(2)(c). ITC is a statutory benefit, not a vested right, and may be made conditional. Actual payment of tax by the supplier is an independent, cumulative condition that is unaffected by bona fides. Refused to read the provision down.
- Binding: affirmed by the SC (above), so the reasoning now carries SC approval.
- Later history: SLP dismissed / affirmed in *Bhandari Scrap Traders* (24 Jul 2026).
- Use in orders: cite with *Bhandari* for the reasoning that ITC is a conditional statutory concession. Confirm the exact title and paragraph numbers from the Gujarat HC text before quoting.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - SC order in Bhandari (confirms date and SCA number)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/161729461/); [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1072601); [taxo.online - SC affirmed validity](https://taxo.online/latest-news/29-07-2026-bona-fide-buyer-defence-rejected-supreme-court-affirmed-the-constitutional-validity-of-section-162c-of-the-cgst-act/)
- Verified: Secondary - search summaries (3 sources agree; case title differs) (not found on Indian Kanoon on 14 Oct 2026; date and SCA No. 749/2025 confirmed from the SC order in *Bhandari*).

### Sahil Enterprises v. Union of India - Tripura High Court - 6 Jan 2026
- Citation / case no.: WP(C) No. 688 of 2022, judgment 06.01.2026; (2026) 154 GSTR 108 (Tri.) (as cited in the SC order in *Bhandari*). Division Bench: M.S. Ramachandra Rao CJ and S. Datta Purkayastha J.
- Holding: s.16(2)(c) is not violative of Arts. 14, 19(1)(g), 265 or 300A, but is read down: it is not to be applied to deny ITC to a purchaser in a bona fide transaction, only where the transaction is not bona fide, collusive or fraudulent. The Department had invoked only s.73, not s.74, and did not dispute that the petitioner paid Rs 1.12 crore GST to the supplier; the denial order was set aside and ITC directed to be allowed. Relied on the DVAT rulings (*On Quest*, *Arise India*).
- Key words: "it should be read down and applied only where the transaction is found to be not bona fide or is a collusive transaction or fraudulent transaction to defraud the revenue." (para 57(b))
- Binding: Tripura only; persuasive elsewhere. **Its reasoning conflicts with *Bhandari Scrap Traders* (SC, later in time), which rejected the read-down approach.**
- Later history: the SC order in *Bhandari* (24.07.2026) records that an SLP against this judgment has been entertained. Outcome not known.
- Use in orders: if the noticee cites it, answer with *Bhandari Scrap Traders*.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/128910236/); [taxo.online](https://taxo.online/latest-news/08-01-2026-a-bona-fide-purchaser-who-has-paid-gst-to-the-supplier-cannot-be-penalised-for-the-suppliers-failure-to-remit-tax-tripura/); [LiveLaw (via KLA library)](https://livelaw.library.keralalawacademy.in/high-court/tripura-high-court/cgst-s-162c-not-unconstitutional-but-itc-cannot-be-denied-to-bona-fide-purchaser-for-supplier-default-tripura-high-court-517128)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; added case number, bench and citation; date 6 Jan 2026 confirmed; SLP "entertained" confirmed from the SC order in *Bhandari*.

### Assistant Commissioner of State Tax v. Suncraft Energy Pvt Ltd - Calcutta HC (2 Aug 2023) / Supreme Court SLP dismissed 14 Dec 2023
- Citation / case no.: Calcutta HC (Division Bench, T.S. Sivagnanam CJ and Hiranmay Bhattacharyya J): MAT 1218 of 2023 with CAN 1 of 2023, judgment 02.08.2023, in appeal from the single-judge order in WPA 12153 of 2023 (21.06.2023). SC: SLP(C) Nos. 27827-27828/2023, order 14.12.2023 (B.V. Nagarathna and Ujjal Bhuyan JJ).
- Holding: (WBGST, FY 2017-18, demand Rs 6.5 lakh) the HC set aside an ITC reversal based on a GSTR-2A/3B mismatch where the buyer had produced invoices and bank proof of payment and the officer had made no enquiry or recovery against the supplier. Relying on the CBIC press release of 04.05.2018, it held the officer must first act against the supplier; reversal from the buyer is only for exceptional cases (collusion, missing supplier, closed business, no assets).
- Key words: "before directing the appellant to reverse the input tax credit and remit the same to the government, the first respondent ought to have taken action against the fourth respondent the selling dealer" (HC, para 9)
- Binding: Calcutta HC. The SC dismissed the SLP "having regard to the facts and circumstances of this case(s) and the extent of demand being on the lower side", so the dismissal does not affirm the law. Its weight on the merits is now limited by *Bhandari Scrap Traders* (2026), but its procedural point (pursue the supplier first) is still cited.
- Later history: SLP dismissed 14.12.2023 on the small-amount ground. Effectively qualified by *Bhandari* (2026) on the substantive question.
- Use in orders: show in the order what steps were taken against the defaulting supplier before reversing from the recipient.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Calcutta HC 02.08.2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/110803637/); [Indian Kanoon - SC order 14.12.2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/129861655/); [Taxmann - SLP dismissed](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/slp-dismissed-against-hc-order-where-demand-of-itc-reversal-was-set-aside-holding-that-investigation-against-supplier-is-a-pre-requisite/); [VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/blog/itc-cant-denied-recipient-recovery-effort-supreme-court-dismissed-departments-slp)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; added HC case number, date (2 Aug 2023) and benches; confirmed the SC dismissal expressly rests on the small amount involved.

### Arise India Ltd v. Commissioner of Trade & Taxes - Delhi High Court - 26 Oct 2017 (SC SLP dismissed 10 Jan 2018)
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) 2106 of 2015, order dated 26.10.2017 (S. Muralidhar and Chander Shekhar JJ), disposed of by the common judgment of the same date in *On Quest Merchandising India Pvt Ltd v. GNCTD*, W.P.(C) 6093/2017 and batch. SLP dismissed 10.01.2018 (as recorded by the Calcutta HC in *Suncraft*; SLP number not found).
- Holding (Delhi VAT): the expression "dealer or class of dealers" in s.9(2)(g) DVAT Act was read down to exclude a purchasing dealer who bona fide bought from a validly registered seller on proper tax invoices with no 2A/2B mismatch; otherwise the provision would violate Art. 14. The remedy is against the defaulting seller; s.40A can be used where collusion is shown. Default assessments set aside.
- Key words: "the remedy for the Department would be to proceed against the defaulting selling dealer to recover such tax and not deny the purchasing dealer the ITC." (On Quest common judgment, para 54)
- Binding: VAT law. **The SC in *Bhandari Scrap Traders* (2026) held that DVAT precedents cannot be equated with the CGST Act**, so it is of limited use under GST.
- Later history: SLP dismissed 2018; the SC in *Shanti Kiran* (9 Oct 2025) noted the On Quest SLP was disposed of without interference. Distinguished for GST by *Bhandari* (2026).
- Use in orders: if the noticee relies on it, distinguish it as a VAT decision, citing *Bhandari*.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order and common judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/50881698/); [taxo.online - judgment note](https://taxo.online/judgment/arise-india-ltd-v-ctt-wpc-2106-of-2015-dated-26-10-2017-the-delhi-high-court/); [IndiaLaw - SC upholds ITC rights of bona fide dealers](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/tax/supreme-court-upholds-itc-rights-of-bona-fide-dealers/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; corrected: the reasoning is in the common *On Quest* judgment and rests on Art. 14 only (not Art. 19(1)(g)); added bench.

### Commissioner, Trade & Tax, Delhi v. Shanti Kiran India (P) Ltd - Supreme Court - 9 Oct 2025
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal Nos. 2042-2047 of 2015 with Civil Appeal No. 9902 of 2017; order dated 09.10.2025 (Manoj Misra and N. Kotiswar Singh JJ); 2025 TAXSCAN (SC) 317 (secondary). Against the Delhi HC judgment of 04.01.2013.
- Holding (DVAT): dismissed the department's appeals. The sellers were registered on the transaction date and neither the transactions nor the invoices had been doubted on any inquiry, so there was no reason to interfere with the HC's direction to grant ITC after due verification. Noted that the On Quest SLP (No. 36750/2017) was disposed of without interfering.
- Key words: "there is no dispute regarding the selling dealer being registered on the date of transaction and neither the transactions nor invoices in questions have been doubted, based on any inquiry into their veracity" (para 7)
- Binding: SC, but on the DVAT Act, and a short fact-based order. Distinguished for GST by *Bhandari Scrap Traders* (2026).
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: distinguish as VAT-specific. Note that it turns on the absence of any inquiry into genuineness; where the officer has enquired and recorded findings, it does not help the noticee.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - SC order 09.10.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/37020422/); [ICAI CA Journal](https://cajournal.icai.org/article-details/judicial-affirmation-of-bona-fide-credit-entitlement); [Taxscan weekly round-up](https://www.taxscan.in/other-taxations/supreme-court-high-courts-weekly-round-up-1435316)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; exact date 9 Oct 2025 and connected CA 9902/2017 added; holding narrowed to its fact-based ground.

### D.Y. Beathel Enterprises v. State Tax Officer (Data Cell), Madurai - Madras High Court - 24 Feb 2021
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(MD) No. 2127 of 2021 and batch (17 petitions), Madurai Bench, G.R. Swaminathan J; (2021) 127 taxmann.com 80 (Mad).
- Holding: (TNGST) quashed orders reversing ITC from buyers where the sellers had collected tax and not paid it, for two reasons: the sellers were not examined or confronted in the enquiry (especially as the department alleged no movement of goods), and no recovery action had been taken against the sellers. Remitted for a fresh enquiry with the sellers examined as witnesses and, in parallel, recovery action against them. The court also said the VAT-era rule that ITC cannot be reversed for the seller's default "may not be straight-away applicable" under GST.
- Key words: "if the tax had not reached the kitty of the Government, then the liability may have to be eventually borne by one party, either the seller or the buyer." (para 12)
- Binding: Tamil Nadu; persuasive elsewhere. Contrary Madras view in *Pinstar Automotive* (primary liability on supplier, but reversal by the recipient allowed as a protective measure). Substantively limited by *Bhandari* (2026).
- Later history: appeal status not found. Cited in *LGW Industries* (Calcutta) and *S.S. Metals* (Madras).
- Use in orders: include the supplier in the enquiry (examine and offer cross-examination where the noticee asks), start recovery against the supplier, and record the outcome.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/64616243/); [VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/blog/supplier-defaults-payment-taxes-then-primary-liability-lies-supplier-itc-not-reversible-by-recipient); [BDO alert](https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/insights/alerts-updates/indirect-tax-alert-gst-shall-be-recovered-from-the-suppliers-who-failed-to-remit-the-tax-after-col)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; corrected: remand was for a fresh enquiry examining the sellers, with recovery against them in parallel (not "first"); added bench and batch.

### McLeod Russel India Ltd v. Union of India - Gauhati High Court - 9 Dec 2025
- Citation / case no.: WP(C) No. 5725 of 2022; 2025:GAU-AS:16974-DB; oral judgment of Ashutosh Kumar CJ and Arun Dev Choudhury J.
- Holding: the challenge was to **s.16(2)(aa)** of the CGST and Assam GST Acts (not s.16(2)(c)). The court declined to strike it down but read it down "for the present": where the supplier is at fault, a bona fide purchaser must be given an opportunity to prove bona fides, verifiable from tax invoices and other documents, before ITC is denied. The read-down is to last only until CBIC devises a practical solution.
- Key words: "before denying the ITC benefits to a bona fide purchaser, he ought to be given an opportunity to prove his bona fides, which can be verified by the tax invoices and other documents." (para 20)
- Binding: Assam/NE states; persuasive. Belongs with Issue 2 (s.16(2)(aa)). Read-down reasoning is in tension with *Bhandari* (2026); the "opportunity to prove bona fides" point remains sensible practice.
- Later history: not found. (Indian Kanoon also lists unrelated McLeod Russel v. Union of India orders of 18 Sep 2026 on an industrial-incentive matter; do not confuse.)
- Use in orders: in a s.16(2)(aa) case, give the recipient a specific opportunity to show bona fides with invoices, payment and receipt documents, and deal with them.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/24531388/); [taxo.online](https://taxo.online/latest-news/before-denying-itc-to-a-bona-fide-purchaser-on-account-of-the-suppliers-default-the-department-must-provide-an-opportunity-to-the-purchaser-to-establish-his-bona-fides/); [KPMG flash - McLeod Russel](https://www.in.kpmg.com/taxflashnews/KPMG-Flash-News-Mcleod-Russel.pdf)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; **corrected: the provision read down is s.16(2)(aa), not s.16(2)(c)**; added date, case number and bench.

### Inam Impex Pvt Ltd v. Assistant Commissioner (ST) - Telangana High Court - 31 Mar 2026
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 9234 of 2026, order 31.03.2026; Aparesh Kumar Singh CJ and G.M. Mohiuddin J. TGST (State Tax, Secunderabad Division), assessment year 2021-22; OIO dated 30.12.2025.
- Holding: declined to entertain the vires challenge to s.16(2)(c) and relegated the taxpayer to statutory appeal (s.107 with pre-deposit). The State's own counsel said the SCN and order contained no allegation or finding that the supplier failed to deposit tax; the proceedings were for ITC in excess of what suppliers declared. Whether tax was paid against the invoices and goods received are factual questions for the authorities.
- Key words: "It is not evident from the order-in-original that the supplier has failed to deposit the taxes paid by the petitioner in lieu of the supplies made against invoices raised by it." (para 5)
- Binding: **BINDING IN TELANGANA** (on entertaining writs; no ruling on vires).
- Later history: none found. Consistent with *Bhandari* (SC) on validity.
- Use in orders: support for the appeal remedy. Its facts also warn that the SCN/order must contain a verified finding of the supplier's non-payment if s.16(2)(c) is invoked, not just a figure gap.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/79849375/); [taxheal - challenge dismissed, statutory remedy](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=130408); [thetaxcorp - Telangana HC on s.16(2)(c)](https://thetaxcorp.in/article/itc-reversal-for-supplier-default-under-section-162c-telangana-high-court)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; added case number and bench; summary confirmed.

### Rythu Seva Kendram, Bairanpally v. Superintendent of Central Tax - Telangana High Court - 30 Mar 2026
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 4967 of 2026, order 30.03.2026; Aparesh Kumar Singh CJ and G.M. Mohiuddin J. Respondent: Superintendent of Central Tax, Siddipet GST Range. OIO No. 92/2024-25-Adjn (Supdt) dated 13.02.2025; appeal rejected in APL-02 dated 13.11.2025.
- Holding: the appellate authority rejected the appeal without any hearing because it was time-barred (as CBIC counsel admitted). The rejection was set aside and the appeal remitted for a fresh order after hearing. The vires of ss.16(2)(c) and 16(2)(aa) can be raised after the appeal remedy is exhausted. If the pre-deposit has been paid, the OIO stays pending the appeal.
- Key words: "The matter is remitted to the appellate authority to pass a fresh order in accordance with law after granting opportunity of hearing to the petitioner." (para 7)

### Best Crop Science Pvt Ltd v. Principal Commissioner, CGST - Delhi High Court - 24 Sep 2024
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) 10980/2024 and batch (8 petitions); Vibhu Bakhru and Sachin Datta JJ.
- Holding: rule 86A permits disallowing debit only up to the ITC available in the ECL on the date of the order; blocking beyond that ("negative blocking") would turn it into a recovery order. ### Laxmi Fine Chem v. Assistant Commissioner - Telangana High Court - 18 Mar 2024

## Issue 8 - Retrospective cancellation of the supplier's registration

### LGW Industries Ltd v. Union of India - Calcutta High Court - 13 Dec 2021
- Citation / case no.: WPA 23512 of 2019 and connected petitions, order 13.12.2021; Md. Nizamuddin J (single judge).
- Holding: ITC reversal cases arising from the suppliers' retrospective cancellation were remanded for fresh consideration. The authority must examine the documents on genuineness, whether the price with GST was actually paid to the suppliers, whether the purchases were before or after cancellation, and whether the buyer verified the suppliers' identity. If the purchases are genuine, documented and before cancellation, ITC is to be allowed. The order does not expressly require proof of movement of goods. Some petitions in the batch were listed for later hearing.
- Key words: "If it is found upon considering the relevant documents that all the purchases and transactions in question are genuine and supported by valid documents and transactions in question were made before the cancellation of registration of those suppliers" (operative part)
- Binding: West Bengal; persuasive.
- Later history: followed in *Gargo Traders* (2023). Appeal not found.
- Use in orders: examine the transaction date, payment trail and the buyer's verification of the supplier, and record findings. Do not rely on the cancellation date alone.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/109803748/); [gstgyaan](https://gstgyaan.com/ms-lgw-industries-ltd-ors-v-union-of-india-ors-s); [taxheal - remanded](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=99067)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; exact date and judge added; holding restated from the operative part (remand; movement-of-goods point not in the order).

### Gargo Traders v. Joint Commissioner, Commercial Taxes (State Tax) - Calcutta High Court - 12 Jun 2023

### Fathima Traders v. Deputy Commercial Tax Officer - Madras High Court - 12 Jun 2026

## Issue 11 - Rule 42/43 reversal for exempt supplies

### Hemraj Rice Mill v. Assistant Commissioner, CGST & CX - Calcutta High Court - 16 Jun 2025
- Citation / case no.: WPA 3159 of 2025, order 16.06.2025; Raja Basu Chowdhury J. Respondent: Assistant Commissioner, CGST & CX, Bardhaman Division. Tax periods 2017-18 to 2018-19.
- Holding: the adjudicating authority confirmed a reversal of about Rs 55.11 lakh under s.17(2) without applying the rule 42/43 computation. The appellate authority recognised this but dismissed the appeal because no reconciliation was filed; under s.107(12) it should have called for the statements. As the taxpayer was also at fault, the OIO (27.12.2023), the appellate order (11.11.2024) and the DRC-07 (03.01.2024) were set aside and the matter remanded to the adjudicating authority; the taxpayer may file reconciliation statements.
- Key words: "the appellate authority having found that the petitioner had failed to afford appropriate reconciliation statement, ought to have called upon the petitioner to produce such statements to arrive at a correct conclusion" (para 5)
- Binding: West Bengal; persuasive. A CGST matter, so directly relevant.
- Later history: not found.
- Use in orders: set out the rule 42/43 computation (T1-T4, C1-C3, D1/D2 etc.) in the order. Call for the reconciliation statement; appellate authorities should call for it under s.107(12).
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/142760781/); [taxo.online 01.07.2025](https://taxo.online/latest-news/01-07-2025-failure-to-follow-rule-42-43-in-itc-reversal-proper-officer-must-apply-rule-42-43-formula-before-disallowing-itc-calcutta-high-court/); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/hc-remands-itc-reversal-case-for-non-compliance-with-rules-42-and-43)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; judge and dates added; holding restated (the order does not use the word "estimation"; remand was to the adjudicating authority).

### BirlaNu Ltd v. Union of India - Telangana High Court - Dec 2025 (reported Jan 2026)
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 14564 of 2024; (2026) 38 Centax 172 (Telangana) (as cited by the AP HC in *Re Sustainability Ltd v. Union of India*, 27.04.2026).
- Holding: rule 39(1)(a) of the CGST Rules is ultra vires s.20 (as it stood before the 2024 amendment) to the extent it mandates that an ISD distribute ITC in the same month. The consequential audit report and SCN (penalty about Rs 8.38 crore) were quashed. The ruling concerns FY 2017-18 and 2018-19. s.20 was amended to allow time limits from 1.4.2025.
- Binding: **BINDING IN TELANGANA.**
- Later history: appeal by the Union not found. Relied on by the AP HC (27.04.2026).
- Use in orders: do not deny ISD credit for pre-1.4.2025 periods only because distribution was not in the same month.
- Source: [LiveLaw Biz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/gst/high-court/telangana-high-court-strikes-down-gst-rule-mandating-monthly-itc-distribution-by-isds-518411); [taxo.online 17.01.2026](https://taxo.online/latest-news/17-01-2026-telangana-high-court-struck-down-rule-391a-to-the-extent-it-mandates-same-month-distribution-of-itc-by-an-isd-as-the-parent-statute-did-not-prescribe-or-permit-any-time-limit/); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/rule-39-itc-norms-invalid-under-section-20-hc)
