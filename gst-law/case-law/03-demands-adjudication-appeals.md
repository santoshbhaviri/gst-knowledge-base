# GST Case Law: Demands, Adjudication, Recovery and Appeals

**Scope.** This file covers show cause notices under ss.73/74/74A CGST Act: whether they are adequate (a DRC-01 summary versus a proper SCN, signatures, vagueness), consolidated multi-year SCNs, the challenges to the s.168A limitation-extension notifications (13/2022, 9/2023, 56/2023), personal hearing under s.75(4), the bar on exceeding the SCN in s.75(7), service through the portal's "Additional Notices" tab, s.74 suppression and s.73/74 conversion, s.128A waiver, s.107 appeal limitation and condonation, GSTAT, writs despite an alternative remedy, provisional attachment under s.83, recovery under ss.78/79 and e-way bill detention under s.129. It is written for an Assistant Commissioner, CGST, Sangareddy Division (Telangana). Telangana High Court rulings are flagged **BINDING IN TELANGANA**.

**Date compiled:** 13 Oct 2026

## Quick answers

**1. DRC-01 summary and unsigned notices or orders.** Courts have held that a summary in DRC-01 does not replace a proper SCN that states the contraventions, for example the Jharkhand HC in NKAS Services. Earlier Telangana DB rulings set aside unsigned SCNs and orders (Silver Oak Villas 2024; Bigleap Technologies 2025), but a **Telangana Full Bench (Gudla Vanaja, 30.09.2026)** has overruled that line: SCNs and orders electronically authenticated and uploaded on the portal are valid without a visible signature, but are invalid without a DIN/RFN. *Takeaway:* issue a full, reasoned SCN under s.73(1)/74(1) with the proposal, grounds and quantification, and attach it to the DRC-01. Make sure each SCN and order carries a DIN/RFN; signing digitally remains good practice.

**2. Consolidated SCN covering several financial years.** The courts are split.
- *Delhi HC (Ambika Traders 2025, followed in Mathur Polymers and Technosys) and now the Karnataka HC DB (23.04.2026):* a consolidated SCN is permissible, and in fraud/ITC cases "necessary". The SLPs were dismissed (Ambika as "not pressed"; Mathur Polymers in limine), so the Supreme Court has laid down no law.
- *Against consolidation:* Madras HC (Titan, affirmed by a DB on 21.03.2025), Kerala HC (Lakshmi Mobile Accessories; Tharayil Medicals) and Bombay HC (Milroc; Rithwik Projects, June 2026). A different Bombay bench referred the question to a Larger Bench in Rollmet LLP (17 Apr 2026). The Karnataka single-judge line (Bangalore Golf Club, Veremax) was **reversed by a Karnataka Division Bench on 23.04.2026** (W.A. 1751/2024 & batch), which held consolidation permissible.
- **Telangana HC (Goldstone Infra, DB, 27.01.2026)** followed Mathur Polymers and upheld a composite s.74 SCN and order for several years, holding there is no prejudice if each year is within its s.74(10) limit.

*Takeaway:* in Telangana a composite s.74 SCN/order is defensible (Goldstone Infra), provided each year is within its own limitation and the figures are shown year-wise. For s.73 cases, a separate SCN and order for each financial year remains the safest course.

**3. Limitation-extension notifications (13/2022, 9/2023, 56/2023).**
- *Upheld or not interfered with:* Telangana HC (Brunda Infra / HCC-SEW-MEIL-AAG JV batch, 02.01.2025: declined to interfere because the Supreme Court's Covid exclusion applies, but observed that 56/2023 lacked the mandatory GST Council recommendation), Allahabad HC on 9/2023 (Graziano) and Patna HC on 56/2023 (Barhonia Engicon, 27.11.2024).
- *Quashed:* Gauhati HC quashed 56/2023 (Barkataki Print, 19.09.2024). Madras HC (single judge, Tata Play batch) held both 9/2023 and 56/2023 illegal (12.06.2025).
- *Supreme Court:* the SLP against the Telangana judgment, **SLP (C) 4240/2025, HCC-SEW-MEIL-AAG JV v. Asst. Commr. of State Tax**, is pending. Notice was issued on 21.02.2025 and the matter was admitted before Pardiwala and Mahadevan JJ. No final verdict was found as of the latest reports.

*Takeaway:* the Telangana ruling binds Telangana officers, so orders passed within the extended dates are not invalid here. Expect writs and remands elsewhere (Delhi HC is remanding such cases pending the Supreme Court). Record in the order the date of the SCN, the notification relied on and the due date. Where possible, act within the un-extended limitation.

**4. Personal hearing under s.75(4).** A hearing is mandatory wherever an adverse decision is contemplated, even if the taxpayer did not ask for one or tick "no".
- Telangana HC: B.K. Steels / WP 18654 & 18766 of 2024 (25.07.2024; order set aside by consent for want of a s.75(4) hearing).
- Followed by the Madras, Madhya Pradesh, Patna, Gujarat, Punjab & Haryana, Allahabad, Calcutta and Uttarakhand HCs.
- A notice that shows "N.A." against the hearing date, or no date, time and venue, does not count as a hearing.

*Takeaway:* always issue a separate hearing notice with the date, time and venue, record the outcome, and offer an adjournment within the s.75(5) limit (three adjournments).

**5. Order cannot exceed the SCN (s.75(7)).** The order cannot demand more tax, interest or penalty than the SCN proposed, and cannot rest on grounds the SCN did not raise. Leading cases: Allahabad HC (Vrinda Automation 2025; Sanjay Construction 2026) and Karnataka HC (WP 18888/2025). *Takeaway:* quantify the interest and penalty in the SCN. If fresh grounds or figures emerge, issue a fresh or supplementary SCN within limitation.

**6. "Additional Notices" tab.** Delhi HC (ACE Cardiopathy, 2024:DHC:4108-DB, and many later cases) set aside ex parte orders where the SCN or hearing notices were uploaded only under the "Additional Notices & Orders" tab, which was not prominent before 16 Jan 2024. *Takeaway:* if the taxpayer did not respond, use another mode of service under s.169 as well (email, RPAD) and record it. Do not pass an ex parte order on portal upload alone.

**7. Section 74 invoked without specific allegations.** The **Supreme Court, G.R. Infra Projects Ratlam v. State of M.P. (19.08.2026, CA 11277/2026)**, held:
- The facts showing fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression must be set out in the SCN itself.
- A bland recital of "fraud or suppression" is not enough. Using "or" between the grounds shows the officer was unsure which applied.
- Deficiencies cannot be cured later through a counter-affidavit.

*Takeaway:* in every s.74/74A(5) SCN, plead the specific suppressed facts, the evidence and the intent. Do not use alternative boilerplate.

**8. Conversion from s.74 to s.73 (s.75(2)).** If fraud is not established, the demand is re-determined as if under s.73. Whether s.73 limitation then bars it is unsettled. The GSTAT (Sterling & Wilson, reported) held that the *appellate* authority cannot itself convert a s.74 order into a s.73 order. Gujarat HC (R B Pandey and Sons, Feb 2026) refused conversion where the findings of fraud stood. *Takeaway:* make clear findings on fraud. Where fraud fails, re-determine under s.75(2) and check s.73 limitation.

**9. Waiver scheme (s.128A).**
- Madras HC: waiver is available for self-assessed tax once the department proceeded under s.73 (Incompressible Fluid Control, 28.07.2026). A rejection citing ITC from non-existent suppliers was set aside because the proceedings had been under s.73, not s.74 (A. Gurusamy, 16.06.2026).
- Karnataka HC: the filing deadline is directory (Sri Laxmi Borewell, 17.04.2026). The Madras HC disagreed and held the Rule 164 time limits mandatory (Incompressible Fluid Control).

No Telangana HC ruling was found. *Takeaway:* decide s.128A applications strictly on the statutory and Rule 164 conditions, and give reasons.

**10. Appeal limitation (s.107) and writs.**
- The Supreme Court (GlaxoSmithKline, 06.05.2020; note: an AP VAT case, not Central Excise or 2024) held that a writ should not be used to bypass a time-barred statutory appeal.
- Several HCs (relying on Singh Enterprises, SC) hold that the appellate authority cannot condone delay beyond 3 months + 1 month. The Supreme Court stayed (30.08.2024) an HC ruling that s.5 of the Limitation Act applies, and in July/Aug 2026 issued notice on SLPs raising the point, so the question is pending.
- The Telangana HC usually remits delay questions to the appellate authority. It also requires the appellate authority to hear the appellant on a condonation application (s.107(8)).

*Takeaway:* for officers acting as appellate authority, give a hearing on condonation and pass a reasoned order. Do not condone beyond the s.107(4) cap.

**11. GSTAT.**
- Formally launched 24.09.2025. Chennai Bench reported operational from 22.01.2026.
- Backlog appeals deadline: originally 30.06.2026 (S.O. 4220(E), 17.09.2025), extended to 31.07.2026 by a 30.06.2026 notification.
- Later orders follow the normal 3-month limit under s.112.
- Madras HC (Revenue Bar Association, 20.09.2019) held the original composition of GSTAT unconstitutional.

*Takeaway:* once an appeal is filed with the pre-deposit, recovery of the balance is stayed (s.112(9) and s.107(7)). Lift attachments once proof is shown.

**12. Writ despite an alternative remedy.** The Supreme Court in Radha Krishan Industries (20.04.2021) held that an alternative remedy is a rule of discretion, not a bar. Writs lie for breach of natural justice, lack of jurisdiction or vires challenges. *Takeaway:* orders that breach natural justice (no hearing, no reasons, defective service) will be quashed in writ even though an appeal lies.

**13. Provisional attachment (s.83).**
- The Commissioner must form an opinion on tangible material that attachment is necessary to protect revenue. Power delegated to a Joint Commissioner is exercised as the Commissioner's delegate (Radha Krishan, SC).
- An attachment lapses automatically after one year (Kesari Nandan Mobile, SC 2025 INSC 983; Krish Overseas, Delhi HC).

*Takeaway:* record the reasons and material in the file. Do not continue a bank freeze beyond one year, and release it on Rule 159(5) objections or on pre-deposit and appeal where the law requires.

**14. Recovery before the appeal period ends (ss.78/79).**
- No recovery within 3 months of service of the order unless the proper officer records written reasons under the s.78 proviso (Cargotec India, Madras HC 2024).
- An overdraft account cannot be attached under s.79 (Madras HC 2026).
- Filing an appeal with the pre-deposit stays recovery (Calcutta and Delhi HCs).

*Takeaway:* do not issue a DRC-13 garnishee notice or debit the ledger early without a recorded, specific reason.

**15. E-way bill detention and penalty (s.129).**
- Telangana HC in Satyam Shivam Papers (02.06.2021) held that where an e-way bill expired for a genuine reason (a road blockade) and there was nothing to suggest evasion, detention and penalty under s.129 were wrongly invoked, and imposed costs on the officer. The Supreme Court dismissed the department's SLP on 12.01.2022. **BINDING IN TELANGANA.**
- Allahabad HC has repeatedly quashed s.129 penalties for technical errors without intent to evade.

*Takeaway:* before a s.129 order, record the explanation offered and a specific finding on intent to evade or on actual tax risk.

## 1. SCN requirements: DRC-01 summary, signature, form

### NKAS Services Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Jharkhand - Jharkhand HC (DB: Aparesh Kumar Singh and Anubha Rawat Choudhary JJ.) - 06.10.2021 (W.P.(T) No. 2444 of 2021); second writ W.P.(T) No. 2659 of 2021 (order of 09.02.2022 as reported)
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(T) No. 2444 of 2021, judgment dated 06.10.2021. A second writ by the same petitioner, W.P.(T) No. 2659 of 2021 (s.73 SCN dated 14.06.2021), was heard on 10.01.2022 "on same principles"; its final order (reported as 09.02.2022) was not found.
- Holding: A s.74 SCN that did not state the foundational allegations (fraud, wilful misstatement, suppression) was vague and violated natural justice; a summary in Form GST DRC-01 under Rule 142(1) "cannot substitute" a proper SCN. Both the SCN and the DRC-01 were quashed, with liberty to start fresh proceedings within four weeks (paras 17-18).
- Key words: "A summary of show-cause notice as issued in Form GST DRC-01 in terms of Rule 142(1) of the JGST Rules, 2017 ... cannot substitute the requirement of a proper show-cause notice." (para 17)
- Binding: Persuasive (Jharkhand HC).
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: always issue a self-contained SCN setting out facts, contraventions, provisions and quantification, and attach it to the DRC-01.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Jharkhand HC W.P.(T) 2444/2021, 06.10.2021](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/9472001/); [Indian Kanoon - W.P.(T) 2659/2021, order 10.01.2022](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/61231496/); [Taxo - Jharkhand HC: mere summary in DRC-01 cannot substitute SCN](https://taxo.online/latest-articles/mere-summary-in-form-drc-01-cannot-substitute-the-requirements-of-a-proper-show-cause-notice-under-section-73-or-74-jharkhand-high-court/); [Taxmann - Summary of SCN in DRC-01 cannot substitute SCN (court not named in snippet)](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/summary-of-scn-in-form-gst-drc-01-cannot-substitute-statutory-requirement-of-scn-under-cgst-act-hc)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026. **Corrected:** the reasoned judgment is W.P.(T) 2444/2021 dated 06.10.2021 (not 09.02.2022); bench added (Aparesh Kumar Singh J., now Chief Justice of the Telangana HC, wrote it). The 09.02.2022 order in W.P.(T) 2659/2021 was not found.

### Silver Oak Villas LLP v. Assistant Commissioner (ST) - Telangana HC (Division Bench) - 14.03.2024 - **SUPERSEDED: see Full Bench, Gudla Vanaja (30.09.2026)**
- Citation / case no.: WP No. 6671 of 2024 (as reported); [2025] 137 GSTR 620 (Telangana) (cite as given by the Full Bench).
- Holding: Relying on Rule 26(3) CGST Rules and the Bombay HC in Ramani Suchit Malushite, held that an unsigned order lacks efficacy; the SCN and order were set aside (as described by the Telangana Full Bench, which read it).
- Binding: **No longer reliable.** A Telangana DB in Bigleap Technologies (28.02.2025) held its Rule 26(3) reasoning per incuriam (Rule 26(3) is in the registration chapter), and the Full Bench in Gudla Vanaja (30.09.2026) held that SCNs and orders under Chapter XVIII electronically authenticated on the portal are valid without a visible signature.
- Later history: See Binding. The Full Bench (paras 34-38, 143-146) treated Silver Oak Villas, Sahithi Marketers and Bigleap as conflicting DB decisions, which is why the question was referred.
- Use in orders: do not rely on it. Follow Gudla Vanaja (Full Bench): make sure every SCN and order carries a DIN/RFN and is uploaded through the officer's authenticated portal login; signing digitally remains good practice.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Telangana HC Full Bench, Gudla Vanaja, 30.09.2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/140433973/); [TaxHeal - Summary attachment to DRC-01 without duly authenticated SCN invalidates s.73 demand](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=138722)
- Verified: Primary (indirect) - the 14.03.2024 judgment itself was not found on Indian Kanoon on 14 Oct 2026; its date, citation and reasoning are confirmed from the Full Bench judgment in Gudla Vanaja (read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026). **Corrected:** now superseded.

### Bigleap Technologies and Solutions Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Telangana - Telangana HC (DB: Acting CJ Sujoy Paul and Renuka Yara J.) - 28.02.2025 - **OVERRULED by Full Bench, Gudla Vanaja (30.09.2026)**
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 21101 of 2024 and batch; [2025] 142 GSTR 608 (Telangana).
- Holding: SCNs and orders placed on the portal without the physical or digital signature of the proper officer (admitted by the State) "cannot sustain judicial scrutiny", because the statutory DRC forms require a signature; it disagreed with Silver Oak Villas on Rule 26(3) but reached the same result through Chapter XVIII and the forms. Notices and orders set aside, with liberty to issue fresh ones and limitation "will not be a hurdle" (paras 47-48).
- Key words: "the impugned show cause notices and the orders which are not pregnant with the signature of the Proper Officer cannot sustain judicial scrutiny." (para 47)
- Binding: **Overruled.** The Full Bench in Gudla Vanaja (30.09.2026) held that Bigleap and similar decisions "do not lay down the correct position of law" (para 154).
- Later history: An SLP against Bigleap was pending without stay (as recorded in Gudla Vanaja); its outcome was not checked. Overruled by the Full Bench on 30.09.2026.
- Use in orders: do not rely on it. See Gudla Vanaja below.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Bigleap Technologies, 28.02.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/131900579/); [Indian Kanoon - Telangana HC Full Bench, Gudla Vanaja, 30.09.2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/140433973/); [LiveLaw - Telangana HC ruling: GST SCN without digital signature](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/telangana-high-court/telangana-high-court-ruling-gst-show-cause-notice-without-digital-signature-central-goods-and-services-act-285563)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026. **Corrected:** party name, case number and date identified (previously "party name not found"); the holding has since been overruled by a Full Bench.

### Gudla Vanaja v. Assistant Commissioner (ST), Saroor Nagar-I Circle - Telangana HC (**Full Bench**: CJ Aparesh Kumar Singh, N. Tukaramji and G.M. Mohiuddin JJ.) - 30.09.2026 - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 20397 of 2025 and a batch of about 200 writ petitions (reference made by a Division Bench on 12.06.2026). Reserved 15.07.2026, pronounced 30.09.2026.
- Holding: SCNs and orders under Chapter XVIII (Demands and Recovery) that are electronically authenticated by the proper officer and uploaded on the common portal are valid even without a visible digital or physical signature on the form, and their upload is valid service under s.169(1)(d). **A notice or order without a DIN or RFN is invalid.** Limitation for appeal runs from the date of upload. Bigleap Technologies and similar decisions do not lay down the correct law. Petitioners were given two weeks to appeal (paras 154-155).
- Key words: "absence of DIN or RFN on any of these documents would render it invalid. The period of limitation to avail statutory remedy or to make other compliances would run from the date on which the show cause notice or order is uploaded". (para 154)
- Binding: Binding on all Telangana officers and on Telangana HC benches of lesser strength.
- Later history: none found (any SLP not checked; the judgment is two weeks old).
- Use in orders: when a taxpayer argues that an SCN or order is void for want of a signature, cite this Full Bench. Make sure every SCN, DRC-01/DRC-07 summary and order has a DIN/RFN and is issued from the officer's own authenticated login. Record the upload date as the date of service.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Telangana HC Full Bench, 30.09.2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/140433973/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; new entry added during verification (it overrules the Bigleap line and supersedes Silver Oak Villas).

### M/s Balaji Filling Station v. State Tax Officer, Bodhan and Nizamabad - Telangana HC (DB: CJ Aparesh Kumar Singh and G.M. Mohiuddin J.) - 14.08.2026 - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 25574 of 2026 (TGST Act).
- Holding: For FY 2017-18, the SCN dated 28.12.2023 was followed by an order on 30.12.2023. On the State's concession that the SCN was contrary to s.73(2) (SCN at least 3 months before the s.73(10) time limit), the SCN and order were set aside (paras 2-5).
- Key words: "the impugned order dated 30.12.2023 arising out of the show cause notice dated 28.12.2023 ... cannot be sustained in the eye of law being in teeth of the specific mandate of timeline to be adhered under Section 73(2)". (para 4)
- Binding: Binding in Telangana. It turned on a concession, so its precedential weight is limited.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: under s.73, the SCN must issue at least 3 months before the due date for the order.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Telangana HC W.P. 25574/2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/146036745/); [TheTaxCorp - Telangana HC quashes GST order and SCN for FY 2017-18 for violation of s.73(2)](https://thetaxcorp.in/article/telangana-high-court-quashes-gst-order-and-scn-for-fy-2017-18-due-to-violation)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; party, bench, case number and date (14.08.2026) added; summary confirmed. Note the order date (30.12.2023), two days after the SCN.

## 2. Consolidated SCN covering several financial years

### Ambika Traders through Proprietor Gaurav Gupta v. Additional Commissioner, Adjudication DGGSTI, CGST Delhi North - Delhi HC (DB: Prathiba M. Singh and Rajneesh Kumar Gupta JJ.) - 29.07.2025
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) No. 4853 of 2025; 2025 SCC OnLine Del 6913 (as cited by the Delhi HC in Sanskriti Exim, 18.09.2026).
- Holding: Following an earlier Delhi DB order (Vallabh Textile, W.P.(C) 13855/2024, 03.10.2024), a consolidated s.74 SCN/order for FY 2017-18 to 2021-22 is permissible; the provisions do not prohibit it, and where fraudulent ITC (here over Rs 83 crore) is alleged it may be "required". The order set out year-wise amounts, so it was decipherable (paras 52-55). Writ disposed of with Rs 25,000 costs; appeal allowed by 31.08.2025 without limitation objection (paras 67, 69).
- Key words: "A consolidated notice is, therefore, not merely permissible but, in fact, required in such cases in order to establish the illegal modality adopted by such businesses and entities." (para 54)
- Binding: Persuasive outside Delhi.
- Later history: SLP (C) No. 23774 of 2025 (Pardiwala and K.V. Viswanathan JJ.) **dismissed as not pressed** on 01.09.2025 (order read on Indian Kanoon). This creates no Supreme Court precedent. Followed in Sanskriti Exim (Delhi HC, 18.09.2026).
- Use in orders: can be cited outside Delhi only as persuasive. Do not describe it as approved by the Supreme Court.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Delhi HC W.P.(C) 4853/2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/158945360/); [Indian Kanoon - SC order 01.09.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/32081952/); [LiveLaw - Consolidated SCN for multiple FYs necessary to establish wrongful ITC: Delhi HC](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/delhi-high-court/consolidated-scn-for-multiple-financial-years-necessary-to-establish-wrongful-availment-of-itc-delhi-high-court-299763); [TaxHeal - SC order on SLP](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=106069); [Jurishour - consolidated GST SCN jurisdiction](https://www.jurishour.in/gst/consolidated-gst-scn-jurisdiction/)
- Verified: Primary - HC judgment and SC order read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date, case number, citation confirmed; the SLP link is now confirmed from the SC order itself.

### M/s Mathur Polymers v. Union of India - Delhi HC (DB: Prathiba M. Singh and Shail Jain JJ.) - 26.08.2025
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) No. 2394 of 2025 (challenge to OIO dated 02.02.2025).
- Holding: Following Ambika Traders, in fraudulent-ITC cases spread over several years a consolidated SCN/order "may in fact be required"; the statute does not prevent it. Hearing notices sent to the email registered on the portal (even if it was the CA's) were valid service; the petitioner had concealed material facts. Writ dismissed with Rs 50,000 costs (paras 21-25).
- Key words: "in cases involving allegations of fraudulent availment of ITC, where the transactions are spread across several years, a consolidated notice may in fact be required". (para 22)
- Binding: Persuasive outside Delhi.
- Later history: SLP **dismissed** by the Supreme Court (Sanjay Kumar and Alok Aradhe JJ.) on 07.11.2025: "no good ground and reason to interfere" (a non-speaking dismissal, not a declaration of law). A later miscellaneous application was withdrawn on 05.01.2026. The Bombay HC in Rollmet LLP (2026) reportedly cited the dismissal when referring the issue to a Larger Bench.
- Use in orders: supports a consolidated order in fraud/ITC cases. Rely on it with caution.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Delhi HC W.P.(C) 2394/2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/61101895/); [Indian Kanoon - SC order 07.11.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/187470167/); [TaxGuru - SC dismisses SLP of Mathur Polymers](https://taxguru.in/?p=1000495); [CAClubIndia - Bombay HC refers consolidated SCN question to Larger Bench](https://www.caclubindia.com/judiciary/consolidated-gst-show-cause-notice-for-multiple-years-bombay-hc-refers-matter-to-larger-bench-5969.asp)
- Verified: Primary - HC judgment and SC orders (07.11.2025, 05.01.2026) read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date, bench, case number added; the SCN/order was multi-year and fraud-based (confirmed).

### M/s Technosys Integrated Solutions Pvt Ltd v. Union of India - Delhi HC (DB: Nitin Wasudeo Sambre and Ajay Digpaul JJ.) - 16.03.2026
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) No. 5581 of 2025.
- Holding: An audit-originated s.74 SCN and OIO (31.01.2025; tax about Rs 19.97 crore on a rate-classification issue, not fraudulent ITC) covering several years was upheld against the consolidation challenge. The Court refused to confine Ambika Traders to fraudulent-ITC cases and read it as accepting consolidation under both s.73 and s.74; the Bombay HC's contrary view did not displace binding Delhi precedent; pendency before the Supreme Court is no reason to defer (paras 21-25). Writ dismissed, leaving other remedies open.
- Key words: "this Court, in the said judgment, contemplated and accepted consolidation of SCNs covering multiple financial years under both Section 73 and Section 74 of the CGST Act." (para 22)
- Binding: Persuasive outside Delhi.
- Later history: none found. (The earlier note of a "later Delhi bench (Aug 2025)" calling the issue res integra was not checked.)
- Use in orders: shows the Delhi view extends to s.73. It conflicts with Madras, Karnataka and Bombay.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Delhi HC W.P.(C) 5581/2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/128021198/); [TaxGuru (Delhi HC consolidated SCN)](https://taxguru.in/?p=1039290); [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=129652)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026. **Corrected:** date is 16.03.2026 (not 2025); case number and bench added; facts confirm a non-fraud (classification) case.

### Titan Company Ltd. v. Joint Commissioner of GST & Central Excise, Salem - Madras HC (Krishnan Ramasamy J., single judge) - 18.12.2023
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 33164 of 2023 (with W.P. 33120 of 2023); (2024) 15 Centax 118 (Mad.) (as cited by the Bombay HC in Rollmet).
- Holding: "Bunching" of s.73 SCNs for several years is against the spirit of s.73, because limitation runs separately for each financial year from its annual return; relied on State of J&K v. Caltex (each assessment year can be split up) (paras 14-15). Rather than quashing, the Court directed the authority to decide the petitioner's representation to split the SCN year-wise, extended the 2017-18 adjudication deadline by the 26-day stay period, and deferred proceedings meanwhile (para 16).
- Key words: "issuing bunching of show cause notices is against the spirit of provisions of Section 73 of the Act". (para 15)
- Binding: Persuasive (Madras HC).
- Later history: Affirmed by a Madras Division Bench on 21.03.2025 in W.A. Nos. 2389 and 1397 of 2024 (as recorded by the Madras HC in Balamore Estates, 28.07.2025; the DB order itself was not read). Followed in Madras (R.A. and Co., 21.07.2025), Karnataka (Veremax, 04.09.2024; Bangalore Golf Club) and Kerala (Lakshmi Mobile Accessories; Tharayil Medicals, 08.04.2025). ### HCC-SEW-MEIL-AAG JV v. Assistant Commissioner of State Tax (W.P. No. 32883 of 2024, part of the Brunda Infra batch W.P. No. 1154 of 2024 & batch) - Telangana HC (DB: Sujoy Paul and Dr. G. Radha Rani JJ.) - 02.01.2025 - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**

- Holding:
  - The batch challenged Notifications 13/2022, 9/2023 and 56/2023. ### G.R. Infra Projects Ltd., Ratlam v. State of Madhya Pradesh - **Supreme Court** (J.B. Pardiwala and K. Vinod Chandran JJ.) - 19.08.2026

- Holding:

- Use in orders: every s.74/74A(5) SCN must plead the specific facts of fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression, the evidence relied on and the intent. Avoid "fraud or suppression" boilerplate.

## 8. Appeals: s.107 limitation, condonation, writs

### Assistant Commissioner (CT) LTU, Kakinada v. GlaxoSmithKline Consumer Health Care Ltd. - **Supreme Court** (A.M. Khanwilkar and Dinesh Maheshwari JJ.) - 06.05.2020
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal No. 2413 of 2020 (arising from SLP (C) No. 12892 of 2019); AIR 2020 SC 2819; (2020) 19 SCC 681 (SCC cite as cited by the Telangana HC in Sri Sai Krupa Traders, 02.04.2026). An **AP VAT Act, 2005 (s.31)** case decided in **2020**, not a 2024 Central Excise case.
- Holding: Where the statutory appeal period (60 days under s.31 AP VAT Act, plus the condonable period) has expired, the High Court cannot disregard it and entertain a writ under Art. 226 against the assessment order as a matter of course; that would make the statutory scheme otiose. A writ filed within the appeal period on grounds of lack of jurisdiction or breach of natural justice may still be entertained (para 15).
- Key words: "if the writ petitioner choses to approach the High Court after expiry of the maximum limitation period of 60 days prescribed under Section 31 of the 2005 Act, the High Court cannot disregard the statutory period". (para 15)
- Binding: **Binding (Art. 141).**
- Later history: applied to GST by many HCs (e.g. Gujarat HC 2026). The Telangana HC has noted it but often still permits a delayed appeal with a condonation application and pre-deposit.
- Use in orders: cite when opposing writs filed after the appeal period, and when rejecting appeals beyond 3 + 1 months.

### Radha Krishan Industries v. State of Himachal Pradesh - **Supreme Court** (D.Y. Chandrachud and M.R. Shah JJ.) - 20.04.2021
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal No. 1155 of 2021 (arising from SLP (C) No. 1688 of 2021); AIR 2021 SC 2114; (2021) 3 SCR 406 (per the digiscr link). Arose under the HPGST Act.
- Holding:
  - A Joint Commissioner ordering provisional attachment acts as the Commissioner's delegate under s.5(3), so no s.107(1) appeal lay and the writ was maintainable; the HC erred in dismissing it (para 72(i)-(iii)).
  - The s.83 power is "draconian"; the Commissioner must form an opinion on tangible material that the assessee is likely to defeat the demand and that attachment is necessary to protect revenue (para 72(iv)-(vii)). It is not to be used to harass or cause irreversible harm to the business.

  - Once a final s.74(9) order is passed, the attachment ends; on appeal, s.107(6)-(7) govern stay of recovery (para 72(xi)-(xii)).
  - Alternative remedy is a rule of discretion; exceptions are enforcement of a fundamental right, breach of natural justice, proceedings wholly without jurisdiction, or a vires challenge (para 27(iii)).
  - Attachment orders dated 28.10.2020 set aside (para 74).
- Key words: "Before ordering a provisional attachment the Commissioner must form an opinion on the basis of tangible material that the assessee is likely to defeat the demand, if any". (para 72(v))
- Binding: **Binding (Art. 141).**
- Later history: widely followed (e.g. Allahabad HC 2025).
- Use in orders: a s.83 order must record the material and reasons. Expect writs to be entertained for breach of natural justice despite an appeal remedy.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - SC judgment 20.04.2021](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/62362537/); [SC judgment PDF (api.sci.gov.in)](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2021/1775/1775_2021_36_1502_27668_Judgement_20-Apr-2021.pdf); [ITATOnline digest](https://itatonline.org/digest/verdicts/radha-krishan-industries-vs-state-of-himachal-pradesh-orssupreme-court)

## 10. Provisional attachment (s.83) and recovery (ss.78/79)

### Kesari Nandan Mobile v. Office of Assistant Commissioner of State Tax (2), Enforcement Division-5 - **Supreme Court** (Dipankar Datta and Augustine George Masih JJ.) - 14.08.2025
- Citation / case no.: 2025 INSC 983; Civil Appeal No. 9543 of 2025, from Gujarat HC (DB) order of 29.01.2025 in SCA No. 16339 of 2024.
- Holding: A provisional attachment under s.83 ceases by operation of law one year after issue (s.83(2)), and the CGST Act gives no power to issue a second attachment order, on substantially the same grounds, after the first lapses. Fresh orders of 13.11.2024 and 18.12.2024 issued after the 2023 orders lapsed were set aside and the bank accounts de-frozen (paras 14, 30-31, 41). The investigation itself could continue (para 43).
- Key words: "fresh issuance of a provisional attachment order premised on substantially the same grounds as the earlier one would be in disregard to the safeguard provided in sub-section (2)." (para 31)
- Binding: **Binding (Art. 141).**
- Later history: none found. Followed by HCs, including a reported decision setting aside attachments dated 11.11.2024 that had outlived one year, and rejecting the argument that pending proceedings keep an attachment alive.
- Use in orders: diarise the one-year expiry. Do not ask banks to continue a freeze after that; a fresh order needs a fresh basis.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - SC judgment 14.08.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/173193457/); [TaxGuru - SC: provisional attachment lapses after one year](https://taxguru.in/?p=1058307); [TaxHeal - bank attachment ceases after one year under s.83(2)](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=111768)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; full party name, date, bench and case number added; holding sharpened: the actual question was a *second* attachment after the first lapsed (the Gujarat HC had allowed it).

### M/s Krish Overseas v. Commissioner Central Tax, Delhi West - Delhi HC (DB: Sanjeev Sachdeva and Ravinder Dudeja JJ.) - 16.04.2024

## 11. E-way bills and detention (s.129)

### Assistant Commissioner (ST) v. Satyam Shivam Papers Pvt. Ltd. - **Supreme Court** (Dinesh Maheshwari and Hrishikesh Roy JJ.) - 12.01.2022; arising from Telangana HC (DB: M.S. Ramachandra Rao and T. Vinod Kumar JJ.) W.P. No. 9688 of 2020 decided 02.06.2021 - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- Citation / case no.: SC: SLP (C) No. 21132 of 2021. HC: 2021 SCC OnLine TS 698; (2021) 50 GSTL 459
- Holding:
  - A truck carrying paper (invoice and e-way bill dated 04.01.2020) could not deliver in time because an anti-CAA/NRC agitation blocked traffic at Basheer Bagh on 04.01.2020; 05.01.2020 was a Sunday. The goods were detained on 06.01.2020 after the e-way bill expired.
  - The Telangana HC held that no intent to evade can be presumed merely because the e-way bill lapsed; the officer had to consider the explanation. It set aside the tax and penalty of Rs 69,000 and ordered the officer to pay Rs 10,000 costs.
  - The Supreme Court found no reason to interfere, dismissed the SLP as misconceived, and enhanced the costs.
- Key words: "On account of non-extension of the validity of the e-way bill by petitioner or the auto trolley driver, no presumption can be drawn that there was an intention to evade tax". (HC para 42, quoted with approval by the SC)
- Binding: **The Telangana HC judgment binds Telangana officers.** The Supreme Court order is a dismissal of an SLP, which is not a declaration of law under Art. 141, but it ended the department's challenge.
- Later history:
  - **Confirmed from the SC order:** the Supreme Court imposed a further Rs 59,000 costs on the petitioners (over and above the HC's Rs 10,000), payable within four weeks, and allowed the State to recover the costs from the person(s) responsible for the litigation. It refused to keep the s.129 question of law open, saying the case raised no question of law.
  - Followed by Kerala HC (2022), which holds that the officer must consider the explanation for an expired e-way bill (secondary).
  - The s.129 penalty scheme was amended from 01.01.2022 (Finance Act 2021); the impact was not checked.
- Use in orders: before detaining goods or imposing a penalty under s.129, record the explanation offered and a reasoned finding on intent to evade. Do not penalise a genuine, explained lapse.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - SC order 12.01.2022](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/17589683/); [Indian Kanoon - Telangana HC W.P. 9688/2020](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/13104689/); [SupremeCourtCases - Asst. Commr. (ST) v.
