# GST Case Law 04: Arrest, Search, Summons, Prosecution, and Landmark Constitutional and Levy Rulings

**Scope.** This file covers two areas. **Part A** covers coercive powers under the CGST Act: arrest (s.69 read with s.132), summons (s.70), search and seizure (s.67, including seizure of cash), payments taken during searches, anticipatory bail and bail, the CBIC arrest and summons instructions as the courts have read them, and how the CrPC/BNSS applies. **Part B** covers landmark constitutional and levy rulings that a field officer needs when drafting orders. These are: the status of GST Council recommendations (Mohit Minerals), secondment (Northern Operating Systems), lottery and online gaming (Future Gaming, Gameskraft), royalty (Mineral Area Development Authority), clubs and mutuality (Calcutta Club, IMA v. Union of India), corporate guarantee valuation (Torrent Power), the legal weight of CBIC circulars and advance rulings, and judicial discipline (Kamlakshi Finance). Telangana High Court rulings are flagged **BINDING IN TELANGANA**.

**Date compiled:** 13 October 2026.

## 1. Quick answers

**A1. Are ss.69 and 70 valid, and what must an arrest file contain?**
The Supreme Court upheld ss.69 and 70 in *Radhika Agarwal v. Union of India* (27 Feb 2025, 3 judges, 2025 INSC 272, (2025) 6 SCC 545), but it attached safeguards:
- "Reasons to believe" must rest on verifiable material and be recorded in writing.
- Written grounds of arrest must be given to the arrestee.
- CrPC/BNSS safeguards apply to GST arrests.
- Tax liability does not have to be finally assessed before an arrest.

*Rakesh Kumar v. UoI* (SC, 29 Jun 2026) treats the question of validity as closed.
**For orders:** the Commissioner's file should record the material-based reasons, the s.132 threshold, and why arrest is necessary. Give written grounds as an annexure to the arrest memo, with a DIN and an acknowledgement.

**A2. Must the s.69 authorisation be communicated before arrest?**
Yes. *Union of India v. Sunil Biyani* (SC, 2026 INSC 849; decided 12 Aug 2026) held that the s.69 order must be communicated before arrest, by email or mobile number given under Rule 8 or another lawful mode, so that the person can seek anticipatory bail. Without that communication, "question of arrest would not arise."
**For orders:** do not arrest on an authorisation the person has not been served with.

**A3. Can a person who has only been summoned get anticipatory bail?**
Generally no. In *State of Gujarat v. Choodamani Parmeshwaran Iyer* (SC, 17 Jul 2023) and *Bharat Bhushan v. DGGI* (SC, 5 Jul 2024), the Court held that s.438 CrPC is not available at the summons stage because no FIR exists; the remedy is Article 226, used sparingly. The Telangana High Court followed this in *Vemula Yougander* (14 Nov 2023, **BINDING IN TELANGANA**).
*Radhika Agarwal* and *Sunil Biyani*, however, recognise anticipatory bail once there is a real apprehension of arrest, that is, once a s.69 order exists.
Where the alleged amount is below Rs 5 crore, the offence is non-cognizable and bailable (s.132(4)–(5)), and the SC declined to interfere with refusal of anticipatory bail (*Harish Wadhwani*, 2026).

**A4. Is a summoned person an "accused"?**
No. *Radhika Agarwal* reiterates that a s.70 summons does not make the person an accused, so Article 20(3) is not engaged at that stage.
The Telangana High Court has refused to quash s.70 summons and requires the person to appear (*Devendra Surana*, 8 Apr 2026, **BINDING IN TELANGANA**).
**For orders and summons:** give reasonable time to respond. Follow Instruction 03/2022-23 (do not summon senior management as a matter of routine).

**A5. Can arrest come before adjudication?**
Yes, if the record supports it. Authorities:
- *P.V. Ramana Reddy* (Telangana HC, 18 Apr 2019; SLP dismissed May 2019) — **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- *Vimal Yashwantgiri Goswami* (Gujarat HC final judgment, 20 Oct 2020)
- *Radhika Agarwal* (2025)

The 2016 MakeMyTrip ruling (Delhi HC: no arrest without a show cause notice) was decided under the service tax law and should not be relied on for GST.

**A6. Is tax collected during a search valid?**
Only if it is genuinely voluntary. In *Bhumi Associate* (Gujarat HC, 2021, interim), the court directed:
- no recovery during search, by any mode;
- any voluntary DRC-03 to be filed on the next day, after the officers have left.

*Vallabh Textiles* (Delhi HC, 20 Dec 2022) and *Neeraj Paper Marketing* (Delhi HC) ordered refunds of amounts deposited during or around searches, with interest. *Radhika Agarwal* also condemned coercion to pay during search.
**For orders:** never rely on a DRC-03 paid during a search as an admission. Record the voluntariness separately, and issue a DRC-04 acknowledgement.

**A7. Can cash be seized under s.67(2)?**
The majority High Court view is no; cash is "money," not goods. Supporting rulings:
- *Deepak Khandelwal* (Delhi HC, 17 Aug 2023; Revenue's SLP and review dismissed)
- Rajasthan, Karnataka, Gujarat and Kerala High Courts (reported)

The Madhya Pradesh High Court took the opposite view in *Kanishka Matta* (2020). The Supreme Court issued notice on the point in Oct 2024; no final ruling was found.
**For orders:** do not seize cash merely as "unaccounted"; return seized items not relied on in the SCN (s.67(3)).

**B1. Are GST Council recommendations binding?**
Not on Parliament or the State legislatures; they have persuasive value (*Mohit Minerals*, SC, 19 May 2022). The same case struck down IGST under reverse charge on ocean freight in CIF imports, because it amounted to double levy on a composite supply.

**B2. Is secondment of employees a taxable supply?**
In *Northern Operating Systems* (SC, 2022), secondment was treated as manpower supply under the service tax law, and the reasoning is used in GST cases. CBIC Instruction 05/2023-GST (13 Dec 2023) tells officers not to apply it mechanically and not to invoke the extended period without suppression.
**For orders:** analyse the specific contract.

**B3. Online gaming.**
*DGGI v. Gameskraft* (SC, 27 May 2026, 2026 INSC 595) reversed the Karnataka High Court:
- operators supply actionable claims (betting), and are not intermediaries;
- the whole amount staked is taxable (no deduction for prize pools), valued under Rules 31B/31C, which the Court held retrospective; the SCNs applied 18% to 24.01.2018 and 28% after;
- the 2023 amendments are clarificatory;
- the show cause notices are restored for adjudication.

The rate is 40% from 22 Sep 2025.

**B4. Lottery.**
*Union v. Future Gaming Solutions* (SC, Feb 2025): no service tax on lottery distributors, because lottery is "betting and gambling," which only the States may tax (Entry 62, List II). The ruling concerns the pre-GST Finance Act, 1994 period.

**B5. Royalty.**
*Mineral Area Development Authority* (SC, 9 judges, 25 Jul 2024, 8:1): royalty is not a tax. This removes the argument that "GST cannot be levied on a tax." The separate question of whether royalty is a taxable supply is still being litigated; a Telangana High Court writ was at notice stage in Nov 2025. No final Supreme Court ruling on GST on royalty was found.

**B6. Clubs and associations.**
- *Calcutta Club* (SC, 3 Oct 2019): mutuality applies, so there is no sales tax (and, per summaries, no service tax) on club supplies to members.
- *IMA v. UoI* (Kerala HC Division Bench, 11 Apr 2025): s.7(1)(aa) and s.2(17)(e) CGST struck down as unconstitutional.
- The SC issued notice on the Union's appeal (25 Jul [2025]) with "no recovery steps against IMA."

The Kerala ruling is persuasive in Telangana, not binding, but expect it to be cited.

**B7. Corporate guarantee.**
*Torrent Power v. UoI* (Gujarat HC, 14 Aug 2026):
- Rule 28(2) is valid but read down; where actual consideration exists, it is taxed even if below 1%;
- the 1% value applies where the guarantee is gratuitous or its value cannot be determined;
- no levy for guarantees given before 26 Oct 2023;
- value is based on the subsisting guarantee balance.

The ruling is persuasive in Telangana.

**B8. Circulars, advance rulings and judicial discipline.**
- CBIC circulars bind officers but not courts or taxpayers. A circular contrary to an SC or HC ruling should not be enforced (*Ratan Melting & Wire*, SC Constitution Bench, 2008; CBEC Circular 1006/13/2015-CX).
- Advance rulings bind only the applicant and its jurisdictional officer (s.103).
- Lower authorities must follow orders of the appellate authorities and the jurisdictional High Court (*UoI v. Kamlakshi Finance*, SC).

**For orders:** follow Telangana High Court decisions even if a circular says otherwise. Do not rely on another taxpayer's advance ruling as binding (an HC set aside an SCN that did this).

## 2. Part A: Arrest, search, summons and prosecution

### A-I. Validity of arrest and summons powers, and the safeguards

### Radhika Agarwal v. Union of India - Supreme Court (3 judges) - 27 Feb 2025
- Citation / case no.: 2025 INSC 272; (2025) 6 SCC 545; [2025] 2 S.C.R. 1331; 2025 SCC OnLine SC 449. Lead case W.P.(Crl.) No. 336 of 2018 with connected criminal appeals and writ petitions. Three judges: Sanjiv Khanna CJI (main judgment, joined by M.M. Sundresh J) and Bela M. Trivedi J (separate concurring opinion).
- Holding:
  - Upheld ss.69 and 70 of the CGST Act and the Customs Act arrest powers.
  - "Reasons to believe" must rest on verifiable material and statutory monetary thresholds, not mere suspicion. Final assessment of liability is not a precondition.
  - Written grounds of arrest must be furnished.
  - CrPC/BNSS provisions "are equally applicable" to arrests under the GST and Customs Acts.
  - Anticipatory bail can be sought even without an FIR.
  - A person summoned under s.70 is not, merely by the summons, an accused.
  - The Court also held that s.74(5) "voluntary" payment "does not postulate payment under force, coercion or threat of arrest", that CBIC's circulars on this are binding, and that taxpayers who paid under threat, force or coercion may move the courts for refund (main judgment paras 64-68).
- Binding: Yes, Supreme Court (Art. 141). Binding on all officers.
- Later history: Followed in *Rakesh Kumar v. UoI* (SC, 29 Jun 2026) and *UoI v. Sunil Biyani* (SC, 2026). No review or reversal found.
- Use in orders: Cite for validity of ss.69/70. Arrest files must show material-based reasons recorded in writing, the s.132 threshold, written grounds, and CrPC/BNSS compliance (D.K. Basu, intimation, production before Magistrate within 24 hours).
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – Radhika Agarwal v. UoI, 27 Feb 2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/144838233/); [TaxGuru – SC upholds arrest powers with strict safeguards](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/section-69-gst-sc-upholds-arrest-powers-strict-safeguards.html); [Lawfoyer – Radhika Agarwal 2025 INSC 272](https://lawfoyer.in/radhika-agarwal-v-union-of-india-and-others-2025-2-s-c-r-1331-2025-insc-272/); [Taxmann – CrPC applies to GST/Customs arrests](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/provisions-of-code-of-criminal-procedure-would-also-apply-for-search-seizure-and-arrest-under-gst-act-and-customs-act-sc); [SC Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/journal/sc-calls-for-stricter-regulation-of-warrantless-arrests-by-revenue-officers/amp/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; bench and case number confirmed; coercion/refund point now confirmed from the text (paras 64-68 of the main judgment).
- Key words: "the reasons to believe must be explicit and refer to the material and evidence underlying such opinion. There has to be a degree of certainty to establish that the offence is committed". (para 59)

### Rakesh Kumar v. Union of India - Supreme Court - 29 Jun 2026
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(Crl.) No. 439 of 2024. Manmohan and K. Vinod Chandran, JJ.
- Holding: Challenge to the validity of s.69 disposed of as already settled by *Radhika Agarwal*. Interim order vacated. A short order, not a reasoned judgment.
- Binding: Supreme Court (short order applying *Radhika Agarwal*).
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: Cite to answer any renewed challenge to s.69's validity. The safeguards on how the power is exercised still apply.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – Rakesh Kumar v. UoI, order 29 Jun 2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/154767970/); [TaxHeal – validity of s.69 upheld following SC precedent](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=136116); [A2Z Taxcorp – validity conclusively settled](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/constitutional-validity-of-arrest-powers-under-section-69-of-the-cgst-act-stands-conclusively-settled-by-the-supreme-court/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; summary confirmed (noted it is a short order).
- Key words: "The legal validity of the said provision has been upheld by a three Judge Bench of this Court in Radhika Agarwal ... Consequently, the present Writ Petition is disposed of in terms of the aforesaid judgment". (order)

### Union of India v. Sunil Biyani - Supreme Court - 12 Aug 2026
- Citation / case no.: 2026 INSC 849. Criminal Appeal arising out of SLP (Crl.) No. 12535 of 2026. Dipankar Datta (author) and Sheel Nagu, JJ. Judgment dated 12 Aug 2026 (other reported dates are wrong). Arose from a Bombay High Court order of 13 Feb 2026 in ABA No. 3001/2025.
- Holding:
  - A s.69 arrest authorisation, being a "sine qua non to seek anticipatory bail," must be communicated to the person before arrest. It may be served by the email and mobile number given under Rule 8 CGST Rules or another lawful mode.
  - Without communication, "question of arrest would not arise." Communication does not obstruct investigation.
  - Once an anticipatory bail application is dismissed as not maintainable, a High Court or Sessions Court cannot grant independent interim protection. The Bombay High Court's one-week protection was set aside.
  - Rejection of pre-arrest bail at the bare summons stage was affirmed (summons alone does not make a person an accused).
  - The Court added (para 20) that the order is open to judicial review and that withholding the "reasons to believe" "could have drastic consequences on the right to liberty". The operative direction was to the Commissioner in this case, but the reasoning is general.
- Binding: Supreme Court.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: Before any arrest, serve the s.69 order and keep proof of service in the file. An arrest on an uncommunicated authorisation is at serious risk.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – UoI v. Sunil Biyani, 12 Aug 2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/180631499/); [Verdictum – UoI v Sunil Biyani 2026 INSC 849](https://www.verdictum.in/supreme-court/union-of-india-v-sunil-biyani-2026-insc-849-order-sec69-cgst-act-anticipatory-bail-accused-arrest-1619773); [TaxGuru – prior communication of arrest authorisation](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/no-secret-arrest-gst-supreme-court-restores-fairness-coercion.html); [Taxo – 14.08.2026 report](https://taxo.online/latest-news/14-08-2026-supreme-court-mandates-communication-of-gst-arrest-authorization-before-arrest-reinforces-right-to-seek-anticipatory-bail/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date fixed as 12 Aug 2026, SLP number added, para 20 remark on reasons added.
- Key words: "we require the Commissioner to communicate the order under Section 69 of the GST Act to the respondent ... We reiterate, without such communication, question of arrest would not arise." (para 22)

### P.V. Ramana Reddy v. Union of India - Telangana High Court - 18 Apr 2019 — **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- Citation / case no.: W.P. Nos. 4764, 4769, 4892, 5074, 5130, 5329, 6952 and 7583 of 2019 (batch), decided 18.04.2019 (the 2020 date in one commentary is an error). Division Bench: V. Ramasubramanian and P. Keshava Rao, JJ. SC: SLP (Crl.) No. 4430/2019, reported as 2019 (26) G.S.T.L. J175 (SC).
- Holding:
  - Dismissed the writs seeking protection from arrest. GST officers may pursue arrest in fake ITC cases before assessment.
  - Under s.69(1), "reasons to believe" need only be found in the file; they need not be disclosed in the authorisation (contrast "reasons to be recorded" in s.41A(3) CrPC).
  - Held the writs maintainable (Article 226 can be invoked even where s.438 CrPC does not apply) and that ss.41/41A CrPC protection may be available, but refused relief on the facts (alleged circular trading of about Rs 1,289 crore and ITC of about Rs 225 crore) (paras 47, 58-62).
- Binding: Telangana High Court, binding on Sangareddy officers. Read with the later Supreme Court rulings.
- Later history:
  - SLP (Crl.) No. 4430/2019 dismissed by the SC on 27 May 2019 (confirmed in the SC's *Sapna Jain* order of 29 May 2019 and in *Choodamani*, 2023, which expressly agreed with paras 34-43 of this judgment).
  - In *UoI v. Sapna Jain* (SC, 29 May 2019), the Court reminded High Courts of this dismissal and referred the broader question to a larger bench.
  - **Partly overtaken:** *Radhika Agarwal* (2025) and *Sunil Biyani* (2026) now require written grounds, communication of the s.69 order, and allow anticipatory bail. The "reasons only in the file" point still fits the Allahabad HC's *Jai Kumar Aggarwal* (2026: reasons need not be supplied, but grounds must be). Grounds must still be given in writing.
- Use in orders: Cite for arrest before adjudication in fake-ITC cases. Do not cite it to resist written grounds or communication of the authorisation.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – P.V. Ramana Reddy v. UoI, Telangana HC, 18 Apr 2019](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/87700723/); [Taxo – P.V. Ramana Reddy SLP 4430/2019](https://taxo.online/judgment/p-v-ramana-reddy-vs-union-of-india-2019-26-g-s-t-l-j175-supreme-court/); [LiveLaw – SC upholds Telangana HC](https://livelaw.in/top-stories/gst-evaders-can-be-arrested-145309); [Casemine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/in/telangana-high-court-upholds-arrest-under-cgst-act,-limiting-use-of-article-226-writ-petitions/view)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date 18.04.2019 and bench confirmed; holding refined (writ maintainable, relief refused on facts).
- Key words: "it is enough if the reasons are found in the file, though not disclosed in the order authorizing the arrest." (para 46)

### Union of India v. Sapna Jain - Supreme Court - 29 May 2019
- Citation / case no.: SLP (Crl.) Nos. 4322-4324/2019 and connected matters (from Bombay HC orders of 11.04.2019). Vacation Bench: the Chief Justice (Ranjan Gogoi) and Aniruddha Bose J. Record of proceedings (order), not a judgment.

### Vimal Yashwantgiri Goswami v. State of Gujarat - Gujarat High Court - Aug 2019 (interim) and 20 Oct 2020 (final)
- Citation / case no.: R/Special Civil Application No. 13679 of 2019 with SCA Nos. 3209, 4468, 4456 of 2020 and 13893, 14141 of 2019. Interim order 7 Aug 2019: J.B. Pardiwala and A.C. Rao, JJ. Final CAV judgment 20 Oct 2020: J.B. Pardiwala and Bhargav D. Karia, JJ.
- Holding:
  - **Interim (2019):** Stayed arrest. Arrest powers under s.69 must be used "with lot of care and circumspection," and prosecution should normally follow adjudication.
  - **Final (2020):** The Commissioner may authorise arrest under s.69 read with s.132 before adjudication or determination of liability. Reasons to believe must be recorded but a copy need not be given to the person; grounds of arrest must be informed. D.K. Basu guidelines apply to GST officers; an arrest memo is mandatory. Petitions rejected (para 77, 85).
- Binding: Persuasive (Gujarat HC).
- Later history: Appeal not found. Consistent with *Radhika Agarwal* (2025).
- Use in orders: Cite the 2020 final judgment, not the 2019 interim remarks, which taxpayers often quote.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – Vimal Yashwantgiri Goswami, Gujarat HC, 20 Oct 2020](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/25859143/); [Indian Kanoon – interim order 7 Aug 2019](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/64439129/); [Vaish Law – Commissioner authorised to arrest prior to assessment](https://www.vaishlaw.com/commissioner-authorized-to-arrest-prior-to-completion-of-assessment-under-the-cgst-act-gujarat-hc/); [TheTaxTalk – power to arrest](https://thetaxtalk.com/2020/10/gst-power-to-arrest-assessment-and-adjudication-of-alleged-evasion-of-gst/); [Taxo – PDF](https://taxo.online/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Vimal-Yashwantgiri.pdf)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026 (both the 7 Aug 2019 order and the 20 Oct 2020 judgment); final bench corrected to Pardiwala and Bhargav D. Karia JJ; summary confirmed.
- Key words: "the power to arrest as provided under section 69 of the CGST Act can be invoked if the Commissioner has reason to believe ... without there being any adjudication for the assessment". (para 77)

### Make My Trip (India) Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India - Delhi High Court - 1 Sep 2016
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) 525/2016 and W.P.(C) 1283/2016 (Ibibo), decided together. The "(44) STR 481" reference could not be confirmed; neutral reference 2016 SCC OnLine Del 4951 (as cited in *Radhika Agarwal*). Bench: S. Muralidhar and Vibhu Bakhru, JJ.
- Holding (**service tax law, Finance Act 1994**):
  - Two petitions (MakeMyTrip and Ibibo; no Ebiz petition in the judgment). The arrest of MMT's Vice-President without an SCN and without following s.73A(3)-(4) was held contrary to law and violative of Article 21; the searches were held to violate s.82 Finance Act.
  - Payments made during the searches were held not voluntary and ordered refunded within four weeks (6% simple interest if delayed), with costs of Rs 1 lakh in each petition (paras 116-119).
  - *Radhika Agarwal* (2025, para 58-59) agreed that arrest must be used with circumspection but rejected the broad proposition that assessment must precede arrest under GST.
- Binding: Persuasive only, and under a different statute.
- Later history: Reports conflict. One says the SC restrained coercive action; another says the SC upheld the High Court and DGCEI refunded the amounts. For GST, the opposite view prevails (*P.V. Ramana Reddy*, *Vimal Goswami* final, *Radhika Agarwal*).
- Use in orders: If cited by the taxpayer, distinguish it as a service tax case superseded for GST by *Radhika Agarwal* and the Telangana HC.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – Makemytrip v. UoI, Delhi HC, 1 Sep 2016](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/183692293/); [Bar & Bench – Delhi HC concern over arrest](https://www.barandbench.com/news/delhi-high-court-concern-over-arrest-makemytrip-official-tax-evasion); [The Quint – refund to travel portals](https://www.thequint.com/amp/story/news/india/delhi-hc-asks-govt-to-refund-service-tax-to-three-travel-portals)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; case numbers added; "Ebiz" removed (only MMT and Ibibo were petitioners); SC later history still not confirmed.
- Key words: "The Court is unable to accept that payment by the two Petitioners of alleged service tax arrears was voluntary." (para 116(ix))

### A-II. Grounds of arrest, DIN and arrest memo

### Kshitij Ghildiyal v. DGGI - Delhi High Court - 16 Dec 2024
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(CRL) 3770/2024. Single Judge: Anish Dayal J.
- Holding: A DGGI arrest was declared illegal and the remand order set aside because written grounds of arrest were not furnished at arrest (the remand application could not substitute for them). The Court also criticised summons whose DINs were generated after the time fixed for appearance (paras 33-50).
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: CBIC Instruction 01/2025-GST (Inv.) dated 13 Jan 2025 followed it. That instruction requires written grounds as an annexure to the arrest memo, with acknowledgement, and amends para 4.2.1 of Instruction 02/2022-23. One TaxGuru summary says the instruction "followed" *Radhika Agarwal*, but the dates make that impossible (Jan 2025 is before Feb 2025).
- Use in orders: Always serve written grounds and obtain acknowledgement.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – Kshitij Ghildiyal v. DGGI, Delhi HC, 16 Dec 2024](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/171445460/); [Taxscan – GST Investigation Wing guidelines](https://www.taxscan.in/?p=478893); [TaxGuru – arrest illegal for missing grounds / DIN](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-arrest-illegal-due-missing-grounds-cbic-din-arrest-memo-allahabad-hc.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date, case number and judge added; DIN-on-summons point added.
- Key words: "the requirement to communicate the grounds of arrest in writing is sacrosanct ... a copy of such written grounds was to be furnished to the arrestee as a matter of course, without exception". (para 33)

### Ashish Tyagi v. DGGI - Allahabad High Court - 29 May 2026
- Citation / case no.: Habeas Corpus Writ Petition No. 509 of 2026; 2026:AHC:123306-DB. Siddharth and Vinai Kumar Dwivedi, JJ.
- Holding: Arrest and detention held illegal and release ordered. The arrest memo did not state specific grounds or the place of arrest (D.K. Basu), grounds were not shown to be annexed, and the written grounds did not bear a CBIC-DIN as required by Instruction 02/2022-23 (as amended 11.03.2025) (paras 6-9). The quotation previously given here ("Grounds of arrest not bearing a CBIC-DIN cannot satisfy the mandate of law") is not in the judgment text.
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: Same bench decided *Sushant Goel v. DGGI* (13 May 2026) to the same effect.
- Use in orders: Generate a DIN for the grounds of arrest. State the place and time of arrest in the memo.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – Ashish Tyagi v. DGGI, Allahabad HC, 29 May 2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/84138962/); [TaxGuru – arrest illegal: missing grounds, CBIC DIN](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-arrest-illegal-due-missing-grounds-cbic-din-arrest-memo-allahabad-hc.html)

### A-III. Anticipatory bail and bail

### State of Gujarat v. Choodamani Parmeshwaran Iyer - Supreme Court - 17 Jul 2023
- Citation / case no.: Criminal Appeals arising out of SLP (Crl.) Nos. 4212-4213 of 2019 (from Gujarat HC order of 24.12.2018 in SCRA Nos. 11010 and 11076 of 2018). Reportable order. J.B. Pardiwala and Prashant Kumar Mishra, JJ (author: P.K. Mishra J).
- Holding: A person summoned (under s.70 CGST and s.145 Central Excise as applied to service tax) cannot seek anticipatory bail under s.438 CrPC, because no FIR is registered before s.69(1) is invoked. Pre-arrest protection lies only under Article 226, used sparingly. The HC order directing adjudication in 8 weeks was set aside; the Court agreed with *P.V. Ramana Reddy* (paras 16-20).
- Binding: Supreme Court.
- Later history:
  - Followed in *Bharat Bhushan v. DGGI* (SC, 5 Jul 2024) and *Vemula Yougander* (Telangana HC, 14 Nov 2023).
  - Qualified by *Radhika Agarwal* (2025), which allows anticipatory bail without an FIR where arrest is apprehended, and by *Sunil Biyani* (2026), which makes communication of the s.69 order the trigger.
  - The current reconciliation: no s.438 relief at the bare summons stage; available once a s.69 order exists.
- Use in orders and counter-affidavits: Cite at the summons stage to oppose s.438 petitions.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – State of Gujarat v. Choodamani Parmeshwaran Iyer, 17 Jul 2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/155401808/); [Verdictum – person summoned cannot invoke s.438](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/supreme-court/person-summoned-under-section-69-cgst-act-cannot-invoke-section-438-crpc-1486436); [SC order PDF 17-Jul-2023](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2019/13019/13019_2019_4_30_45289_Order_17-Jul-2023.pdf); [MetaLegal](https://www.metalegal.in/post/supreme-court-affirms-anticipatory-bail-not-maintainable-for-summons-under-s-69-of-the-cgst)

### A-V. Payments during search ("voluntary" DRC-03)

### Bhumi Associate v. Union of India - Gujarat High Court - 16 Feb 2021 (interim order)
- Citation / case no.: R/Special Civil Application No. 3196 of 2021 with SCA Nos. 2426, 2515 and 2618 of 2021; 2021 (2) TMI 925 (Guj); 2021 (46) GSTL 36. J.B. Pardiwala and Ilesh J. Vora, JJ. ### Union of India v. Mohit Minerals Pvt. Ltd. - Supreme Court - 19 May 2022
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal No. 1390 of 2022 and connected appeals. D.Y. Chandrachud (author), Surya Kant and Vikram Nath, JJ. Neutral or SCC citation not confirmed.
- Holding:
  1. GST Council recommendations under Art. 279A are not binding on Parliament or State legislatures (Art. 246A gives them simultaneous power); they have persuasive value. But the Government, when making rules under the CGST and IGST Acts, is bound by the Council's recommendations (para 148(i)(c)).

  3. Affirmed the Gujarat HC (2020).
- Binding: Supreme Court.
- Later history: The ocean-freight reverse charge entries were omitted from 1 Oct 2023 on Council recommendation (per general knowledge — not confirmed in this search).
- Use in orders:
  - Do not demand IGST on ocean freight from CIF importers.
  - When a taxpayer argues that a Council recommendation overrides the statute or rules, note that the binding law is the Act, Rules and notifications. The recommendation is persuasive only.

### C.C., C.E. & S.T., Bangalore (Adjudication) v. Northern Operating Systems Pvt. Ltd. - Supreme Court - 19 May 2022
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal Nos. 2289-2293 of 2021; [2022] 18 S.C.R. 901; 2022 INSC 598 (per LawSathi, not checked). Three judges: U.U. Lalit, S. Ravindra Bhat (author) and P.S. Narasimha, JJ.
- Holding (service tax): The overseas group company seconding employees to an Indian entity (the Indian entity reimbursing salaries at cost) was a taxable supply of manpower. The Indian recipient was liable under reverse charge. The CESTAT's "employer-employee" view was reversed. The extended limitation period was not upheld (no wilful suppression); the appeals were partly allowed and demands confined to the normal period (paras 62-68).

- Later history:

  - Karnataka HC (*Huawei*) took a contrasting fact-specific view where employees were integrated into the Indian entity.
- Use in orders: Examine the secondment agreement (control, who bears the cost, the employment contract). Do not issue s.74 notices on secondment without evidence of suppression.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – CCE&ST Bangalore v. ### Union of India v. Future Gaming Solutions Pvt. Ltd. - Supreme Court - 11 Feb 2025
- Citation / case no.: 2025 INSC 181; 2025 LiveLaw (SC) 189; Civil Appeal Nos. 4289–4290 of 2013 and connected appeals. B.V. Nagarathna (author) and N. Kotiswar Singh, JJ.
- Holding:
  - Lottery distributors are not liable to service tax under the Finance Act, 1994.
  - Lottery is "betting and gambling" (Entry 62, List II), which only the States may tax.

  - Sikkim HC affirmed.
- Binding: Supreme Court (service tax period).
- Later history: For GST, lottery is taxable as an actionable claim under Schedule III (exclusion carved out). This ruling does not decide GST. Read with *Gameskraft* (2026).
- Use in orders: Not authority against GST on lottery. Distinguish it as a pre-GST case on legislative competence.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – UoI v. Future Gaming Solutions, 11 Feb 2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/117744026/); [LiveLaw – lottery distributor not liable to service tax](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/lottery-distributor-not-liable-to-pay-service-tax-supreme-court-rejects-unions-appeals-283545); [Verdictum – 2025 INSC 181](https://www.verdictum.in/app-lite/court-updates/supreme-court/union-of-india-v-future-gaming-solutions-pvt-ltd-2025-insc-181-lottery-service-tax-1567895); [SC judgment PDF 11-Feb-2025](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2013/4727/4727_2013_7_1501_59216_Judgement_11-Feb-2025.pdf)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date 11 Feb 2025 and appeal numbers confirmed; reasoning point corrected to principal-to-principal.
- Key words: "There being no agency and no service rendered by the respondents-assessees herein as an agent to the Government of Sikkim, service tax is not leviable". (para 18.7)

### Directorate General of GST Intelligence v. Gameskraft Technologies Pvt. Ltd. - Supreme Court - 27 May 2026
- Citation / case no.: 2026 INSC 595; Civil Appeal Nos. 8241–8244 of 2026 arising out of SLP(C) Nos. 19366–19369 of 2023 (with C.A. 8240/2026 and Crl. A. 2933/2026, and a connected judgment on the writ petitions). J.B. Pardiwala and R. Mahadevan, JJ.
- Holding:
  - Online money-gaming platforms are suppliers of actionable claims in betting and gambling, not mere intermediaries.
  - Skill versus chance is irrelevant where money is staked on an uncertain outcome.
  - The whole amount staked is consideration; no deduction for prize pools or winnings. The 2023 amendments (Entry 6 of Schedule III, Rules 31B and 31C) are clarificatory and retrospective, so pending SCNs are to be valued under Rule 31B (online gaming) or 31C (casinos) (para 84(ix)-(xi), 86). The rate in the SCNs (18% to 24.01.2018, 28% from 25.01.2018) was the Revenue's case; the judgment's operative part does not itself fix a rate.
  - The Karnataka HC judgment of 11.05.2023 was set aside and the SCNs of 23.09.2022 restored. Taxpayers may raise factual and legal defences before the adjudicating authorities. Replies are due within 8 weeks of receiving the judgment, and orders within 12 weeks after that (para 86.1-86.2).
  - A related decision upheld State power to prohibit online money gaming.
- Binding: Supreme Court.
- Later history:
  - Rate became 40% from 22 Sep 2025.
  - Online money gaming was prohibited under the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 (sources differ on whether the 1 May 2026 date relates to the Act or the 2026 Rules).
  - Whether any portion (e.g. limitation, pre-2023 valuation under Rule 31A) remains open is not clear from summaries.
- Use in orders: For pending gaming SCNs, adjudicate on merits, valuing under Rule 31B/31C as the SC directed, within the SC's time limits. Still deal with each defence (limitation, quantification) on its own terms, since the SC left them open.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – DGGI v. Gameskraft, 27 May 2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/69261133/); [Bar & Bench – SC upholds 28% GST](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/supreme-court-upholds-28-gst-levy-on-online-gaming-bets-says-skill-chance-irrelevant-if-money-staked); [Verdictum – 2026 INSC 595](https://www.verdictum.in/supreme-court/directorate-general-of-goods-and-services-tax-intelligence-hqs-v-gameskraft-technologies-private-limited-2026-insc-595-28-gst-1615025); [SC Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/supreme-court-observer-law-reports-scolr/directorate-general-of-goods-and-services-tax-intelligence-hqs-vs-gameskraft-technologies-private-limited-goods-and-service-tax-on-online-gaming/)

### Torrent Power Ltd. v. Union of India - Gujarat High Court (DB) - 14 Aug 2026
- Citation / case no.: R/SCA No. 12175 of 2024 and 12 connected SCAs; 2026:GUJHC:51510-DB (neutral citation not seen in the Indian Kanoon text). A.S. Supehia and Vaibhavi D. Nanavati, JJ.
- Holding:

- Binding: Persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: Appeal not found (decision is two months old).
- Use in orders: For Telangana demands, avoid taxing guarantees for periods before 26 Oct 2023 on the deemed 1%. Compute on the outstanding exposure.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – Torrent Power v. ### State of West Bengal v. Calcutta Club Ltd. - Supreme Court (3 judges) - 3 Oct 2019

### Commissioner of Central Excise, Bolpur v. Ratan Melting & Wire Industries - Supreme Court (Constitution Bench) - 14 Oct 2008
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal No. 4022 of 1999 and connected; (2008) 13 SCC 1 (as cited by the SC in *Samtex Fashions*, 2026). Five judges: K.G. Balakrishnan CJI, Arijit Pasayat (author), Harjit Singh Bedi, P. Sathasivam and J.M. Panchal, JJ.
- Holding:

  - A circular contrary to a judgment of the SC or an HC cannot be enforced.
  - The Revenue is not barred from appealing merely because of its own circular.
- Binding: Supreme Court.
- Later history:

  - Applied in *Samtex Fashions v. CIT* (SC, 18 Sep 2026, non-reportable).
- Use in orders: Follow CBIC circulars (they bind you), except where a Telangana HC or SC ruling is contrary; then the ruling prevails. Do not tell taxpayers a circular binds the court.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon – CCE Bolpur v. - Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date, case number and bench added; summary confirmed.
- Key words: "So far as the clarifications/circulars issued by the Central Government and of the State Government are concerned they represent merely their understanding of the statutory provisions. They are not binding upon the court." (para 6)

### Union of India v. Kamlakshi Finance Corporation Ltd. - Supreme Court - 24 Sep 1991

### Advance rulings: status (statute and High Court treatment)
- **Section 103 CGST:** an advance ruling binds only the applicant and its concerned or jurisdictional officer, and only while the law, facts and circumstances are unchanged. Section 103(1A) (National Appellate AAR) was inserted by Finance (No. 2) Act 2019 but reported as not yet effective; check.
- **High Court rulings (court names not identified in snippets):**
  - (i) The BEML/BMRCL matter: an advance ruling is in personam, so a third party (BMRCL) is not an "aggrieved person" and has no locus. Writ dismissed. Likely Karnataka HC (unconfirmed).
  - (ii) An SCN relying on an advance ruling obtained by an unrelated taxpayer was set aside, because advance rulings are person-specific.
- Commentary treats AAR rulings as persuasive only for others.
- **Use in orders:** Do not base a demand on another taxpayer's advance ruling. Reason independently, and cite it only as persuasive if at all.
- Sources: [GSTGyaan – s.103](https://www.gstgyaan.com/section-103-of-the-cgst-act-applicability-of-advance-ruling); [Jurishour – third parties and advance rulings](https://www.jurishour.in/gst/third-parties-gst-advance-rulings-financial-impact/); [A2Z Taxcorp – AAR of another GSTIN not binding](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/aar-ruling-of-another-distinct-gstin-holder-even-under-same-brand-cant-be-applied-bindingly-to-a-different-gstin-holder-with-an-independent-business/)
