# GST Case Law - Telangana High Court (with persuasive Andhra Pradesh HC rulings)

**Scope.** GST judgments of the Telangana High Court (including the pre-2019 common High Court at Hyderabad) and a few Andhra Pradesh High Court rulings that a Telangana officer may meet as persuasive authority. Telangana HC rulings bind CGST and State Tax officers in Sangareddy. Coverage: arrest, e-way bill detention, the s.168A limitation notifications (13/2022, 9/2023, 56/2023), s.73 time limits and composite notices, service through the portal ("Additional Notices" tab), unsigned notices and orders, DRC-01 summaries, provisional attachment, cancellation of registration, ITC (s.16(4), s.16(5), s.16(2)(c)), interest under s.50, refunds, TRAN-1, appeal limitation and pre-deposit, and stay of recovery while GSTAT is not functioning.

**Date compiled:** 13 October 2026.

> **Compiled from web-search summaries; on 14 Oct 2026, 28 of 34 entries were checked against the judgment text (marked "Primary"). Entries still marked "Secondary" must be verified before citing in an order.**
> The web-search budget for this session ran out before every issue could be covered (refund interest, more e-way bill cases, AP HC portal-service rulings and works-contract rate litigation were not searched). See "Gaps and doubts".

## Quick answers

**Arrest (s.69/s.132).** Telangana HC in *P.V. Ramana Reddy* (2019, SLP dismissed) upheld arrest before assessment in fake-ITC cases and held "reasons to believe" need only be recorded on file, not in the arrest authorisation. A 2023 Telangana ruling (*Vemula Yougander*, following the SC in *Choodamani Parmeshwaran Iyer*) holds anticipatory bail under s.438 CrPC is not available at the summons stage. **Takeaway:** record reasons to believe in the file before authorising arrest; keep the record ready for the Court. Check the Supreme Court's later ruling on arrest safeguards (see Gaps) before relying on Ramana Reddy alone.

**E-way bill detention.** *Satyam Shivam Papers* (2021; SC dismissed the department's SLP in 2022 and enhanced costs): expiry of an e-way bill caused by a genuine reason is not evidence of intent to evade. **Takeaway:** a MOV-09 order must record why the officer infers evasion; a mere technical lapse with a plausible explanation will not support s.129 tax and penalty, and costs can be awarded personally.

**Limitation-extension notifications 9/2023 and 56/2023.** Telangana HC in *Brunda Infra* (02.01.2025) neither upheld nor struck them down: it treated the challenge as academic in view of the SC's Covid exclusion (15.03.2020-28.02.2022), while holding that later ratification cannot substitute a Council recommendation (relevant to 56/2023). The point is pending before the SC (SLP(C) 4240/2025). **Takeaway:** for FY 2019-20 orders relying on 56/2023, expect challenge; record the SC pendency and avoid conceding invalidity. For SCNs, compute the s.73(2)/(10) dates carefully: Telangana and AP HCs have quashed late notices.

**s.73(2) time limits and composite notices.** Telangana HC quashed a late s.73 SCN (*Balaji Filling Station*); AP HC holds the 3-month s.73(2) gap mandatory, even 2 days' delay fatal. AP HC (from 17.09.2025) quashes multi-year composite orders, but a Karnataka HC Division Bench (23.04.2026) holds composite SCNs permissible; Telangana HC has stayed a composite ss.74/74A/130 SCN (*RR Metal Industries*). **Takeaway:** issue SCNs at least 3 months before the s.73(10) deadline; issue separate SCNs and orders per financial year.

**Notices only on the portal ("Additional Notices" tab).** Telangana HC has not quashed orders outright on this ground in the cases found; it relegates taxpayers to appeal with a delay-condonation application, which the appellate authority decides on its own assessment (*Bollant Industries*, *Sri Sai Ram Traders*). **Takeaway:** also serve by email/RPAD under s.169 and record service; an appellate authority should not dismiss such appeals on delay without a hearing (s.107(8) - the State conceded this in one 2026 case).

**Unsigned SCNs / orders / DRC-07.** **Update 14 Oct 2026: the Full Bench in *Gudla Vanaja* (30.09.2026, file 03) overruled Bigleap. Electronically authenticated SCNs and orders are valid without a visible signature, but a notice or order without a DIN/RFN is invalid. Takeaway now: always quote the DIN/RFN and issue from the officer's own login; digital signature remains good practice.** Earlier position: *Bigleap Technologies* (Division Bench, 28.02.2025) holds unsigned SCNs and orders cannot stand (with liberty to re-issue, limitation no bar); *Water Tech Engineers* (22.04.2025) follows; *Lakshmi Enterprises* (2026) is unverified. In *SBC Chem* (2026) the petitioner itself chose the appeal route, so the point was not decided. **Takeaway:** digitally sign every SCN, order and DRC-07/DRC-01 summary and ensure the signed PDF (not just the summary) is uploaded.

**DRC-01 summary vs SCN.** No Telangana ruling found on summary-instead-of-SCN; Gauhati HC (*Construction Catalysers*) quashes such orders. Telangana HC will not entertain writs against an SCN itself (*S. Shivraj Reddy Constructions*). **Takeaway:** always issue a full reasoned SCN with the DRC-01 summary.

**Provisional attachment (s.83).** Telangana HC consistently holds attachment lapses after one year (*Greenfinch*, *KMC Constructions*, *Aarush Enterprises*). **Takeaway:** diarise the one-year date; a fresh attachment needs fresh material and a fresh order.

**Cancellation of registration.** Orders on a ground not in the SCN, or without hearing, are quashed (*JK Technology*, *Sheeshmahal*); a notice is upheld where documents made the allegation clear and the taxpayer replied (*Trillion Lead Factory*). **Takeaway:** state the specific s.29(2) clause and facts in REG-17, attach documents, give a hearing, and give reasons for any retrospective date.

**ITC.** s.16(4) time-bar was upheld (AP HC, *Thirumalakonda Plywoods*), but s.16(5) (Finance (No.2) Act 2024, retrospective) now protects FY 2017-18 to 2020-21 ITC claimed in returns filed by 30.11.2021 (AP HC *H.R. Constructions*, 2026). s.16(2)(c) challenge pending in Telangana HC. **Takeaway:** drop s.16(4) demands covered by s.16(5); in supplier-default cases, record why the recipient's claim fails, and note the pending Telangana batch.

**Interest (s.50).** *Megha Engineering* (2019) held interest is on gross liability, but s.50 was later amended (proviso, made retrospective from 01.07.2017 - verify) to charge interest on the cash portion for timely-filed returns. **Takeaway:** do not cite Megha Engineering for gross-liability interest.

**Refunds.** Telangana HC remands refund rejections for hearing (*Aurobindo Pharma*, 2026) and allowed IDS refunds for concessional-rate supplies (*Micro Systems*, 2022); some 2026 benches relegate to appeal. **Takeaway:** issue RFD-08 with specific grounds and give a hearing before RFD-06; apply the SC Covid exclusion to s.54 limitation (AP HC view).

**TRAN-1.** *Standard Chartered Bank* (2024): transitional credit allowed despite filing through another State's branch portal due to glitch; SLP reportedly dismissed. **Takeaway:** do not reject TRAN-1 claims solely on portal/technical grounds.

**Appeals, pre-deposit and GSTAT.** Telangana HC routinely gives taxpayers 2 weeks to 45 days to file s.107 appeals with condonation and pre-deposit, rather than deciding merits. In Apr 2026 it directed compliance with Circular 224/18/2024 (20% + undertaking within 15 days) for stay of recovery. **Takeaway:** once 20% is paid and the undertaking filed, stop recovery of the balance; record the s.112(8)/(9) position before any recovery.

## 1. Arrest and summons

### P.V. Ramana Reddy v. Union of India - Telangana HC (Division Bench: V. Ramasubramanian and P. Keshava Rao JJ) - 18.04.2019
- Citation / case no.: W.P. Nos. 4764, 4769, 4892, 5074, 5130, 5329, 6952 and 7583 of 2019; SC: SLP (Crl.) No. 4430 of 2019; reported as 2019 (26) G.S.T.L. J175 (SC) for the SC order.
- Holding: Writs under Art. 226 are maintainable against threatened s.69 arrest, but protection was refused and the writs dismissed; arrest can precede assessment/adjudication; "reasons to believe" must be on file but need not be stated in the arrest authorisation.
- Key words: "If reasons to believe are recorded in the files, we do not think it is necessary to record those reasons in the authorization for arrest under Section 69(1) of the CGST Act." (para 46)
- Binding: BINDING IN TELANGANA.
- Later history: SC dismissed the SLP on 27.05.2019 (per secondary sources; SC order not found on Indian Kanoon). Karnataka and Bombay HCs took a different line on anticipatory bail. The SC's 2025 judgment on GST/customs arrests (Radhika Agarwal v. Union of India) lays down safeguards - check it alongside this case.
- Use in orders: Support for arrest authorisation where reasons to believe are recorded on file.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/87700723/); [Casemine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/in/telangana-high-court-upholds-arrest-under-cgst-act,-limiting-use-of-article-226-writ-petitions/view); [LiveLaw - SC upholds Telangana HC](https://livelaw.in/top-stories/gst-evaders-can-be-arrested-145309); [taxo.online SLP 4430/2019](https://taxo.online/judgment/p-v-ramana-reddy-vs-union-of-india-2019-26-g-s-t-l-j175-supreme-court/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; bench and full W.P. numbers added; holding refined (writ maintainable but protection refused). SLP dismissal still secondary.

### Vemula Yougander v. Union of India - Telangana HC (K. Surender J) - 14.11.2023
- Citation / case no.: Criminal Petition No. 10430 of 2023 (s.438 CrPC; DGGI Hyderabad summons). Related: W.P. No. 34106 of 2023 decided 19.12.2023; SC: SLP (Crl.) No. 1154 of 2024 dismissed 29.01.2024.
- Holding: Following the SC in State of Gujarat v. Choodamani Parmeshwaran Iyer (2023 SCC OnLine SC 1043), a person summoned under the CGST Act cannot seek s.438 CrPC anticipatory bail because no FIR precedes a s.69(1) arrest; petition disposed of with liberty to invoke writ jurisdiction.
- Key words: "The Honourable Supreme Court held that at the stage of summons the person summoned cannot invoke Section 438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure." (para 7)
- Binding: BINDING IN TELANGANA (the binding rule is really the SC's in Choodamani Parmeshwaran Iyer - cite that).
- Later history: The petitioner's follow-up writ (W.P. 34106/2023, decided 19.12.2023) went to the SC; SLP (Crl.) 1154/2024 dismissed in limine on 29.01.2024 (Sanjiv Khanna and Dipankar Datta JJ).
- Use in orders: Oppose s.438 applications filed at summons stage; cite Choodamani Parmeshwaran Iyer (SC) as the main authority.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - HC order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/26153819/); [Indian Kanoon - SC order 29.01.2024](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/199058343/); [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1086968)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; CORRECTED: date was "2026" -> 14.11.2023, single judge, case no. added, SC basis and SLP history added.

## 2. E-way bill detention (s.129)

### Satyam Shivam Papers Pvt Ltd v. Asst. Commissioner (ST) - Telangana HC (Division Bench: M.S. Ramachandra Rao and T. Vinod Kumar JJ) - 02.06.2021
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 9688 of 2020; SC: SLP(C) No. 21132 of 2021, order 12.01.2022.
- Holding: E-way bill lapsed because an anti-CAA agitation blocked traffic and the next two days were a weekend; no presumption of intent to evade arises merely from expiry; the officer ignored the MOV-07 reply. MOV-09 levy of Rs 69,000 set aside, refund with 6% interest, Rs 10,000 costs on the officer.
- Key words: "On account of non-extension of the validity of the e-way bill by petitioner or the auto trolley driver, no presumption can be drawn that there was an intention to evade tax." (para 42)
- Binding: BINDING IN TELANGANA; SC order also binding.
- Later history: SC (Dinesh Maheshwari and Hrishikesh Roy JJ) dismissed the department's SLP on 12.01.2022, found no question of law, and imposed further costs of Rs 59,000 on the petitioners (total costs Rs 69,000).
- Use in orders: A s.129 order must find intent/evasion on facts, not rest only on expiry of validity; deal with the MOV-07 explanation.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - HC judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/13104689/); [Indian Kanoon - SC order 12.01.2022](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/17589683/); [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/gst-sc-imposes-rs69000-penalty-for-harassing-assessee-for-delay-in-goods-transportation-due-to-road-blockade/66238.html); [Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/hc-correctly-set-aside-tax-demand-penalty-for-expiry-of-e-way-bill-as-goods-not-delivered-in-time-due-to-genuine-reason-sc)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; bench added; costs resolved (HC Rs 10,000; SC added Rs 59,000); reason for blockade corrected (agitation, not a "political rally").

## 3. Limitation: s.168A notifications and s.73 time limits

### Brunda Infra Pvt Ltd v. Additional Commissioner of Central Tax (and batch) - Telangana HC (Division Bench: Sujoy Paul and Dr G. Radha Rani JJ) - 02.01.2025
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 1154 of 2024 and batch (lead petitioner M/s Brunda Infra Pvt Ltd; Indian Kanoon lists the common judgment under several batch titles, e.g. Sri Venkateswara Films); 2025 (1) TMI 299 (per one source).
- Holding: (i) s.168A notifications need a prior GST Council recommendation; "may" does not make it directory, and later ratification cannot substitute the recommendation (said in the context of 56/2023, which rested on a Law Committee decision) (paras 94-96, 102). (ii) But the SC's suo motu order excluding 15.03.2020-28.02.2022 applies to GST limitation, so the challenge to 13/2022, 9/2023 and 56/2023 became academic and was not decided (paras 116, 118). Writs disposed of; liberty to appeal within 45 days, appeal to be decided on merits, not limitation.
- Key words: "we are unable to follow the line of argument of learned Senior Standing Counsel for CBIC and learned Additional Solicitor General that 'ratification' can be a substitute of 'recommendation'." (para 96)
- Binding: BINDING IN TELANGANA. Note: it did NOT uphold 56/2023 and did NOT hold that a Council recommendation is unnecessary (that was the Revenue's submission at para 98, misreported by some summaries). The operative ratio is the Covid-exclusion route.
- Later history: Under challenge in SC, SLP(C) No. 4240/2025 (HCC-SEW-MEIL-AAG JV) - see next entry. Other HCs split: Allahabad (Graziano Transmissioni) and Patna (Barhonia Engicon) cited in this judgment; Gauhati quashed 56/2023; Madras reportedly held 56/2023 ultra vires.
- Use in orders: For FY 2018-19/2019-20 adjudications, rely primarily on the SC Covid exclusion (15.03.2020-28.02.2022) to show the order is in time; do not rely on 56/2023 alone, since this bench doubted it. Keep a record of the actual dates of SCN and order.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - common judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/90854768/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelawbiz.com/tax-cases/telangana-hc-upholds-2023-notification-extending-lmitation-issuing-gst-notices-after-covid-19-pandemic-ceased-recommendation-of-gst-council-cant-be-substituted-by-subsequent-ratification-280404); [Jurishour](https://jurishour.in/gst/gst-council-recommendation-not-a-precondition-for-issuing-s-168a-notification-telangana-high-court); [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/telangana-hc-upholds-validity-gst-notification-no-09-2023-56-2023.html); [A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/hc-upholds-gst-notifications-extending-notice-deadlines/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; CORRECTED: date Jan 2025 -> 02.01.2025, case no. W.P. 1154/2024 added; holding corrected - notifications were neither upheld nor struck down; Court held ratification cannot replace recommendation but decided the batch on the SC Covid exclusion; Jurishour's "recommendation not a precondition" was the Revenue's argument, not the holding.

### HCC-SEW-MEIL-AAG JV v. Assistant Commissioner of State Tax - Supreme Court (J.B. Pardiwala and R. Mahadevan JJ) - notice issued 21.02.2025 (pending)

- Later history: Listed again (e.g. 21.03.2025, 09.04.2025, 23.04.2025 - adjourned to 14.05.2025, with connected SLPs). No final SC judgment found on Indian Kanoon as of 14 Oct 2026.

### Balaji Filling Station v. State Tax Officer, Bodhan - Telangana HC (Division Bench: Aparesh Kumar Singh CJ and G.M. Mohiuddin J) - 14.08.2026
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 25574 of 2026.
- Holding: On the State's concession on instructions, an SCN dated 28.12.2023 for FY 2017-18 (order passed two days later, 30.12.2023) was contrary to s.73(2) TGST Act; SCN and order set aside. A concession-based order: no reasoning on how the 3-month gap is computed.
- Key words: "the impugned order dated 30.12.2023 arising out of the show cause notice dated 28.12.2023 ... cannot be sustained in the eye of law being in teeth of the specific mandate of timeline to be adhered under Section 73(2) of the Act." (para 4)
- Binding: BINDING IN TELANGANA (but weak precedent - rests on the State's concession).
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: SCN must issue at least 3 months before the s.73(10) deadline; late SCNs will be quashed (the State will not defend them).

## 10. Interest under s.50

### Megha Engineering & Infrastructures Ltd v. Commissioner of Central Tax - Telangana HC (Division Bench: V. Ramasubramanian and P. Keshava Rao JJ) - 18.04.2019
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 44517 of 2018; AIR 2019 Telangana 23; 2019 (4) TMI 1319.
- Holding: Delay in GSTR-3B (Oct 2017-May 2018); tax is "paid" only when the return is filed and the ledger debited, so interest under s.50(1) was payable on the whole liability including the portion discharged by ITC; the GST Council's 31st-meeting in-principle approval to charge interest on net cash liability could not be used to read s.50 while the Act was unamended. Writ dismissed.
- Key words: "the claim made by the respondents for interest on the ITC portion of the tax cannot be found fault with. Hence, the Writ Petition is dismissed." (para 45)
- Binding: Was binding when decided.
- Later history: A review petition was reportedly admitted with interim stay (one source; not found on Indian Kanoon). s.50(1) was later amended by a proviso charging interest only on the cash portion for timely-filed returns; that it was made retrospective from 01.07.2017 is from general knowledge and was not confirmed in this check - verify against the statute notes.
- Use in orders: Do not rely on this case for gross-liability interest.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/159736439/); [IRIS GST](https://irisgst.com/?p=17754); [VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/blog/pay-interest-on-gross-tax-liability-for-late-gst-returns); [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/confusion-interest-delayed-gst-payment.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; CORRECTED: exact date 18.04.2019, bench, W.P. 44517/2018 and AIR citation added; it was a Telangana HC bench (not the "common HC"); summary confirmed.

## 12. TRAN-1 / transitional credit

### Standard Chartered Bank v. Principal Commissioner of Central Tax - Telangana HC (Division Bench: Sujoy Paul and Namavarapu Rajeshwar Rao JJ) - 11.07.2024
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 648 of 2024; SC: SLP (C) Diary No. 2203 of 2025, dismissed 28.02.2025.
- Holding: TRAN-1 could not be filed on the Maharashtra portal due to a glitch (not disputed by Revenue); it was filed through the Telangana branch registration and the credit transferred the same day, which the last proviso to s.140(8) permits for a centralised registrant; the department must keep its portal functional and cannot take advantage of its own wrong. SCN dated 03.09.2021 and OIO dated 31.10.2023 set aside.
- Key words: "it was the duty of the Department to keep their portal functional. If the portal was not functional or having technical glitch ... the petitioner cannot be saddled with demand, interest and penalty." (para 27)
- Binding: BINDING IN TELANGANA.
- Later history: SC (J.B. Pardiwala and R. Mahadevan JJ) condoned delay and dismissed the Revenue's SLP on 28.02.2025 ("we see no reason to interfere").
- Use in orders: Do not deny transitional credit on purely technical/portal grounds where the credit is genuine and s.140(8) permits the transfer.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/184007786/); [Indian Kanoon - SC order 28.02.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/114408747/); [AMLegals](https://amlegals.com/standard-chartered-bank/); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/?p=494538)
