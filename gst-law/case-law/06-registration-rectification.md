# 06 - Registration (cancellation, revocation, suspension), rectification under s.161, and vague show cause notices

**Scope.** Case law on (i) cancellation of GST registration under s.29 and rule 22 (SCN in REG-17, order in REG-19, hearing, reasons, retrospective effect), (ii) revocation under s.30 / rule 23 and court-ordered restoration after the time limit, (iii) suspension under rule 21A, (iv) cancellation for non-existence at the premises, (v) cancellation on the taxpayer's own request, liabilities that survive cancellation (s.29(3)), fresh registration after cancellation and refusal of registration (REG-05), (vi) rectification of returns (s.37(3)/s.39(9), Circulars 183/2022 and 193/2023) and rectification of orders under s.161, and (vii) vague show cause notices as an issue on their own. Written for a CGST Assistant Commissioner, Sangareddy Division, Telangana. The effect of a supplier's retrospective cancellation on the recipient's ITC is covered in **02-input-tax-credit.md (Issue 8)** and is only cross-referred here. Telangana HC registration cases already in **05-telangana-high-court.md (section 8)** are summarised briefly, not repeated in full.

**Date compiled:** 14 Oct 2026.

## Quick answers

**1. What the cancellation SCN (REG-17) must contain.**
Courts quash SCNs that only repeat the words of s.29(2) ("fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression", "contravention of the Act") without facts, periods or documents. Leading cases: SC *Brindavan Beverages* (2007, SCN is the foundation; vague SCN = no real opportunity); Gujarat HC *Aggarwal Dyeing* (24.02.2022); Delhi HC 2023-24 line; Madras *PAV Warehouse* (2024); Telangana *A1 Adil Traders* (2025; reasons must be in the SCN itself, not in an annexed internal letter) and *Rayees Metals*. Telangana *Trillion Lead Factory* (2026; SLP dismissed 27.02.2026) is the counterweight: no relief where the documents attached made the charge clear and the taxpayer replied in detail.
*Takeaway:* in REG-17 state the clause of s.29(2), the facts, the tax periods (for non-filing: which returns, which months), the inspection date and findings, and attach the relied-upon documents. Give a date and time for the personal hearing. Missing facts cannot be supplied later in the REG-19 order (*A1 Adil Traders*).

**2. Order on a ground not in the SCN; non-speaking REG-19; no hearing.**
An order that cancels on a different clause than the one proposed (Telangana *JK Technology*: SCN under s.29(2)(e), order under s.29(2)(c)) or that gives no reasons, or that is passed without hearing (Telangana *Sheeshmahal*: remand for non-application of mind; Madras *Suresh Trading*), is set aside. Gauhati HC (2026) requires reasons in REG-19 itself (single paywalled report; order not identified).
*Takeaway:* REG-19 must deal with the reply, record findings on the proposed ground only, and give reasons. If a new ground surfaces, issue a fresh or supplementary REG-17.

**3. Retrospective cancellation.**
Delhi HC (consistent 2023-2025 line: *Riddhi Siddhi Enterprises* W.P.(C) 8061/2024, *Aditya Polymers* W.P.(C) 14493/2022, *Aryan Timber* in file 02; *Subhana Fashion* is not a retrospectivity ruling - see Issue B): the power to cancel from a back date must rest on objective criteria and stated reasons, must have been proposed in the SCN, and cannot be used mechanically (e.g. back to 01.07.2017 for six months' non-filing). Remedies vary: *Riddhi Siddhi* quashed the order with liberty to proceed afresh; *Aditya Polymers* moved the date to the SCN date by consent of both sides; other Delhi orders use the SCN date, the suspension date or the date of the taxpayer's application.
*Contentions across courts:* Delhi HC is the main source; Gujarat and Telangana (*JK Technology*: "give reasons for retrospective date") are consistent. No contrary HC view found. No SC ruling found. No CBIC circular found that defends mechanical back-dating.
*Takeaway:* propose the retrospective date expressly in REG-17 with the reason (e.g. non-existence found on inspection dated X; bogus invoices from date Y) and justify that date in REG-19, noting that recipients' ITC will be affected.

**4. Revocation after the time limit; restoration on payment of dues.**
Statute: application within 90 days of service of the cancellation order, extendable by up to 180 days by the Commissioner/authorised officer (rule 23 as amended by Notf. 38/2023-CT; s.30 proviso omitted by Finance Act 2023 w.e.f. 01.10.2023). One-time amnesty: Notf. 3/2023-CT (cancellations up to 31.12.2022; apply by 30.06.2023, later 31.08.2023 per Notf. 23/2023 - see Gaps). Courts nevertheless restore registrations cancelled for non-filing once the taxpayer files all returns and pays tax, interest and late fee: Madras *Suguna Cutpiece Centre* (31.01.2022) and its many followers; Gauhati *Motaleb Bhuyan* (2025) and 2025-26 followers (restoration plus re-start of s.73(10) limitation); Telangana 2026 orders (*Adarsha Transport*, *KBG Associates*, *Check Security Force*) allowing a physical revocation application beyond the portal's 270-day lock and directing a decision within about 3 weeks.
*Contentions across courts:* Madras, Gauhati, Telangana, Uttarakhand, Kerala and Rajasthan HCs grant restoration or a fresh revocation window; Gujarat (*Radhe Packaging*) pointed the taxpayer to the amnesty notification. No SC ruling found on Suguna (no SLP found). Department's position: the rule 23 limits are statutory; the portal blocks applications after 270 days.
*Takeaway:* where the only ground was non-filing and the taxpayer has filed all returns and paid all dues, consider the revocation application on merits (drop proceedings under the rule 22(4) proviso / pass REG-22) rather than refusing only on time, especially once a Telangana HC order directs it. Record that restoration does not wipe out liabilities (s.29(3)).

**5. Suspension under rule 21A.**
Rule 21A (as amended Dec 2020) allows suspension with the SCN without a prior hearing. Courts still quash suspensions imposed automatically or without any factual basis: Madras *El Tech Power System* (10.03.2026, suspension in REG-17 "unwarranted"); Delhi *Shakti Shiva Magnets* (2022); Telangana *A1 Adil Traders* and *Rayees Metals* (suspension fell with a vague SCN). A Bombay HC challenge to the 2020 amendment (*SAT Industries*, 29.08.2022) is pending; the Court granted an ad-interim stay of that suspension; final outcome not found.
*Takeaway:* suspend only where the SCN itself shows a reason to stop business pending decision, and decide the SCN quickly (suspension is meant to last only till the decision).

**6. Non-existence at premises.**
Cancellation based on an inspection report is set aside where the report is vague (no date, hearsay from unnamed neighbours) or was not shared: Delhi *Umesh* (W.P.(C) 831/2024, 11.09.2024); Kerala *F R Trade Links* (05.08.2021; officer must hold his own rule 25 enquiry, not act only on an intelligence report). Where the procedure was followed and books not produced, Patna HC (W.P. 6794/2025) declined writ relief.
*Takeaway:* record the date, time, officer, persons met, photographs and GPS of the verification, give the report with the SCN, and consider the taxpayer's documents (rent deed, electricity bill, photographs) in REG-19.

**7. Cancellation on request; liabilities after cancellation; fresh registration; REG-05.**
Cancellation does not wipe out past liabilities (s.29(3)): Telangana DB *Manikanta Electronics Services Center* (W.P. 1362/2026) - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**. A rejected cancellation request or a refusal of registration needs reasons and the documents relied on (Bombay *Om Impex*, Oct 2024). Earlier cancellation is no automatic bar to fresh registration; the officer must record findings under s.29(2)(b)/(c) per Circular 95/14/2019 (Patna *Maharaj Ji Enterprises*, July 2025). Several HCs dismissing writs left the taxpayer free to apply afresh (Delhi *Kirpal Singh*, Jan 2025).
*Takeaway:* when cancelling on request, cancel from the date sought (or record why another date); keep demand and recovery proceedings alive under s.29(3). When refusing new registration, give a reasoned REG-05 after a REG-03 query.

**8. Rectification of returns (GSTR-1/GSTR-3B) after the s.37(3)/s.39(9) deadline.**
SC *Bharti Airtel* (28.10.2021): GSTR-3B can be corrected only through s.39(9) / rule 61 in the period the error is noticed; no unilateral reopening. But for bona fide, revenue-neutral clerical errors (wrong GSTIN, wrong head), Bombay HC *Star Engineers* (14.12.2023) and *Aberdare Technologies* (2024) allowed GSTR-1 correction after the deadline, and the SC dismissed CBIC's SLP in *Aberdare* (21.03.2025) with observations. That order is not a reasoned judgment under Art. 141; it prima facie disapproved Telangana HC's *Yokohama India*; and CBIC's review petition was still pending (tagged on 11.03.2026). Karnataka *Wipro* (2023) and followers; Madras, Patna, Tripura HCs follow. Circular 183/15/2022 (FY 2017-18, 2018-19) and Circular 193/05/2023 (01.04.2019 to 31.12.2021) give a verification route for GSTR-2A/3B mismatches caused by supplier GSTR-1 errors.
*Contentions across courts:* *Bharti Airtel* (substantive re-working of 3B to claim refund) versus *Aberdare* line (clerical error, no revenue loss). The two are reconciled on facts; The SC order in *Aberdare* does not mention *Bharti Airtel*, and as an SLP dismissal it cannot overrule it. Department's position: the statutory timelines apply; Circulars 183/193 are the administrative relief.
*Takeaway:* in ITC-mismatch adjudication, apply Circular 183/193 where the period fits and the supplier certificate / CA certificate is produced; do not reject a request for a clerical correction only because the portal window closed - record whether there is revenue loss.

**9. Rectification of orders under s.161.**
Scope is limited to errors apparent on the face of the record. Substantive re-writing by way of "rectification" is invalid (Calcutta *Sajal Kumar Das*, 2024); a rectification that adversely affects the taxpayer needs a hearing (Delhi *Future Consumer*, 10.10.2025, including rejection of the taxpayer's own application; Calcutta *JCI Chemicals*, 24.11.2025, rejection with civil consequences); s.161 cannot be used to raise a fresh demand (Madras, Dec 2025 - single report, order not identified). Telangana HC (W.P. 20731/2025 batch, 15.10.2025) treats duplicate SCNs/orders for the same period as rectifiable under s.161, with the six-month bar not applying (second proviso) - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**.
*Takeaway:* taxpayer's application within 3 months of the order; officer's own rectification within 6 months, except clerical/arithmetical errors. Pass a speaking rectification order, hear the taxpayer if the result is adverse, and do not use s.161 to re-decide merits (that is the appeal route).

## Statutory framework (for reference; not cases)

- **s.29(1)/(2), rule 22:** REG-17 SCN; reply in REG-18 within 7 working days; REG-19 order within 30 days of reply; REG-20 to drop. Proviso to rule 22(4): where cancellation was proposed for non-filing and the person files all returns and pays dues, the officer shall drop proceedings (REG-20). (Text not re-verified by search; Gauhati HC orders summarised below rely on it.)
- **s.29(3):** cancellation does not affect liability for any period before cancellation, whether determined before or after.
- **s.30 / rule 23:** revocation within 90 days of service of cancellation order; extendable by up to 180 days by the Commissioner or an officer authorised by him (not below Joint/Additional Commissioner) for recorded reasons (Notf. 38/2023-CT, 04.08.2023, w.e.f. 01.10.2023). Rule 23 is subject to rule 10B (Aadhaar authentication). Notf. 3/2023-CT (31.03.2023): amnesty for cancellations up to 31.12.2022 for non-filing.
- **rule 21A:** suspension pending cancellation; the December 2020 amendment removed "after affording a reasonable opportunity of being heard".
- **Rule 10A (bank account details) and rule 10B (Aadhaar authentication):** no High Court ruling on either was found in this round (see Gaps).
- **s.161:** rectification on own motion or when brought to notice by an officer or the affected person within 3 months of the order; no rectification after 6 months from the order, except clerical/arithmetical errors; adverse rectification requires natural justice.
- **s.37(3) / s.39(9):** correction of GSTR-1 / GSTR-3B in a later period, up to 30 November following the end of the FY (or the annual return, if earlier) - the deadline at issue in *Star Engineers* and *Aberdare*.

## Issue A - Vague show cause notices and the content of REG-17

### Commissioner of Central Excise, Bangalore v. Brindavan Beverages (P) Ltd - Supreme Court (Pasayat and Kapadia JJ.) - 15 Jun 2007
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal Nos. 3417-3425 of 2002; (2007) 5 SCC 388; AIR 2007 SC (Supp) 1212.
- Holding: The SCN is the foundation of the department's case. Allegations that are not specific, vague, lacking details or unintelligible are enough to hold that the noticee had no proper opportunity to meet them. On facts the SCN did not allege the respondents were party to the arrangement and no material was produced; Revenue's appeals dismissed.
- Key words: "The show cause notice is the foundation on which the department has to build up its case. If the allegations in the show cause notice are not specific and are on the contrary vague, lack details and/or unintelligible that is sufficient to hold that the noticee was not given proper opportunity" (para 10)
- Binding: **SC - binding on all.** (Central Excise case; applied by GST courts to SCNs generally.)
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: cite to explain why the REG-17 / DRC-01 must spell out facts, periods and evidence; the order cannot go beyond the SCN.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/363245/); [LawSathi - judgment page](https://lawsathi.in/judgements/sc/2007/commissioner-central-excise-bangalore-brinda-van-beverages-ltd/); [Taxonation - vague elements in GST SCNs](https://taxonation.com/index.php/show-detail-article/196416/navigating-the-effects-of-vague-elements-in-gst-show-cause-notices)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; citation (2007) 5 SCC 388 confirmed; summary confirmed.

### Aggarwal Dyeing and Printing Works v. State of Gujarat - Gujarat HC (J.B. Pardiwala and Nisha M. Thakore JJ.) - 24 Feb 2022
- Citation / case no.: Special Civil Application No. 18860 of 2021 and batch; reported [2022] 137 taxmann.com (Guj) (page number not confirmed).
- Holding: Vague cancellation SCNs and orders without particulars (explained by the State as portal "technical glitches") set aside on natural-justice grounds, with liberty to issue fresh notices with reasons and details. Directions: until the portal allows full details, issue the SCN and final order in physical form with all particulars by RPAD; the order must not travel beyond the SCN, and any inspection report or document relied on must first be put to the dealer.
- Key words: "If the authority wants to rely upon any particular piece of evidence then it owes a duty to first bring it to the notice of the dealer so that if the dealer has anything to say in that regard, he may do so." (para 18.2)
- Binding: Gujarat; persuasive elsewhere.
- Later history: Followed by Gujarat DB in a June 2023 case where the SCN was "very vague and cryptic" (party name not confirmed); LiveLaw reports a later DB criticising continued cryptic SCNs "despite 2022 directions" (date not confirmed). No appeal found.
- Use in orders: REG-17 should state facts, not just the statutory clause; share inspection reports before relying on them in REG-19.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/10577071/); [SCC Online blog - Gujarat HC quashes cancellation for cryptic SCN](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2023/06/27/gujarat-high-court-quashes-order-cancelling-firms-gst-registration-legal-news/); [LiveLawBiz - Gujarat HC pulls up GST authority over cryptic SCN](https://www.livelawbiz.com/tax-cases/gujarat-high-court-ruling-gst-authorities-reasoned-show-cause-notices-gst-registration-cancellations-270569)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; bench added; summary confirmed with the operative directions (physical SCN/order by RPAD; prior disclosure of evidence).

### Cuthbert Oceans LLP v. Superintendent of CGST Range 109 - SCN alleging fraud without particulars - Delhi HC (Vibhu Bakhru and Amit Mahajan JJ.) - 08 Aug 2023
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) 10421/2023.
- Holding: An SCN that baldly says registration was obtained by fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression, without alluding to any such misstatement or fact, cannot elicit a meaningful response and was set aside; the REG-19 (cancelling retrospectively from 07.02.2020 with no reasons) was "equally cryptic" and also set aside. Writ entertained despite the appeal remedy because of a clear breach of natural justice.
- Key words: "Merely making the bald statement that the registration was obtained by fraud, willful misstatement or suppression of facts without alluding to any such misstatement or the allegedly suppressed facts, provides no clue to the noticee as to the allegation against him." (para 7)
- Binding: Delhi; persuasive.
- Later history: none found. Part of a long Delhi line (2023-2025) where SCN and order "bereft of any details" were set aside (e.g. W.P.(C) 4383/2024).
- Use in orders: an allegation of fraud in REG-17 must name the transactions, suppliers or facts.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/161718316/); [Delhi HC judgment PDF W.P.(C) 10421/2023](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/VIB08082023CW104212023_160640.pdf); [LiveLaw - GST registration can't be cancelled on unspecific SCN](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/delhi-high-court/gst-registration-cancelled-unspecific-show-cause-notice-delhi-high-court-266232)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; party name (Cuthbert Oceans LLP), date 08.08.2023 and bench confirmed; summary confirmed.

### Shakti Shiva Magnets Pvt Ltd v. Assistant Commissioner - Delhi HC (Manmohan and Navin Chawla JJ.) - 31 Jan 2022
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) 1559/2022 (earlier order 27.01.2022).
- Holding: The Delhi GST Commissioner admitted the SCN of 11.11.2021 (proposing cancellation and suspending registration) contained no fact or reason and no supporting document. As the registration had been suspended for over two months on such an SCN (rules 21A/22 contemplate about 30 days), the SCN was quashed and registration restored forthwith; liberty to issue a fresh SCN with all facts and reasons within a week; Commissioner directed to issue a practice direction.
- Key words: "Since in the present case, petitioner's registration has been lying suspended for more than two months on the basis of a show cause notice which is bereft of any reason or fact, this Court quashes the impugned show cause notice" (order of 31.01.2022)
- Binding: Delhi; persuasive.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: a reasonless SCN also brings down the suspension made with it; do not leave a registration suspended for long.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order 31.01.2022](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/140285831/); [Busy caselaw - Shakti Shiva Magnets](https://busy.in/caselaw/shakti-shiva-magnets-private-limited-vs-assistant-commissioner-ors-delhi-high-court); [taxo.online 21.02.2022](https://taxo.online/latest-news/21-02-2022-show-cause-notice-quashed-being-bereft-of-any-reason-or-fact-issued-for-suspension-of-gst-registration-of-the-assessee/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date 31.01.2022, bench and W.P.(C) 1559/2022 added; summary confirmed (department conceded the defect).

### PAV Warehouse v. Assistant Commissioner (ST), Poonamallee - Madras HC (Senthilkumar Ramamoorthy J.) - 29 Feb 2024
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 2393 of 2024.
- Holding: SCN of 29.12.2022 alleged only "Non compliance of any specified provisions in the GST Act or the Rules", no document enclosed (State confirmed); the REG-19 of 17.02.2023 both referred to the reply and said no reply was filed. Cancellation quashed; registration to be restored forthwith; department free to start fresh proceedings in accordance with law.
- Key words: "The show cause notice does not indicate as to which provisions of the applicable GST statutes were not complied with by the petitioner." (para 6)
- Binding: Tamil Nadu; persuasive.
- Later history: A later order between the same parties (20.04.2026) is listed on Indian Kanoon; not checked.
- Use in orders: enclose documents, cite the specific provision, and make the REG-19 consistent with the record.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/110584096/); [Taxmann - SCN and order lacked clarity](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/scn-and-order-for-cancellation-of-gst-registration-lacked-clarity-on-the-nature-of-non-compliance-hc-quashed-cancellation-order/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date 29.02.2024 and W.P. 2393/2024 added; summary confirmed.

### Suresh Trading Corporation v. Assistant Commissioner (Circle) of SGST - Madras HC (M. Sundar J.) - 01 Oct 2021
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 21109 of 2021.
- Holding: Cancellation order of 30.10.2019 set aside solely because the preceding SCN (11.12.2018) was not in the REG-17 template under rule 22(1) and did not mention the date and time of personal hearing (Revenue conceded its records did not show a REG-17); fresh SCN in the prescribed format allowed; no view on merits.
- Key words: "set aside solely on the ground that SCN which preceded the same has not been issued in the prescribed template i.e., REG-17 under Rule 22(1) of TN-GST Rules, as it does not mention the date and time of personal hearing" (para 13(a))
- Binding: Tamil Nadu; persuasive.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: use the REG-17 form and fill in the hearing date, time and venue.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/60738274/); [TaxTMI blog feed](https://www.taxtmi.com/rss_feed_blog?id=730587); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/?p=136968)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date 01.10.2021, judge and W.P. 21109/2021 added; summary confirmed.

### A1 Adil Traders v. Deputy State Tax Officer - Telangana HC (Sujoy Paul ACJ and Renuka Yara J.) - 04 Mar 2025 (SCN 01.11.2024, cancellation 16.01.2025) - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 4539 of 2025; 2025 (3) TMI 369 (as cited in Trillion Lead Factory).
- Holding: REG-17 that suspended registration listed only rule 21(e), 21(b), 21(a) and s.29(2)(e) without any factual details; the department recorded "conclusions", not "reasons". Reasons must be in the SCN itself - the assessee is not expected to dig them out of an annexed internal departmental letter. A cryptic SCN cannot gain life from reasons given in the final order. SCN (with suspension) and cancellation order set aside; liberty to proceed afresh. Follows W.P. 20080/2024 and W.P. 17400/2024 (SCN of 29.02.2024 that only reproduced s.29(2)(e)).
- Key words: "if the show cause notice itself is cryptic, it cannot gain life if certain reasons are assigned in the final order." (para 11)
- Binding: **BINDING IN TELANGANA.**
- Later history: Distinguished in *Trillion Lead Factory* (02.02.2026), where the petitioner had replied in detail to the annexed DGGI material (no prejudice). The two rulings pull in different directions on annexed documents: safest course is to write the facts into the REG-17 itself.
- Use in orders: all facts must be in REG-17 (not only in an annexure); reasons added only in REG-19 will not save the order.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/112338058/); [TaxGuru - Telangana HC sets aside cryptic suspension SCN](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/telangana-hc-sets-gst-registration-suspension-scn-cryptic.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; CORRECTED: name is "A1" (not "AI") Adil Traders; date 04.03.2025, bench and W.P. 4539/2025 added; added the holding that facts in an annexed internal letter are not enough.

### Rayees Metals v. Deputy State Tax Officer - Telangana HC (Sujoy Paul and Namavarapu Rajeshwar Rao JJ.) - 08 Jul 2024 (SCN 29.02.2024) - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 17400 of 2024.
- Holding: REG-17 with suspension whose only "reason" was the text of s.29(2)(e) is cryptic and shows non-application of mind (the State conceded it lacked details); following *T S R Exports v. Superintendent* ([2024] 162 taxmann.com 415 (Telangana)) and SC *Canara Bank v. Debasis Das*, SCN and suspension set aside; the Court deprecated mechanical suspensions ("LIFE" and "FILE" contain the same letters), no costs on the State's assurance.
- Key words: "Apart from this bald statement, there exists nothing in the show cause notice which can throw light as to what is the nature of 'fraud' or 'willful misrepresentation' or 'suppression of fact' by the petitioner. Thus, show cause notice is cryptic" (para 6)
- Binding: **BINDING IN TELANGANA.**
- Later history: Followed in W.P. 20080/2024 and in *A1 Adil Traders* (04.03.2025).
- Use in orders: do not issue a REG-17 that only copies the words of s.29(2)(e).
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/11024953/); [TaxGuru - cancellation set aside, SCN lacked factual reasons](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-registration-cancellation-set-show-notice-lacked-factual-reasons.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date 08.07.2024, bench and W.P. 17400/2024 added; summary confirmed (State conceded).

### JK Technology v. Deputy State Tax Officer, Charminar - Telangana HC (Aparesh Kumar Singh CJ and G.M. Mohiuddin J.) - 09 Jul 2026 (SCN 08.08.2023; order 22.01.2024) - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 21690 of 2026. (Full entry in 05-telangana-high-court.md, section 8.)
- Holding: REG-17 proposed only s.29(2)(e) (fraud/suppression), with no supporting documents; REG-19 cancelled under s.29(2)(c) (non-filing) w.e.f. 01.01.2023. Quashed solely because the ground was never put to the petitioner; SCN also quashed, registration restored, liberty for a fresh SCN with specific grounds and supporting documents. (No separate ruling on the retrospective date.)
- Key words: "quashing the order of cancellation of registration for the sole reason that it is based upon a ground upon which the petitioner was never put to notice." (para 4)
- Binding: **BINDING IN TELANGANA.**
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: the REG-19 ground must match the REG-17 ground.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - order](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/8041411/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/telangana-hc-quashes-gst-registration-cancellation-grounds-scn.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date, bench and W.P. number added; summary confirmed, except that the Court gave no specific ruling on the retrospective date.

### Sheeshmahal Enterprises Pvt Ltd v. Superintendent of Central Tax - Telangana HC (Ujjal Bhuyan CJ and N. Tukaramji J.) - 28 Mar 2023 - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 8344 of 2023 (CGST).
- Holding: Suo motu cancellation (25.03.2021, non-filing) where the petitioner swore it never received the SCN, yet the order recorded that its "reply dated 18.03.2021" had been considered: mechanical, non-application of mind. Cancellation and appellate order (rejected on delay) set aside; remanded for a fresh order after reply and hearing within three months; returns may be filed meanwhile.
- Key words: "This only goes to show the mechanical approach adopted by respondent No.1 and reflects non-application of mind." (para 7)
- Binding: **BINDING IN TELANGANA.**
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: ensure the SCN is actually served and REG-19 reflects the true record; hear before REG-19.

## Issue B - Retrospective cancellation

### Riddhi Siddhi Enterprises v. Commissioner of GST (CGST), South Delhi - Delhi HC (Division Bench: Yashwant Varma and Ravinder Dudeja JJ.) - 25 Sep 2024
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) 8061/2024 (also spelled "Ridhi Sidhi").
- Holding: s.29(2) permits retrospective cancellation, but the mere conferral of power does not justify its use; the order must itself give reasons for the retrospective effect, and the power can be "neither robotic nor routinely applied". Neither SCN nor order gave reasons; the cancellation order (25.07.2023) was quashed, without prejudice to fresh proceedings and to recovery of dues. (It did NOT itself move the date to the SCN date - that remedy appears in other Delhi orders reported by LiveLaw.)
- Key words: "the power to cancel retrospectively can neither be robotic nor routinely applied unless circumstances so warrant." (para 5)
- Binding: Delhi; persuasive.
- Later history: Followed in many Delhi HC orders (e.g. *Mohit Enterprises*, W.P.(C) 1086/2025, 04.03.2025), with *Subhana Fashion* and *Balaji Industries* (W.P.(C) 11913/2024). Appeal not found.
- Use in orders: record in REG-19 why the chosen back date is justified.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/149073062/); [LiveLaw - retrospective cancellation without specific reasons takes effect from SCN date](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/tax-cases/delhi-hc-absence-of-specific-reasons-retrospective-cancellation-of-gst-registration-cancellation-take-effect-from-date-of-issuance-of-scn-delhi-hc-275697); [Delhi HC 2025 order citing Ridhi Sidhi](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/PMS13082025CW34922025_162014.pdf)

## Issue G - Rectification of returns (s.37(3), s.39(9)) and Circulars 183/2022 and 193/2023

### Union of India v. Bharti Airtel Ltd - Supreme Court (A.M. Khanwilkar, Dinesh Maheshwari and C.T. Ravikumar JJ.) - 28 Oct 2021

### Star Engineers (I) Pvt Ltd v. Union of India - Bombay HC (G.S. Kulkarni and Jitendra Jain JJ.) - 14 Dec 2023

### JCI Chemicals India Pvt Ltd v. State of West Bengal - natural justice in s.161 rejections - Calcutta HC (single judge) - 24.11.2025
- Citation / case no.: WPA 21260 of 2025.
- Holding: The CGST Deputy Commissioner had rejected an application to rectify an OIO without reasons or a hearing, although a hearing was requested. The application alleged the DRC-07 and the OIO showed different tax amounts, which affects pre-deposit. Revenue said the third proviso to s.161 applies only where rectification adversely affects someone. The Court held that where a rejection has civil consequences, natural justice is "almost mandatory". It set the rejection aside and remanded the matter for a hearing within three weeks, without going into the merits.
- Key words: "If the action or order would have civil consequence, observance of natural justice would be almost mandatory." (para 9)
- Binding: West Bengal; persuasive.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: a rejection of a s.161 application should be reasoned and preceded by a hearing where it affects the taxpayer, especially when a hearing was requested.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - JCI Chemicals India Pvt Ltd v. State of West Bengal, Calcutta HC, 24.11.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/172824445/); [TaxHeal - HC quashes cryptic rejection of rectification application](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=115598); [taxo.online](https://taxo.online/?p=543128)

### Hi Reach Broadband Pvt Ltd v. Assistant Commissioner (ST) and batch - duplicate SCNs/orders for the same period rectifiable under s.161 - Telangana HC (Aparesh Kumar Singh CJ and G.M. Mohiuddin J.) - common order 15.10.2025 - **BINDING IN TELANGANA**
- Citation / case no.: W.P. Nos. 20731, 24077, 27416, 28893 and 30267 of 2025 (lead: Hi Reach Broadband, W.P. 20731/2025).
- Holding: Multiple SCNs/orders for the same tax period. The Advocate General produced the Commercial Taxes SOP (CCT's Ref. Petitioners agreed; the Court found the stand "in consonance" with s.161 and disposed of the writs for rectification within a reasonable time with intimation to the assessees. - Key words: "in view of the stand of the Department, which appears to be in consonance with the power of rectification conferred upon the proper officer under Section 161 of the TGST Act, we deem it proper to dispose of the writ petitions" (para 9)

**s.161 versus appeal:** s.161 is an alternative remedy, not a bar to appeal. Commentary warns that pursuing rectification does not by itself extend the appeal period; officers can rely on that when a taxpayer later seeks condonation. No HC ruling squarely on this point was found in this round (see file 03 on s.107 limitation).

**Rectification route for s.16(5)/(6) cases:** Circular 237/31/2024-GST (15.10.2024) provides a rectification route for orders under ss.73/74/107/108 that denied ITC now saved by s.16(5)/(6) - see file 02.
