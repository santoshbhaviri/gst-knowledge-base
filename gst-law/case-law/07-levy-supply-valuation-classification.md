# GST case law: levy and scope of supply, valuation, classification, rates, place and time of supply, reverse charge, exemptions

**Scope.** This file covers case law on what is a "supply" under s.7 CGST Act and Schedules I-III: assignment of leasehold rights, transfer of development rights (TDR/JDA), sale of land and developed plots, liquidated damages, notice pay, employee canteen recoveries, director's remuneration and cross-charge between distinct persons. It also covers valuation (reimbursements and pure agent, the one-third land deduction), classification of goods and services by the Supreme Court and High Courts, works-contract rates for government work, change in rate (s.14), place of supply and tax paid under the wrong head, reverse charge on renting residential property, and how exemption notifications are read (Dilip Kumar, hostels, education). Some topics are only cross-referenced because other files cover them: corporate guarantees and rule 28 (Torrent Power), Mohit Minerals and ocean freight, online gaming and actionable claims, and clubs and mutuality (all in file 04); intermediary and s.77 refunds (file 01); GSTAT procedure (file 03). Prepared for the Assistant Commissioner, CGST, Sangareddy Division (Telangana).

**Compiled:** 14 Oct 2026.

> **Compiled from web-search summaries. On 14 Oct 2026, 27 of the 37 entries were checked against the judgment text on Indian Kanoon (marked "Primary"); the rest remain "Secondary". Verify Secondary entries before citing in an order.**

**Binding status key.** Supreme Court = binding on everyone (Art. 141). Telangana HC = **BINDING IN TELANGANA**. Other High Courts = persuasive. AAR/AAAR = binds only the applicant and its jurisdictional officer (s.103). CESTAT (service tax / excise) = persuasive. CBIC circulars bind officers.

## Quick answers

**1. Assignment of leasehold rights (GIDC/MIDC/TSIIC-type industrial plots).** *Gujarat Chamber of Commerce & Industry* (Guj HC, 03.01.2025) held that assigning long leasehold rights in land and building is a transfer of an interest in immovable property, akin to sale of land (Sch. III para 5). It is not a supply of services. *Aerocom Cushions* (Bom HC, 09.01.2026) followed it. **The SC dismissed the Revenue's SLPs**: in *Aerocom* on 22.05.2026 (SC order read; no reasons given) and against the Gujarat judgment on 21.07.2026 (Narasimha and Aradhe JJ). *Takeaway:* don't raise demands on outright assignment of leasehold rights by a lessee to a third party. The **original allotment** or lease by the development corporation (premium or lease rent) is a different transaction and stays a taxable supply, subject to any exemption entry.

**2. Transfer of development rights under a JDA (Telangana).** *Prahitha Constructions* (**Telangana HC, 09.02.2024**, BINDING IN TELANGANA): a JDA does not transfer title, so it is not "sale of land". TDR is a taxable supply, and Notf. 4/2018-CT(Rate) only fixes the time of supply. The SC issued notice in SLP(C) 11079/2024 (13.05.2024) **without a stay**; no final decision was found. *Takeaway:* TDR/JDA demands can continue in Telangana. Record that the matter is pending in the SC and frame the order so it does not depend on the outcome of other issues.

**3. Liquidated damages, notice pay, compensation.** Circular 178/10/2022 (03.08.2022): an amount is taxable only if it is consideration for a separate agreement to tolerate or refrain. Pure compensation for breach is not. *Krazybee* (Kar HC, WP 16471/2024) quashed a DGGI SCN on liquidated damages. *Aavanti Solar* (Kar HC) set aside an order that **did not discuss Circular 178**. For notice pay, *GE T&D India* (Mad HC, service tax) and a line of CESTAT rulings hold it is not consideration. *Takeaway:* an SCN under Sch. II para 5(e) must identify a separate, independent promise to tolerate an act, with evidence, and must deal with Circular 178 expressly. A demand on liquidated damages alone will be quashed.

**4. Employee recoveries and director's remuneration.** Circular 172/04/2022: perquisites under the employment contract, and canteen recoveries where a contractor supplies the food, are not supplies. Gujarat AARs consistently hold that recoveries from **permanent** employees are not taxable but recoveries from **contract** workers are. Circular 140/10/2020 (10.06.2020): director's pay taxed as salary under s.192 IT Act is outside GST (Sch. III para 1). Fees to non-employee directors, or pay under s.194J, attract **RCM** (Notf. 13/2017 entry 6). *Takeaway:* test the TDS section and the accounts entry before raising RCM on directors. (The brief's "Circular 140/2019" is in fact Circular 140/10/2020-GST.)

**5. Cross-charge between distinct persons (HO and branch).** Sch. I para 2 taxes supplies between distinct persons even without consideration. Circular 199/11/2023 says that where the recipient branch is eligible for full ITC, the invoice value (including nil) is deemed open-market value under rule 28. A Delhi HC ruling (June 2025, party name not found) set aside an IGST demand on HO services that were not cross-charged where full ITC was available, and remanded in light of the circular. *Takeaway:* don't raise cross-charge demands against branches that are fully ITC-eligible. Focus on recipients with exempt or blocked turnover.

**6. Valuation: reimbursements and pure agent.** *Intercontinental Consultants* (SC, 07.03.2018, service tax) struck down rule 5(1) of the 2006 Valuation Rules because tax applies only to the value of the service itself. Under GST, s.15(2)(c) expressly includes incidental expenses, so the pure-agent exclusion in **rule 33** is the only route and it is applied strictly (WB AAR, toll charges). The one-third deemed land deduction (Notf. 11/2017 para 2) was **read down** (not struck down) by Gujarat HC in *Munjaal Manishbhai Bhatt* (06.05.2022): it is optional, and available only where the actual land value can't be ascertained. The Union's SLP is pending. *Takeaway:* to deny pure-agent status, record which of the rule 33 conditions fails. Outside Gujarat, accept the actual land value where the sale deed gives it. Persuasive only; no SC ruling was found.

**7. Classification.** The SC dismissed the Revenue's SLPs in *Sri Vijaya Visakha Milk Producers* (flavoured milk is heading 0402, 5%; AP HC 10.12.2024; SC May 2025) and in the *fusible interlining cloth* case (Guj HC; not 5903; SC around May 2025). *Madhan Agro* (SC, 18.12.2024, excise): small packs of pure coconut oil are edible oil under heading 1513 unless Chapter 33 Note 3 is met. *Cadila Health Care* (SC, 05.10.2026, MP VAT): classify by the form of the goods at sale, not end use. *X'SS Beverage* (Gauhati HC, 04.03.2025): carbonated fruit drinks are 12%, not 28%. *Takeaway:* the burden of proving the higher heading is on the Revenue. Apply the GRIs in order and the section/chapter notes, and deal with the HSN Explanatory Notes. Dismissing a contrary HC ruling in a single paragraph will not survive.

**8. Classification disputes and writs in Telangana.** *Sahasra Enterprises* (**Telangana HC**, BINDING IN TELANGANA): rate and classification disputes involving facts go to a s.107 appeal, not Art. 226. *Takeaway:* the order-in-original is likely to be tested in appeal, so make the factual findings complete. Also justify any s.74 invocation separately: a rate dispute alone is not suppression (see *G.R. Infra*, SC, in file 03).

**9. Works contracts for government (12%/18%).** "Government Entity/Authority" was removed from entry 3(vi) of Notf. 11/2017 from 01.01.2022 (Notf. 15/2021), so 18% applies thereafter (Telangana AAR rulings, file 05). *STS-KEC JV* (Mad HC, 28.01.2025): an RVNL railway works contract (railways being in entry 3(v)) stayed at 12% because the **nature of the work**, not the recipient's identity, decides the entry. *Takeaway:* check entry 3(v) (railways, roads, bridges, etc.) before 3(vi). The recipient's status matters only under 3(vi). No Telangana HC merits ruling was found.

**10. Rate change and time of supply (s.14).** When the rate changes, s.14 overrides ss.12/13: for a supply completed before the change, if both invoice and payment fall after the change the new rate applies; if either the invoice or the payment came before the change, the date of that earlier event generally fixes the time of supply and the old rate. (Paraphrase of s.14; read the section.) Madras HC (*SPK and Co*, 10.12.2025) refused writ relief to a contractor who finished work before 18.07.2022 but invoiced afterwards. *Takeaway:* record all three dates (completion, invoice, payment) in the order.

**11. Place of supply and wrong head (IGST vs CGST/SGST).** Karnataka HC (*GR Tech Services*, June 2026) and Madras HC (*SYA Homes*, 07.07.2026) held that IGST wrongly paid on an intra-State supply must be adjusted, or recovery must await the s.77 refund. Tax already paid in full under the wrong head is not "unpaid tax". *Takeaway:* before demanding CGST/SGST on a supply treated as intra-State, give credit or adjustment for IGST paid and don't levy interest or penalty as if nothing was paid. Ocean freight (*Mohit Minerals*) is in file 04; s.77 refunds are in file 01.

**12. RCM on renting residential dwellings.** From 18.07.2022 (Notf. 5/2022), renting a residential dwelling to a registered person attracts RCM. *Seema Gupta* (Del HC, 27.09.2022), relying on the government's affidavit and clarification: renting by a proprietor in a **personal capacity** for their own residence stays exempt (the government's affidavit says the same for partners). *Takeaway:* check who uses the dwelling and for what before demanding RCM.

**13. Exemptions: strict interpretation, then liberal application.** *Dilip Kumar* (SC Constitution Bench, 30.07.2018): an exemption notification is read strictly and any ambiguity goes to the Revenue (once eligibility is established, the notification may be construed liberally: paras 46-47). **Telangana HC applied it** in *Care College of Nursing v. KNRUHS* (17.10.2023, BINDING IN TELANGANA): affiliation and inspection fees paid to a university are not exempt under Notf. 12/2017 entry 66. Madras HC DB (*Bharathidasan University*, 10.02.2026) held the same. *Takeaway:* cite Dilip Kumar for the **eligibility** question and set out exactly which condition of the notification the assessee fails.

**14. Hostels and residential dwelling.** *State of Karnataka v. Taghar Vasudeva Ambrish* (SC, 04.12.2025, 2025 INSC 1380) affirmed Karnataka HC (2022): leasing residential premises to an operator who runs a hostel for students or working professionals is "renting of residential dwelling for use as residence" (entry 13, Notf. 9/2017-IT(Rate)). The lessee need not live there itself. The period at issue was before 18.07.2022. *Takeaway:* drop demands on landlords leasing residential buildings to hostel or PG operators for periods up to 17.07.2022. For later periods, check the amended entry and the RCM entry (Notf. 5/2022) before taxing.

## 1. Assignment of leasehold rights, TDR/JDA, sale of land

**Contentions across courts.** *Leasehold assignment:* Gujarat HC (*Gujarat Chamber*, *Dhiraj Can*) and Bombay HC (*Aerocom Cushions*) hold it is not a supply. The Revenue's SLPs were dismissed in 2026, and no contrary HC was found. CBIC's position before these rulings was that it is taxable (SCNs under Sch. II para 2(b)/5(b)). *TDR/JDA:* Telangana HC (*Prahitha*) holds it taxable, and the SC appeal is pending without a stay. *Developed plots:* MP HC (*Shraddha Tiwari*) and Circular 177/09/2022 (as summarised) treat sale of developed land as sale of land. Gujarat AAR took the opposite view; Kerala AAR (*Dharmic Living*, 2021) held it not a supply.

### Gujarat Chamber of Commerce and Industry v. Union of India - Gujarat HC - 03.01.2025
- Citation / case no.: Special Civil Application No. 11345 of 2023 and connected matters (lead number as cited in *Dhiraj Can*).
- Holding: Assignment by sale of leasehold rights in a GIDC plot (with building) by the lessee to a third party for consideration is a transfer of benefits arising out of immovable property. S.7(1)(a) read with Sch. II para 5(b) and Sch. III para 5 does not apply to it, so no GST is leviable under s.9. SCNs and orders were quashed (paras 83-85, as quoted in *Dhiraj Can* and *Aerocom*). The point that the original GIDC allotment is a taxable but exempt supply comes from summaries only.
- Key words: "assignment/sale/ transfer of benefits arising out of \"immovable property\" by the lessee-assignor in favour of third party-assignee" (para 83, quoted in *Dhiraj Can*, para 5).
- Binding: Persuasive in Telangana. The SC's SLP dismissal leaves it standing; see below.
- Later history: LiveLaw (24.10.2025) reported that the SC (Mithal and Varale JJ) was to examine the issue in SLP(C) Diary No. 33270/2025. Later reports say the SC (P.S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe JJ) **dismissed the Revenue's SLP on 21.07.2026**. Commentators differ on whether a dismissal at the SLP stage settles the law.
- Use in orders: Don't demand tax on assignment of leasehold rights by an existing lessee. Distinguish the original lease or allotment premium.
- Source: [Dhiraj Can (Guj HC) quoting paras 83-85 - Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/118604422/); [TaxGuru - SC dismissed Revenue SLP](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/sc-dismissed-revenue-slp-gst-levy-gidc-leasehold-rights-transfer.html); [taxo.online 22.07.2026](https://taxo.online/latest-news/22-07-2026-no-gst-on-assignment-of-industrial-leasehold-rights-supreme-court-affirms-gujarat-high-courts-landmark-verdict/); [LiveLaw - SC to examine](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-hearing-whether-leasehold-rights-transfer-attract-gst-307737)
- Verified: Secondary - judgment itself not found on Indian Kanoon on 14 Oct 2026 (3 searches); operative paras 83-85 read as quoted verbatim in *Dhiraj Can* (Guj HC) and *Aerocom* (Bom HC) on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026. Holding reworded to the court's words (it does not say "akin to sale of land"). SC dismissal of 21.07.2026 not found on Indian Kanoon on 14 Oct 2026.

### Dhiraj Can Co. Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India - Gujarat HC - 04.07.2025
- Citation / case no.: R/Special Civil Application No. 3596 of 2025 (DB: Bhargav D. Karia, Pranav Trivedi JJ).
- Holding: Following *Gujarat Chamber* (SCA 11345/2023), which the State could not distinguish, assignment of leasehold rights in a GIDC plot is a transfer of benefits arising out of immovable property and is not liable to GST. The s.73 order and DRC-07 of 29.08.2024 and the DRC-01 SCN of 30.05.2024 were quashed.
- Key words: "the same would not be subject to levy of GST as provided under Sec.9 of the Act." (para 6)
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: Shows the Gujarat line extends to s.73 orders already passed.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/118604422/); [taxo.online 11.07.2025](https://taxo.online/latest-news/11-07-2025-transfer-of-leasehold-rights-is-not-a-taxable-supply-under-gst-as-it-is-akin-to-sale-of-land-which-is-excluded-under-schedule-iii-gujarat-high-court/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; case no. and bench added; holding reworded (court did not say "akin to sale of land").

### Aerocom Cushions Pvt. Ltd. v. Assistant Commissioner (Anti-Evasion) - Bombay HC (Nagpur Bench, DB) - 09.01.2026
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 2145 of 2025; 2026:BHC-NAG:348-DB (DB: Anil L. Pansare, Nivedita P. Mehta JJ).
- Holding: Assignment of a 95-year MIDC lease for Rs 1.5 crore, with MIDC's consent, is a transfer of benefits arising from immovable property and not a supply of services. The s.74 SCN dated 20.12.2024 (Rs 27,00,000) was quashed. Classifying the assignment as "other miscellaneous services" (entry 35, Notf. 11/2017) was also held wrong. Once the rights are assigned, the assignor's rights end, so there is no lease or letting under Sch. II. The court followed Gujarat HC as a ruling on a central Act (citing *Godavari Devi Saraf*, Bom HC 1978).
- Key words: "the transaction would not be subject to levy of GST in terms of the GST Act." (para 14)
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: SC (Dipankar Datta, Satish Chandra Sharma JJ) condoned delay and dismissed the Revenue's SLP(C) Diary No. 26041/2026 on 22.05.2026 ("not inclined to interfere"; no reasons given).
- Use in orders: Same as Gujarat Chamber. Note the *Godavari Devi Saraf* reasoning: absent a contrary HC, a HC ruling on a central Act is followed across States.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - HC](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/23506487/); [Indian Kanoon - SC order 22.05.2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/173317666/); [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/bombay-high-court/aerocom-cushions-private-limited-v-assistant-commissioner-2026bhc-nag348-db-assignment-leasehold-rights-not-taxable-gst-1605125); [Mondaq (SAM)](https://www.mondaq.com/no-gst-on-assignment-of-industrial-leasehold-rights-bombay-high-court-grants-relief-to-industrial-lessees-holding-midc-plots/1839628)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; HC judgment and SC order both read; SLP number corrected (was "SLP(C) 18772/2026", order shows Diary No. 26041/2026); bench and WP no. added.

### Prahitha Constructions Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India - Telangana HC - 09.02.2024
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 5493 of 2020 (DB: P. Sam Koshy, Laxmi Narayana Alishetty JJ).
- Holding: A JDA does not transfer title to the developer, so transfer of development rights is not "sale of land" under Sch. III para 5. It is a taxable supply linked to construction service. The challenge to Notf. 4/2018-CT(Rate) (as amended by Notf. 23/2019) failed, because the notification only fixes the time of supply and levies nothing.
- Key words: "the transfer of development rights as it stands is amenable to GST and cannot be brought within the purview of Entry 5 of Schedule-III of the GST Act." (para 39)
- Binding: **BINDING IN TELANGANA.**
- Later history: SLP(C) No. 11079/2024. The SC (Khanna and Datta JJ) issued notice on 13.05.2024. Reports say **no stay** was granted, so tax stays payable. No final decision was found.
- Use in orders: Rely on it for TDR/JDA demands in Telangana. Note the pending SLP and confirm the time of supply under Notf. 4/2018 (as amended) and, from 01.04.2019, the RCM entry for TDR.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/63683670/); [EY alert Feb 2024](https://www.ey.com/en_in/technical/alerts-hub/2024/02/hc-holds-transfer-of-development-rights-by-landowner-to-developer-under-jda-is-exigible-to-gst); [CAclubindia - SC notice](https://www.caclubindia.com/judiciary/sc-issues-notice-against-hc-judgment-imposing-gst-on-transfer-of-development-rights-to-real-estate-developers-by-joint-development-agreement-5678.asp); [taxo.online 14.05.2024](https://taxo.online/latest-news/14-05-2024-supreme-court-seeks-gst-clarity-on-joint-development-agreements-by-real-estate-developers/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; summary confirmed; bench added. SC notice/no-stay in SLP(C) 11079/2024 not found on Indian Kanoon on 14 Oct 2026 (secondary only).

### Munjaal Manishbhai Bhatt v. Union of India - Gujarat HC - 06.05.2022
- Citation / case no.: R/SCA No. 1350 of 2021 with SCA Nos. 6840 of 2021 and 5052 of 2022 (DB: J.B. Pardiwala, Nisha M. Thakore JJ).
- Holding: The mandatory one-third land deduction in para 2 of Notf. 11/2017-CT(Rate) (and the State notification) is ultra vires the GST Acts and violates Art. 14. It was **read down, not struck down**: the one-third is available only at the taxpayer's option where the actual value of land or undivided share is not ascertainable. Refund with 6% interest was ordered to the petitioner-buyer.
- Key words: "read down to the effect that the deeming fiction of 1/3rd will not be mandatory in nature. It will only be available at the option of the taxable person" (para 124)
- Binding: Persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: The Union's SLP(C) No. 21703/2022 (with connected SLPs and W.P.(C) 1382/2023) is pending in the SC without any stay shown in the orders read; on 18.08.2026 it was listed for 07.12.2026.
- Use in orders: Where a builder shows the actual land value (sale deed or guideline value) and asks for it, don't insist on the one-third. Where the notification is applied, record why the actual value can't be ascertained.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - HC](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/48062053/); [Indian Kanoon - SC order 18.08.2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/95800048/); [Taxmann case analysis](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/case-analysis-munjaal-manishbhai-bhatt-v-union-of-india); [AMLegals](https://amlegals.com/mandatory-deduction-of-1-3rd-of-land-value-is-ultra-vires-the-cgst-act/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; "struck down" corrected to "read down" (para 124); bench and case nos. added; pending SLP found (was "no appeal found").

### Shraddha Tiwari and others v. Bhopal Development Authority - Madhya Pradesh HC (DB: Sheel Nagu, Prakash Chandra Gupta JJ) - 27.10.2022
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 257 of 2022 (Jabalpur).
- Holding: Relying on the CBIC circular of 03.08.2022 (para 14) (sale of land after levelling or laying drainage is still sale of land under Sch. III para 5), the court disposed of the writ by directing the BDA to reconsider levying 12% GST on developed plots sold to the petitioners. It did not itself decide the point.
- Key words: "directing the Bhopal Development Authority to reconsider the question of levy of GST on the sale of developed plots ... in the light of aforesaid circular" (para 4)
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: Sale of developed plots alone (no construction) is generally outside GST. Development services supplied to the developer remain taxable.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/184414473/); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/?p=221459); [busy.in](https://busy.in/caselaw/shraddha-tiwari-and-others-vs-bhopal-development-authority-and-others-madhya-pradesh-high-court)

## 2. Liquidated damages, notice pay, compensation (Sch. II para 5(e); Circular 178/10/2022)

**Contentions across courts.** Karnataka HC (*Krazybee*, *Aavanti Solar*) follows Circular 178 in favour of taxpayers. Under service tax, Madras HC (*GE T&D*) and several CESTAT benches held notice pay and damages not taxable. Maharashtra AAR (*MSPGCL*, before the circular) held liquidated damages taxable at 18% as "tolerating" non-performance. Gujarat AAR (*JBM Ecolife*, Nov 2025) follows the circular. No SC ruling. **Department position:** Circular 178/10/2022 says compensation for breach is not consideration unless it pays for a separate promise to tolerate or refrain. Some payments (e.g. late payment charges, cancellation charges where the contract provides for them) are treated as part of the main supply.

### Krazybee Services Pvt. Ltd. v. Additional Director, DGGI - Karnataka HC (S.R. Krishna Kumar J) - 08.12.2025
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 16471 of 2024 (T-RES); NC: 2025:KHC:51726.
- Holding: Liquidated damages an NBFC recovered from a lending service provider for breach or delay under a master service agreement are compensation, not consideration for "tolerating an act" under Sch. II para 5(e). The DGGI SCN dated 25.04.2024 was quashed, relying on paras 7.1 to 7.1.6 of Circular 178/10/2022, and refund of Rs 5 crore paid under protest, with interest, was ordered.
- Key words: "the amount received by the petitioner from its LSPs by way of liquidated damages fall outside the purview of GST in terms of the said Circular" (para 10)
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: A para 5(e) demand needs evidence of a separate agreement to tolerate. A penalty clause in the main contract is not enough.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/125199237/); [LiveLaw Biz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/high-court/karnataka-high-court/no-gst-on-liquidated-damages-for-breach-of-contract-karnataka-high-court-quashes-scn-516620); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/liquidated-damages-gst-misconceptions-notices-legal-position.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date 08.12.2025 confirmed; SCN date and refund direction added.

### Aavanti Solar Energy Pvt. Ltd. v. Joint Commissioner of Central Tax - Karnataka HC (S.R. Krishna Kumar J) - 09.09.2024
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 19477 of 2024 (T-RES); NC: 2024:KHC:36649.
- Holding: The order-in-original dated 28.03.2024 demanding GST on liquidated damages had not considered Circular 178/10/2022 (03.08.2022). That vitiated the order, which was set aside and remitted for fresh decision bearing the circular in mind. No ruling on merits.
- Key words: "had not been adverted/considered in the impugned order, which is vitiated on account of non-consideration of the said circular." (para 5)
- Binding: Persuasive.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: **Always discuss Circular 178 in a damages or compensation case.** Leaving it out is enough to get the order quashed.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/140676627/); [TaxGuru - Karnataka HC remits](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/karnataka-hc-remits-gst-case-liquidated-damages.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date, bench and WP no. added; summary confirmed.

### GE T&D India Ltd. v. Deputy Commissioner of Central Excise - Madras HC (Anita Sumanth J) - 07.11.2019 (service tax)
- Citation / case no.: W.P. Nos. 35728 to 35734 of 2016 (07.11.2019); followed in W.P. No. 26292 of 2018 (13.12.2019). Reporter citations differ: [2020] 119 taxmann.com 55 (Mad.); 2019 (12) TMI 1566; 2020 (1) TMI 1096.
- Holding: An employer who lets an employee leave early on payment of the notice-pay amount is not "tolerating an act" for consideration. It is only facilitating exit on compensation, so notice pay is not taxable. The judgment deals only with notice pay; it notes that breach of a non-compete stipulation could be a different case.
- Key words: "the employer has not 'tolerated' any act of the employee but has permitted a sudden exit upon being compensated by the employee in this regard." (para 11)
- Binding: Persuasive (service tax; s.66E(e) Finance Act 1994 has the same wording as GST Sch. II para 5(e)).
- Later history: Followed by CESTAT in *Rajasthan Rajya Vidyut Prasaran Nigam* (Delhi), *Intas Pharmaceuticals*, *Cosmo First*, *Ami Lifesciences* (Ahmedabad), *Balaji Medical* (Delhi). No reversal found.
- Use in orders: Don't demand GST on notice-pay recoveries without a reasoned distinction. In GST, Sch. III para 1 (employer-employee) also applies.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - 07.11.2019](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/36001213/); [Indian Kanoon - 13.12.2019](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/110741215/); [Taxmann - CESTAT on notice pay](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/no-service-tax-on-notice-pay-recovery-by-employer-from-employee-cestat); [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=10565)

## 3. Employee recoveries, director's remuneration, cross-charge

**Contentions.** *Canteen:* Gujarat AARs (Suzuki Motor Gujarat; Torrent Pharmaceuticals; a Dec 2025 ruling) say recoveries from permanent employees are not taxable but recoveries from contract or deputed workers are. ## 4. Valuation: reimbursements and pure agent

**Contentions.** Under service tax, the SC (*Intercontinental*) excluded reimbursements before 14.05.2015. Under GST, s.15(2)(c) includes incidental expenses, so only rule 33 (pure agent) excludes them. AARs apply rule 33 strictly (WB AAR, toll charges: taxable; Feel Good Hospitality: taxable) but sometimes allow it (WB AAR, electricity recovered at cost from a sub-lessee: excluded). No HC ruling under GST was found.

### Union of India v. Intercontinental Consultants and Technocrats Pvt. Ltd. - Supreme Court (A.K. Sikri, Ashok Bhushan JJ) - 07.03.2018
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal No. 2013 of 2014 with connected appeals and transferred cases (affirms Delhi HC, 2012).
- Holding: Rule 5(1) of the Service Tax (Determination of Value) Rules, 2006 (including reimbursed expenses in value) was ultra vires ss.66/67 Finance Act 1994. Tax is only on the value of the service actually rendered. Reimbursements became includible only after s.67 was amended (14.05.2015), a substantive and not declaratory change. The Revenue's appeals were dismissed.
- Key words: "it is the value of the services which are actually rendered, the value whereof is to be ascertained for the purpose of calculating the service tax payable thereupon." (para 23)
- Binding: SC, binding, but on service tax.
- Later history: Overtaken for GST by s.15(2)(c) CGST Act and rule 33. Still cited for the principle that rules cannot widen the charging section.
- Use in orders: Don't cite it to exclude reimbursements under GST. Test them against rule 33 instead.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/64314027/); [TaxGuru - judgment PDF link](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Union-of-India-ANR.-Vs-Ms-Intercontinental-Consultants-and-Technocrats-Ltd.-Supreme-Court-of-India.pdf); [StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/no-service-tax-on-reimbursements-prior-to-may-14-2015-held-by-sc-22056.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; summary confirmed; bench and case no. added.

### West Bengal AAR on pure agent (toll charges) - 20/WBAAR/2018-19 - 02.11.2018
- Citation / case no.: 20/WBAAR/2018-19.
- Holding: Toll charges recovered from banks by a cash-logistics provider are part of the value because the banks never authorised the applicant as pure agent or accepted the toll as their own liability.
- Binding: AAR, binding only on the applicant.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: When denying pure-agent status, cite the specific rule 33 condition not met (contractual authorisation, separate invoice line, recipient's liability, no title).
- Source: [TaxHeal](https://taxheal.com/pay-gst-on-reimbursement-of-toll-taxes-aar.html); [Taxmann - electricity charges (contrary facts)](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/electricity-charges-reimbursed-by-sub-lessee-not-taxable-as-lessee-acts-as-pure-agent-aar)

**Contentions.** *Flavoured milk:* AP HC (5%, heading 0402) was upheld after the SC dismissed the Revenue's challenge; Karnataka HC followed it in Jan 2026. ## 6. Works contracts and rates; change in rate (s.14)

**Contentions.** Madras HC (*STS-KEC JV*) says the nature of the work (entry 3(v): railways) decides the rate, regardless of who the recipient is. Telangana AARs (KPC Projects, Shree Constructions, Siddhartha Constructions; see file 05) say that after 01.01.2022, works for "Government Entity" under entry 3(vi) attract 18%, and that before then TSIIC warehouses used for business were not covered. ### STS-KEC JV v. State Tax Officer - Madras HC (Mohammed Shaffiq J) - 28.01.2025

### Commissioner of Customs (Import), Mumbai v. Dilip Kumar and Company - Supreme Court (Constitution Bench) - 30.07.2018
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal No. 3327 of 2007; AIR 2018 SC 3606 (Ranjan Gogoi, N.V. Ramana, R. Banumathi, Mohan M. Shantanagoudar, S. Abdul Nazeer JJ).
- Holding: An exemption notification is construed strictly. The burden is on the assessee to show it comes within it, and any ambiguity about eligibility is resolved in favour of the Revenue. *Sun Export* (1997), which had given the benefit of ambiguity to the assessee, was overruled. The Court also concurred (paras 46-47) with the line that once eligibility is strictly established, the notification may be construed liberally.
- Key words: "When there is ambiguity in exemption notification which is subject to strict interpretation, the benefit of such ambiguity cannot be claimed by the subject/assessee and it must be interpreted in favour of the revenue." (para 52)
- Binding: SC, binding.
- Later history: Applied by Telangana HC under GST in *Care College of Nursing* (2023). The "liberal once eligible" point is in paras 46-47 of the judgment itself (approving *Parle Exports*: "Do not extend or widen the ambit at stage of applicability. But once that hurdle is crossed, construe it liberally").
- Use in orders: Cite it when holding that the assessee has not shown it is eligible. Identify the exact condition or wording that is not met.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/29030278/); [supremecourtcases.com](https://www.supremecourtcases.com/commissioner-of-customs-import-mumbai-v-m-s-dilip-kumar-and-company-ors/); [Manupatra (MANU/SC/0789/2018)](https://www.manupatracademy.com/LegalPost/MANU_SC_0789_2018)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; bench and citation added; Sun Export confirmed overruled; liberal-construction corollary confirmed at paras 46-47.

### Care College of Nursing and others v. Kaloji Narayana Rao University of Health Sciences - Telangana HC (DB: P. Sam Koshy, Laxmi Narayana Alishetty JJ) - 17.10.2023
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 34617 of 2022 and batch (34 W.P.s of 2023); [2023] 156 taxmann.com 450 (Telangana). Judgment pronounced 17.10.2023 (Indian Kanoon lists it as "Care Institute of Medical Sciences ... v. KNRUHS").
- Holding: Affiliation and inspection fees that colleges pay to a university are not exempt under entry 66 of Notf. 12/2017-CT(Rate). Entry 66 covers services **by** educational institutions to students, faculty and staff, and services **to** them relating to admission or examinations. Applying *Dilip Kumar*, the exemption can't be stretched. The court disagreed with the Karnataka HC rulings (*Rajiv Gandhi University of Health Sciences*, *Bangalore University*) as based on s.66D of the Finance Act 1994. The 35 writ petitions were dismissed.
- Key words: "inspection and affiliation fees charged by the 1st respondent-University from the educational institutions has not been specifically exempted ... the said benefit cannot be extended to the petitioners." (para 34)
- Binding: **BINDING IN TELANGANA.**
- Later history: Appeal not found. Consistent with Kerala HC DB and Madras HC DB (2026).
- Use in orders: For universities and boards in Telangana, affiliation and inspection fees are taxable. Cite this case together with *Dilip Kumar*.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/181678350/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/telangana-high-court/telangana-high-court-gst-exemption-doesnt-cover-affiliation-fee-and-inspection-fee-paid-by-colleges-to-university-242470); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/inspection-fee-and-affiliation-fee-charged-by-universities-from-colleges-is-not-exempt-under-gst-hc/); [taxo.online 30.10.2023](https://taxo.online/latest-news/30-10-2023-telangana-high-court-cancels-plea-against-gst-on-fees/)

### State of Karnataka v. Taghar Vasudeva Ambrish - Supreme Court (J.B. Pardiwala, K.V. Viswanathan JJ) - 04.12.2025
- Citation / case no.: Civil Appeal No. 7846 of 2023 with C.A. No. 7847 of 2023; 2025 INSC 1380 (judgment by J.B. Pardiwala J).
- Holding: Leasing a residential building to a lessee who runs it as a hostel for students and working professionals is "renting of residential dwelling for use as residence" under entry 13 of Notf. 9/2017-IT(Rate) (12/2017-CT(Rate) equivalent). The exemption does not require the lessee to live there itself. "Residential dwelling" takes its common-parlance meaning, and long-stay hostels differ from hotels and guest houses. Entry 13 is an activity-specific, not person-specific, exemption. The Revenue's appeals were dismissed.
- Key words: "the exemption is extended to cases wherein residential dwelling is rented out and ultimately used as residence, irrespective of the person using it." (para 61)
- Binding: SC, binding.
- Later history: The decision covers the period before the 18.07.2022 amendment. The Court said that from 18.07.2022 no exemption is available where the dwelling is rented to a registered person, and that applying the amendment to earlier periods would be impermissibly retrospective (para 66). It also noted the Explanation added from 01.01.2023 for proprietors renting for own residence (paras 67-68). The judgment says nothing about refunds under s.54; that point is commentary only.
- Use in orders: Drop demands on landlords leasing residential buildings to hostel or PG operators for periods up to 17.07.2022. For later periods, analyse the amended entry 12 and the RCM entry.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/171394234/); [SCObserver](https://www.scobserver.in/supreme-court-observer-law-reports-scolr/gst-treatment-of-residential-buildings-used-as-hostels/); [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/app-lite/court-updates/supreme-court/2025-insc-1380-state-of-karnataka-v-taghar-vasudeva-ambrish-1600097?hasShare=1); [taxo.online 05.12.2025](https://taxo.online/latest-news/05-12-2025-leasing-out-of-residential-premises-to-an-entity-which-is-using-it-as-hostel-for-students-and-working-professionals-is-exempted-from-gst-supreme-court/)
