## Quick answers

**1. Interest on delayed returns: net cash or gross?**
- The s.50(1) proviso was substituted by the Finance Act 2021 (s.112) with retrospective effect from 01.07.2017 and brought into force by Notf. 16/2021-CT (01.06.2021). Interest is charged only on tax paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger.
- The exception: where the return is filed after proceedings under s.73/74 have commenced, interest is on the gross amount.
- *Megha Engineering* (Telangana HC 2019, gross-liability view) is overtaken by this amendment; see file 05.
- *Takeaway:* compute s.50(1) interest on the cash portion only, unless the return was filed after the s.73/74 proceedings began. If so, record the date those proceedings began.

**2. Interest on ITC wrongly availed but not utilised (s.50(3), rule 88B).**
- Calcutta HC (*Utpal Das*, 2024, following *L&T*, 2022) and Orissa HC (*Manoja Kumar Nayak*, Apr 2026) hold that there is no interest unless the ITC is both availed **and** utilised.
- Orissa tests utilisation against the **total** credit-ledger balance, not head-wise.
- Madras HC (June 2026, name not found) gave "wrongful availment and utilisation" a wide reading, extending it to GSTR-3B/2A mismatches. It is not confined to fraud.
- *Takeaway:* before demanding s.50(3) interest, show in the order, with ledger figures, that the balance fell below the disputed ITC and when. Charge interest only from the date of utilisation to the date of reversal (rule 88B(3)).

**3. Can interest be recovered straight through DRC-07 or s.79 without adjudication?**
- **Jharkhand HC** (*Mahadeo Construction*; *R.K. Transport*; *Bluestar Malleable*; *East India Udyog*, 2024): where interest is disputed, it must be adjudicated under s.73/74. A DRC-07 without an SCN was quashed.
- **Gujarat HC** (*Reliance Formulation*, June 2025; *Bombay Art*, Oct 2025): interest on self-assessed tax can be recovered under s.79 only after an intimation in **DRC-01D** under rule 142B (in force from 04.08.2023).
- The older Gujarat view (*Rajkamal Builder*, 2021) treated interest as recoverable under s.79 without an SCN.
- *Takeaway:* for undisputed interest on self-assessed tax, issue DRC-01D first. For disputed interest, issue an SCN under s.73/74. Never go straight to DRC-07 or DRC-13.

**4. Penalty under s.122: mens rea; civil or criminal; independent of s.74?**
- *Patanjali Ayurved* (Allahabad HC, 29.05.2025) held that s.122 penalty is a civil liability adjudicated by the proper officer. Mens rea is essential for prosecution; for a penalty it depends on the statute's scheme and is not needed for breach of a civil obligation.
- Section 122 proceedings are independent of s.73/74 and survive the dropping of the s.74 demand.
- For **s.122(1A)** on persons who are not taxable persons, the High Courts are split:
  - Bombay HC: the person must be a taxable person. The Revenue's SLP was reportedly dismissed with the question of law kept open.
  - Delhi HC (2025) and Gauhati HC (2026): "any person", including directors and partners.
- *Takeaway (Telangana):* no binding Telangana or SC ruling was found on s.122(1A). For acts before 01.01.2021 (when s.122(1A) came in), do not invoke it, because Article 20(1)/retrospectivity challenges are live. Record the specific benefit the person retained and the person's role.

**5. Penalty where tax is paid before or within 30 days of the SCN (s.73(5)/(8)).**
- Under s.73(8), proceedings conclude without penalty if tax and interest are paid within 30 days of the SCN.
- Kerala HC (*Global Plastowares*, 2023): this does not cover tax **collected** from customers but not deposited. Section 73(11) penalty still applies to that.
- *Takeaway:* if the taxpayer pays within 30 days, drop the s.73 penalty unless the s.73(11) facts (tax collected and not paid) are recorded.

**6. General penalty under s.125.**
- Madras HC (*Kandan Hardware Mart*, 2026, followed in *NKR Traders*, 2026) holds that s.125 is available only where no specific penalty applies.
- Section 125 cannot be imposed for late returns when a late fee under s.47 applies.
- *Takeaway:* do not add a s.125 penalty on top of a s.47 late fee or any specific penalty.

**7. s.129 versus s.130, and e-invoices.**
- *Panchhi Traders* (Gujarat HC DB, 11.12.2025) held ss.129 and 130 independent. Section 130 can be invoked directly where facts show a clear intent to evade, ordinarily formed within the rule 138B/138C timelines.
- Earlier *Moon Traders* (Gujarat 2020) took the sequential view, as did the AP HC view reported contra; see file 03 for Satyam Shivam Papers (Telangana).
- *Nancy Trading* (Allahabad HC 2024): no s.129 penalty for a missing e-invoice where the other documents were present and there was no intent to evade.
- *Takeaway:* any s.130 order must contain a reasoned, fact-based finding of intent to evade.

**8. Late-fee waivers.**
- The HP HC (*R.T. Pharma*), Madras HC (*Kandan Hardware Mart*) and Kerala HC (*Anishia Chandrakantha*, 2024) extended the GSTR-9/9C amnesty (Notf. 7/2023 and 25/2023-CT) to returns filed **before** 01.04.2023. No late fee was allowed on GSTR-9C for FY 2017-18 to 2019-20.
- No refund is due of late fee already paid above Rs 10,000 (Kerala).
- *Takeaway:* do not demand late fee above the amnesty cap for those years.

**9. s.128A waiver (file 03 has the Madras and Karnataka cases).**
- Circular 238/32/2024 (15.10.2024) excludes self-assessed tax and interest on delayed returns.
- Madras HC (*Incompressible Fluid Control*, 2026, per secondary reports) held that circulars cannot cut down the statute.
- A Bombay HC writ challenging the circular is pending (notice issued).
- Circular 248/05/2025 says GSTR-3B payment before 01.11.2024 does not bar eligibility.
- Madras HC (*WFB Baird*, 07.07.2026): where an order was wrongly passed under s.74, denying s.128A on that ground is unsustainable. The case was remanded for a fresh s.73 order and a fresh s.128A application.
- No Telangana ruling was found.
- *Takeaway:* decide SPL applications on the s.128A and rule 164 conditions only, with reasons. Expect the Circular 238 exclusions to be challenged.

**10. Anti-profiteering (s.171).**
- *Reckitt Benckiser* (Delhi HC DB, 29.01.2024) upheld s.171 and rules 122-134 (selected). The SC issued notice in Excel Rasayan's SLP in Feb 2024; the outcome was not found.
- The GSTAT Principal Bench has heard these cases since 01.10.2024 (Notf. 18/2024-CT).
- Notf. 19/2024-CT: no new applications are accepted from 01.04.2025. Pending cases continue.
- The first GSTAT order was *DGAP v. Urban Essence* (05.08.2025).
- *Takeaway:* there are no new complaints after 01.04.2025. Officers are unlikely to originate s.171 cases.

**11. Parallel Central/State proceedings (s.6(2)(b)).**
- *Armour Security* (SC, 14.08.2025) held that a **summons, search or seizure is not "initiation of proceedings"**. The bar is triggered by an SCN on the "same subject matter".
- Its guidelines direct that action from audit or scrutiny be taken by the administration to which the taxpayer is assigned.
- The Telangana HC (2025-26) applies "first SCN prevails" and tests whether the issues and periods truly overlap.
- *Takeaway:* before an SCN, check the GST portal and the State or Centre for an earlier SCN on the same issue and period, and record that check in the SCN. Audit and scrutiny of State-assigned taxpayers should not be taken up by Central officers.

**12. DGGI as proper officer to issue SCNs.**
- Most High Courts (Madras, Rajasthan, Delhi 2026) uphold DGGI's pan-India power to issue SCNs (Notf. 14/2017-CT).
- *NCS Pearson* (Karnataka HC, 16.07.2025) quashed a DGGI s.74 SCN where the officer was not authorised.
- Adjudication follows Circulars 31/05/2018, 169/01/2022 and 239/33/2024 (All-India Additional/Joint Commissioners, Notf. 27/2024-CT from 01.12.2024).
- *Takeaway:* adjudicating officers should confirm they are the notified adjudicating authority for a DGGI SCN and say so in the order.

**13. Audit (ss.65/66).**
- *Koenig Solutions* (Delhi HC, 09.07.2026) quashed a special-audit ADT-03 and DRC-01 because the s.73 time limit had expired.
- *Varian Medical* (Delhi HC 2025): an SCN issued before the reply time on a pre-SCN expired violates natural justice.
- *Takeaway:* special audit does not extend the s.73/74/74A limitation. Allow the full reply period on pre-SCN or audit communications.

## 1. Interest under s.50

**Contentions across courts.**
- **Net or gross:** the retrospective proviso settled it (cash portion only) except for returns filed after s.73/74 proceedings commenced.
- **Utilisation:** Calcutta and Orissa require actual utilisation measured on the total ledger balance. Madras (2026) reads s.50(3) broadly to include GSTR-3B/2A mismatches but still ties interest to utilisation via rule 88B(3).
- **Recovery route:** Jharkhand requires adjudication once interest is disputed. Gujarat requires DRC-01D before s.79.
- No SC ruling was found on these questions. The CBIC position (per secondary reports) is that the s.50 proviso is retrospective and interest is on net cash liability.

### Utpal Das v. State of West Bengal - Calcutta HC (Raja Basu Chowdhury J.) - 18.07.2024
- Citation / case no.: WPA 18241 of 2022; 2024 (10) TMI 1016 (as reported).
- Holding: ITC wrongly availed through a clerical error in GSTR-9 and reversed by debiting the credit ledger (DRC-03) after a DRC-01A, but **not utilised** (the credit balance never fell below the Rs 5 lakh involved), attracts no interest under s.50(3), amended retrospectively from 01.07.2017 by the Finance Act 2022. A DRC-01A does not commence s.73 proceedings; they begin only with the SCN. The s.73 order and the appellate order were set aside and the interest and penalty quashed (paras 14-20). It followed *Larsen & Toubro v. State of West Bengal* (Calcutta HC, WPA 2654 of 2020, 13.12.2022), *Ranjan Sarkar*, *Grundfos Pumps* (Madras) and *Deepak Sales* (P&H).
- Key words: "unless the ITC is both availed and utilized, interest cannot be levied on the registered tax payer." (para 17)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: do not demand s.50(3) interest without a finding that the wrong ITC was actually utilised.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Calcutta HC WPA 18241/2022, 18.07.2024](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/51857497/); [TaxTMI blog](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_blog_details?id=775211); [Taxo - 04.11.2024 HC quashes interest order](https://taxo.online/latest-news/04-11-2024-hc-quashes-order-imposing-interest-clarifies-itc-interest-cant-be-levied-unless-both-availed-and-utilized/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026. **Corrected:** date is 18.07.2024 (not Oct 2024); case no. WPA 18241 of 2022 and single-judge bench added; DRC-01A point added.

### Manoja Kumar Nayak v. Commissioner, GST & Central Excise, Rourkela (with Babamani Roadways & Borewells) - Orissa HC (DB: Harish Tandon CJ and Murahari Sri Raman J.) - 08.04.2026
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) Nos. 12682 and 12686 of 2025.
- Holding: Read together, s.50(3), rule 88B and the CBIC circular of 17.07.2023 mean interest arises only where ITC is both wrongly availed and utilised. Utilisation is tested on the **total** ITC balance, not head-wise. The disputed ITC of Rs 4,39,970 was reversed before the SCN and the ledger balance always exceeded it, so there was no interest. Demanding the same amount again without credit for the reversal was double taxation, and the penalty fell too. The s.74 OIO (03.02.2025) was quashed (paras 9.5-12).
- Key words: "since there was availability of surplus/excess balance left in the Electronic Credit Ledger after adjustment of input tax credit so voluntarily reversed, no interest is exigible" (para 11)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: give credit for pre-SCN reversals. Compute interest only for days when the total credit balance fell below the wrongly availed amount.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Orissa HC W.P.(C) 12682/2025, 08.04.2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/65248458/); [Taxo - 13.04.2026 Orissa HC](https://taxo.online/latest-news/13-04-2026-wrong-itc-does-not-attract-interest-unless-it-is-actually-utilised-and-utilisation-must-be-tested-on-total-itc-balance-not-head-wise-orissa-high-court/); [TheTaxCorp](https://thetaxcorp.in/article/orissa-high-court-no-gst-demand-or-interest-after-voluntary-itc-reversal-with)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026. **Corrected:** judgment date 08.04.2026 (13.04.2026 was the report date); case nos. and bench added; summary otherwise confirmed (the total-balance test is taken from the CBIC circular of 17.07.2023, which the court applied).

### Madras HC (party name not found) - Senthilkumar Ramamoorthy J. - reported 26.06.2026
- Citation / case no.: not found.
- Holding: A GSTR-3B vs GSTR-2A mismatch can amount to "wrongful availment and utilisation". Section 50(3) is the special provision for ITC cases and is not limited to fraud or fake invoices. Interest runs from utilisation to reversal (rule 88B(3)). The cash-ledger argument under the s.50(1) proviso was rejected. The writ was dismissed.
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: support for s.50(3) interest on mismatch-based ITC, but still record the utilisation dates.
- Source: [Taxo - 26.06.2026 Madras HC](https://taxo.online/latest-news/26-06-2026-wrongful-availment-and-utilisation-of-itc-includes-gstr-3b-gstr-2a-mismatch-interest-payable-under-section-503-madras-high-court/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1057785)
- Verified: Secondary - search summaries (2-3 sources agree; party name not found) (not found on Indian Kanoon on 14 Oct 2026).

### Mahadeo Construction Co. v. Union of India - Jharkhand HC (DB: H.C. Mishra and Deepak Roshan JJ.) - 21.04.2020
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(T) No. 3517 of 2019; cited in the later Jharkhand HC judgment in *East India Udyog* as 2020 (36) GSTL 343 (Jhar.) (another source gave vol. 33).
- Holding: If the assessee disputes liability to interest under s.50 (its calculation or leviability), the revenue must adjudicate under s.73 or s.74; until then the interest is not an "amount payable" and cannot be recovered under s.79. The Superintendent's order (08.03.2019) and the s.79 garnishee notice to the bank for Rs 19,59,721 were quashed, with liberty to adjudicate (paras 21-24).
- Key words: "without initiation of any adjudication proceedings, no recovery proceeding under Section 79 of the Act can be initiated for recovery of the interest amount." (para 22)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: an SLP was reportedly filed in the SC; the outcome was not found. The case was followed in *R.K. Transport* (16.02.2022, W.P.(T) 1404/2020) and *Bluestar Malleable* (18.08.2022, W.P.(T) 2043 and 2051/2020).
- Use in orders: if the taxpayer disputes interest, issue an SCN rather than a recovery letter.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Jharkhand HC W.P.(T) 3517/2019, 21.04.2020](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/31840117/); [Taxo - Jharkhand HC interest needs adjudication](https://taxo.online/latest-articles/interest-cannot-be-demanded-without-initiating-any-adjudication-proceedings-under-section-73-or-74-jharkhand-high-court/); [Taxo - Bluestar Malleable](https://taxo.online/judgment/bluestar-malleable-pvt-ltd-vs-the-state-of-jharkhand-ors-in-w-p-t-no-2043-of-2020-with-w-p-t-no-2051-of-2020-high-court-jharkhand)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date (21.04.2020) and bench added; summary confirmed. SLP status still secondary.

### M/s East India Udyog Ltd v. State of Jharkhand - Jharkhand HC (DB: Shree Chandrashekhar ACJ and Navneet Kumar J.) - 13.03.2024
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(T) Nos. 2853, 2854 and 2855 of 2023.
- Holding: A demand of interest (Rs 92.96 lakh in the lead case) on delayed GSTR-3B returns, raised through a DRC-07 dated 04.01.2021 "under s.73" without any adjudication, was set aside together with the appellate orders and APL-04s, following *Mahadeo Construction*, *R.K. Transport* and *Narsingh Ispat*. The department was left at liberty to initiate adjudication proceedings in accordance with law (paras 15-17).
- Key words: "there was no adjudication before a demand for the payment of interest was raised because of the delayed filing of the Returns." (para 15)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: a DRC-07 must follow an adjudication order. It is not a substitute for one.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Jharkhand HC W.P.(T) 2853/2023, 13.03.2024](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/115091900/); [Taxo - 13.05.2024 Jharkhand HC](https://taxo.online/latest-news/13-05-2024-interest-cannot-be-demanded-on-delayed-filing-of-returns-without-proper-adjudication-where-liability-has-been-disputed-jharkhand-high-court/); [LiveLaw - Jharkhand HC](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/tax-cases/jharkhand-high-court-justice-aparesh-kumar-singh-justice-deepak-roshan-cgst-act-194809)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; party names, bench and case numbers confirmed; summary confirmed (liberty was to "initiate the adjudication proceeding", not specifically an SCN under s.73(1)).

### Reliance Formulation Pvt Ltd v. Assistant Commissioner of State Tax, Ghatak 21 - Gujarat HC (DB: Bhargav D. Karia and Pranav Trivedi JJ.) - 27.06.2025
- Citation / case no.: R/SCA No. 5453 of 2025 (oral order).
- Holding: The challenge to an advisory (18.12.2024) on outstanding s.50(1) interest was disposed of without interference. The court held that since rule 142B (from 04.08.2023) the department cannot go straight to s.79 recovery: it must first issue an intimation in **Form DRC-01D**, and then give an opportunity to reply and a hearing if requested (s.75(4)). *Mahadeo Construction* was distinguished as rendered before rule 142B and DRC-01D (paras 11-13).
- Key words: "after issuance of the notice in Form GST DRC-01D, as per the provisions of Section 75(4) of the GST Act, the respondent-Authority is bound to provide an opportunity to file reply as well as an opportunity of hearing" (para 12)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: followed in *Bombay Art v. Union of India* (Gujarat HC, 10.10.2025, R/SCA 16855 of 2024; read on Indian Kanoon), which quashed a DRC-13 bank attachment (20.05.2024) for interest and allowed fresh proceedings by DRC-01D with reply and hearing, and in a further Gujarat ruling reported on 06.11.2025 (name not found).
- Use in orders: issue DRC-01D and consider the reply before any recovery of s.50(1) interest.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Gujarat HC SCA 5453/2025, 27.06.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/134461778/); [Indian Kanoon - Bombay Art, 10.10.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/23074237/); [Taxmann - Recovery of GST interest needs DRC-01D](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/hc-recovery-of-gst-interest-needs-drc-01d-notice-hearing/); [A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/issuance-of-notice-in-drc-01d-for-recovery-of-interest-mandatory-prior-to-recovery-under-section-79-of-the-cgst-act/)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026 (with *Bombay Art*); bench added; holding refined: the writ was disposed of without quashing anything, and the court treated DRC-01D plus reply and hearing (not a s.73/74 SCN) as the route, distinguishing *Mahadeo*.

### Rajkamal Builder Infrastructure Pvt Ltd v. Union of India - Gujarat HC (DB: J.B. Pardiwala and Ilesh J. Vora JJ.) - 23.03.2021
- Citation / case no.: R/SCA No. 21534 of 2019.
- Holding: In view of the retrospective substitution of the s.50(1) proviso (then clause 103 of the Finance Bill 2021), interest is on the net (cash) tax liability, not gross. A DRC-01 cannot be issued for interest under s.50 (rule 142(1)(a) does not cover s.50); under s.75(12), unpaid interest on self-assessed tax is recovered under s.79 "notwithstanding" ss.73/74, by notice in DRC-07. The DRC-01 was quashed with liberty to start fresh proceedings (paras 6-19).
- Key words: "the interest under Section 50 of the CGST Act, 2017 can only be levied on the net tax liability and not on the gross tax liability." (para 6)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: on the procedure point, overtaken in practice by rule 142B / DRC-01D (2023) and *Reliance Formulation* (2025).
- Use in orders: do not rely on it for direct recovery. Use the DRC-01D route.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Gujarat HC SCA 21534/2019, 23.03.2021](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/127260360/); [StudyCafe - net liability, Gujarat HC](https://studycafe.in/interest-under-gst-can-only-be-levied-on-the-net-tax-liability-and-not-on-the-gross-tax-liability-gujarat-hc-109350.html); [StudyCafe - DRC-07 instead of DRC-01](https://studycafe.in/interest-to-be-demanded-only-on-net-tax-liability-in-form-drc-07-instead-of-drc-01-97879.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date (23.03.2021), case number and bench added; summary confirmed.

### Retrospective s.50(1) proviso (statutory) and Telangana/Madras/Gujarat follow-ups
- Citation / case no.: s.112, Finance Act 2021; Notf. 16/2021-CT dated 01.06.2021. One source gives a different commencement date; check the Gazette.
- Holding: The proviso was substituted from 01.07.2017. Interest on delayed returns is on the cash-ledger portion only, unless the return is filed after s.73/74 proceedings commence. Other reported rulings:
  - Madras HC (*Srinivasa Stampings*, per one source): the proviso is retrospective.
  - **Telangana HC (*Orange Auto Pvt Ltd v. Superintendent of Central Tax*, W.P. Nos. 18236 of 2019 & batch, DB: Ujjal Bhuyan and P. Madhavi Devi JJ., 24.12.2021 - read on Indian Kanoon):** noting the retrospective substitution of the proviso by the Finance Act 2021, the HC allowed the writs and directed the authorities to issue notice, hear the petitioners and re-quantify interest under the amended s.50 (paras 6-8).
  - Gujarat HC (*Symphony Ltd*, per one source): no interest for the period between cash deposit and filing of GSTR-3B.
- Key words: "respondents shall issue notice and hear the petitioners before quantifying the interest to be paid by the petitioners as per the amended provision of Section 50 of the CGST Act." (Orange Auto, para 8)
- Binding: statute. *Orange Auto* (Telangana HC DB, 24.12.2021) is **BINDING IN TELANGANA** for the direction to re-quantify on the amended basis after a hearing.
- Later history: a later Telangana order in *Megha Engineering* reportedly directed re-quantification under the amended s.50 after a hearing (see file 05).
- Use in orders: state the cash-portion computation and the date of commencement of any s.73/74 proceedings.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Orange Auto, Telangana HC, 24.12.2021](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/183094458/); [LKS - proviso retrospective](https://lakshmisri.com/newsroom/news-briefings/interest-on-delayed-payment-proviso-to-s-50-of-cgst-act-is-applicable-retrospectively); [TaxGuru - Budget 2021 interest on net liability](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/budget-2021-interest-net-tax-liability-u-s-50-cgst-act.html); [busy.in - Orange Auto (Telangana HC)](https://busy.in/caselaw/orange-auto-private-limited-and-others-vs-superintendent-of-central-tax-and-others-telangana-high-court)

## 2. Penalties: ss.73/74, 122, 122(1A), 125

**Contentions across courts.**
- **s.122(1A) on non-taxable persons:** Bombay HC says only taxable persons (Revenue's SLP reportedly dismissed, question kept open). Delhi HC (2025) and Gauhati HC (2026) say "any person" who retains the benefit.
- **Retrospectivity of s.122(1A) for pre-01.01.2021 acts:** one HC barred it under Art. 20(1). Others say the SCN date controls, and a Delhi bench reportedly disagreed with the SCN-date test.
- **Mens rea for s.122:** Allahabad HC says not required for a civil penalty.
- No SC ruling on the merits was found. No CBIC circular on s.122(1A) was found in this search.

### Patanjali Ayurved Ltd v. Union of India - Allahabad HC (DB: Shekhar B. Saraf and Vipin Chandra Dixit JJ.) - 29.05.2025
- Citation / case no.: Writ Tax No. 1603 of 2024; 2025:AHC:92242-DB.
- Holding:
  - A DGGI SCN (19.04.2024) under ss.74 and 122 proposed a penalty (reported as Rs 273.51 crore) under s.122(1)(ii) and (vii). The s.74 tax demand was dropped by the adjudication order of 10.01.2025.
  - The HC held that s.122 is a penalty provision adjudicated by the proper officer (SCN in DRC-01, order in DRC-07), not a prosecution under s.134; prosecution is under ss.132-138. On mens rea, it held that for prosecution it is essential, while for a penalty it depends on the scheme of the statute; a penalty for breach of a civil obligation does not need it (paras 38-39).
  - Being taxed first under s.73/74 is not a precondition for a s.122 penalty, and dropping the s.74 proceedings does not abate s.122 proceedings for distinct contraventions such as fake invoices (paras 41, 51-54). The writ was dismissed and the s.122 proceedings directed to continue (para 55).
- Key words: "abatement of proceedings under Section 74 of the CGST Act does not ipso facto abate the proceedings under Section 122 which are for completely different offences." (para 53)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: appeal or SLP status not found.
- Use in orders: a s.122 penalty for fake-invoice or ITC-pass-through contraventions can be adjudicated even where no tax demand stands. Frame the SCN on the specific clause of s.122(1).
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Allahabad HC Writ Tax 1603/2024, 29.05.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/195105563/); [Allahabad HC judgment PDF](https://www2.allahabadhighcourt.in/files_ilr/english/splitted/4716032024_29-05-2025_english.pdf); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/allahabad-high-court/allahabad-high-order-gst-penalty-proceedings-against-patanjali-294033); [EY alert](https://www.ey.com/en_in/technical/alerts-hub/2025/06/hc-holds-penalty-imposed-under-section-122-is-a-civil-liability)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026. **Corrected:** the mens rea point was overstated: the court did not say a penalty never needs mens rea; it said this depends on the statute's scheme and is not needed for breach of a civil obligation. Neutral citation added. The Rs 273.51 crore figure was not located in the text and remains secondary.

### Amit Manilal Haria v. Joint Commissioner, CGST & Central Excise - Bombay HC (DB: G.S. Kulkarni and Aarti Sathe JJ.) - 25.02.2026
- Citation / case no.: Writ Petition No. 5001 of 2025; 2026:BHC-AS:9556-DB.
- Holding: Penalties of Rs 133.6 crore each under s.122(1A) on three employees of Shemaroo Entertainment (OIO 01.02.2025) were quashed as without jurisdiction. Section 122(1A) reaches only a "taxable person" (s.2(107)) who retains the benefit of a transaction under s.122(1)(i), (ii), (vii) or (ix) and at whose instance it is conducted; the order recorded no finding that the employees retained any benefit. It followed the earlier Bombay HC decision in *Shantanu Sanjay Hundekari v. Union of India*, 2024 (89) GSTL 62 (Bom.), which is the source of the "ill-conceivable" (no vicarious liability under ss.122/137) words. It also held s.122(1A), in force from 01.01.2021, cannot apply to July 2017 to 01.01.2021 (Art. 20(1)) (paras 26-30).
- Key words: "There could not have been any retrospective application of Section 122(1A) of the CGST Act in issuing the impugned show cause notice for the period July 2017 to 1 January 2021" (para 30)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: the SC dismissed the Revenue's SLPs against *Hundekari* (a separate Maersk-employee case, reported (2025) 27 Centax 14 (SC)), saying it saw no reason to interfere but "the question of law as regards the two provisions ... is kept open" (order quoted in Haria, para 26). Appeal against Haria itself not found.
- Use in orders: before a s.122(1A) penalty on an employee or director, record why the person is a taxable person or retained the benefit. Expect this decision to be cited.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Bombay HC WP 5001/2025, 25.02.2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/133627148/); [Lakshmisri - penalty only on taxable person](https://lakshmisri.com/newsroom/news-briefings/gst-penalty-under-section-122-1a-can-only-be-imposed-on-a-taxable-person); [LiveLaw - Delhi HC differs from Bombay HC](https://www.livelawbiz.com/high-court/delhi-high-court/penalty-us-1221a-of-cgst-act-can-be-imposed-on-both-taxable-and-non-taxable-person-delhi-high-court-291406)

### Gurudas Mallik Thakur v. Commissioner of CGST (with Dinesh Kumar Raghav) - Delhi HC (DB: Prathiba M. Singh and Rajneesh Kumar Gupta JJ.) - 23.04.2025

### Panchhi Traders v. State of Gujarat - Gujarat HC (DB: A.S. Supehia and Pranav Trivedi JJ.) - 11.12.2025
- Citation / case no.: R/SCA No. 9250 of 2020 and allied matters (SCAs of 2021, 2022 and 2025); reported [2026] 113 GST 318 / 104 GSTL 289 (Guj.) (one source).
- Holding:
  - Sections 129 and 130 operate independently, even after the 2022 amendments. Section 130 can be invoked directly where facts disclose a clear, demonstrable intent to evade.
  - The proper officer must form that opinion within the rule 138B/138C timelines (ordinarily about six days from interception). Otherwise the goods are released under s.129.
  - Confiscation may follow later if incriminating material emerges (following *Synergy Fertichem*).
  - The petitions were not decided on merits: all MOV-10/MOV-11 notices and orders were remanded for re-examination in light of these observations within 12 weeks, with two weeks' protection before any confiscation (paras 65-67).
- Key words: "the opinion of "intention to evade the payment of tax', has to be confined within the aforesaid period for confiscation, and if no opinion is formed, the goods and conveyance are required to be released by resorting to the provisions of Section 129" (para 65)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: followed by later Gujarat orders disposing of writs in its terms.
- Use in orders: record the material showing intent to evade and the date the opinion was formed, within the rule timelines.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Gujarat HC SCA 9250/2020 batch, 11.12.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/170350193/); [LiveLaw - Gujarat HC upholds confiscation power](https://www.livelawbiz.com/amp/high-court/gujarat-high-court/cgst-gujarat-high-court-upholds-power-to-confiscate-goods-during-transit-for-tax-evasion-clarifies-scope-of-sections-129-130-513256); [Taxo - 15.12.2025](https://taxo.online/latest-news/15-12-2025-section-130-can-be-independently-invoked-for-confiscation-where-the-facts-disclose-a-clear-and-demonstrable-intent-to-evade-payment-of-tax-gujarat-high-court/); [Taxheal (case nos.)](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=113078)

## 7. Cross-empowerment and parallel proceedings (s.6(2)(b))

**Contentions across courts.**
- Before *Armour Security*, High Courts were split on whether a summons is "initiation of proceedings". The SC settled this: it is not; an SCN is.
- On "same subject matter", the Delhi HC (Sept 2026) and Telangana HC (United Bakers, 2026, court to be confirmed) look at whether the issue, period and liability are truly identical. The same assessee or an overlapping period is not enough.
- A Feb 2025 Delhi ruling (following *DLF Home Developers*) held simultaneous proceedings impermissible.
- Department position: Circulars 31/05/2018, 169/01/2022 and 239/33/2024 on adjudication; no 2025 CBIC instruction following Armour was found.

### Armour Security (India) Ltd v. Commissioner, CGST, Delhi East Commissionerate - Supreme Court (J.B. Pardiwala and R. Mahadevan JJ.) - 14.08.2025
- Citation / case no.: 2025 INSC 982; SLP (C) No. 6092 of 2025 (disposed of with conclusions and guidelines; leave not granted). [2025] 177 taxmann.com 478 (SC) and AIR 2025 SC 3854 as reported (not seen on Indian Kanoon).
- Holding:
  - "Initiation of any proceedings" in s.6(2)(b) means formal adjudicatory proceedings by an SCN; summons, search, seizure and other evidence-gathering are not proceedings (para 96(v)-(vi)).
  - "Subject matter" is the tax liability or obligation from a particular contravention; an identical **or partial overlap** attracts the bar, but distinct infractions do not, even if the amount is similar. Two-fold test: same liability/offence on the same facts, and identical demand (para 96(vii)-(x)).
  - Audit and detailed-scrutiny action must be initiated by the administration to which the taxpayer is assigned; intelligence-based enforcement can be taken by either, but the other should not start parallel proceedings (para 96(ii)-(iv)).
  - Guidelines (para 97): taxpayer must still comply with summons; must tell the later authority in writing of the overlap; authorities must verify and reply with reasons; an SCN on a liability already covered by an existing SCN "shall be quashed"; if they cannot agree, the authority that started first continues.
- Key words: "The expression 'initiation of any proceedings' occurring in Section 6(2)(b) refers to the formal commencement of adjudicatory proceedings by way of issuance of a show cause notice, and does not encompass the issuance of summons". (para 96(vi))
- Binding: **BINDING ON ALL (Art. 141)**.
- Later history: applied by the Delhi HC in *Sanskriti Exim* and the Sept 2026 DGGI batch.
- Use in orders: investigation can proceed in parallel. Before issuing an SCN, verify that no SCN on the same subject matter has been issued by the other administration, and record that check. Audit and scrutiny of State-assigned taxpayers stay with the State.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - SC judgment 14.08.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/47476951/); [TaxGuru - Armour Security clarifies s.6(2)(b)](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/armour-security-case-supreme-court-clarifies-section-6-2-b-gst-investigation-guidelines.html); [SC Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/supreme-court-observer-law-reports-scolr/subject-matter-and-statutory-bar-in-cgst-proceedings-summons-m-s-armour-security-india-pvt-ltd-v-commissioner-cgst/); [AMLegals](https://amlegals.com/parallel-gst-proceedings-triggered-only-upon-issuance-of-scn-and-not-by-summons/)

(the Datta/Masih attribution is wrong; that bench decided Kesari Nandan the same day); neutral citation 2025 INSC 982 added; conclusions are para 96 and guidelines para 97 (not para 96).

- Holding: The pre-SCN (DRC-01A dated 25.11.2024) allowed submissions until 28.11.2024, but the SCN was issued on 27.11.2024, one day early. That violated natural justice, so the SCN was set aside and the matter relegated to the pre-SCN stage, with the time spent in the writ excluded from limitation (paras 19-22). The audit-limitation challenge **failed**: under the Explanation to s.65, audit "commences" when the registered person makes the records available (here its final submission on 11.10.2024), and the audit report of 11.02.2025 was held not time-barred (paras 14-17).
- Key words: "surprisingly, the authority has decided to issue the SCN itself one day before the said day expires ... Thus, this would be completely in violation of the principles of natural justice" (para 19)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: wait for the full reply period on DRC-01A or audit observations before issuing the SCN.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Delhi HC W.P.(C) 1064/2025, 28.10.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/198406201/); [Delhi HC judgment text](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/PMS28102025CW10642025_174455.txt); [CourtKutchehry](https://courtkutchehry.com/judgments/varian-medical-systems-international-india-pvt-ltd-vs-union-of-india-and-ors)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 15 Oct 2026. **Corrected:** case no. is W.P.(C) 1064/2025 (not 10642/2025); date and bench added; on commencement of audit the court used the date records are made available (not "the later of" two dates) and rejected the limitation challenge.

## 10. Compensation cess refunds

**Contentions across courts.** The Gujarat HC (*Patson Papers*, 28.03.2025, followed in *Atul Ltd*) allows refund of unutilised compensation-cess ITC where goods are exported on payment of IGST. Bombay HC (*Crystal Overseas*, 2025) involved denial on an LUT export; its outcome was not clearly stated. The department's position relied on Circulars 45/19/2018 and 125/44/2019, which the Gujarat HC said were misread. The SC dismissed the Revenue's SLPs in both (27.10.2025 and 13.02.2026) but left the question of law open. Neither is a Supreme Court ruling on the point.

### Patson Papers Pvt Ltd v. Union of India - **Gujarat HC** (DB: Bhargav D. Karia and D.N. Ray JJ.) - 28.03.2025; SLP dismissed by the Supreme Court (Pankaj Mithal and Prasanna B. Varale JJ.) on 27.10.2025, question of law left open
- Citation / case no.: HC: R/SCA No. 26250 of 2022 with SCA Nos. 20341 of 2022 and 19279 of 2023. SC: SLP (C) Diary No. 49578/2025 (later numbered SLP (C) Nos. 30826-30828/2025, per the SC order in Atul).
- Holding: Where the exporter pays IGST on a zero-rated supply under s.16(3) IGST Act and claims refund of that IGST, it cannot use the compensation-cess ITC (cess is not payable on the export), so the proviso to s.11(2) of the Cess Act does not apply and the unutilised cess ITC on coal used to make the exported goods is refundable; Circulars 45/19/2018 and 125/44/2019 do not bar it (paras 21-22). The SC, "in view of the finding recorded in paragraph 21", declined to interfere and dismissed the SLP "leaving the question of law, if any, open".
- Key words: "the petitioner would be entitled to refund of the unutilized input tax credit on cess paid on purchase of coal utilized for the purpose of manufacture of goods which are exported." (HC para 21)
- Binding: **Not a Supreme Court ruling.** It is a Gujarat HC judgment (persuasive in Telangana); the SLP dismissal is not a declaration of law under Art. 141, and the question of law was expressly left open.
- Later history: Followed by the Gujarat HC in Atul Ltd (24.07.2025); the Revenue's SLP in Atul was dismissed on 13.02.2026 because the Patson SLP had been dismissed.
- Use in orders: do not reject cess-ITC refund claims on the IGST-paid export route on this ground alone where the Gujarat view is followed. Do not cite it as Supreme Court law.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Gujarat HC SCA 26250/2022, 28.03.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/26206233/); [Indian Kanoon - SC order 27.10.2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/60669455/); [TaxGuru - cess refund, exports on payment of IGST (SC)](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/cess-refund-denied-exports-payment-igst-sc.html); [Taxheal - SC upholds refund of unutilised cess ITC](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=112043)
- Verified: Primary - HC judgment and SC orders (13.10.2025, 27.10.2025) read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026. **Corrected:** it is a Gujarat HC judgment, not a Supreme Court judgment; the SC only dismissed the Revenue's SLP with the question of law left open.

### Atul Ltd v. Union of India - Gujarat HC (DB: Bhargav D. Karia and Pranav Trivedi JJ.) - 24.07.2025
- Citation / case no.: R/SCA No. 20038 of 2022 with SCA No. 19949 of 2022. (SCA No. 26250 of 2022, earlier thought to be this case, is Patson Papers.)
- Holding: Facts "absolutely identical" to Patson Papers: unutilised compensation-cess ITC on coal used for exported goods (exports on payment of IGST) is refundable; refund rejections relying on Circulars 45/19/2018 and 125/44/2019 quashed (paras 7.2-8).
- Key words: "In view of the decision and law laid down by this Court in the case of Patson Papers (supra), the present petitions stands allowed." (para 8)
- Binding: persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: Revenue's SLP (C) No. 6586/2026 dismissed on 13.02.2026 (Manoj Misra and N.V. Anjaria JJ.) because the SLP in Patson Papers had been dismissed on 27.10.2025.
- Use in orders: as above.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - Gujarat HC SCA 20038/2022](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/134390581/); [Indian Kanoon - SC order 13.02.2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/122628176/); [Taxmann - refund of compensation cess ITC on coal](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/hc-allows-refund-of-unutilised-compensation-cess-itc-on-coal-used-for-export-goods); [TaxTMI blog](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_blog_details?id=842239)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026 (HC judgment and SC order of 13.02.2026); date, bench and case number added; it follows Patson Papers rather than deciding the point afresh.
