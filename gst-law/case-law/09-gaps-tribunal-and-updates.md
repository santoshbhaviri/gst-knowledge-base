# GST case law: gaps from round one, tribunal-level decisions and 2026 updates

**Scope.** This file follows up the "Gaps and doubts" lists at the end of files 01 to 05. It covers:
- what happened to the pending matters those files flagged (the Supreme Court s.168A case, *Sahil Enterprises*, the *Rollmet* reference, cash seizure, GST on royalty);
- refund points those files could not search (refund of tax on cancelled supplies, withholding under s.54(10)/(11), cash-ledger refunds, *Bansal International*);
- Telangana gaps (cash seizure, tax paid during search, works-contract rate);
- the first reported decisions of the GST Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT) and its procedure rulings;
- a short set of conflicting Advance Rulings that officers meet often;
- how Finance Act 2026 and the 57th GST Council meeting (8 Oct 2026) affect the case law.

Entries are grouped under the earlier file they belong to (for example, "Additions to refunds"). Read this file with those files, not on its own.

**Date compiled:** 14 Oct 2026. **Updated:** 17 Oct 2026 (5 entries added; 57th Council status re-checked).

> Compiled from web-search summaries. On 14 Oct 2026, 21 entries were checked against the judgment text on Indian Kanoon (marked "Primary"); the rest remain secondary. See the verification log at the end.

**Binding status key.** Supreme Court: binding on all (Art. 141). Telangana HC: **BINDING IN TELANGANA** (Sangareddy). Other High Courts: persuasive in Telangana. GSTAT: binding on the parties and in practice followed by lower authorities in that bench's area, but not precedent in the way a High Court ruling is; treat as strongly persuasive. AAR/AAAR: binds only the applicant and its jurisdictional officer (s.103); persuasive at most elsewhere.

## 1. Quick answers

**1. SC s.168A case (Notifications 9/2023 and 56/2023), SLP(C) 4240/2025, *HCC-SEW-MEIL-AAG JV*.** No final judgment was found as of 14 Oct 2026. Notice was issued on 21 Feb 2025. High Courts are split: Allahabad upheld 9/2023, Patna upheld 56/2023, Gauhati quashed 56/2023. Delhi HC (Jan 2026) declined to rule on vires "keeping in view judicial discipline". *Officer:* in any FY 2018-19 / 2019-20 order that relies on the extended dates, record that the validity of the notification is sub judice before the SC and that the order is subject to its outcome.

**2. Finance Act 2026 and the 57th Council.** Finance Act 2026 (Act 4 of 2026, assent 30.03.2026) did **not** amend s.54(3) or s.69. Its GST clauses (ss.153-157) deal with discounts and credit notes (s.15, s.34), provisional refund for inverted duty (s.54(6)), the s.54(14) minimum, NAA appeals and the intermediary place-of-supply rule. These are not in force except s.156 and s.157 (per the project's statute notes in `01-acts.md`). The 57th Council (8 Oct 2026) **recommended** omitting s.69 and refunding input-services ITC under inverted duty for ITC availed from 1 Nov 2026. As of 14 Oct 2026 these are recommendations only; no Bill, ordinance or notification was found. *Officer:* until the law is amended, *VKC Footsteps* (SC 2021) still bars refund of input-services ITC under inverted duty, and s.69 still applies, subject to *Radhika Agarwal* and *Sunil Biyani*.

**3. Refund of tax on a supply that was cancelled.** Karnataka HC (DB) in *Nam Estates* (W.A. 1195/2024) held that where the contract was cancelled and no supply took place, GST paid cannot be kept by the State just because the supplier did not issue a credit note. *Officer:* do not reject a recipient's refund only because no credit note was issued. Examine whether a supply took place and whether the same tax has already been refunded or adjusted at the supplier's end (to avoid a double refund).

**4. Withholding a refund under s.54(10)/(11).** Orissa (*Rashmi Agency*, 25 Jun 2026), Patna (*KPIL-JWIL JV*, 13 Aug 2026) and Telangana (*ADV Industrial Services*, Sep 2026, in file 01) agree on three points. Withholding needs an appeal or proceeding actually pending on the date of the order. It needs the Commissioner's own recorded opinion that paying the refund will hurt revenue. It needs a hearing and a speaking order. The fact that time to appeal is still running is not enough, and neither is an intention to appeal. But Delhi HC (*Devi Electronics*, W.P.(C) 15557/2024, 1 Oct 2026) upheld a withholding where an anti-evasion investigation was already pending and the Commissioner had recorded an opinion of fraud: "any other proceedings" covers a pending investigation. *Officer:* if you want to hold back a refund, get a reasoned Commissioner-level order under s.54(11), after a hearing, once the appeal is filed. Do not simply reject the RFD.

**5. Cash-ledger refunds.** No unjust-enrichment certificate is needed (rule 89(2)(l)/(m) does not apply). Delhi HC (*Harbhajan Singh Thukral*, W.P.(C) 3967/2025, Aug 2025) ordered payment with interest of an accepted cash-ledger refund that had been adjusted against a demand already cancelled. The 57th Council recommends full auto-sanction. *Officer:* any adjustment must follow s.54(10) or s.79 with notice and an order.

**6. Cash seizure under s.67.** Cash seizure is now opposed by Delhi (*Deepak Khandelwal*), Bombay DB (*Smurti Waghdhare*, 10 Mar 2026), Gauhati (*Ankit Choudhary*) and others. Only MP (*Kanishka Matta*, 26 Aug 2020) supports it. The SC has issued notice (2024) but has not decided. **No Telangana HC GST cash-seizure ruling was found.** *Officer:* do not seize cash unless it is itself the subject of the offence, with recorded reasons to believe. Never hand cash over to Income Tax. Issue the s.67(7) notice within six months.

**7. GSTAT.** Benches are working: Principal Bench since late 2025, and Hyderabad from 20 Apr 2026. The first substantive decision was *Sterling & Wilson* (PB, 11 Feb 2026, APL/1/PB/2026). It held that a return mismatch alone is not a ground for s.74, and that only the proper officer can re-determine tax under s.73 after s.74 fails, so the case was remanded. *Augustan Textile Colours* (Ernakulam, Sep 2026) held that a refund cannot be rejected as "ineligible ITC" without a s.73/74 order. *Officer:* these are the first signals of how GSTAT will review our orders. Invoke s.74 only with specific findings of fraud or suppression, and do not decide ITC eligibility inside a refund proceeding.

**8. Pre-deposit for GSTAT.** The SC (*PVCON Engineering*, SLP(C) 28059/2026, 7 Sep 2026) issued notice on whether the post-1.10.2025 pre-deposit for penalty-only appeals applies to older matters. As interim relief, it allowed a GSTAT appeal to be heard without pre-deposit. GSTAT Cuttack (*Manoranjan Dash*, 2026) gave credit for the first-appeal deposit. Orissa HC (15 May 2026) called s.112(8) pre-deposit mandatory. *Officer:* the point is unsettled. Follow the statute but do not take coercive steps where the SC has granted interim protection to the specific appellant.

**9. Rule 86A.** A block lapses after one year (r.86A(3)). Delhi HC (*Shri Sai Ram Enterprises*, 28 Apr 2025) and Calcutta HC (12 Jan 2026) lifted blocks older than one year. Negative blocking is illegal (Gujarat, Telangana, Delhi and Bombay, per *King Enterprises*, 18 Nov 2025). *Officer:* diarise the one-year lapse and block only the balance actually available.

**10. Section 16(2)(c), supplier default.** The SC **stayed** the Tripura HC's *Sahil Enterprises* read-down (Diary No. 33460/2026; Pardiwala and Vinod Chandran JJ.). Read with *Bhandari Scrap Traders* (SC, 24 Jul 2026, in file 02), the read-down is not good law for now. But *Safecon Lifescience* (SC dismissed the Revenue's SLP, 17 Jul 2026) leaves intact the rule that s.74 cannot rest on another wing's report that was never verified or disclosed to the buyer. *Officer:* recover ITC under s.16(2)(c) on evidence specific to the buyer. Put the investigation report to the noticee. Use s.74 only where the buyer's own fraud or collusion is shown.

## 2. Statutory developments that overtake or qualify case law

### Finance Act, 2026 (Act 4 of 2026) - Parliament - assent 30 Mar 2026
- Citation / case no.: Act 4 of 2026. GST clauses are ss.153-157; commencement is in s.1(2). This summary is from the project's statute notes (`/mnt/project-files/gst-law/01-acts.md`), which were checked against the CBIC copy of the Act.
- Holding (content):
  - s.153/s.154: post-sale discounts reduce value if a credit note is issued and the recipient reverses the ITC.
  - s.155: s.54(6) provisional refund extended to inverted-duty refunds, and the s.54(14) minimum-amount bar lifted for exports with payment of tax.
  - s.156: NAA appeals go to the GSTAT Principal Bench, in force 01.04.2026.
  - s.157: IGST s.13(8)(b) omitted, so intermediary services now take the recipient's location, from 30.03.2026.
- Binding: statute.
- Later history: ss.153-155 start only on a date to be notified; **none had been notified as of 12.10.2026**. **The Act does not amend s.54(3) or s.69.** A TaxGuru article about the Finance Bill 2026 said s.54(3) was "rewritten" to overturn *VKC Footsteps*. The project's reading of the enacted Act does not bear that out, so treat the article as wrong or as describing a proposal.
- Use in orders: *VKC Footsteps* (SC 2021) still governs inverted-duty refunds of input-services ITC. For intermediary disputes, *Dharmendra M. Jani* (file 01) is overtaken for supplies from 30.03.2026 but stays relevant for earlier periods.
- Source: [CBIC - Finance Acts page](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/explore-others); [TaxGuru - s.54(3) "rewritten" in Finance Bill 2026 (contrary claim)](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/section-54-3-rewritten-finance-bill-2026-overturns-vkc-footsteps-extending-inverted-duty.html)
- Verified: Primary for the Act (via the project's statute notes). The TaxGuru claim is a single source and is contradicted.

### 57th GST Council meeting - 8 Oct 2026 (recommendations, not law)
- Citation / case no.: PIB press release, 8 Oct 2026 (PRID 2320934), and Council FAQs. Details are in the project's `05-gst-council-meetings.md`.
- Holding (content relevant to case law):
  - **Omit s.69 (arrest).**
  - Raise the prosecution threshold from ₹1 crore to ₹5 crore, omit s.132(1)(i), and narrow s.132(1)(c).
  - **Inverted-duty refund to cover input services for ITC availed on or after 1 Nov 2026.**
  - Capital-goods ITC refund over 60 months for ITC availed from 1 Apr 2027.
  - Auto-sanction of cash-ledger refunds, and 90% provisional refunds.
  - Remove the 1.5x cap in rule 89(4)(C).
  - No SCN where tax is below ₹10,000.
  - Pre-deposit cap of ₹40 crore for penalty-only appeals.
  - Rule 86A: a right to object and to a hearing.
  - E-way bill interception limits, and no s.130 confiscation of goods in transit.
- Binding: no. The release itself says the recommendations take effect only through notifications, circulars or amendments to the Act.
- Later history: **no amending Bill, ordinance or notification was found as of 14 Oct 2026, nor on a re-check on 17 Oct 2026.** From 15-16 Oct 2026 the CBIC Chairman and Member (GST) held outreach meetings with trade on the 57th Council reforms (Hyderabad, Chennai, Bengaluru), and sought feedback on a proposed centralised GST administration for taxpayers under Central jurisdiction (a proposal, not a decision). One news source says the changes are expected from 1 Apr 2027. Sources differ slightly on the date (8 vs 9 Oct); the official release says 8 Oct.
- Use in orders: do not apply these as law yet. Arrest law under s.69 still runs (*Radhika Agarwal*, *Sunil Biyani*, file 04). Expect noticees to cite the Council's "intent" (for example, on 86A hearings or e-way bill transit checks). Answer that the provisions are unamended, while giving a hearing anyway where natural justice already requires one.
- Source: [Moneylife - Council recommends scrapping arrest powers](https://www.moneylife.in/article/gst-council-recommends-scrapping-arrest-powers-raises-prosecution-threshold-to-5-crore/81904.html); [Taxscan - 57th Council proposes refund of capital goods and input services ITC](https://www.taxscan.in/gst/refund-of-accumulated-itc-57th-gst-council-proposes-inclusion-of-capital-goods-and-input-services); [StudyCafe - proposed removal of s.69](https://studycafe.in/57th-gst-council-meeting-proposed-removal-of-arrest-provision-under-section-69-and-changes-to-prosecution-rules-427363.html)
- Verified: Secondary (news, several agree), plus the official release as recorded in the project notes.

## 3. Additions to refunds (file 01)

### Joint Commissioner of Commercial Taxes (Appeals-1), Bengaluru v. Nam Estates Pvt Ltd - Karnataka HC (Division Bench: Krishna S. Dixit and G. Basavaraja JJ.) - 9 Jan 2025
- Citation / case no.: Writ Appeal No. 1195 of 2024 (T-RES); NC: 2025:KHC:680-DB (oral judgment). Appeal from the single judge's order of 18.04.2024 in W.P. 9075/2024.
- Holding: The recipient paid GST on an advance of about ₹14.09 crore (tax about ₹2.54 crore). The supplier defaulted, the contract was cancelled and the advance was recovered by encashing the bank guarantee. With no supply, there was no GST liability, so the tax could not be kept by the State only because the supplier issued no credit note. The single judge relied on unjust enrichment and restitution. The DB upheld him: the purchaser can claim refund (s.54(1) "any person", following *Oswal Chemicals*), and the Revenue could not refuse it because the vendor issued no credit note, since goods were never delivered. Refund within eight weeks, with interest at the statutory rate if delayed.
- Binding: Karnataka; persuasive in Telangana.
- Later history: SLP not found.
- Use in orders: before rejecting a recipient's refund for want of a credit note, record whether supply took place and whether the supplier has already reduced its liability or claimed a refund for the same tax. A finding of double benefit is the only safe ground to reject.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - WA 1195/2024, 9 Jan 2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/137439029/); [Taxmann - GST on advance not to be retained where contract cancelled](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/gst-paid-on-advance-amount-is-not-to-be-retained-by-state-if-contract-is-cancelled-due-to-breach-of-contract-hc); [TaxHeal - WA 1195 of 2024](https://www.taxheal.com/1195-of-2024-t-res.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date (9 Jan 2025), bench and NC added; holding confirmed.
- Key words: "Caesar cannot retain monies of citizens without statutory justification." (para 5.4)

### Emerald Haven Realty Developers (Paraniputhur) Pvt Ltd v. S.V. Ramesh - Madras HC (Division Bench: J. Nisha Banu and R. Sakthivel JJ.) - 24 Jan 2025
- Citation / case no.: C.M.S.A. No. 55 of 2024 (second appeal under s.58 of the RERA Act, from the TN Real Estate Appellate Tribunal).
- Holding: **Corrected.** The court upheld the RERA orders making the promoter refund the GST component (₹11,40,376) to an allottee who cancelled. The promoter's "GST loss" deduction (its s.34(2) credit-note window had closed) was not provided for in the agreement, and it did not prove it had paid GST twice. Noting the allottee's own refund application and undertaking to pass any GST refund to the promoter, it directed the GST department to process that application within two months. It did **not** hold that the buyer must apply under s.54 instead of the builder (the blog overstated it). Circular 188/20/2022-GST (refund to unregistered buyers on cancellation) is not discussed in the operative part.
- Binding: persuasive.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: for a flat buyer's refund on cancellation, process the buyer's s.54 / rule 89 application on its merits (the court directed exactly that), and check that the builder has not also reduced its liability for the same tax.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - CMSA 55/2024, 24 Jan 2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/116456944/); [CA Sahuja - GST implications on property](https://www.casahuja.com/2025/02/gst-implications-on-property.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; party names, court, date and holding corrected (blog summary overstated). Circular reference still from general knowledge.
- Key words: "it is for the appellant/ Promoter to prove that he has paid GST two times for the same apartment before the competent Authority, which has not been done in the present case." (para 8)

### Bansal International v. Commissioner of DGST - Delhi HC (Vibhu Bakhru and Amit Mahajan JJ.) - 21 Nov 2023
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) 11629/2023.
- Holding: **Corrected.** The refund had already been granted after appellate proceedings; the dispute was the interest claim (₹13,12,761 at 9%), rejected on 11.07.2023. Held: s.56 interest at 6% runs from day 61 after the **original** s.54(1) application; where the claim succeeds in appeal and a fresh application is filed, 9% runs from day 61 after **that** later application, with 6% continuing for the period in between. The rejection was set aside and the interest application (16.05.2023) directed to be processed accordingly.
- Binding: Delhi; persuasive in Telangana. Later Delhi HC orders treat it as setting out the "entire scheme" of refunds, e.g. W.P.(C) 7485/2024 (20 May 2025) and W.P.(C) 16315/2025 (28 Oct 2025).
- Later history: followed by the Telangana HC in *Qualcom India* / *Microsoft Global Services* (W.P. 8869 and 8871/2023, 20.03.2024; entry below). The blog's two-stage description is the correct one.
- Use in orders: compute s.56 interest at 6% from day 61 after the original application; where the refund follows an appellate order, 9% from day 61 after the post-appeal application, and 6% for the gap before it. Telangana's own authority on interest is *Qualcom* and *Synchrony* (file 01).
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - W.P.(C) 11629/2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/3180268/); [SAG Infotech - Delhi HC on interest after 60 days](https://blog.saginfotech.com/delhi-hc-interest-gst-refunds-60-days-filing-refund-application-finalization-refund-claim); [Delhi HC W.P.(C) 16315/2025 citing Bansal](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/PMS28102025CW163152025_174620.pdf)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; facts and interest computation corrected; bench added.
- Key words: "The applicant claiming a refund is entitled to interest at the rate of 6% per annum from a date immediately after the expiry of sixty days from making an application under Section 54(1) of the CGST Act." (para 33)

### M/s Rashmi Agency v. Deputy Commissioner CT & GST, Cuttack-I City - Orissa HC (Harish Tandon CJ and M.S. Raman J.) - 25 Jun 2026
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) No. 17470 of 2026 (reported 1 Jul 2026).
- Holding: ₹33 lakh paid in DRC-03 during a DGGI search became refundable under a first-appeal order of 27.02.2026. The officer refused to consider the refund (RFD-06, 02.05.2026) as "premature" because the State's six-month window under s.112(3) was open. Held: s.54(11) needs an appeal or other proceeding pending when the order is passed; none was (the State appealed to GSTAT only on 24.06.2026). Order set aside; remitted for fresh decision within two weeks after a hearing.
- Binding: Orissa; persuasive.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: process the consequential refund. If the Department will appeal, file the appeal first and then pass a reasoned s.54(11) order after a hearing.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - W.P.(C) 17470/2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/173916935/); [taxo.online - 01.07.2026 Orissa HC](https://taxo.online/latest-news/01-07-2026-refund-cannot-be-withheld-merely-because-time-to-file-departmental-appeal-has-not-expired-orissa-high-court/); [TaxHeal - refund cannot be withheld without express order and hearing](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=140174)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; party name and decision date (25.06.2026, not 1.07.2026) added; relief is remand, not a direct refund order.
- Key words: "Unless there has already been proceeding pending, invocation of said provision smacks arbitrariness and tainted with irrational application of mind." (para 7.1)

### Devi Electronics Pvt Ltd v. Commissioner CGST Delhi South - Delhi HC (Anil Kshetarpal and Shail Jain JJ.) - 1 Oct 2026
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) 15557/2024.
- Holding: **Corrected: the petition was dismissed and the withholding upheld.** IGST export refunds of ₹3,15,30,605 allowed by an order-in-appeal (19.06.2024) were withheld by the Commissioner under s.54(11) on 24.09.2024. Held: "any other proceedings under this Act" covers an anti-evasion investigation already pending (from 26.03.2024), and s.54(11) works without any court stay where the Commissioner records an opinion of fraud on contemporaneous material. Cases where the Revenue relied only on a proposed appeal (*Alex Tour & Travel*, *Mandy Enterprises*) were distinguished; a mere decision to appeal is still not enough. Merits left to GSTAT.
- Binding: Delhi; persuasive.
- Later history: an earlier bench (Yashwant Varma J., 16.01.2025) had prima facie doubted that an investigation counts as "other proceedings"; the final bench held that it does.
- Use in orders: a s.54(11) withholding can rest on a pending investigation, but only through a Commissioner's reasoned order, after a hearing, recording the material showing malfeasance or fraud. A bare intention to appeal is not a ground.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - W.P.(C) 15557/2024, 1 Oct 2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/152005308/); [TaxGuru - mere decision to file appeal no stay (headline reflects the cases distinguished, not the result)](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/mere-decision-file-appeal-automatically-stay-gst-refund-delhi-hc.html); [Delhi HC order](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/75001102026CW155572024_153733.pdf)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; party name and date added; holding was wrong (petition dismissed, withholding upheld) and is rewritten.
- Key words: "Section 54(11) is a self-contained, statutorily engrafted safeguard for protection of revenue, operating independently of, and not contingent upon, any separate stay granted by a court or tribunal." (para 15)

### KPIL-JWIL Joint Venture v. State of Bihar - Patna HC (Anil Kumar Sinha and Vikash Kumar JJ.) - 13 Aug 2026
- Citation / case no.: CWJC No. 5398 of 2026.
- Holding: A ₹1.75 crore cash-ledger refund, allowed in first appeal (31.12.2024), was stalled by a deficiency memo (RFD-03, 01.03.2025) citing past dues; the State had filed a GSTAT appeal (30.06.2026). Since no s.54(11) exercise had been undertaken, the court directed the Commissioner to issue a notice within a week, hear the petitioner and pass a specific reasoned s.54(11) order on payment or withholding within a month of the hearing.
- Binding: Patna; persuasive.
- Later history: none found.
- Use in orders: a checklist for any s.54(11) order: (i) pending appeal number and date; (ii) Commissioner's recorded reasons; (iii) hearing notice and speaking order.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - CWJC 5398/2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/63152793/); [TaxHeal - cannot reject consequential refund without stay or withholding safeguards](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=140784); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1078532)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; case number and bench added; holding narrowed to what the court directed.
- Key words: "we direct the Commissioner to pass a specific order in terms of Section 54(11) ... after giving Show Cause Notice and proper opportunity of hearing to the petitioner." (para 10)
- **s.54(10) note:** no 2025-26 ruling specific to s.54(10) (withholding against unpaid returns or dues) was found. The cases above are all s.54(11).

### Royale Edible Company v. State Tax Officer, SGST Dept, Thrissur - Kerala HC (A.K. Jayasankaran Nambiar J.) - 18 Dec 2020
- Citation / case no.: WP(C) No. 22593 of 2020.
- Holding: (The balance arose from TDS credits; the officer wrongly treated the claim as one under s.51(8).) A balance left in the electronic cash ledger after all liabilities are paid can be claimed under the proviso to s.54(1). The officer should refund the excess after providing for known liabilities.
- Binding: Kerala; persuasive.
- Later history: none found. The 57th Council (Oct 2026) recommends full auto-sanction of cash-ledger refunds.
- Use in orders: do not reject cash-ledger refunds on the ground that the money "may be needed later". Adjust only known, crystallised dues under the proper procedure.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - WP(C) 22593/2020](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/120924035/); [taxo.online - Royale Edible](https://taxo.online/?p=52627); [Busy - case summary](https://busy.in/caselaw/royale-edible-company-vs-the-state-tax-officer-and-others-kerala-high-court)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; date and judge added; summary confirmed.
- Key words: "if there was such a balance, the refund had necessarily to be granted to the petitioner." (para 5)

### Harbhajan Singh Thukral v. GNCTD, Department of Trade and Taxes - Delhi HC (Prathiba M. Singh and Shail Jain JJ.) - 20 Aug 2025
- Citation / case no.: W.P.(C) 3967/2025.
- Holding: **Narrowed.** A cash-ledger refund accepted on 12.04.2023 was appropriated (order 18.09.2023) against a demand that had already been cancelled by a DRC-08A of 24.07.2023, which was never uploaded to the portal. On the Department's own admission, the court directed the refund (₹9,09,727) to be paid with statutory interest within two months. It is a fact-specific order and does not hold that an accepted refund can never be adjusted against live dues.
- Binding: Delhi; persuasive.
- Later history: none found. Contrast the Delhi HC ruling of Nov 2024 (party name not found) that the Commissioner may withhold cash- or credit-ledger refunds in a proper case (reported under s.108 / s.54).
- Use in orders: before appropriating a refund against dues (s.54(10) / s.79, with notice and an order), confirm that the demand is still live on the portal and in the records (check for DRC-08A rectifications). Never adjust silently.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - W.P.(C) 3967/2025, 20 Aug 2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/115866020/); [Dev International v. UoI (Delhi HC) summary](https://busy.in/caselaw/dev-international-vs-union-of-india-and-others-delhi-high-court); [LiveLaw - Commissioner empowered to withhold refunds](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/tax-cases/cgst-act-commissioner-empowered-withhold-refunds-electronic-cash-ledger-electronic-credit-ledger-delhi-hc-275329)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 14 Oct 2026; party name and bench added; holding narrowed (the adjusted demand had been cancelled). The "Dev International" link is a different case.
- Key words: "let the refund application be processed and the refund be paid to the Petitioner along with statutory interest in accordance with law within a period of two months from today." (para 7)

### Gujarat HC: mistaken self-assessed payment not barred by s.54(1) - Gujarat HC - Jan 2025 (party name not found)
- Citation / case no.: not found.
- Holding: ₹40 lakh paid by mistake from the cash ledger, when no notification required it, was ordered refunded and not treated as time-barred under s.54(1). No interest was allowed because the payment was voluntary.
- Binding: Gujarat; persuasive.
- Later history: none found. A related Jan 2025 ruling found on Indian Kanoon, *Aculife Health Care v. UoI* (Gujarat HC DB, SCA 17800/2023, 9 Jan 2025; https://indiankanoon.org/doc/38235202/), ordered ₹15,90,288 paid under a mistake of law refunded with 9% interest and held s.54(1) limitation inapplicable. Its facts (amount, interest) do not match this entry, so it is not the same case.
- Use in orders: for money paid without any liability, limitation under s.54(1) is a weak ground of rejection. Courts treat it as a deposit, not tax.

## 9. Advance Rulings: conflicting rulings officers meet (persuasive only)

AAR and AAAR rulings bind only the applicant and its jurisdictional officer (s.103). They are not precedent for other taxpayers. Where two States' AARs conflict, follow the statute, CBIC circulars and High Court or SC rulings.

### ITC on canteen services: Tata Motors (Gujarat AAR) vs Aditya Auto Products (Karnataka AAR)
- Citation / case no.: *Tata Motors Ltd*, GUJ/GAAR/R/39/2021, 30 Jul 2021. *Aditya Auto Products*, Karnataka AAR, ADRG 25/2026, 19 May 2026.
- Holding:
  - Tata Motors: ITC on canteen services was blocked under s.17(5)(b)(i), on a narrow reading of the proviso. GST was payable only on the amount recovered from employees.
  - Aditya Auto: ITC was allowed where the canteen is mandatory under s.46 of the Factories Act, limited to the cost borne by the employer for regular employees. No ITC on the portion recovered from employees or for contract workers.
  - A Gujarat AAR ruling of Sep 2022 (applicant not named) held no GST on employee recoveries.
- Binding: applicants only.
- Later history: Circular 172/04/2022-GST (6 Jul 2022) clarified that the s.17(5)(b) proviso (ITC allowed where the employer must provide the item by law) applies to the whole of clause (b). That overtakes Tata Motors' narrow reading. (Circular from general knowledge; not search-checked this round.)
- Use in orders: allow ITC on statutory factory canteens to the extent of the employer's cost; disallow the employee-recovered share.
- Source: [Taxmann - GST on canteen recovery](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/gst-on-canteen-recovery-from-employees-aar/); [Grant Thornton alert](https://grantthornton.in/link/545e2d0fa0154cd98233cdddb7c15571.aspx)
- Verified: Secondary - search summaries (2-3 sources) (not found on Indian Kanoon on 14 Oct 2026).

### ITC on demonstration vehicles: WB and Kerala AARs vs MP and Haryana AAR/AAAR
- Citation / case no.: *Toplink Motorcar* (WB AAR, 2022) and *Sai Service* (Kerala AAR, 2024) allowed ITC. MP AAR (2021) and Haryana AAR/AAAR denied it. *Chowgule* (Goa AAR, 2019) denied it under s.17(5)(a).

### M/s SSL Industries Ltd v. Superintendent of Central Tax - Telangana HC (DB: CJ Aparesh Kumar Singh and Vakiti Ramakrishna Reddy J.) - 17.09.2026 - **BINDING IN TELANGANA (procedural)**
- Citation / case no.: W.P. No. 30752 of 2026.
- Holding: The writ challenged a DRC-01 summary (30.09.2025), an order-in-original and DRC-07 (26.12.2025) for 2021-22 as void for want of a DIN and of a valid physical or digital signature; the petitioner said it learned of them only from a demand notice of 01.06.2026. At the petitioner's request the court did not go into the merits (para 6), gave liberty to appeal within two weeks with pre-deposit and a condonation application, and directed the appellate authority to consider the delay and, if satisfied, decide the appeal on merits with all grounds open (paras 7-8).
- Binding: Telangana; procedural only (no ruling on DIN). Decided 13 days before the Full Bench in *Gudla Vanaja* (30.09.2026, file 03), which now holds that an SCN or order without a DIN/RFN is invalid.
- Later history: none.
- Use in orders: (1) the Telangana HC is routing DIN and signature challenges to the s.107 appeal where the taxpayer opts for it, so Commissioner (Appeals) and JC (Appeals) will now meet *Gudla Vanaja* grounds in appeals and must apply it; (2) for the Division: check DIN/RFN on every SCN and order before recovery (as already pending for S1-S10), because a DRC-07 recovery on a DIN-less order will be set aside on appeal.
- Source: [Indian Kanoon - W.P. 30752/2026, 17 Sep 2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/183128166/); [TaxGuru (7 Oct 2026)](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/telangana-hc-gst-appeal-raising-din-digital-signature-grounds.html)
- Verified: Primary - read on Indian Kanoon 17 Oct 2026 (bench, W.P. number, date, paras 2-8). *Gudla Vanaja* is not cited in it (it predates the Full Bench).
