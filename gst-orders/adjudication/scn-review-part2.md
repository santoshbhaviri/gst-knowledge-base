## S7: scrutiny SCN, FY 2022-23, s.73

**Issues and period.** (1) ITC of CGST and SGST in GSTR-3B Table 4(A)(5) is far above what GSTR-2B shows. About 96% of the CGST ITC claimed has no GSTR-2B match. (2) Tax declared in GSTR-1 is above tax paid in GSTR-3B. The GSTR-3B payment covers only about half the GSTR-1 liability. Period: April 2022 to March 2023.

**Section invoked.** s.73 read with s.122(2)(a). s.73 is the right section, since the year is before FY 2024-25 and there is no fraud allegation. **Score 5.0/10.** The SCN is within time, its tables are clear and it reaches s.73(8). But the ITC charge cites the wrong limb of s.16(2), and interest, penalty and hearing are left open.

**Strengths worth keeping**
- Each issue has its own heading, a source-labelled table (GSTR-3B table numbers, GSTR-2B excluding RCM, GSTR-1 tables 4A+4C+5+6+7...) and a contravention paragraph.
- The pre-SCN letters are cited with their dates, DINs and "no reply received" (check 4, A3).
- s.73(8) is explained to the noticee. The reply period is 30 days.
- Service is by speed post and e-mail.

**Defects, most likely to be quashed first**
1. **Wrong legal basis for the ITC demand (H1, H3, B3).** The SCN infers that suppliers "had paid" tax on the GSTR-2B amount and "had not" paid on the rest, then charges s.16(2)(c). Nothing shows that any supplier's tax was verified as unpaid (no supplier GSTR-3B or ledger check). For FY 2022-23 the direct provision is **s.16(2)(aa)** (in force since 01.01.2022), read with **rule 36(4)** (ITC limited to invoices communicated in GSTR-2B). Neither is cited. *Bhandari Scrap Traders* (SC 2026) upholds s.16(2)(c), but orders without a verified finding of non-payment get remanded (*Inam Impex*, Telangana). The s.73 window for this year has closed (30.09.2026), so no corrigendum is possible. **Fix in adjudication:** call for the invoices. Frame the finding on the fact stated in the SCN (credit taken in excess of what was communicated in GSTR-2B). Rely on s.160(1) for the citation error. Make a verified s.16(2)(c) finding supplier by supplier, or drop the part you cannot verify. **Template fix:** for FY 2021-22 (from 01.01.2022) onward, cite s.16(2)(aa) and rule 36(4). Keep s.16(2)(c) for cases with a verified supplier default.
2. **s.50(3) interest with no utilisation shown (H6, check 37).** The SCN proposes s.50(3) and rule 88B(3) but gives no ledger table. Under rule 88B(3), interest runs only while the credit-ledger balance is below the disputed ITC. Annex a month-wise ledger table.
3. **Interest and penalty not quantified (B6, check 16).** The SCN says "interest at applicable rate" and a penalty "under s.73 read with s.122(2)(a)", with no figures. State the penalty as the higher of 10% or Rs 10,000 under s.73(9), issue by issue. Cite s.122(2)(a) as the measure, not as a second penalty (s.75(13)).
4. **Personal hearing (D1, check 19).** Paragraph 13 presumes that the noticee "do[es] not desire to be heard" unless it asks. This is contrary to s.75(4): a hearing is mandatory where an adverse decision is contemplated (*B.K. Steels*, Telangana). Give a date, time and venue, or say a separate hearing notice will follow.
5. **No DIN or printed date on a notice served by speed post and e-mail (C1, check 30).** The DIN field is blank. A communication outside the portal needs a DIN (Circulars 122/41/2019 and 128/47/2019). Circular 249/06/2025 exempts only portal documents that carry an RFN. Print the issue date as well. Limitation is otherwise provable only from the PDF signature. *Note 14.10.2026: the legal basis of this defect is now stronger. The Telangana HC Full Bench in Gudla Vanaja (30.09.2026) held that a notice or order without a DIN or RFN is invalid (and that a visible signature is not required for an electronically authenticated notice). If neither the served copy nor a portal copy carries a DIN/RFN, treat the notice as invalid and re-issue it if the time limit allows.*
6. **No relied-upon documents list (B5, check 12).** List the GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-2B extracts and the scrutiny letters, and say they are enclosed.
7. **Outdated statutory text (drafting).**
   - s.42 is cited, but it was omitted w.e.f. 01.10.2022.
   - s.41 is quoted in its pre-01.10.2022 "provisional basis" form. The current s.41(2) (reversal where the supplier has not paid) actually helps the department.
   - s.16(4) is quoted with the old "return for September" cut-off; it now reads 30 November.
   - Rule 61 is quoted with "FORM GSTR-3".
   - Paragraph 16 refers to the "Finance Act", left over from service-tax templates.
   - The GSTR-1/3B table carries a heading from the e-way bill template ("Tax payable on reconciliation of EWB data").
8. **No State-overlap or jurisdiction record (A1, A2, checks 1 and 2).** Add the portal check and the Circular 31/05/2018 monetary band.

**Limitation.** The GSTR-9 for FY 2022-23 was due 31.12.2023, so the s.73(10) deadline is 31.12.2026. The latest SCN date under s.73(2) was 30.09.2026. The SCN was signed 23.09.2026, which is **within time, with 7 days to spare**. The order is due by 31.12.2026. Diarise it now: no extension notification covers this year.

**s.74 check.** Not invoked. Correct.

**Quantification.** Tax is quantified. Interest and penalty are not. The negative IGST difference is correctly left out of the demand.

**Arithmetic spot-checked.**
- The CGST ITC difference and the CGST/SGST short payment are each one rupee off from a recomputation, apparently paise rounding.
- The IGST row shows "-41.44" where the whole-rupee figures give -42.
- The GSTR-1/3B totals are off by 1 to 2 rupees.
- None of this changes the case, but state "figures rounded to the rupee".

**Relied-upon documents:** none listed. **Hearing:** none fixed; waiver presumed. **Signature:** digitally signed. **DIN:** blank. **Service:** speed post and e-mail stated. Portal upload not stated.

## S8: scrutiny SCN, FY 2022-23, s.73

**Issues and period.** (1) ITC (IGST, CGST, SGST, compensation cess) in GSTR-3B is above GSTR-**2A**. About a quarter of the CGST/SGST credit is unmatched. (2) Late fee under s.47 on five late GSTR-1 returns. Period: April 2022 to March 2023.

**Section invoked.** s.73, s.47, and Compensation Cess Act s.11(1). The section is right. **Score 4.5/10.** The structure is clear and cess is handled, but the notice was issued at the last possible moment and the DRC-03 handling is wrong.

**Strengths worth keeping**
- The figures go to the paise and the arithmetic is exact (all four heads recomputed).
- Compensation cess is brought in through s.11(1) of the Compensation Act. Most templates miss this.
- The ASMT-10 is cited with its date and DIN, and the absence of an ASMT-11 reply is recorded (check 4).
- A DRC-03 paid before the SCN is found in the back office and recorded with its ARN and date. This is the right instinct (check 20).

**Defects, most likely to be quashed first**
1. **Limitation margin of one day (C2, check 23).** The s.73(2) bar for FY 2022-23 is 30.09.2026. The PDF was signed **29.09.2026**. If the DRC-01 upload or issue happened on or after 01.10.2026, the whole notice is time-barred and cannot be cured. **Action now:** pull the portal DRC-01 issue date and the RFN and put them on file. **Template fix:** issue s.73 notices at least a month before the s.73(2) date.
2. **Pre-SCN DRC-03 handled contrary to s.73(5) to (7) (H8, check 20).** s.73(6) bars a notice "in respect of the tax so paid". s.73(7) allows a notice only for the shortfall. Yet the SCN demands the full gross ITC and then says "**I appropriate**" the DRC-03 in the operative part. That is a finding no SCN can make before the reply. Also:
   - The payment's CGST and SGST shares are very unequal (SGST about twice CGST), while the demand is equal on both heads.
   - The payment carries no IGST, though IGST is demanded.
   - Whether interest was paid is not examined.

   **Fix:** state the ascertained liability, the amount paid head by head, whether interest was paid, and the balance proposed under s.73(7). Use "proposed to be appropriated". Reconcile the CGST/SGST imbalance and ask the noticee to correct it.
3. **Wrong legal basis and wrong comparator for the ITC (H1, H3).** The SCN uses the s.16(2)(c) "Government did not receive the tax" reasoning without verifying any supplier, as in S7. It compares with GSTR-2A, but for FY 2022-23 the statutory communication is GSTR-2B (s.16(2)(aa), rule 36(4)). Using 2A probably understates the gap, which favours the noticee, but the legal basis is still wrong.
4. **Wrong late-fee notification and day counts (B3, citation).**
   - "Notification No. 04/2019-Central Tax dated 23.01.2018" does not exist in that form. 04/2019-CT (29.01.2019) concerns Joint Commissioner (Appeals) jurisdiction. The GSTR-1 waiver was 4/2018-CT (23.01.2018), superseded from June 2021 by 20/2021-CT, which is the one that governs FY 2022-23 and is also cited.
   - Day counts recomputed: June 2022 is 30 days, not 29, and November 2022 is 20 days, not 19. The other three are correct. The fee is slightly understated, in the noticee's favour, but the table is wrong on its face.
   - The quoted s.47 is mis-numbered "(4)" and "(5)".
5. **Interest and penalty not quantified; no s.50(3) utilisation (B6, H6).** Same as S7. The penalty is again "s.73 read with s.122(2)(a)": quantify it under s.73(9) alone.
6. **Hearing presumed waived (D1).** Same paragraph as S7.
7. **No DIN on the face of a notice served by speed post and e-mail; date not printed (C1).**
8. **No relied-upon documents list (B5).**
9. **Drafting.** Two paragraphs are numbered 6. The SCN refers to the "TSGST Act". It repeats S7's outdated s.41 and s.16(4) text. The operative clause (ii) refers to "para 5" for the demand.

**Limitation.** GSTR-9 due 31.12.2023, so the s.73(10) deadline is 31.12.2026 and the latest SCN date under s.73(2) was 30.09.2026. The SCN was signed 29.09.2026: **within time only if the portal issue date is also 29 or 30 September.** The order is due by 31.12.2026.

**s.74 check.** Not invoked.

**Quantification.** Tax and late fee are quantified. Interest and penalty are not. The DRC-03 credit is mis-applied (above).

**Arithmetic spot-checked.** All four ITC differences are exact, and the total ITC demand equals the sum of the heads. Late-fee days: 2 of 5 rows are wrong (above).

**Relied-upon documents:** none listed. **Hearing:** none fixed. **Signature:** digitally signed. **DIN:** none on the SCN. **Service:** speed post and e-mail stated.

## S9: unregistered mining leaseholder, FY 2020-21 to 2022-23, s.74

**Issues and period.** (1) Forward-charge GST at 5% on sales of stone slabs, valued from dispatch quantities in mining-department data, a department "average sale price" and a unit-conversion factor. (2) RCM at 18% under Notification 13/2017-CT(R) Sl. 5 on royalty/seigniorage, DMF, SMET/NMET and permit fee paid to the State. (3) Penalty for failure to register. Three financial years in one notice. The source is a Director General of Audit (Central) performance-audit observation.

**Section invoked.** s.74(1). Penalty under s.74(1), "s.74(1) read with s.122(2)(b)", and separately s.122(1)(xi). s.74 is the only section still open for FY 2020-21 and 2021-22: the s.73 windows closed on 30.11.2024 and 30.09.2025. So the demand stands or falls on s.74. s.63 (best-judgment assessment of unregistered persons) was the more natural route and is not mentioned. **Score 3.0/10.** The rate entry, the RCM entry and Circular 164/20/2021 are cited and there is a fact-based s.74 paragraph. But the core valuation appears wrong by two orders of magnitude, the years are combined, and the penalties overlap.

**Strengths worth keeping**
- Issue-wise structure with the rate notification and entry, the RCM entry (Notification 13/2017-CT(R) Sl. 5) and the exemption exclusion (Notification 12/2017-CT(R) Sl. 6) cited. Circular 164/20/2021 (18% on mining-rights services) is cited correctly (it is in `circulars.csv`).
- The s.74 paragraphs (9, 9.2) give facts, not only the statutory words: non-registration, no returns, no reply to the document-call letter of 28.11.2025 (Explanation 2, second limb), and detection only through external audit data.
- s.74(5) (15%) and s.74(8) (25% within 30 days) are quoted correctly.
- The penalty is quantified ("equivalent to" the tax).
- A relied-upon documents paragraph exists.

**Defects, most likely to be quashed first**
1. **Valuation appears inverted, and the forward-charge demand is grossly overstated (E6, B3).**
   - Taxable value = dispatch quantity in square metres × 12.5 × average sale price per tonne.
   - The SCN says the 12.5 factor comes from the seigniorage notification, which fixes a fee per square metre and a fee per tonne in the ratio 8:100. Royalty in the SCN's own table is exactly the square-metre quantity × the per-square-metre fee, which confirms that ratio.
   - On that ratio, one square metre corresponds to 1/12.5 of a tonne. Square metres must be **divided** by 12.5 to get tonnes, not multiplied. That matches the physical weight of a slab of usual thickness (about 0.08 t/m²).
   - Multiplying instead inflates the value by **12.5² ≈ 156 times** for FY 2020-21 and 2021-22.
   - Cross-check: on the SCN's figures, royalty is about 0.2% of the "sales". On the corrected figures it is about a third, which is plausible for a minor mineral.

   On the corrected conversion, each year's turnover would fall **below the s.22 registration threshold**. That would remove the forward-charge issue and the s.122(1)(xi) penalty altogether. The RCM issue would remain, and s.24(iii) would require registration.

   **Before adjudication:** obtain the conversion basis in writing from the Mines and Geology department, and the noticee's actual sale invoices or transit permits. If the inversion is confirmed, drop or re-quantify. An order cannot enhance (s.75(7)). If a correction is needed downward, the order can do it. An upward correction is impossible.
2. **Valuation by "average sale price" with no statutory basis (E6, H8).** s.15 requires transaction value. An estimate needs either s.63 best judgment or the valuation rules, with reasons. The SCN invokes neither and does not explain why actual prices could not be found.
3. **Three financial years in one SCN (B7, check 18).** The tax table that drives the demand also **swaps the years**: FY 2020-21's value is labelled 2021-22 and the reverse. That matters because the years have different limitation dates. Issue separate year-wise SCNs (safer practice), or at least year-wise tables and limitation statements. *RR Metal Industries* (Telangana, interim) stayed a composite s.74 SCN. *Note 14.10.2026: a Telangana DB has since upheld a composite multi-year s.74 SCN and order (Goldstone Infra, 27.01.2026), so the multi-year form is not itself unlawful in Telangana; the swapped years and missing year-wise limitation remain the real defect.*
4. **Overlapping penalties (E7, H7, s.75(13)).**
   - s.74(1) penalty equal to tax.
   - "s.74(1) read with s.122(2)(b)" in paragraphs 9.2 and 10.
   - s.122(1)(xi), also "equivalent to the amounts demanded at (ii) and (iv)".

   s.75(13) bars a second penalty for the same act once a s.74 penalty is imposed. s.122(1)(xi) for non-registration can stand only as a separate act. Even then, its measure is the higher of Rs 10,000 or the tax evaded, which here is the same tax. Keep s.74(1) only, or s.122(1)(xi) at Rs 10,000 with reasons why it is a separate act.
5. **s.74 grounds are thin for Issue 2 and qualified throughout (B4, G.R. Infra, check 14).**
   - The SCN says the facts "if established, … prima facie constitute suppression".
   - Royalty was paid to the State Government. The facts were therefore on a government record.
   - Whether royalty, DMF and SMET are consideration for a taxable supply is still litigated: an SC notice on an SLP against a Rajasthan HC ruling, and a Telangana HC writ on GST on royalty, DMF and SMET at notice stage since Nov 2025 (case-law files 04 and 09).
   - A pure interpretation dispute is not suppression (*G.R. Infra*, SC 19.08.2026). For the forward-charge issue, non-registration plus non-reply to a written call (Explanation 2) is a better foundation, but intent to evade still needs a stated fact.

   **Fix in the order:** a separate finding on each ingredient. Record the pending royalty litigation (E5, check 28 of 1B).
6. **Third-party audit material not supplied (B5, *Safecon*).** The relied-upon documents are "letter from DGA" and the document-call letter. The mining-department data actually used (lease, dispatches, average sale price, royalty statements, conversion basis) is neither listed nor said to be enclosed. Supply it or fix an inspection date.
7. **Limitation not computed in the SCN, and the margin is thin (C2, check 24).** The SCN only says "subject to the applicable limitation". See the computation below.
8. **No DIN, no printed date; service by registered post to an unregistered person (C1).** A non-portal communication without a DIN is the classic Circular 128/47/2019 challenge. Give the DIN, a dated acknowledgement through the range (as directed in the copy list), and e-mail if one is known.
9. **Hearing (D1).** Paragraph 15 refers to "the appointed day", but no day is appointed.
10. **Citation and clerical slips.**
    - The operative clause cites **rule 49** (bill of supply). It should be rule 46, or no rule.
    - s.9(3) is quoted under the number "(1)".
    - Paragraph 5.7 cross-refers to "Para 4.12", which does not exist.
    - The RCM tax in words is one rupee less than in figures.
    - Paragraph 11 says the noticee "has been given opportunity … under s.74(5)", but only a document-call letter was sent, not a DRC-01A ascertainment. Delete or support it.
    - Penalty clause (vi) invokes the IGST Act, though no IGST is involved.
11. **Classification asserted, not reasoned (H8, check 40).** The SCN gives a heading, the Schedule I entry and the rate, but no GRI step and no product evidence (whether the slabs are worked or polished, which would move them to a higher-rate heading). The rate schedule is not in the project library, so the entry number was not verified here.
12. **Proper officer (A2).** The CGST demand sits just under the upper edge of the Assistant Commissioner's monetary band under Circular 31/05/2018 (about 99% of it). Record the band and the assignment. A State-overlap check is essential for an unregistered person (A1): the State may also have acted on the same audit data.

**Limitation (year-wise, s.74).**

| FY | GSTR-9 due date | s.74(10) order deadline | Latest SCN (s.74(2)) | SCN signed | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020-21 | 28.02.2022 (rule 80(1A)) | 28.02.2027 | 28.08.2026 | 23.08.2026 | In time, **5 days to spare** |
| 2021-22 | 31.12.2022 | 31.12.2027 | 30.06.2027 | 23.08.2026 | In time |
| 2022-23 | 31.12.2023 | 31.12.2028 | 30.06.2028 | 23.08.2026 | In time (s.73 was also still open) |

If s.74 fails, s.75(2) re-determination under s.73 is barred for FY 2020-21 and 2021-22 because their s.73(10) dates have passed. So the s.74 finding is decisive for those years. The FY 2020-21 order must issue by 28.02.2027. No Covid-era extension (13/2022, 9/2023, 56/2023) applies to these years.

**Quantification.** Tax is given year-wise and in total. Interest is unquantified, with no year-wise start dates. The penalty is quantified but duplicated (above).

**Arithmetic spot-checked.**
- The SCN's own products are internally consistent: quantity × 12.5 × price, and 2.5% CGST.
- Totals add up.
- Royalty + DMF + SMET + permit fee per year adds up, and 18% of each year is exact after rounding.
- The **method**, not the multiplication, is the problem (defect 1).
- The year labels are swapped in the tax table.

**Relied-upon documents:** two listed; the key mining data is missing. **Hearing:** none fixed. **Signature:** digitally signed. **DIN:** none. **Service:** registered post through the range.

## S10: audit SCN, FY 2020-21 to 2024-25, issued by an Audit Commissionerate officer for adjudication by this Division

**Issues and period.**
- (i) GSTR-1 late fee (one return, FY 2024-25).
- (ii) Interest on three late GSTR-3B returns (FY 2022-23 to 2024-25).
- (iii) Rate misclassification of boiler ash, 5% vs 18% (FY 2020-21 to 2022-23).
- (iv) RCM on label-registration fees and other licence and registration fees paid to government (FY 2022-23 and 2023-24).
- (v) The same as (iv) for FY 2024-25.

**Sections invoked.** s.47; s.50(1); s.74 with s.122(2)(b) for (iii); s.73 with s.122(2)(a) for (iv); s.74A(1) with s.74A(5)(i) for (v). The section choice per year is **right in principle**: s.73/74 up to FY 2023-24, s.74A from FY 2024-25. But all of them sit in one notice. **Score 3.5/10.** The s.74A treatment and the audit trail are good. The notice is headed "DRAFT", misdescribes the business, invokes s.74 on a disclosed rate dispute and overstates the misclassification demand.

**Strengths worth keeping**
- Correct split: s.74A for FY 2024-25 with the s.74A(5)(i) penalty, and the 60-day no-penalty option under **s.74A(8)(ii)**. s.73(8) and s.74(8) options are each explained "in respect of paras where…". This is the only SCN in the set that gets the post-2024 regime right.
- Paragraph 10.1 records why DRC-01A was not issued: rule 142(1A) is optional after Notification 79/2020-CT. It also cites the spot memo and the Final Audit Report, with dates and DINs (A3, check 4).
- The interest computation on late GSTR-3B is exact: 18% p.a. × days × cash-paid tax, all three months and all heads recomputed. It applies the s.50(1) proviso correctly (cash portion only).
- Circular 121/40/2019 is engaged on the liquor-licence point, rather than ignored.

**Defects, most likely to be quashed first**
1. **The notice is headed "DRAFT SHOW CAUSE NOTICE" (B1, B3).** It is signed, numbered and e-mailed, but its own title says it is a draft. A noticee will argue that no SCN under s.73/74/74A(1) was ever issued, only a draft, and that the limitation clock was not stopped. This is fatal to FY 2020-21: its s.74(2) date was 28.08.2026, and a corrected notice now would be late. **Action:** check what was uploaded as DRC-01 and when. If a final SCN was issued on or before 28.08.2026, record its number and date on file and in the order. **Template fix:** never send a document titled "draft" to a taxpayer. If the Audit Commissionerate's practice is to send the draft to the adjudicating authority, the version served must be re-titled and re-signed.
2. **Wrong description of the taxpayer's business (B3).** Paragraph 1 says "Trading of solar panels and products" under headings 2203, 1701 and 1901. Paragraph 6 says the noticee manufactures a product of heading 2203. That is alcoholic liquor for human consumption, outside GST under s.9(1). This is a template carry-over. It undermines the "facts found" foundation (*Brindavan Beverages*, SC; *AI Adil Traders*, Telangana).
3. **s.74 invoked for a rate dispute that was disclosed in returns, on boilerplate grounds (B4, H8, checks 14 and 15).**
   - Paragraph 9 says the "contraventions as detailed in Para 3, 4 and 5" show suppression. Para 3 is the statutory provisions, para 4 is late fee and para 5 is interest. The misclassification is para 6, so **the s.74 ground does not point at the s.74 issue at all**.
   - The grounds are the standard "came to knowledge only in audit, not disclosed" text.
   - The noticee declared the HSN and the 5% rate in its own returns.
   - The noticee itself moved to the 18% heading from FY 2023-24, which looks more like a bona fide change than intent.

**Defects, most likely to be quashed first**

A pre-SCN letter has already come back "No such person". No mode of service is stated. 2. **s.74 wording in a penalty-only notice (B4, E7).**

3. **Clause 14(iii) asks that ITC "passed on" be held irregular under s.16(2) (B3, E1).** s.16(2) governs the recipient's entitlement. 4. **Paragraph 10 is cut off on the page.** The penalty amounts and part of the wording run past the right margin and do not print. The operative paragraph 14 repeats them correctly, but the body is illegible at that point. Check the layout before signing.
5. **Unexplained figures (B3).**

7. **Paragraph 17 "reserves the right to add, amend, modify, delete" (E1, D4).** Any change after issue needs a corrigendum or supplementary SCN and a fresh opportunity. Remove the clause. It invites a s.75(7) argument.
8. **Relied-upon documents incomplete (B5).** The DGARM report (the trigger), the returned-letter envelope and the electricity-board enquiry are not listed. List and enclose them.

### Drafting rules these SCNs suggest

1. **Front page block:** SCN number, **printed date**, DIN or RFN (mandatory: without one the notice is invalid, *Gudla Vanaja*, Telangana HC Full Bench, 30.09.2026), the section and sub-section, the period (one FY), the proper-officer authority (notification or circular and monetary band), and the State-overlap check sentence (A1).
2. **Limitation box on page 1:**
   - due date of the annual return;
   - s.73(10)/74(10) date, or for s.74A the 42-month date;
   - latest SCN date under s.73(2)/74(2);
   - this notice's date.

   Office rule: an s.73 SCN goes out **at least one month** before the s.73(2) date. An s.74 SCN goes out at least one month before the s.74(2) date. S8, S9 and S11 would all have failed a one-week slip.
3. **One FY per SCN** (safer practice, not a legal requirement in Telangana: *Goldstone Infra*, TG HC DB, 27.01.2026, upheld a composite s.74 SCN). If an investigation spans years, issue parallel SCNs on the same day. Never combine s.73, s.74 and s.74A in one notice.
4. **ITC mismatch, FY 2021-22 (from 01.01.2022) onward:** cite s.16(2)(aa) and rule 36(4) against **GSTR-2B**. Use s.16(2)(c) only with a verified supplier default (supplier's GSTR-3B and ledger), naming the supplier. Before 01.01.2022, invite Circular 183/193 certificates.
5. **Interest paragraph:** the section (s.50(1) or s.50(3)), the start date per period, the rate, an amount up to the SCN date, and for s.50(3) a month-wise credit-ledger table (rule 88B(3)). Undisputed interest on self-assessed tax goes through DRC-01D, not an SCN.
6. **Penalty paragraph:** one clause per act and its amount: s.73(9), s.74(1), s.74A(5)(i) or (ii), or s.122(1)(x). Do not write "s.73 read with s.122(2)(a)". Check s.75(13) before adding any s.122 or s.125 penalty.
7. **s.74 / s.74A(5)(ii) paragraph:** pick one ingredient (fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression). State the specific facts, the evidence and the relied-upon document references. Say why intent to evade follows. Never use it for a rate, classification or interpretation dispute disclosed in returns. Cross-check that the paragraph refers to the right issue numbers.
8. **Payments before the SCN:** apply s.73(5) to (7) and s.74(5) to (7). Show the payment head by head, check interest, and propose only the shortfall. Say "proposed to be appropriated", never "I appropriate".
9. **Rate and valuation issues:** show the GRI steps and product evidence; give credit for tax already paid (demand the differential only); state the valuation method and its legal basis (s.15, the valuation rules or s.63); show every conversion factor with its source and a sanity check against an independent figure (e.g. royalty-to-value ratio).
10. **RCM on government services:** for each fee, state the statute charging it, the service, the RCM entry, and why each exemption entry (and Circular 121/40/2019 for liquor) does not apply.
11. **Hearing:** a fixed date, time, venue or video link, and the officer's name. Delete the "presumed not to desire a hearing" sentence from every template.
12. **Relied-upon documents annexure:** every document used, marked "enclosed" or "available for inspection on [date]". This includes third-party data (audit, DGARM, mining or other departments' records).
13. **Service paragraph:** the portal (RFN), the registered e-mail from an official address, and speed post. Where the address is doubtful or the firm is non-existent, also affixing or publication under s.169(1)(e) and (f).
14. **Clean-up of template text:**
    - remove s.42 and s.43 citations;
    - use the current s.41 and s.16(4) text;
    - rule 61 refers to GSTR-3B;
    - delete "Finance Act";
    - delete "reserves the right to amend";
    - late fee for GSTR-1 after June 2021 is under Notification 20/2021-CT;
    - check the due dates against extension notifications (e.g. 01/2025-CT, 02/2025-CT) before charging late fee or interest;
    - never send a document headed "DRAFT", and never use private e-mail addresses.
15. **Pre-sign check:** recompute each table, compare words with figures, check the year labels, and print-preview so that no paragraph runs off the page.
