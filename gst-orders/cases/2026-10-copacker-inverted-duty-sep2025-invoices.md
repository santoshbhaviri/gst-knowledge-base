# Co-packer's inverted duty refund for September: invoice analysis across GST 2.0

Prepared 13.10.2026 from 17 sample invoices supplied by the officer (2 outward invoices to principals, 1 outward job work invoice, 14 inward invoices). The applicant, its GSTIN and its amounts are not named here. Supplier and customer names are used only to identify the goods.

Read with: [2026-10-inverted-duty-sep2025-gst2-views.md](2026-10-inverted-duty-sep2025-gst2-views.md) (whole-month rule and formula defects) and [../refund-order-learnings.md](../refund-order-learnings.md).

## 0. Conclusion first

**The applicant is eligible for an inverted duty refund under section 54(3)(ii) for September 2025, but only partly, and not on the whole of its business.**

1. **From 22.09.2025** the packed Boost and Horlicks it sells to HUL (HSN 1901 90 90) fell from 18% to 5%. Its main packing inputs (plastic jars, caps and laminates, Chapter 39) stayed at 18%. That is a genuine inverted duty structure: different goods, taxed at different rates at the same point of time.
2. **From 1 to 21 September 2025** the same HUL supplies were taxed at 18%, and so were all their inputs. There was no inversion in those three weeks. Those sales go into Adjusted Total Turnover but **not** into "turnover of inverted rated supply".
3. **The job work for Mayora** (Choki Choki, SAC 998816) is at 5% before and after 22.09.2025. It is an inverted-rated supply for the whole month, if the applicant uses 18% inputs for it.
4. **The co-packing service to Nestle** is invoiced at 18%. It is not an inverted-rated supply and stays only in Adjusted Total Turnover.
5. **Credit on bulk Boost/Horlicks powder bought from HUL at 18% (before 22.09.2025) and sold in packs at 5% after 22.09.2025 is not refundable.** Input and output are the same goods (1901 90 90); the credit piled up only because the rate fell over time. Circular 135/05/2020 para 3.2, as substituted by Circular 173/05/2022, excludes exactly this. Remove that credit from Net ITC. It is not lost: it stays in the ledger for paying output tax (for example, the 18% tax on the Nestle service).

So the refund is: **whole-month formula, numerator limited to the 5% supplies, Net ITC without the 18% bulk powder credit, input services and capital goods.** The remaining checks are in section 6.

**A point to settle first.** The outward invoices supplied are for **September 2026** (dated 02.09.2026, 30.09.2026, and 13.10.2026 for the September 2026 co-packing), and 13 of the 14 inward invoices are from February to October 2026. Only the Taurus laminate invoice (06.08.2025) is from 2025. These samples therefore show post-GST 2.0 rates only. If the claim is really for September 2025, ask for the September 2025 outward invoices, especially those of 1 to 21 September. If the claim is actually for September 2026, see section 7: the whole month is then post-GST 2.0 and the bulk powder exclusion falls away.

## 4. Is there an inverted duty structure? Period by period

Section 54(3)(ii) allows refund where "the credit has accumulated on account of rate of tax on inputs being higher than the rate of tax on output supplies (other than nil rated or fully exempt supplies)", except for goods or services notified by the Council. None of these outputs is in Notification 5/2017-CT(Rate) (goods) or Notification 15/2017-CT(Rate) (services).

| Line | 1–21 September 2025 | 22–30 September 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| A (HUL goods) | Output 18%; inputs 18% (bulk powder, plastics, cartons). **No inversion.** | Output 5%; bulk powder 5%, cartons 5%, holograms 5%, but plastic jars, caps and laminates 18%. **Inverted.** |
| B (Nestle service) | Output 18%. **Not inverted.** | Output 18%. **Not inverted.** |
| C (Mayora job work) | Output 5%; any 18% inputs used. **Inverted.** | Same. **Inverted.** |

## 5. How the refund must be worked out for September 2025

The September 2025 tax period is one month, and rule 89(5) uses the whole month's figures (this was settled in the earlier case note, section 1). The formula:

> Maximum refund = (Turnover of inverted rated supply × Net ITC ÷ Adjusted Total Turnover) − tax payable on that inverted rated supply

Applied to this applicant:

1. **Turnover of inverted rated supply** = only the 5% supplies of September 2025:
   - line A sales to HUL invoiced at 5% on or after 22.09.2025, and
   - line C job work for Mayora for the whole month.
   - **Leave out** line A sales of 1–21 September at 18% and line B (Nestle) at 18%.
2. **Tax payable on inverted rated supply** = 5% of item 1 (as shown in GSTR-1 Table 12 by HSN and rate).
3. **Net ITC** = ITC on inputs (goods) availed for the whole of September 2025, less:
   - capital goods and input services (rule 89(5) Explanation (a); upheld in *VKC Footsteps*, Supreme Court, 13.09.2021). The Finance Act 2026 change to section 54(3) is not yet in force, so this exclusion still applies;
   - blocked credit under section 17(5);
   - **ITC on bulk Boost/Horlicks powder (1901) bought at 18%** before 22.09.2025 that went into packs sold at 5% after 22.09.2025. Under Circular 135/05/2020 para 3.2 (as substituted by Circular 173/05/2022), where input and output are the same goods taxed at different rates at different points in time, the accumulation is not refundable. Bulk malt powder and the same powder in retail packs are the same goods under the same tariff item. Circular 173 para 3.3 (concessional rate at the same time) does not help here: this is a rate change over time, not a concession.
     - Karnataka HC in *South Indian Oil Corp.* (12.12.2025) read para 3.2 as applying only where credit built up because the rate on the same goods changed over time. That is exactly this case, so the exclusion is consistent with that judgment. Telangana HC in *Micro Systems* (2022) held Circular 173 retrospective; it does not stop para 3.2 applying here. (Both citations are from secondary sources in the case law library and are not yet verified against the judgments.)
     - The exclusion does **not** extend to cartons, CLDs or plastic packing bought at 18% before 22.09.2025: those are different goods from the output, so their credit is structural inversion and refundable.
4. **Adjusted Total Turnover** = the whole month's turnover of all three lines (both rates), excluding exempt supplies, from GSTR-3B Table 3.1(a)/(b) and matching GSTR-1.
5. **Refund payable** = the least of: the formula amount, the credit ledger balance at the end of September 2025 after filing GSTR-3B, and the balance at the time of filing (Circular 125/44/2019 para 37). Never more than the amount claimed and debited (rule 89(3)).

Why the formula is fair here even though inputs before and after 22.09 are pooled: Net ITC is multiplied by the share of the month's turnover that is at 5%. Credit on inputs used for the 18% supplies (Nestle, and HUL sales before 22.09) is apportioned out by the formula itself.

## 7. If the claim is actually for September 2026

The whole month is then after GST 2.0:
- Lines A and C (5%) are inverted for the whole month; line B (18%) is not.
- The bulk powder is bought at 5% and sold at 5%, so the Circular 135 para 3.2 problem does not arise; its credit simply stays in Net ITC.
- The formula, the input services and capital goods exclusion, and the checks in section 6 (items 4 to 10) apply in the same way.
- The applicant is **eligible**; the amount follows the formula.
