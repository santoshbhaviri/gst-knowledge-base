# Inverted duty refund, September 2025: eligible amount and whether to reject

Prepared 13.10.2026 after reading the 31-page case file in full: revised computation, adjournment request, reply to the RFD-08, the RFD-08 (two versions), GSTR-3B, GSTR-1, the office working sheet and the RFD-01. This is the same case as [2026-10-inverted-duty-sep2025-gst2-views.md](2026-10-inverted-duty-sep2025-gst2-views.md); the business and rates are in [2026-10-copacker-inverted-duty-sep2025-invoices.md](2026-10-copacker-inverted-duty-sep2025-invoices.md).

Following the project rule, the applicant is not named and no amounts are copied here. The figures are in the case file and in the thread reply of 13.10.2026.

## 1. Answer

- **Do not reject.** A rejection on the RFD-08 grounds would not survive appeal.
- **Sanction the full amount claimed in the RFD-01.** On consistent whole-month figures the rule 89(5) formula gives about three times the claim.
- **Do not sanction the higher revised amount.** Under rule 89(3) only the amount claimed and debited can be refunded. The difference needs a fresh RFD-01 within limitation.

## 2. Why the notice's negative figure is wrong

The RFD-08 took turnover of inverted rated supply and Adjusted Total Turnover for the **whole month** (from GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B), but Net ITC and ITC on inputs and input services from Annexure-B, which covered **only 22–30 September**. The office working sheet does the same. Mixing a whole-month denominator with part-month credit produces the negative result. The notice itself relies on rule 89(4)(F) ("relevant period" = the period of the claim), and applying that rule to the credit figures too removes the negative.

## 4. Recomputation

- The section 17(5) amounts in the notice (part goods, part services) are deducted from inputs and from input services respectively. The formula amount falls by only a few hundred rupees.
- If the "not considered" credit is also deducted, the formula amount barely moves.
- Least of three (Circular 125/44/2019 para 37): the formula amount, the ledger balance at the end of September 2025, and the balance at filing. Both balances are far above the claim, so the claim is the limit.

## 5. The one open point: bulk malt powder (Circular 135 para 3.2)

Credit on bulk Boost/Horlicks powder bought at 18% before 22.09.2025 and sold in packs at 5% after it is not refundable: input and output are the same goods, and the credit built up only because the rate fell. The Net ITC in the reply includes all input credit for the month.

- **Break-even:** the full claim is still covered unless this excluded powder credit exceeds about **39%** of the month's input-goods credit. (Removing it from both Net ITC and "ITC on inputs and input services" keeps the formula at or above the claim up to that point.)
- **Action:** ask for (a) the total ITC on 1901 bulk powder invoiced at 18% in September 2025, and (b) closing stock of that powder on 21.09.2025. If (a) is below the break-even, there is nothing more to do. If it is above, exclude only the 18% powder that was sold at 5% (from (b)), recompute, and sanction the lower of the formula amount and the claim. Reject only that difference under rule 92(3), with PMT-03 re-credit.

## 5A. "Same HSN" as a ground, and the officer's four options (added 13.10.2026)

**Rates.** Bulk powder (1901 90 90): 18% up to 21.09.2025, 5% from 22.09.2025 (the HUL bulk invoice of 26.03.2026 is at 5%). Packed output (1901 90 90): 18% up to 21.09.2025, 5% from 22.09.2025. At any one moment the bulk powder and the output carry the **same** rate; the only difference is over time.

**Is "same HSN" a ground to reject the whole claim? No, on its own it is not.**
- Section 54(3)(ii) asks why the credit accumulated. Circular 135 para 3.2 (as substituted by Circular 173) bars refund only of the accumulation caused by the *same* goods being taxed differently at different points of time.
- That describes the **bulk powder** credit exactly. That part is not refundable.
- It does not describe the **plastic jars, caps, laminates and pouches** (3920/3923). They are different goods, taxed at 18% at the same time that the output is taxed at 5% (from 22.09.2025). That is a genuine structural inversion, and that part is refundable.
- Telangana HC (*Micro Systems*, 2022, binding here) and Karnataka HC (*South Indian Oil Corp.*, 12.12.2025) both require a finding on why the credit actually accumulated, not a rejection only because input and output share an HSN. (Secondary sources; verify before citing.)
- **Cartons and shippers (4819)** bought at 18% before 22.09.2025: **do not exclude** (corrected 13.10.2026). They are different goods from the output, so para 3.2, which needs input and output to be the same, does not reach them. Their credit stays in Net ITC.

**The four options**
1. **Ask them to withdraw and file afresh: not available.** Rule 90(5) allows withdrawal in RFD-01W only *before* an RFD-04, RFD-05, RFD-06, RFD-07 **or RFD-08** is issued. The RFD-08 was issued on 22.09.2026, so the portal will not accept a withdrawal. The application has to be decided by an RFD-06.
2. **Ask them to use the credit and claim from October: same problem.** It needs a withdrawal. And from October 2025 onwards the claim is clean anyway: bulk powder 5% and output 5% (neutral), plastics 18% and output 5% (refundable). That does not need persuasion.
3. **Reject in full: not sustainable.** The plastic-packing inversion is real and refundable. A full rejection on "same HSN" would be set aside on appeal, with interest under section 56.
4. **Sanction with a reasoned exclusion: recommended.**
   - Exclude from Net ITC (and from "ITC on inputs and input services") only the credit on bulk 1901 powder invoiced at 18%, under Circular 135 para 3.2.
   - Recompute on the remaining credit, which is mainly plastic packing.
   - Sanction the lower of that amount and the claim. Reject any balance under rule 92(3) with PMT-03 re-credit.
   - Break-even: the full claim survives if the excluded credit stays under about 39% of the month's input-goods credit.

**What to ask for before the hearing on 21.10.2026:** an invoice-wise split of the September 2025 input-goods credit (reply Annexure 5) into (a) 1901 bulk powder at 18%, (b) plastics 3920/3923, (c) cartons 4819, and (d) others. If they do not give it, the unverified part can be refused under rule 92(3). The burden of proving the refundable accumulation is on the applicant. Pass the order by 23.10.2026.

## 6. Procedure points for the order

4. The differential can be claimed by a fresh RFD-01 for September 2025 within two years of the GSTR-3B due date (Explanation 2(e) to section 54).
5. Sixty days from filing (24.08.2026) end on **23.10.2026**. Pass the order by then, or interest under section 56 runs.
