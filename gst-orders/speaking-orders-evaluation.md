## The overall picture

### What these orders do better than the orders from other divisions
1. **A real verification section.** Most orders have tables comparing GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, RFD-01, Annexure-B and ICEGATE or bank data for each figure in the formula. No other division's order showed its sources so clearly.
2. **No order rejects a claim that should have been allowed.** Every disallowance is small, specific and accepted by the taxpayer. None has the kind of appeal risk that Orders C, H and I carry.
3. **The inversion is shown.** Every inverted-duty order has an input-against-output table with HSN and rates. The orders from your earlier Sangareddy batch did not.
4. **Formal rejection with PMT-03.** Orders 68, 69, 71 and 78 reject the balance in the operative ORDER and direct PMT-03 re-credit on undertaking. Only one of the 17 earlier orders did this.
5. **Speed.** Every order was passed in 15 to 46 days, well inside the 60 days of s.54(7).
6. **Procedural record.** RFD-08 reference numbers and dates, the reply, the hearing or its written waiver, the date of the range's no-dues e-mail, the ledger debit reference, official e-mail and copy to Review are all recorded.
7. **Earlier template errors are gone.** There is no s.54(8)(a) miscitation in the inverted-duty orders, no export-only findings in non-export orders, and no personal e-mail addresses.
8. **SO 84 handles provisional refund properly.** It refers to the RFD-04 and RFD-05 and adjusts them in a net-payable table. No order in either set did this better.
9. **Some findings show real judgment:**
   - deemed exports taken out of inverted turnover (SO 78);
   - cancelled registration held not to bar a pre-deposit refund (SO 82);
   - earlier-period invoices allowed after a duplication check, in line with Circular 197 (SO 84);
   - the source of a mistaken cash deposit traced (SO 74).

### The ten improvements that matter most (highest legal or money risk first)
1. **Pay interest under s.115 on pre-deposit refunds (SO 76, 82, 91, 92).**
   - Where a pre-deposit is refunded because the appeal succeeded, interest at the s.56 rate (6%) is payable "from the date of payment of the amount till the date of refund". The taxpayer does not have to ask for it.
   - Approximate interest up to each order date: SO 92 ₹73,000, SO 82 ₹26,000, SO 91 ₹23,000, SO 76 ₹8,000.
   - It keeps running until payment. If these orders are not yet final, a supplementary order for interest would avoid four certain appeals.
2. **Deal with provisional refund in every zero-rated order and every inverted-duty order filed on or after 01.10.2025.**
   - Fifteen of the 18 orders are silent on it. Only SO 84 handles it, and the other two (the cash-ledger and pre-deposit orders) are outside s.54(6).
   - Rule 91(2) (Notification 13/2025-CT) and Instruction 06/2025-GST para 5.1 require 90% in RFD-04 within 7 days, or reasons recorded.
   - Add a standard paragraph: "Provisional refund of ₹… was sanctioned in RFD-04 dated … and is adjusted below", or "Provisional refund was not granted because …".
3. **Drop the "highest Adjusted Total Turnover / lowest zero-rated or inverted turnover" rule (SO 70, 80, 83, 84, 87).**
   - Neither rule 89(4), rule 89(5) nor Circular 125 says this. It always reduces the refund, and an appellate authority will notice.
   - In SO 80 it is most of the disallowance, and it was never put to the taxpayer as a ground.
   - In SO 83, 84 and 87 the order itself works out the correct figure and then sets it aside.
   - For services, rule 89(4)(E) requires the same realised value inside Adjusted Total Turnover as in the numerator.
   - **Use instead:** one reconciled source per component, the same for numerator and denominator, with any gap explained.
4. **Explain the Part B denominator in every inverted-duty order (68, 69, 72, 73, 79).**
   - "ITC availed on inputs and input services" is taken from Annexure-B or GSTR-3B, and it is higher than Net ITC. At the same time the order says no input services were claimed.
   - If the gap is input services, it is correct. If it is capital goods or blocked credit, the denominator is overstated and the refund is too high. In SO 72 that could be up to ₹92,000.
   - **Add one line:** ITC split into inputs, input services, capital goods and blocked credit, reconciled to GSTR-3B.
5. **Make the sanction figure consistent throughout the order (SO 79, 80).**
   - SO 80 shows ₹29,08,272 (computed), ₹29,08,542 (sum of tax heads) and ₹29,08,007 (ORDER, in figures and words). It also gives the rejection as both ₹24,859 and ₹24,589. RFD-05 cannot safely issue on this; rectify it under s.161.
   - SO 79 sanctions ₹4,71,044 against a computed maximum of ₹4,71,042.
   - **Before signing,** check that the computed figure, the head-wise split, the ORDER in figures and the ORDER in words all agree.
6. **Reconcile every rejection to the ITC behind it (SO 68, 69, 70, 80, 83).**
   - Show "ITC excluded ₹x, refund effect ₹y".
   - In SO 68, ₹30,319 of blocked ITC explains only about ₹11,000 of the ₹38,647 rejected. In SO 69, ₹36,618 explains only about ₹4,300 of ₹50,092. The rest comes from denominator changes that are never stated.
   - In SO 70 and 83, items found in Annexure-B were deducted from the lower RFD-01 Net ITC, so they may have been deducted twice.
7. **Give the 15 days, or record that the taxpayer waived them (SO 68, 69, 71, 78, 80).**
   - Rule 92(3) gives 15 days to reply to RFD-08. Several orders fixed the hearing for the next day, or passed the order a day after the notice.
   - The taxpayers accepted, so there is no harm this time. Record the waiver of the 15-day period expressly, or leave the full period.
8. **Cite the right provisions:**
   - **s.54(8):** clause (b) for inverted duty and SEZ under LUT, (a) for exports, (e) for pre-deposit refunds. Every order dismisses unjust enrichment in one line with no clause.
   - **Relevant date:**
     - clause (e) for inverted duty (the due date of the return, not the date filed);
     - (ba) for SEZ;
     - (a) for export of goods (the EGM date, not the shipping-bill date);
     - (c) for services;
     - (d) for pre-deposit refunds (the date of communication of the appellate order; record the actual date of receipt).
   - **Not s.54(2).** SO 76, 82, 91 and 92 cite it for the relevant date; it is the UN-bodies provision.
   - **Not "s.54(14)(ba)"** (SO 70 and 87).
   - **Cash ledger:** s.49(6) read with s.54(1). On limitation, Circular 166/22/2021 (SO 74).
   - **Rejection and re-credit:** rejection under rule 92(3), re-credit under rule 93(2).
   - **Remove** the "in accordance with s.16(3) of the IGST Act" phrase from the rule 89(5) paragraph. It is zero-rated wording.
9. **Replace the appeal preamble.** All 18 orders say "10% of the duty demanded", "filed in duplicate" and "court fee/stamp duty". The correct position:
   - APL-01 is filed electronically (rule 108);
   - pre-deposit is the admitted amount plus 10% of the tax in dispute, capped at ₹20 crore, or 10% of the penalty in a penalty-only order (s.107(6));
   - there is no court fee.
10. **Clean the documents list.** Remove the s.42(2) undertaking, GSTR-2A copies and "invoices not in GSTR-2A" (all dropped by Circular 197 para 2). Remove the CA certificate in ITC refunds (proviso to rule 89(2)(m)). Remove Statement 3 under rule 89(2)(h) in SEZ, export and pre-deposit orders. Remove the s.16(2)(c) undertaking in pre-deposit orders.

### Smaller points that recur
- **Dates:**
  - order and signature dated differently (SO 71, 72, 73, 87);
  - header date blank (SO 84, 91, 92);
  - wrong year (SO 74 says 2025; SO 69 says the period ends March 2026).
- **Leftover sentences:**
  - "the amount claimed as refund may be allowed" in orders that reject part of the claim;
  - "sanctioned under RFD 06 raised";
  - "lowest corroborated value" or "minimum of three" when the figures are identical or only two were compared.
- **Typing and figures:**
  - paise in SO 84 and 89 (s.170);
  - "Rs. Rs.";
  - "/-/-";
  - "liable to sanctioned";
  - GSTIN printed in place of ARN (SO 70).
- **Unneeded names:** supplier trade names and GSTINs printed in the inversion tables.
- **Consistency across months:** the same taxpayer's four consecutive months (SO 73, 78, 79, 80) were checked differently. Deemed exports were checked only in January, blocked credit only in March, and the turnover rule changed in March. One taxpayer's run of monthly claims should get one checklist.
- **Inversion tables:** they list suppliers rather than rates. A rate-wise ITC summary (18%, 12%, 5%) shows the inversion more clearly. SO 73 lists a 5% input that creates no inversion.

    - check whether goods were bought at the 0.1% concessional rate (Notification 40/2017-CT(R) and 41/2017-IT(R));
    - comment on the 9.4% ratio of ITC to export value.
  - **Export findings missing:** LUT, drawback and the 1.5× cap.
  - **No provisional refund.**
  - **Template sentences that do not fit:** "excluding 17(5) and capital goods", "lowest corroborated value" and "goods + services", when none of these apply.
  - **Paise** in the turnover figures.

### SO 91 and SO 92: Pre-deposit refunds (FY 2019-20 and FY 2018-19 demands): **6.5 each** (1.5, 1.5, 1.5, 1.5, 0.5)
- **Good:**
  - clear chain of adjudication order, DRC-07, APL-01 and payment, debit reference, order-in-appeal, and demand set aside;
  - 10% pre-deposit verified and correctly rounded by tax head;
  - limitation tested with real dates;
  - passed in 16 days.
- **Fix:**
  - **s.115 interest:** about ₹73,000 in SO 92 and ₹23,000 in SO 91 to the order date.
  - **Relevant date:** clause (d), not s.54(2).
  - **Pre-deposit provision:** in a penalty-only order, cite the proviso to s.107(6) (10% of the penalty).
  - **Unjust enrichment:** the reason is that a pre-deposit is not tax passed on (s.54(8)(e)), not "a compliance obligation".
  - **Department's appeal:** record whether it was considered, and whether s.54(11) withholding was considered.
  - **Adjudication order number:** given as "113/2024" in the documents and "183/2025" in the findings.
  - **Documents list:** contains the s.16(2)(c) undertaking, which is not relevant here.
  - **Header date** blank.
  - **One order or two:** the same order-in-appeal covers both. Two orders are fine, but one would have done.
