# Template A5: Corrigendum or addendum to a show cause notice (what can and cannot be changed after issue)

> **How to use this template.** First decide, with the table in Part A, whether the change can be made by a corrigendum at all. A corrigendum corrects; it does not create a new demand. Anything that adds tax, a new ground, a new period, a new noticee or the fraud allegation is a **fresh notice**, which must itself be within the time limit in section 73(2), 74(2) or 74A(2). Part B is the corrigendum; Part C is the addendum used to serve documents that were not served with the notice. Part A is guidance: delete it, and every "Drafting note", before issue.

**Part A. Can this change be made by a corrigendum?**

**The law in four lines.** (1) Section 161 allows the authority that issued a notice to rectify an error apparent on the face of the record, on its own motion or when pointed out within three months of the notice; no rectification after six months from issue, except a purely clerical or arithmetical error from an accidental slip or omission; a rectification that adversely affects any person needs natural justice (third proviso). (2) Section 75(7): the order cannot exceed the amount in the notice, and no demand can be confirmed on grounds other than those in the notice. (3) The time limit for a notice is fixed by section 73(2), 74(2) or 74A(2); a change that is really a new notice must meet it. (4) Section 160(1): a proceeding is not invalid for a mistake, defect or omission if it is in substance and effect in conformity with the Act.

| Change wanted | Before the last date for the notice | After the last date for the notice | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typing, clerical or arithmetical slip that does not raise any amount (wrong date, para reference, name spelling, GSTIN digit, totals that do not add) | Yes | Yes (no six-month bar for a purely clerical or arithmetical error) | Corrigendum (Part B) |
| Wrong citation of a section, rule or notification, where the facts and the ground stated are unchanged (e.g. "rule 49" for "rule 46"; "13/2017-Central Tax" for "13/2017-Central Tax (Rate)") | Yes | Yes, within six months of the notice, with fresh time to reply | Corrigendum, with time to reply and a hearing date |
| **Reducing** the demand (wrong rate, double count, credit for a payment, wrong conversion factor) | Yes | Yes | Corrigendum; or drop the excess in the order |
| Change of the adjudicating authority because a statement under section 73(3), 74(3) or 74A(3) takes the amount above the issuing officer's limit | Yes | Yes | Corrigendum by the issuing authority (para 5.3(b) of Circular No. 254/11/2025-GST) |
| Interest or penalty proposed without its sub-section or clause, where the amount and the ground are already stated | Yes | Doubtful; safer to state the clause within six months, with fresh time to reply | Corrigendum, with time to reply |
| **Increasing** tax, interest or penalty; adding an issue or period; adding interest or a penalty never proposed | Only as a **fresh or supplementary notice** within the time limit | **No** | Fresh notice (A1) |
| Changing section 73 to section 74, or adding the fraud facts (Part 5 of A1) | Only as a fresh notice within the section 74(2) time limit | **No**: the facts of fraud must be in the notice itself (*G.R. Infra Projects Ratlam*, Supreme Court, 19.08.2026) | Fresh notice |
| Section 74 to section 73 (fraud dropped) | The notice need not be changed; the order decides | The order decides; tax is determined as if under section 73 only within that section's time limit | Finding in the order |
| Correcting the financial year or the noticee | Only as a fresh notice within the time limit | No | Fresh notice |
| Serving a relied-upon document that was not enclosed | Yes | Yes | Addendum (Part C), with fresh time to reply and a hearing date |

> **Drafting note (Part A).** The eleven notices reviewed in 2026 were mostly issued days before the last date, which left no room for any correction that adds tax; hence the office rule in A1 of issuing at least a month early. A notice that "reserves the right to add, amend, modify or delete" gains nothing: every change still needs a corrigendum or a fresh notice, served with time to reply. Where a valuation error is found after the time limit (for example, an inverted unit conversion), issue a corrigendum that **reduces** the demand, or drop the excess in the order; never correct upwards.

> **Drafting note (Part B).** (1) Only the authority that issued the notice (or its successor in office) issues the corrigendum. (2) Do not use it to answer the reply or to add reasons; reasons go in the order. (3) Check the six-month bar in section 161 for anything other than a clerical or arithmetical slip, counted from the date of the notice. (4) Update the DRC-01 summary or upload the corrigendum against it on the portal so that the portal figures match; record how this was done. (5) The order must refer to the corrigendum and use the corrected figures; the DRC-07 must not exceed them.

> **Drafting note (Part C).** (1) Use the addendum only for documents already listed and relied on in the notice. A document that supports a new allegation needs a fresh notice. (2) Give a fresh, full period to reply and fix the hearing after it; an order resting on a document the noticee never received is likely to be set aside. (3) If a document cannot be supplied (for example, part of a third party's record), offer inspection on a stated date and record what was shown.
