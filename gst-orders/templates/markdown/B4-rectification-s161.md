> **How to use this template.** Use it to correct an error apparent on the face of the record in an order, notice or other document issued by this office, or to reject an application for rectification with reasons. Section 161 is not a review: it cannot re-decide merits, take in new evidence that needs verification, or create a fresh demand. > **Drafting note:** the three-month limit governs when an officer or the person affected brings the error to notice; own-motion rectification is open within six months; a purely clerical or arithmetical slip can be corrected at any time. The Telangana HC has treated duplicate State and Central orders for the same period as rectifiable under section 161 and recorded the State's concession that the six-month bar did not apply (W.P. No. 20731 of 2025 batch, 15.10.2025, binding in Telangana). For orders that denied credit now saved by section 16(5) or 16(6), the special procedure under Notification No. 22/2024-Central Tax (section 148) applied within the time fixed by that notification; check whether that window is still open before relying on it.

**4. Whether the error is apparent on the face of the record**

4.1 An error apparent on the face of the record is one that is plain from the order and the record itself, without long reasoning, fresh evidence or a re-appreciation of the merits. A different view on the law or facts, a point that needs verification of new documents, or a new ground of demand is not such an error.

4.3 [Option B] The application does not point to an error apparent on the record. It asks the undersigned to [re-examine the evidence on [ ] / accept new documents [ ] that were not produced during adjudication / take a different view of [provision]]. That is a request for review on merits, which section 161 does not permit. [The documents filed, A-[ ] to A-[ ], would need verification against [ ], which is beyond rectification.] The proper remedy is an appeal under section 107.

> **Drafting note:** rectification cannot be used for substantive rewriting of an order (*Sajal Kumar Das*, Calcutta HC, 2024, persuasive) or to raise a fresh demand (Madras HC, December 2025, persuasive). A new or larger demand needs a fresh SCN within the time limits. A rejection must state reasons; a cryptic rejection has been quashed (Calcutta HC, 27.11.2025, persuasive).

Even a correction that only increases the arithmetic total of a demand already reasoned in the order is adverse: give notice.
