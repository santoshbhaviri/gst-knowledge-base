> **Drafting note (Variants B and C):** use section 74A for input tax credit relating to financial year 2024-25 onwards, and for an excess provisional refund paid in or after that year (the forty-two months then run from the date of the erroneous refund), and sections 73 / 74 for earlier years. Check the time limit for the notice: under section 74A(2), within forty-two months from the due date of the annual return for the year concerned (or the date of erroneous refund); under sections 73(2) / 74(2), the earlier limits. Invoke the fraud provisions (section 74 or the penalty in section 74A(5)(ii)) only where the facts of fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression are stated in the notice. Sections 73 and 74 apply only to periods up to financial year 2023-24 (sections 73(12) and 74(12)). No notice is issued under section 74A where the amount for a financial year is less than one thousand rupees (proviso to section 74A(1)). The final order must be issued within twelve months of the notice under section 74A(7) (extendable as that section allows), or within the limits in sections 73(10) / 74(10). The final order decides every proposal (para 11.3, and the recovery para: 11.5A in T1 to T4, 11.3A in T5 and T6) and issues the DRC-07 summary.

> **Worked example (hypothetical figures; delete before issue).** Export of goods under LUT, rule 89(4). Net ITC ₹10,00,000; Adjusted Total Turnover ₹1,00,00,000; zero-rated turnover claimed ₹40,00,000. As claimed: 10,00,000 × 40,00,000 ÷ 1,00,00,000 = ₹4,00,000. Ground 1: shipping bills with FOB value of ₹5,00,000 are beyond two years (Explanation 2(a)), so zero-rated turnover becomes ₹35,00,000 (Adjusted Total Turnover is left at ₹1,00,00,000, as the supply remains part of turnover; state the view taken). As proposed: 10,00,000 × 35,00,000 ÷ 1,00,00,000 = ₹3,50,000. Ledger balances: end of period ₹6,00,000, at filing ₹5,50,000, so the least of the three is ₹3,50,000 (para 37 of Circular 125). Proposed for rejection: 4,00,000 − 3,50,000 = ₹50,000.
