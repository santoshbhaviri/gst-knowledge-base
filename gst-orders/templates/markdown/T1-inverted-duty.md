**2. Legal provisions**

2.1 Under clause (ii) of the first proviso to section 54(3) of the CGST Act, a registered person may claim refund of unutilised input tax credit at the end of a tax period where the credit has accumulated on account of the rate of tax on inputs being higher than the rate of tax on output supplies, other than nil-rated or fully exempt supplies, except supplies of goods or services or both notified by the Government on the recommendations of the Council. Supplies of goods so notified are in Notification No. 5/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017, as amended [including by Notification No. 9/2022-Central Tax (Rate) dated 13.07.2022 for goods of chapters 15 and 27, with effect from 18.07.2022, which applies to applications filed on or after that date (Circular No. 181/13/2022-GST)]; supplies of services so notified are in Notification No. 15/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017, as amended.

2.2 The refund is computed under rule 89(5) of the CGST Rules, as amended by Notification No. 14/2022-Central Tax dated 05.07.2022 (which applies to applications filed on or after 05.07.2022: Circular No. 181/13/2022-GST):

> **Maximum Refund Amount = {(Turnover of inverted rated supply of goods and services) × Net ITC ÷ Adjusted Total Turnover} − {Tax payable on such inverted rated supply of goods and services × (Net ITC ÷ ITC availed on inputs and input services)}**

where "Net ITC" means input tax credit availed on **inputs** during the relevant period, and "Adjusted Total Turnover" and "relevant period" have the meanings in rule 89(4) (Explanation (b) to rule 89(5)): Adjusted Total Turnover is the sum of (a) the turnover in the State as defined in section 2(112), excluding the turnover of services, and (b) the turnover of zero-rated supply of services determined under rule 89(4)(D) and of non-zero-rated supply of services, excluding the value of exempt supplies other than zero-rated supplies, during the relevant period (rule 89(4)(E)).

2.3 Circular No. 125/44/2019-GST clarifies that refund under this head is not available for input services and capital goods (para 53); that Net ITC includes credit on all inputs, whatever their rate (para 54); that credit availed in the relevant period counts even if the invoice is of an earlier period (para 61); and that stores and spares charged to the revenue account are not capital goods (para 62). Under para 3.2 of Circular No. 135/05/2020-GST, as substituted by Circular No. 173/05/2022-GST dated 06.07.2022, credit does not accumulate on account of inverted duty structure where the input and the output are the same goods and the rates differ only because the rate changed over time; under para 3.3 so substituted, refund is admissible where the same goods are supplied under a concessional rate notification, so that the output rate is lower than the input rate at the same point of time (other than nil-rated or fully exempt supplies) under a concessional rate notification. Under para 37 of Circular No. 125/44/2019-GST, the refund is the least of (a) the amount computed by the formula, (b) the balance in the electronic credit ledger at the end of the tax period, and (c) the balance in the electronic credit ledger at the time of filing the claim.

2.4 Under rule 89(2)(h), the applicant files a statement of the invoices received and issued during the period (Annexure-B / Statement 1A on the portal) along with Statement 1 (the formula). Under clause (e) of Explanation 2 to section 54, the relevant date is the due date for furnishing the return under section 39 for the period in which the claim for refund arises.

**5. Provisional refund**

5.1 Section 54(6) of the CGST Act provides for provisional refund of ninety per cent of claims on account of zero-rated supplies. By para 5.1 of Instruction No. 06/2025-GST dated 03.10.2025, the Central Government has decided, as an interim measure until section 54(6) is amended (the amendment made by section 155 of the Finance Act, 2026 has not yet been brought into force), that ninety per cent of claims on account of inverted duty structure filed on or after 01.10.2025 may be sanctioned provisionally in the same manner and on the same statutory conditions (para 5.2). Under rule 91(2), as substituted by Notification No. 13/2025-Central Tax dated 17.09.2025 with effect from 01.10.2025, the proper officer is to issue the provisional refund order in FORM GST RFD-04 within seven days of the acknowledgement, on the basis of the identification and evaluation of risk by the system. Under the first proviso to rule 91(2), the proper officer may, for reasons to be recorded in writing, decide that the applicant is not to be granted refund on a provisional basis. Instruction No. 06/2025-GST dated 03.10.2025 (paras 3.1 to 3.3) directs that ninety per cent of the amount claimed be sanctioned provisionally where the system categorises the application as "low-risk", without further scrutiny for that purpose; that applications not categorised as low-risk get no provisional refund and are scrutinised in detail; and that the first proviso to rule 91(2) be used sparingly, case by case, and not on presumptive grounds or merely because routine proceedings such as scrutiny have begun. Provisional refund is not available to the categories notified under section 54(6) by Notification No. 14/2025-Central Tax dated 17.09.2025, namely a person who has not undergone Aadhaar authentication under rule 10B, and a person engaged in the supply of areca nuts (0802 80), pan masala (2106 90 20), tobacco and manufactured tobacco substitutes (chapter 24) or essential oils (3301); nor, under rule 91(1), to a person prosecuted, during the five years before the tax period of the claim, for an offence where the tax evaded exceeds two hundred and fifty lakh rupees.
