**2. Legal provisions**

2.1 Under section 16(1)(b) of the IGST Act, as amended with effect from 01.10.2023, a supply of goods or services or both **for authorised operations** to an SEZ developer or SEZ unit is a zero-rated supply. Under section 16(4) and Notification No. 01/2023-Integrated Tax dated 31.07.2023, as amended by Notification No. 05/2023-Integrated Tax dated 26.10.2023 (deemed to have effect from 01.10.2023), all suppliers to an SEZ developer or unit undertaking authorised operations may supply goods or services (other than the goods in the Table to that notification, namely pan masala, the listed tobacco products and the listed mint essential oils of heading 3301) for authorised operations on payment of integrated tax, and claim refund of the tax so paid.

2.2 Under the proviso to rule 89(1) relating to supplies to a Special Economic Zone (which rule 89(2)(d) and (e) still describe as the second proviso), the supplier of goods files the application after the goods have been admitted in full in the SEZ for authorised operations, as endorsed by the specified officer; and the supplier of services files it with evidence of receipt of the services for authorised operations, as endorsed by the specified officer. Rule 89(2)(d) and (e) require the corresponding statements of invoices with that endorsement, and **rule 89(2)(f)** requires a declaration that tax has not been collected from the SEZ unit or developer.

2.3 Under clause (ba) of Explanation 2 to section 54, the relevant date is the due date for furnishing the return under section 39 in respect of such supplies.

2.4 This is a refund of tax paid, not of input tax credit. The formula in rule 89(4) does not apply, and the eligibility of the applicant's input tax credit is not an issue in this claim.

**5. Provisional refund**

5.1 Section 54(6) of the CGST Act, read with rule 91 of the CGST Rules, provides for refund on a provisional basis of ninety per cent of the amount claimed on account of zero-rated supply of goods or services. Under rule 91(2), as substituted by Notification No. 13/2025-Central Tax dated 17.09.2025 with effect from 01.10.2025, the proper officer is to issue the provisional refund order in FORM GST RFD-04 within seven days of the acknowledgement, on the basis of the identification and evaluation of risk by the system. Under the first proviso to rule 91(2), the proper officer may, for reasons to be recorded in writing, decide that the applicant is not to be granted refund on a provisional basis. Instruction No. 06/2025-GST dated 03.10.2025 (paras 3.1 to 3.3) directs that ninety per cent of the amount claimed be sanctioned provisionally where the system categorises the application as "low-risk", without further scrutiny for that purpose; that applications not categorised as low-risk get no provisional refund and are scrutinised in detail; and that the first proviso to rule 91(2) be used sparingly, case by case, and not on presumptive grounds or merely because routine proceedings such as scrutiny have begun. Provisional refund is not available to the categories notified under section 54(6) by Notification No. 14/2025-Central Tax dated 17.09.2025, namely a person who has not undergone Aadhaar authentication under rule 10B, and a person engaged in the supply of areca nuts (0802 80), pan masala (2106 90 20), tobacco and manufactured tobacco substitutes (chapter 24) or essential oils (3301); nor, under rule 91(1), to a person prosecuted, during the five years before the tax period of the claim, for an offence where the tax evaded exceeds two hundred and fifty lakh rupees.
