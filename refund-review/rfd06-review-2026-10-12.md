**Reviewed:** 12 Oct 2026. **Basis:** the six orders uploaded to the project, checked against the CGST Act and Rules in [gst-law/text/](../gst-law/text/). Taxpayers are referred to as Order 1 to Order 6 (the upload order), not by name.

## The verdict in brief
- **The orders are sound on the main things.** I recomputed every refund under rule 89(4)/89(5) and each sanctioned amount is arithmetically correct. All claims are within limitation, the RFD-08/RFD-09 hearing step was followed before any rejection (rule 92(3)), and the orders appear well within the 60 days of s.54(7).
- **The weak spots are drafting and documentation, not outcomes.** Template text copied from export orders, an outdated appeal preamble, figures that are adopted without showing their source, and a few factual inconsistencies would give an appellate authority or audit easy points.
- **One procedural step is missing in five orders:** the re-credit of the rejected amount to the credit ledger (rule 93(2), FORM PMT-03).

---

## 1. Points that affect the legal soundness of the orders

**1.1 Rejected ITC is not re-credited (Orders 1, 2, 3, 5, 6).** Rule 93(2) says the amount debited from the electronic credit ledger, to the extent rejected, "shall be re-credited … by an order made in FORM GST PMT-03". The Explanation treats a refund as finally rejected once the claimant undertakes in writing not to appeal. In every partly rejected order the taxpayer accepted the rejection, so the order should record that undertaking and direct PMT-03 re-credit. Without it the taxpayer loses ₹1,23,507 of ITC across these five orders, which is a likely grievance or appeal.

**1.2 Order 4 used the wrong instrument for an outstanding demand.** An apparent GSTR-1/GSTR-3B difference was put into an RFD-08 proposing *rejection*. Under s.54(10) the officer may only *withhold* or *deduct* tax "which the taxable person is liable to pay but which remains unpaid", and only where it has not been stayed; withholding is done in RFD-07 (rule 92(2)), not by rejection. An unconfirmed "apparent difference" is not an unpaid liability. The final order reached the right result, but the notice should not have been issued, and the demand it relied on had already been set aside in appeal eleven months earlier (OIA dated 20.11.2025).

**1.3 The appeal preamble is outdated (all six orders).**
- It speaks of "10% of the duty demanded". Section 107(6) requires the admitted amount in full plus 10% of the *tax in dispute*, capped at ₹20 crore, or 10% of the penalty in a penalty-only order. "Duty" is Central Excise language.
- It says APL-01 "shall be filed in duplicate" and must carry "court fee/stamp duty". Rule 108(1) requires APL-01 to be filed **electronically**; there is no court fee.
- The appellate authority is named inconsistently: "Commissioner (Appeals-II)" in Orders 1–5, "Additional/Joint Commissioner (Appeals-I)" and then "Commissioner (Appeals-I)" in Order 6, while the appeal in Order 4 was in fact decided by a Joint Commissioner (Appeals-II).
- A wrong statement of appeal rights can be argued as a ground for condoning delay, so it is worth fixing once in the template.

**1.4 The unjust-enrichment finding cites the wrong clause (Orders 1, 2, 3, 5).** Para 7(d) quotes s.54(8)(a), "tax paid on export of goods or services". For inverted-duty refunds the right clause is s.54(8)(b), "refund of unutilised input tax credit under sub-section (3)". Order 6 quotes it correctly.

**1.5 Export findings appear in orders that involve no export (Orders 1, 2, 3, 5, 6).** Para 7(g) records that "goods exported out of India are not subjected to export duty" and no drawback was claimed. Order 6 (SEZ supplies) also says shipping bills were "verified with digitally signed RIDs … downloaded from ICEGATE". None of this applies to an inverted-duty or SEZ claim, and a reviewer may read it as a sign the claim was not actually examined.

**1.6 No finding that the inversion exists (Orders 1, 2, 3, 5).** None of the inverted-duty orders records the input and output rates and HSN, or confirms that the outputs are not among the goods excluded from inverted-duty refund under Notification 5/2017-Central Tax (Rate). Order 2 notes that the outputs are supplied at **5% and 18%** and inputs bought at 18% and 5%, but does not show that the 18% supplies were left out of the "turnover of inverted-rated supplies". One paragraph with a rate-wise table of inputs and outputs would close this.

**1.7 Order 6 (zero-rated) does not deal with provisional refund.** For zero-rated supplies, s.54(6) and rule 91(2) provide 90% provisional refund in RFD-04 within 7 days of acknowledgement, unless the officer records reasons for not granting it. The order is silent. It should either cite the RFD-04 and adjust it, or record why provisional refund was not given.

**1.8 Order 6 asked for a certificate the law does not require.** The RFD-08 asked for the CA certificate under rule 89(2)(m). The proviso to that clause exempts claims under s.54(8)(a) to (d) and (f), which covers ITC refunds. Asking for it adds delay and is not a ground for rejection.

## 2. Figures that should be explained in the order

**2.1 Tax payable on inverted supplies is adopted without its source (Orders 1, 2, 3, 5).** This figure drives most of the disallowance, yet each order only says it was "verified from the relevant outward-supply details and returns". For example, in Order 5 the officer adopts ₹19,64,059 against the applicant's figure; in Order 3, ₹8,05,833. A short rate-wise table from GSTR-1 would make the disallowance defensible.

**2.2 The rule for choosing between RFD-01, invoice statement and returns is applied unevenly.** In Order 5 the *lowest* inverted turnover (RFD-01) is combined with the *highest* adjusted total turnover (GSTR-1/3B), which both reduce the refund. In Order 2 the GSTR-1 figure is used for turnover and the RFD-01 figure for adjusted total turnover. A stated policy, ideally taking every component from the same source for the same period, would avoid an argument that the method is selective.

**2.3 The reason for rejection in Order 1 does not match the arithmetic.** The order says ₹4,042 of ITC was excluded under s.17(5), but the Net ITC used in the calculation (₹1,54,95,046) is the RFD-01 figure, and "ITC on inputs and input services" (₹1,66,40,640) is not the Annexure-B total less ₹4,042. The ₹20,259 rejection actually comes from the second part of the formula (tax payable × Net ITC ÷ ITC on inputs and input services). The order also prints the zero-rated formula of rule 89(4) under the heading "Refund Calculation as per Rule 89(5)" and shows no working.

**2.4 Order 6 mixes an ITC amount with its refund effect.** The notice proposed ₹4,774 of ineligible ITC; the reply "accepted the adjustment of ₹2,771". Both are right (₹2,771 is the refund effect of removing ₹4,774 of ITC), but the order should say so. It also gives the eligible refund as both ₹38,40,943.77 and ₹38,40,943.56.

**2.5 Unexplained gaps in the returns.**
- **Order 6:** zero-rated turnover in GSTR-1/3B is ₹3,86,01,810 but only ₹3,10,47,906 was claimed. The ₹75.5 lakh gap may be supplies with IGST paid or a further refund the taxpayer is entitled to; the order should say which.
- **Order 3:** ITC in GSTR-3B is ₹1,45,18,339 but Annexure-B totals ₹99,82,227, a gap of about ₹45 lakh, which is not discussed.

## 4. Scope for improvement

**4.1 One corrected template per refund category.** Most points above come from a single shared template. Fixing it once (preamble, s.54(8) clause, removing export-only findings, a rate-wise inversion table, a PMT-03 paragraph and an explicit source for each figure) would fix every future order. I can draft templates for inverted duty, SEZ/export without tax and cash-ledger refunds.

**4.2 A calculation sheet that produces the order's tables.** All six orders build the same tables by hand, which is where the paise, the wrong formula and the duplicated figures crept in. A spreadsheet that takes RFD-01, Annexure-B and GSTR-1/3B figures and outputs the rule 89(4)/(5) working, the rounding under s.170 and the re-credit amount would remove those errors. I can build it.

**4.3 A pre-order checklist.** Rate and HSN inversion; Notification 5/2017-CT(R) exclusions; provisional refund for zero-rated claims; demands checked against appeal outcomes before any RFD-08; PMT-03 re-credit; dates checked across the order.

**4.4 Changes coming from the 57th Council that will matter for these taxpayers.** None is law until notified.
- Refund of ITC on **input services** is recommended **from 1.11.2026**. That would change these claims sharply: Order 3 shows ₹44.3 lakh of input-service ITC in one quarter, against a refund of ₹86,736.
- Refund of ITC on **capital goods** is recommended **from 1.4.2027**. Order 5 shows ₹1.79 crore of capital-goods ITC.
- **90% provisional, automatic sanction** for inverted-duty and zero-rated claims based on risk scoring, and machine-readable RFD-01 data.
- So the division should expect larger claims, a shift from processing claims to checking risk, and less time per claim.

**4.5 Large accumulated credit is a risk to watch.** Credit-ledger balances far exceed the refunds claimed: about ₹15.8 crore (Order 3), ₹2.8 crore (Order 5), ₹1.6 crore (Order 2) and ₹1.3 crore (Order 1). Once input-service and capital-goods refunds open, these balances become refund claims. Checking now that the underlying ITC is genuine (supplier filing, GSTR-2B match, suppliers' risk profiles) would protect revenue later.

**4.6 Link appeal outcomes to the liability register.** Order 4 shows a demand set aside in appeal still being picked up in refund scrutiny eleven months later. A routine update of DRC-07 / the electronic liability register after each Order-in-Appeal would prevent avoidable notices and taxpayer grievances.

## Correction (added 12 Oct 2026)
**1.7 also applies to the inverted-duty orders.** Instruction 06/2025-GST para 5.1 extends 90% provisional refund to inverted-duty claims filed on or after 01.10.2025, as an interim measure. Orders 1, 2 and 5 were filed after that date, so each should record the RFD-04 and adjust it, or record why provisional refund was not given.
